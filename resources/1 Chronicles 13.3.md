## Commentary
When David says, “Let us bring the ark of our God back to us, for we have not inquired at it since the days of Saul,” he's expressing a desire to restore the presence of God to the center of Israel's national life. To understand the significance of this statement, we need to consider what had happened to the ark in the preceding decades.

During the final years of Eli's priesthood, Israel suffered a devastating defeat at the hands of the Philistines. Rather than seeking the Lord, the elders brought the ark of the covenant into battle, believing its presence would guarantee their victory. Instead, Israel was defeated, Eli's sons were killed, and the ark was captured (==1 Samuel 4:10–11==).

After God afflicted the Philistines, they returned the ark to Israel. It was eventually taken to the house of Abinadab in Kirjath Jearim, where it remained for many years (==1 Samuel 7:1–2==).

The ark was significant because it represented God's covenant presence among His people. The Lord had instructed Moses to construct it, and above its mercy seat, between the two cherubim, God had promised to meet with Moses and speak to him (==Exodus 25:21–22==). It was the place where God had chosen to reveal Himself and communicate His will to Israel.

Yet throughout Saul's reign, the ark remained outside the center of Israel's national worship. This is what David is referring to when he says they had not inquired at it since the days of Saul. He's acknowledging that Israel had neglected something essential to its identity as God's covenant people.

Now that David had been established as king over all Israel, he wanted to correct this. He wanted Israel to become a people who sought the Lord again. He understood that Israel's strength wasn't ultimately found in its king, its armies, or even its possession of the promised land. Their distinction among the nations was that the living God had chosen to dwell among them.
