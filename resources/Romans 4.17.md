## Commentary
"...God, who gives life to the dead and calls those things which do not exist as though they did."

This is how God talks. He stands in front of situations of death, famine, barrenness, and hopelessness, and He *speaks*. "Lazarus, come forth." "Peace, be still." "Let there be light." When He speaks, He creates. Paul reaches for this exact description of God to explain Abraham's faith—a childless old man believing God's word about descendants he could not yet see, because the God who promised is the God who calls things that do not exist as though they did.

We take what God has already said in His word and we speak it over our circumstances—by faith proclaiming things not yet seen as though they were (==2 Corinthians 4:13==, ==Hebrews 11:1==). We don't wait until the situation looks alive to start speaking life.

But we have to be careful not to get this backwards. Calling things that are *not* as though they *were* is faith. Calling things that *are* as though they were *not* is denial—and God never asks us for denial. We don't pretend the diagnosis doesn't exist, the account isn't empty, or the child isn't far from God. We are not delusional. We simply refuse to let the problem have the last word. As Charles Capps put it, "We don't deny sickness, we deny its right to exist in our bodies."

We don't deny the empty bank account—we deny poverty the right to overtake us.
We don't deny the prodigal—we deny the enemy the right to keep them.
We don't deny suffering—we deny suffering the right to destroy our faith and redefine God.
We don't deny reality—we deny reality the right to contradict the word of God.

Notice too *where* we aim our words. The enemy kills; God creates. So we don't spend our breath cursing the rent payment—we speak to the business idea and tell it to live. We speak to the solution God wants to create, not endlessly at the problem. That's what our Father does: He gives life to dead things.

## Application
Pick one situation in your life that currently looks dead. Find what God has actually said that touches it—a promise, a truth about His character, a word of Scripture (not wishful thinking, His word). Declare what God has said over that situation every day this week.
