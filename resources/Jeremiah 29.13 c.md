## Commentary
Imagine showing up to someone’s house unannounced. They crack the door just enough to see who’s bothering them—“Ohhh, hiii, what a surprise”—and everything about their face says you’re not wanted. Now imagine the same knock, but the door swings wide open, they pull you in with a hug: “What an awesome surprise! Come in!”

Many of us think God is a barely-crack-the-door, half-annoyed, arms-crossed kind of god, so we approach Him with hesitation, unsure if we’re interrupting. But that isn’t the God Jesus came to reveal. This is the Father who sees the prodigal a long way off and *runs*, interrupting the rehearsed apology with an embrace (==Luke 15:20==).

Here’s a truth that can reorder your whole life: you have as much of God as you want. You don’t have as much time as you want—time is limited. You don’t have as much money as you want—money is limited. But God is not a limited resource. He’s infinite and inexhaustible, and He isn’t rationing Himself like we’re on spiritual food-stamps. Good fathers don’t hold back from their children: “If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!” (==Luke 11:13==). How much more will God give *God* to those who ask.

Now hear the promise of this verse: “You will seek Me and *find* Me.” Don’t gloss over that. The uncreated God of the universe—the One who upholds creation by the word of His power, before whom the living creatures cry “Holy!”—that very real, very living God wants to be found by little ole’ you. And hear the condition: “when you search for Me with all your heart.” He’s a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him (==Hebrews 11:6==)—not of the curious, not of the lucky. The supply is unlimited. The only limiting factor is you.

You have as much of God as you want. The question is: how much do you want Him?
