## Commentary
When we first began going into Mosul, Iraq for ministry in January of 2017, the city had been occupied by ISIS for two and a half years. Some areas would not be fully liberated until June of that year. For years, the people lived under unspeakable darkness.

Out of necessity, they had adapted to oppressive rule. They were governed by fear and the constant reminder of death.

Even the children’s schoolbooks had been changed to reflect death and numb children to the presence of it. Instead of teaching kids to count by adding apples, the textbooks used bullets and mortars to teach addition and subtraction.

That's what darkness does. It doesn't just threaten people. It disciples them. 

A few weeks after the battle to defeat ISIS began in October of 2016, neighborhoods began to be liberated.

Word started spreading through the streets, "ISIS is no longer in charge here. It's okay to come out of hiding."

But if you had been there, you would have seen that nothing appeared to have changed. The buildings were still destroyed. There were still ISIS snipers hiding in certain places. There was still fear.

But in reality, everything had changed. A new authority was in charge. And that authority came to set people free from the tyranny of the terrorists.

But the people had to decide whether they would keep living under the habits of captivity or begin learning the ways of freedom.

_That’s_ what's beginning to happen here in the second half of Matthew 4. "The people dwelling in darkness have seen a great light.” Jesus declares a message of freedom, “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”

It’s an announcement of liberation. The King has entered occupied territory. The reign of heaven has come.
