## Commentary
Paul is dealing with a serious question: if Israel had been entrusted with the oracles of God, and yet many were unfaithful with what they received, does that mean God’s covenant faithfulness failed? If the people chosen to carry the word of God mishandled that word, does that make the word itself unreliable? If the people marked by covenant were inconsistent, does that mean the covenant-keeping God was inconsistent too?

Paul’s answer is emphatic: absolutely not.

Human failure does not cancel divine faithfulness. What God said He will do does not become untrue because His people mishandled their blessing. The unbelief of man does not have the power to make God unfaithful. Israel may have been entrusted with the oracles of God, but Israel’s failure to rightly steward that trust did not change the nature of the One who gave it.

This is important because many people wrestle with God through the failures of those who claimed to represent Him. They heard the word of God from people who did not live like the God of the word. They saw leaders preach truth and then walk in hypocrisy. They watched churches speak of holiness while tolerating compromise, or speak of love while inflicting wounds. And when that happens, we can begin to wonder, _If this is what God’s people are like, what does that say about God?_

Paul gives us an important distinction: the unfaithfulness of God’s people exposes the condition of God’s people. It does not expose a flaw in God.

## Application
God’s character is not on trial because man is unfaithful. "If we are faithless, He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself" (==2 Timothy 2:13==). His truth does not bend under the weight of human rebellion. In fact, Scripture often reveals God’s faithfulness most clearly against the backdrop of human failure. Israel’s unfaithfulness exposed Israel, not God.

So don't let the unfaithfulness of people become the lens through which you interpret the faithfulness of God. Let God tell you who He is. Let Jesus reveal the Father. Let the cross be the final word about His character.

People may mishandle the things of God. God does not.
