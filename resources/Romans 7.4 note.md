## Commentary
“Therefore, my brethren, you also have become dead to the law through the body of Christ, that you may be married to another—to Him who was raised from the dead, that we should bear fruit to God.”

Paul has just made his point about the jurisdiction of the law by using an illustration: a wife is bound by law to her husband while he lives, but if the husband dies, she is released and free to marry another (==Romans 7:2-3==). Now he applies it, and he changes one detail. In the illustration, the husband dies. In the application, *you* did—“you also have become dead to the law through the body of Christ.” The believer's release from the law didn't come by the law being repealed. It came by a death, and the death happened in a body: when Christ's body hung on the cross, everyone united to Him died there with Him (==Galatians 2:20==). “I through the law died to the law that I might live to God” (==Galatians 2:19==).

Picture a widow on her second wedding day. When she signs her new name, everything from the old life loses its claim on her—the old obligations don't follow her into the new marriage. But the release was never the point. The point is the man waiting at the altar. Paul says the same about the believer: you died to the law *that you may be married to another*—to Him who was raised from the dead. Freedom from the law is not freedom into independence; it's freedom into a new union. The bride belongs to a living Husband now (==2 Corinthians 11:2==).

And the verse ends with the purpose of every marriage in Scripture: fruit. “That we should bear fruit to God.” Under the old union with the law there was fruit too—Paul says in the next verse that our sinful passions, aroused by the law, bore fruit to death (==Romans 7:5==). The new union produces a different harvest, because the new Husband is alive and His life flows into the marriage (==John 15:5==; ==Galatians 5:22-23==). The believer doesn't bear fruit for God by straining under obligation. Fruit comes the way it always comes in a marriage—from union with the One we now belong to, serving in the newness of the Spirit and not in the oldness of the letter (==Romans 7:6==).
