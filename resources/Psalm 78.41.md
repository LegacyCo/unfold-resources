## Commentary
This is one of the most striking lines in Psalm 78. Israel “limited the Holy One of Israel.” This doesn't mean God lost power or ceased to be sovereign. No human unbelief can reduce God’s actual authority, strength, or ability. The Lord is not made smaller by the smallness of human faith. But Israel treated God as though He were smaller than He had already revealed Himself to be.

They saw Him split the sea, then questioned whether He could provide bread. They saw Him provide bread, then questioned whether He could give water. They saw Him provide water, then questioned whether He could give meat (Exodus 14:21–31; 16:1–15; 17:1–7; Numbers 11:4–23).

Again and again, their unbelief reduced their expectation of God to the size of the crisis in front of them. Instead of interpreting their circumstances through the lens of faith, they interpreted God through the pressure of their circumstances.

## Application
One of the dangers of unbelief is that it trains us to expect less from God than He has revealed about Himself.

We begin to pray smaller, obey smaller, hope smaller, and risk smaller. Not because God has changed, but because we have allowed fear, disappointment, or pressure to define what we believe is possible.

Psalm 78 calls us to remember the God who has already revealed Himself. The question is not whether God is able. The question is whether we will trust Him as the God He has already proven Himself to be.
