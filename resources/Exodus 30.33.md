## Commentary
The holy anointing oil was not something Israel was permitted to alter, imitate, or reproduce for its own purposes. Its ingredients and proportions were given by God, and its use belonged entirely to Him. Anyone who manufactured something resembling it was to be cut off from the people. God was making it clear that what is holy cannot be produced by human creativity or approximated through religious technique.

Art Katz writes in _Apostolic Foundations_:

“How many times have we been guilty of fabricating the holy oil? Every time we turn the amplifiers up at our worship services, or give our voices a little boost and add a little razzmatazz, or something out of our own soulishness in order to bring effect, or give an invitation that we know is calculated to play upon the emotional responses of our hearers, then that is false anointing oil. That is making a product like it, but not the real thing. It is not trusting absolutely in God to give the power of the anointing that is needed to obtain the outcome that God desires through His word.”

The danger is not limited to what happens from a platform or within a church service. It reaches into anything we attempt to do for the Lord. We can pray in order to sound spiritual, serve in order to be noticed, give in order to be admired, speak in order to control an outcome, or act with urgency because we are afraid that God will not move unless we help Him. We may obtain the response we wanted and still have acted entirely out of our own soul.

The issue is not whether something is effective. The issue is its source. A thing may look spiritual and still be produced by human strength. It may accomplish something visible and still have no origin in God. False oil is whatever we add from ourselves in order to produce the appearance of divine activity.

This doesn't mean that preparation, excellence, emotion, or effort are inherently wrong. The question is whether they are surrendered to the Holy Spirit or being used as substitutes for Him. The moment we rely upon human energy to produce what only God can give, we have begun mixing an imitation oil. We are no longer trusting God to accomplish His purpose through what He has asked us to do; we are attempting to secure the result ourselves.

The genuine anointing cannot be manufactured, and it does not require manipulation to sustain it. It comes from God, remains under His government, and produces the result He desires—not necessarily the result we hoped to obtain. Whatever we do for the Lord must therefore carry the same spirit expressed by Paul: “My speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power,” so that faith would “not be in the wisdom of men but in the power of God” (1 Corinthians 2:4–5).

## Application
We must examine not only what we are doing for the Lord, but what we are depending upon to make it effective. Are we trusting God to give power to our obedience, or are we adding  human strength because we're afraid simple faithfulness will not be enough? Whether we are praying, serving, giving, speaking, parenting, creating, leading, encouraging, or helping another person, our responsibility is not to manufacture a result. It is to simply obey God with a pure heart, surrender the outcome to Him, and refuse to imitate through human effort what can only be produced by the Holy Spirit.
