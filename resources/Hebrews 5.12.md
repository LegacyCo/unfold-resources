## Commentary
The writer of Hebrews uses two important words in this verse:
-  *Stoicheion* - the vertical stick on a sundial that casts the shadow, to be in line with the rudiments, to conform to the battle line.
	- This word is also used in [[Acts 21.24]], "...they will see that you too are in the ranks as one who keeps the law." Compare this to [[Galatians 5.25]], "Conduct our lives in conformity with the Spirit." The image is of keeping consistency in a given direction according to original design; "follow in the footsteps of the faith of Abraham" ([[Romans 4.12]]).
	- [[Galatians 4.25]] illustrates the line of descent that still keeps the pattern of slavery: Hagar - Sinai - Jerusalem (the law)
- *Arches* - the beginning, starting point, original, to begin first by doing something which is in contrast to what one does later.

The linking of these two words (*stoicheion arches*) has a derogtatory ring that emphasizes the reminder that the recipients of this letter are not familiar with the first principles of the faith, they are **immature**, the elementary doctrines mentioned in [[Hebrews 6.1|Hebrews 6.1ff]]. 



The writer of Hebrews is confronting a contradiction in God's people: they had been around the things of God long enough to be teachers, yet are still living like beginners. While they had been given every opportunity, they had not arrived at the place of maturity. So he says they need someone to teach them again the first principles of the oracles of God. That wording used here should not be ignored.

The word translated “principles” is **_stoicheion_**. It's the vertical stick on a sundial that casts the shadow. It means to be in line with the rudiments, to conform to the battle line.

Then there is the word **_arches_**, which points to **the beginning**, the starting point, the first place. 

Put together, the emphasis has a derogatory tone: these believers were still occupied with the elementary things that belong to the very beginning.

The issue is not that the foundations were wrong. The issue is that they were still living at the level of **_stoicheion_** and **_arches_** when they should have gone on to maturity. They were still at the starting line when they should have been carrying weight.

A beginning is good, but only if it leads somewhere. Foundations are necessary, but they are not the whole house. Milk is right for an infant, but a grown man can't survive on a diet of milk. The writer is exposing the condition of people who had enough time to mature and yet were still circling what should have been established long ago.

The elementary things of God are meant to be the doorway, not the destination. They are meant to be laid as foundation, not revisited endlessly because growth has stalled.

## Application
There comes a point where living at the level of **_stoicheion_** and **_arches_** is no longer cute.

It's one thing to begin there. We all begin there. But it's another thing to remain there year after year, always needing to be brought back to the start because the foundations of the faith have not been formed in you. God does not save us merely to keep us at the level of beginnings. He saves us to bring us into maturity. We are "predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son" (Romans 8:29).
 
Honor the foundation—but go on to maturity.
