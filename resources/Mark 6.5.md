## Commentary
“Now He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them.”

There is a difference between the omnipresence of God and the manifest presence of God. The omnipresence is His always-presence—“the whole earth is full of His glory” (==Isaiah 6:3==), and wherever two or three gather in His name, He is there (==Matthew 18:20==). The manifest presence is different: the revealed, tangible, weighty presence that changes the atmosphere of a room. Nazareth shows the gap between the two. Jesus was present in His hometown—physically standing among the people—and yet He could do no mighty work there. Jesus was there, but Jesus could not be Himself there.

What made the difference? “He marveled because of their unbelief” (==Mark 6:6==). The people of Nazareth had faith, but their faith was in an idea. They believed in who they thought the Messiah was supposed to be—the conquering king on a white horse—and the carpenter's son standing in front of them did not match the idea. Their faith was in a concept, not in the Man before them. 

The church can live in the same condition. Some congregations put their faith in denominational lineage and doctrine; some in weekly attendance numbers; some in the charisma of a leader or the popularity of a worship team; some in the healings they have seen. None of those things are wrong—but when they become the object of faith, Jesus may heal a few sick people among us and still not be Himself in our midst. He is always with His people; He said He would never leave (==Hebrews 13:5==). That is never the question. The question is whether He can be Himself when He is here—and faith in Him, rather than in our idea of Him, is what makes space for Him to be Himself when He is with us.
