## Commentary
“…that if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.”

This verse joins two things that belong together: what the heart believes and what the mouth does about it. In Scripture, believing is never just hearing and agreeing; believing is hearing and *responding*. Noah believed that the God who spoke of rain and a flood was the one true and living God—and his belief took the shape of obedience: he built the ark “according to all that God commanded him” (==Genesis 6:22==). Everyone in his region had heard about the boat. Only eight people responded by getting on it (==1 Peter 3:20==). Knowledge about the ark saved no one; entering it did.

Confession is the response this verse points us to. We believe that God raised Jesus from the dead, and we declare that Jesus is alive. We believe the love God has for us, and we declare that Jesus is the Son of God—“whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God abides in him, and he in God” (==1 John 4:15-16==). Faith that responds to what it believes becomes the key that opens the door of life. The gospel announces that God has prepared a way for you to be with Him; the response of faith is to place your life inside that provision—“Lord, my life is now Yours.”

## Word Study
**Confess** is the Greek *homologeō* (ὁμολογέω)—literally “to say the same thing”: to agree out loud with what God has declared. Jesus uses it of public allegiance: “whoever confesses Me before men, him I will also confess before My Father” (==Matthew 10:32==). John uses it of honest agreement with God about sin (==1 John 1:9==), and Hebrews calls praise “the fruit of our lips, giving thanks to His name”—the same root (==Hebrews 13:15==). Summary: to confess the Lord Jesus is to say with your mouth what heaven has already said about Him—and to stake your life on it.

## Application
Ask whether your believing has ever become responding. Agreeing that the gospel is true is not the same as walking through the door it opens—the crowds around Noah agreed the boat existed. If you have heard and believed but never surrendered, make the confession this verse describes: declare with your mouth that Jesus is Lord—of your life, not just in general—and entrust yourself to Him the way Noah entrusted himself to the ark. The response is what sets a believer apart from an admirer.
