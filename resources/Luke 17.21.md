## Commentary
When the Pharisees demanded to know when the kingdom of God would come, Jesus gave an answer that dismantled their whole timeline: “The kingdom of God does not come with observation… For indeed, the kingdom of God is within you.” Not someday. Not somewhere. Within you, in your midst—present tense. Jesus talked about His kingdom as at hand (==Mark 1:15==), near, accessible, already operating.

Kingdom people live with an awareness: what is going on *up there* is now possible *down here*—and inversely, what isn’t going on up there shouldn’t be tolerated down here. That one conviction explains the whole lifestyle of Jesus People. Why do we pray for sick people to be healed? Because there is no sickness in the kingdom of heaven. Why do we meet the needs of people around us? Because there are no unmet needs in that kingdom. Why do we love our enemies and pray for those who persecute us? Because that’s the way of the King. Why do we endure suffering with hope? Because in this kingdom, power is perfected in weakness (==2 Corinthians 12:9==). Why do we share our faith and live on mission? Because the King desires all to be saved (==1 Timothy 2:4==). None of those are personality traits. They’re the culture of the Kingdom of God.

“But I keep praying for the sick and nothing happens.” Fair—now answer this: does everyone get saved when you share your faith? Does that stop you from sharing your faith? Kingdom people don’t take their orders from their results. They take them from the culture of the King.

Think of it this way. Suppose you came home tonight and found that someone had moved all your furniture out and all their stuff in. You confront them, and they respond, “Sorry, this is my house now.” You wouldn’t negotiate with him, and you wouldn’t cry yourself to sleep in the driveway. You’d call the police, and the law would be enforced—because you hold the contract, and the contract says the house is yours. In the same way, all around us, the enemy is trying to move into houses that don’t belong to him—into our schools, our marriages, our health, our communities—trying to occupy what Jesus secured with the victory of His blood. Kingdom people are the ones who know what the contract says. When we encounter something that’s illegal in the kingdom of God, we don’t shrug at the squatter. As ambassadors of the King (==2 Corinthians 5:20==), we set things back in order—and cause earth to line up with heaven, exactly the way the King taught us to pray (==Matthew 6:10==).
