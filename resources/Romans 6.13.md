## Commentary
“And do not present your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but present yourselves to God as being alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God.”

Sin can only reign in you if you keep presenting yourself to it. Paul has just said, “do not let sin reign in your mortal body” (==Romans 6:12==)—and this verse explains how sin reigns at all. Not by force. Sin has no authority over someone who belongs to Jesus; the certificate of debt is nailed to the cross and the powers are disarmed (==Colossians 2:14-15==). The only way sin reigns in a house is by being handed the keys. The enemy cannot take from you what you do not give him. Paul never says a believer won't sin; he says don't let it *reign*—don't go on presenting yourself to it.

There is a legal concept called squatter's rights: if someone occupies a property long enough without being challenged, he can eventually claim a right to stay. The squatter was never invited and holds no deed—but unchallenged presence becomes a right to remain. That's how the enemy comes to operate with a measure of authority in a believer's life. Not because of a mistake—repentance cancels the mistake—but because the eviction notice stopped being served. Comfortable, consistent compromise gives the enemy ground he otherwise has no right to; tolerate what Christ died to free you from, and the squatter starts making himself at home. Habitual patterns of sin are open doors: what we let ourselves gaze at, what we linger with, the habits we refuse to quit, the accusations we keep speaking over ourselves, the agreements we make with shame and fear (==Psalm 1:1==). And an open door in a person's life is never just their own—the people behind walk through it too. This is how patterns of sin thread through family lines: no one in the line stopped presenting himself to the family sin, and no one told a man in that family that Jesus breaks the pattern and gives him a new name (==Ephesians 4:27==).

## Application
Ask the diagnostic question—not “have you sinned?” (you have), but “where have you sat down in it?” Name the place you keep presenting yourself: the screen, the anger, the bottle, the unforgiveness, the account balance you check for comfort. Then serve the eviction notice: repent, close the door, and present yourself to God as one alive from the dead.
