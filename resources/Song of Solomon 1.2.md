## Commentary
“Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth—for your love is better than wine.”

The Song opens with desire, and the desire is the point. The bride is not asking to be filled; she is asking to be kissed—and the difference between those two kinds of satisfaction explains something important about seeking God. Touching Him, finding Him, experiencing Him satisfies like nothing else—but He satisfies like a kiss, not a ribeye. A steak fills you up and removes the desire for more; that is what it is designed to do. A kiss does the opposite. No lover was ever satisfied with just one; the first kiss is intended to be the beginning, igniting a passion for more. We don't come to God with hunger the way we come to a buffet line. We come to Him with the hunger of a lover—a hunger that His nearness deepens rather than ends (==Psalm 34:8==; ==Psalm 63:1-2==).

This is why seeking Him with a whole heart means refusing to be satisfied with yesterday's blessing—not when His mercies are new every morning (==Lamentations 3:22-23==). Israel could not live on yesterday's manna (==Exodus 16:19-20==). Rejoice in what God did in the past, and let yesterday's blessing be the fuel that drives you to seek Him for more today.
