## Commentary
“The sinners in Zion are afraid; fearfulness has seized the hypocrites: ‘Who among us shall dwell with the devouring fire? Who among us shall dwell with everlasting burnings?’”

Isaiah is not talking about hell here. The question is asked *in Zion*, about dwelling in the presence of God. The devouring fire, the everlasting burnings, is God Himself: “our God is a consuming fire” (==Hebrews 12:29==). The sinners and hypocrites in Zion are terrified because they’ve realized something the comfortable religious mind avoids: God’s presence is not a mild place. The question isn’t whether anyone will encounter the fire of God—everyone will. The question is who can *dwell* in it.

And Isaiah answers his own question in the very next verse: “He who walks righteously and speaks uprightly” (==Isaiah 33:15==). The righteous don’t escape the fire—they’re the ones who can live in it. This is the same confidence Jude reaches at the end of his letter: “to Him who is able to keep you from stumbling, and to present you faultless before the presence of His glory with exceeding joy” (==Jude 1:24==). Faultless, in the presence of the glory—standing in the everlasting burnings, not consumed but *glad*. How? Not by our own flame-proofing. Those found in Christ have been made the righteousness of God (==2 Corinthians 5:21==), crucified with Him and alive with His resurrection life (==Galatians 2:20==)—and what the fire consumes is everything that isn’t Him.

Consider what that means about the day every person meets the fire. The same fire will be at both judgments—the devouring fire that consumes the wicked is the very glory that will make the righteous shine. “Then the righteous will shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father” (==Matthew 13:43==). One fire, two outcomes, and the difference is what it finds when it touches you. Everyone touches the fire eventually, but only the righteous are invited to be baptized in it *now* (==Matthew 3:11==). Let it burn in you today—purifying, igniting, consuming what’s opposed to God.
