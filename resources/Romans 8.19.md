## Commentary
“For the earnest expectation of the creation eagerly waits for the revealing of the sons of God.”

Ask the average person what creation needs and you’ll hear: less of us. Fewer humans, less footprint, smaller impact. Paul says the opposite. Creation isn’t groaning to be rescued *from* humanity—it’s groaning *for* humanity. Specifically, for “the revealing of the sons of God”: image bearers who look like Jesus and walk in His authority. Creation was subjected to futility when its appointed rulers fell (==Genesis 3:17-18==), and Paul says it “will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God” (==Romans 8:21==). Notice whose liberty creation gets swept into—ours. As go the sons, so goes the soil.

Now, I’ll be honest: I don’t claim to fully understand everything this implies. But it seems to say that to the degree you and I live the way Jesus would live if He were here—walking in His character and His restored authority—to that degree creation is released from its descent into decay. So you want to do something about a groaning planet? By all means, steward it well; the earth is the LORD’s and He handed its care to us (==Psalm 115:16==). But maybe start by asking God for a fresh revelation of what “all authority” being restored to the sons and daughters of Adam actually looks like (==Matthew 28:18==). Creation isn’t holding its breath for a policy. It’s standing on tiptoe, watching the horizon for the unveiling of a family that finally looks like its King.
