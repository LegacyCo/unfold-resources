## Commentary
“‘All this,’ said David, ‘the LORD made me understand in writing, by His hand upon me, all the works of these plans.’”

David did not design the temple. Read the chapter and see how far the plans went beyond anything a king would sketch on his own: the vestibule, the treasuries, the upper chambers, the inner rooms, the divisions of the priests, even the exact weight of gold for each lampstand and table (==1 Chronicles 28:11-17==). And David tells Solomon precisely where all of it came from: *the LORD made me understand in writing, by His hand upon me.* Verse 12 says he had the plans “by the Spirit.” The design of God’s house came down from God—received, not invented.

This is the second time in Scripture the blueprints for God’s dwelling arrive this way. Moses didn’t design the tabernacle either; he saw the pattern on the mountain and was commanded to build “according to the pattern” (==Exodus 25:40==). Now the same thing happens for the temple—different builder, different century, same rule: when God wants a house, God supplies the design. He has never once opened the architecture of His dwelling place to human creativity. And notice the phrase “His hand upon me.” The plans didn’t come to David at a drafting table; they came in the presence of God, under His hand—revelation, written down.

God is still building a house—no longer of cedar and gold, but of people (==1 Peter 2:5==; ==Ephesians 2:22==). And the design still isn’t ours to improvise. David had resources, taste, and vision enough to build something magnificent by his own lights—and he submitted all of it to what the LORD put in writing. That’s the posture of everyone who builds anything for God: get under His hand, receive the plans, and build what He wrote.
