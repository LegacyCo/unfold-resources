## Commentary
“Then Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had married; for he had married an Ethiopian woman.”

Some context is helpful for just how significant this complaint against Moses is. Miriam is the sister who stood watch over Moses' basket in the Nile (==Exodus 2:4==). Aaron is the brother who stood beside him in front of Pharaoh. And both of them were real leaders in Israel. Miriam was a prophetess (==Exodus 15:20==), Aaron was the high priest, and God Himself later says, “I sent before you Moses, Aaron, and Miriam” (==Micah 6:4==). So the first serious challenge to Moses' leadership after Sinai comes from his own family, and from people with genuine callings of their own.

The stated issue is Moses' wife. We don't know much about her. “Ethiopian” is literally *Cushite*. She may be Zipporah, since ==Habakkuk 3:7== sets Cushan and Midian side by side, or she may be a second wife Moses married later. Scripture doesn't tell us, and it doesn't tell us exactly what they objected to.

What it does tell us is that the wife wasn't the real issue. The very next verse gives us what they were actually saying: “Has the LORD indeed spoken only through Moses? Has He not spoken through us also?” (==Numbers 12:2==). That has nothing to do with Moses' wife. It's about Moses' place of authority. And when God steps in, He never mentions the wife at all. He goes straight to the question of Moses' position (==Numbers 12:6-8==). 

One more detail. In Hebrew, the verb “spoke” is feminine singular, and Miriam is named first. That suggests she took the lead and Aaron went along with her, which fits what we know of Aaron (==Exodus 32:1-4==). It also helps explain why Miriam is the one who is struck in verse 10.

## Application
Criticism often shows up attached to an issue that isn't the real one. So before you speak against someone, ask yourself what's underneath the obvious feelings. Is this about what they did? Or is it about something deeper? There's a right way to bring a real concern. Nathan went to David directly (==2 Samuel 12:1==), and Paul confronted Peter to his face (==Galatians 2:11==). Miriam and Aaron did neither. They talked *about* Moses, and God heard all of it.
