## Commentary
God commanded Israel to give the land rest.

Before Israel ever stepped into the Promised Land, while they were still in the wilderness, the Lord told them how they were to possess it. They could sow, prune, gather, and eat from its produce for six years, but the seventh year belonged to the Lord. It was to be “a sabbath of solemn rest for the land.”

That means the land was not just property to be used. It was not raw material for man’s ambition. It belonged to the Lord, and the people possessing it were required to steward it according to His ways.

The Sabbath was not only for man. It was for the land.

The land had been caught up in covenant long before Israel arrived. The promises had been made to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, but those promises were not floating in the air. They were attached to a place. The covenant was with the man, but the land stood as witness to the covenant. And in some mysterious way, the covenant extended to the land the covenant man was promised to possess.

So when God gives Israel the command of Leviticus 25, He is teaching them that possession does not mean exploitation. To possess the land rightly, they had to honor the Lord’s covenantal rhythm for it. Six years of labor, one year of rest. Six years of receiving from the land, one year of trusting the God who gave the land.

The Sabbath year revealed whether Israel believed the land belonged to them or to the Lord.

If the land was theirs, they would squeeze everything they could from it. But if the land was the Lord’s, they would let it rest when He commanded it to rest. The seventh year forced Israel to remember that fruitfulness does not begin with human striving. It begins with covenant faithfulness.

The land remembers the word of the Lord.

And Leviticus 25 reminds us that true possession means learning to steward what God gives according to the rhythms God commands.
