## Commentary
“Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: ‘May they prosper who love you.’”

Jerusalem is intended to be a witness in the earth, in every generation, of the heavenly Jerusalem that will one day be established on earth as it is in heaven. God has made a covenant with that city and called it His own (==Ezekiel 16:8==); His eyes are always on it (==Deuteronomy 11:12==); and He has determined that it represent something of the age to come—the New Jerusalem, the holy city, the Bride of the Lamb (==Revelation 21:9-10==). The Jerusalem of today is meant to point to the Jerusalem of tomorrow. That is why it is the most contested real estate on the planet: there is a battle in every generation for control of that city and for the witness that comes from it.

This is the burden behind the psalm's command, and Isaiah gives it a shape: “I have set watchmen on your walls, O Jerusalem; they shall never hold their peace day or night. You who make mention of the LORD, do not keep silent, and give Him no rest till He establishes and till He makes Jerusalem a praise in the earth” (==Isaiah 62:6-7==). To pray for the peace of Jerusalem is to take up that watchman's post—to give God no rest until the city once again mirrors His glory and the reign of the Prince of Peace. The psalm attaches a promise to the prayer: “may they prosper who love you.” Those who carry God's city in their prayers share in what God is doing for it.

## Application
Make Jerusalem a regular part of your prayers, not an occasional one. Pray specifically for its peace—for the salvation of its people, for the restraint of what would destroy its witness, and for the day it becomes “a praise in the earth.” Then let that prayer widen: every believer is also praying for the heavenly city that earthly Jerusalem points to, asking for it to come, and for God's will to be done on earth as it is in heaven (==Matthew 6:10==).
