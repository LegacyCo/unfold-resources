## Commentary
Paul feels privileged to have received great revelations from God. He says that in order to receive mysteries and secrets, one must prove himself faithful in character. True revelations from God are preceded by a corresponding trustworthiness.

The revelation of God through the Scriptures is written on more than one level of meaning. If someone's heart is hard, he can read the same words but miss the deeper revelation. The greater revelations of Scripture are the ones that reveal more of the intimacy of God and Jesus. It takes intimacy and trust to grasp the revelations of intimacy.
  
In John 16:12 Jesus says: "I still have many things to say to you, but you can not bear them now." Jesus did not mean that they were not smart enough nor courageous enough, but that they still had much yet to learn of trust and intimacy.

## Application
Deeper revelation is not mainly a reward for intelligence; it is entrusted to those who have been made trustworthy in heart. A person can study Scripture, master Greek and Hebrew, and still miss the deeper things of God if intimacy and surrender are lacking. God does not just reveal truth to satisfy curiosity. He reveals Himself to those who can carry what He shows them. So if you want greater insight, don't just ask for more understanding—ask God to make you more faithful, more yielded, and to bring you into intimate encounters with Him. The deepest things of God aren't learned, they're revealed.
