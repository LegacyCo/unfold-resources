## Commentary
“For this reason many are weak and sick among you, and many sleep.”

Symbols don’t have the power to harm you. That’s what makes this verse so hard on the merely-symbolic view of communion. Paul tells the Corinthians that because some were eating and drinking in an unworthy manner—not discerning the Lord’s body—many among them were weak and sick, and some had died (==1 Corinthians 11:27-29==). No one gets sick from mishandling a metaphor. Whatever is happening at the Lord’s table, it carries real power—power that blesses when approached rightly and, evidently, power that a careless approach can turn against you.

We’re strangely selective about this. We have no problem believing that handkerchiefs from Paul’s body healed the sick (==Acts 19:11-12==) or that Peter’s shadow carried healing power (==Acts 5:15==). But suggest that the table Jesus Himself instituted has intrinsic power, and many believers get uncomfortable. Here’s a way through the two ditches: the Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation says the bread and wine change into the literal body and blood; Protestants, countering it, swung all the way into the ditch of symbolism. But there’s a truth transubstantiation is reaching for—something *does* change when we take communion. But it isn't the bread and the wine. It’s the faith-filled believer who changes. The one who eats and drinks with faith is nourished, strengthened, and healed by real participation in Christ (==1 Corinthians 10:16==).

## Application
Let this verse change how you come to the table, in both directions. Take the examination seriously: “let a man examine himself, and so let him eat” (==1 Corinthians 11:28==)—deal honestly with unconfessed sin, unforgiveness, and division with the body before you partake. And then take the *power* seriously: come expecting. Before you eat, pray that the Holy Spirit would call you up into the mystery—that you'd be seated at the table where bread is flesh and wine is blood, where the last is first and kings lay down their lives. Don't rush the moment. Many have received a symbol. Come to receive the Lord.
