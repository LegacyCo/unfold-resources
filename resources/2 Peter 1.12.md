## Commentary
“For this reason I will not be negligent to remind you always of these things, though you know and are established in the present truth.”

Peter is writing to saints who already know the truth and are established in it—and he commits the rest of his life to reminding them of it anyway. He says it three times in four verses: I will remind you always; I think it right to stir you up by reminding you as long as I am in this tent; I will ensure you have a reminder after my decease (==2 Peter 1:12-15==). An apostle who walked with Jesus, saw the transfiguration, and carried revelation the church still feeds on, understood his ministry in large part as repetition—putting believers in remembrance of what they already believed.

Peter treats forgetting as a real spiritual danger. A few verses earlier he describes the believer who lacks fruitfulness as one who “has forgotten that he was cleansed from his old sins” (==2 Peter 1:9==)—not one who never knew it, but one who stopped living in the knowledge of it. Established truth does not stay active in a heart automatically; it must be stirred, rehearsed, and kept in front of the eyes, which is why God built reminders into the life of His people from the beginning—memorial stones, feasts, and a table where the church hears “do this in remembrance of Me” as often as it gathers (==Joshua 4:6-7==; ==1 Corinthians 11:24-25==). The teacher who repeats the gospel to people who already believe it is not wasting their time. He is doing what Peter did, and what Paul did when he declared the gospel again to a church that had received it years before (==1 Corinthians 15:1==). The saints do not need an endless supply of new revelation. Sometimes they just need to reminded of the fundamentals.
