## Commentary
“For I will pour water on him who is thirsty, and floods on the dry ground; I will pour My Spirit on your descendants, and My blessing on your offspring.”

The promise moves in one direction: from God’s fullness to human dryness. He does not say “I will pour water on the deserving” or “on the qualified”—He says on the *thirsty*, and floods on the *dry ground*. Thirst is the only condition. The verse then tells us what the water is: “I will pour My Spirit on your descendants.” This is the same promise Joel carried and Pentecost fulfilled (==Joel 2:28==; ==Acts 2:17==), and Jesus announced it in person: “If anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink” (==John 7:37-39==). Dryness is not a disqualification in the kingdom. It's the prerequisite for receiving from God.

In 1949, on the Isle of Lewis off the coast of Scotland, the churches were emptying and an entire generation of young people was gone from them. In a small cottage lived two sisters, Peggy and Christine Smith—Christine eighty-two and bent with arthritis. They had no platform, no title, no ministry to speak of. But as they prayed for their village, this verse gripped Peggy and would not let her go: *I will pour water on him who is thirsty, and floods on the dry ground.* The sisters became convinced God had shown them what He was about to do—the churches would fill again, especially with the young people everyone else had written off. For months they prayed the word over their island while others gathered night after night in a barn, waiting for God to keep His word. And He kept it. Revival broke out and swept through the Hebrides.

Look at what those two women did with a verse, because it is a picture of how a promise becomes a harvest. The word was received by people with no ministry platform. The word landed on those "in the camp," so to speak (==Numbers 11:26==). It was heard entirely for the sake of others—an island that had no idea anyone was praying. And it was stewarded like a seed: received in meekness (==James 1:21==), planted in prayer, given time to germinate, until the promise performed itself (==Luke 1:37==). Two women gave God their ears, and God gave an island a flood. The promise of this verse has not expired—it is still looking for thirsty people and dry ground.
