## Word Study
The word in v13 for righteousness is ***dikaiosyne***.
The word in v14 for mature is ***teleios***.

*Dikaiosyne* - the state of one who is as they were designed to be.

Teleios - most often translated as "perfect" in the NKJV
	- [[Matthew 5.48]] - "Be **perfect** just as your Father in heaven is perfect."
	- [[Hebrews 5.9]] - "And having been **perfected**, He became the author of salvation to all who obey Him."
	- [[Hebrews 11.40]] - "God having provided something better for us, that they should not be made **perfect** apart from us."
The word of righteousness (***dikaiosyne***) belongs to those who are perfected/mature/complete (***teleios***); who by way of practice/habit/doing repeatedly have their organ of sense/ability to discern have been trained to discern both good and evil.

God intended for Adam to become trained in discerning good and evil, to become perfect. This was to come by way of practice and repetition, not instant impartation. Desiring the impartation but rejecting the process of practice and progression is tantamount to desiring the tree of knowledge.

Paul tells Timothy to reactivate/kindle afresh the gift of God that was given to him through the laying on of hands (2 Timothy 1:6) and not to neglect that gift (impartation) so that his progress would be evident to all (1 Timothy 4:14). Paul imparted a gift to young Timothy, but it still came with a process of maturity. There are no shortcuts in the kingdom, no matter who lays hands on you.

The word of righteousness (***dikaiosyne***) is the word of the mature and perfected. The Hebrews were unfamiliar in these things. They needed someone to teach them again the elementary principles (*stoicheion arches*) of the message (milk) of God. These two words used together are important:

*Stoicheion* - the vertical stick on a sundial that casts the shadow, to be in line with the rudiments, to conform to the battle line.
		- This word is also used in Acts 21:24, "...they will see that you too are in the ranks as one who keeps the law." Compare this to Galatians 5:25, "Conduct our lives in conformity with the Spirit." The image is of keeping consistency in a given direction according to original design; "follow in the footsteps of the faith of Abraham" (Romans 4:12).
		- Galatians 4:25 illustrates the line of descent that still keeps the pattern of slavery: Hagar - Sinai - Jerusalem (the law)
*Arches* - the beginning, starting point, original, to begin first by doing something which is in contrast to what one does later.

The linking of these two words (*stoicheion arches*) has a derogatory ring that emphasizes the reminder that the recipients of this letter are not familiar with the first principles of the faith, the elementary doctrines mentioned in Hebrews 6:1ff. 

In short, because they don't understand the original design, they have fallen in line with a deviation. This makes them unacquainted with/ignorant of the message of righteousness (*dikaiosyne*).

Rather than demonstrating to others the image of the faith, the recipients of this letter need someone to align them again to the original design, the starting point, so they can then continue the path of progress — which has as its result perfection.

Perfection will never come when the starting point is out of alignment. Hagar will never lead you to into *dikaiosyne*. Ishmael must be told to leave so that the path of progress can begin with Isaac, the son of promise...he is the *arches*.

Legitimate sons, those with the proper *arches* and *stoicheion*, receive discipline from the Father as proof of their sonship. His discipline and training yeild holiness and the fruit of righteousness.
		- "For they indeed for a few days disciplined us as seemed best to them, but He for our profit, that we may receive/be partakers of His holiness. Now no discipline seems to be joyful in the present, but painful; nevertheless, afterwards it yields the peaceable fruit of righeousness to those who have been trained by it" Hebrews 12:10-11.
The fruit of righteousness comes through the progress and process of discipline. The fact that there is discipline confirms the correct lineage (Isaac).

Why is this important? Becuase Christ Himself became perfect (***teleios***) through suffering (Hebrews 2:10). And we become perfect through obedience.
