## Commentary
“And Abraham said to his young men, ‘Stay here with the donkey; the lad and I will go yonder and worship, and we will come back to you.’”

This is the first time the word “worship” appears in Scripture—and look at what it’s describing. Not a song. Not a service. A man walking up a mountain to lay his promise on an altar. Before worship ever becomes music, it is first a sacrifice. That’s the meaning the word is born with, and it never loses it. Paul carries it straight into the new covenant: “present your bodies a living sacrifice… which is your reasonable *worship*” (==Romans 12:1==). Whatever else worship becomes, at its root it is still this—everything placed on the altar, nothing withheld.

Now notice the other staggering thing in this sentence: “*we* will come back to you.” Abraham is walking up the mountain to sacrifice Isaac, and he tells his servants that both of them are coming down. That’s not denial and it’s not a cover story. Hebrews tells us exactly what was happening in Abraham’s mind: “By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac… concluding that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead” (==Hebrews 11:17-19==). Abraham had done the math of faith. God had promised that the covenant line would run through Isaac (==Genesis 21:12==)—so if God was asking for Isaac, then God was going to have to raise Isaac. Abraham wasn’t coming down that mountain alone, and he knew it.

So the first mention of worship and one of the first flashes of resurrection faith sit in the same sentence—and that’s no coincidence. Worship that costs something is only possible when you believe in a God who gives back. The Lord gives, the Lord takes away, and the Lord gives back again. That’s the conviction that walks a promise up a mountain and says, with a straight face, “We will come back.”
