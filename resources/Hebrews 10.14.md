## Commentary
This verse contains one of the most profound statements in the New Testament:

“For by a single offering He has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified.”

It sounds almost too good to be true, which is probably why so many believers struggle to actually live from it. We have an easier time believing Jesus forgave us than believing Jesus perfected us. We can accept that He washed away our past, but we think our present standing before God is still up for debate.

And then we read Hebrews 10.

The word translated “perfected” is the Greek word _teleioō_, and in Hebrews 10:14 it appears in the perfect tense. That's vital to understand. The perfect tense is used when an action is completed and the result continues on. In other words, Jesus has not just started something in you that might one day make you acceptable. By one offering, He has brought you to completion before God (that's the action), and the effect of that completed work is still present.

Which means both things are true at once: you have been made holy, and you are still learning how to live holy. You have been perfected in Christ, and you are still being formed into the fullness of what He has already made you.

Holiness is not first something you achieve by what you do or do not do; it is something you receive by faith. Jesus did not offer Himself so you could spend the rest of your life trying to become acceptable. He offered Himself because, through Him, you have been made acceptable.

And this is where the New Covenant gets offensive to religious thinking: If you simply get what you deserve based on how you act, then the *New* Covenant isn’t really new, and the *good* news isn’t really good. It may be religious news. It may be moral news. It may be behavior-management news. But it's not the gospel.

The gospel announces that the endless cycle of sacrifice is over. The system of constantly trying to make up for what you lack has been brought to an end in the body of Jesus. Once for all means once for all. Nothing needs to be added to His offering. Nothing needs to be repeated. Nothing needs to be improved.

So the call of Hebrews 10:14 is not to become casual about sin — as Paul says in ==Romans 6:1-2==, "Shall we go on sinning so that grace may abound? Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?" The encouragement of Hebrews is to become confident in Christ and who He now says you are. The more you believe you have been made holy, the more naturally you begin to live holy. Identity always forms behavior. A person who knows they have been perfected in Christ no longer lives to earn a place before God, but from the place Jesus has already secured. So refusing to entertain sin is not an attempt to stay out of hell so much as it is a refusal to do anything that contradicts what Jesus says about you.
