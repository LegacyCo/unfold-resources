## Commentary
One of the awful affects of Adam's sin was now the very presence of God filled him with fear. Sin altered the way Adam saw God. Had God changed? Of course not, He can't change. The Bible says that He is the same yesterday, today, and forever…that He is unchanging in all of His ways. In Malachi 3:6, God says, “I, the Lord, do NOT change.” 

God didn't change as a result of Adam's sin, Adam changed. And as Adam found himself in shame and fear for the first time, he projected those things onto God. And in so doing, Adam created a mythological deity, a god of imagination and legend, a god created in the image of his own brokenness. 

And standing before this mythological god, a god he had never seen before, Adam felt the most dreadful fear as he wondered how this god was going to respond to his guilt.  

C. Baxter Kruger writes this in his book *Jesus and the Undoing of Adam*, "The Fall of Adam constitutes a staggering communication problem for God. Now there is an ugly ditch between who God actually is and who Adam believes God is. From this moment forward, the truth about God will be veiled and His heart will be misunderstood. The very presence of God coming in love will be translated through the fallen mind of Adam as the presence of One whose love is arbitrary and whose blessing comes with strings attached. Adam now interprets God's every move through his lens of brokenness. What God says is one thing, what we hear is quite another. For what we hear is inevitably shaped by our own anxiety and by our own mythology and imaginations. The revelation of God to us, irrespective of how powerful and clear it may be from God's side, is always perceived through our "broken glasses.”

The truth of the matter is that Adam’s sin didn’t change God, Adam’s sin changed Adam. Sin does not have the power to change God. Sin changes us. For example, in the parable of the prodigal son, who had changed; the father or the son? The son. And here in Genesis 3, it was "the son" who changed.

Now let's make it personal: When you sin, who changes, you or God?
Sin doesn’t have the power to change God…sin changes us. 
Sin doesn’t separate God from us, sin separates us from God.

God doesn't need changing, we do. Jesus didn’t go to the cross to change God. Jesus went to the cross to change us. Jesus went to the cross to undo what Adam had done in Genesis 3, and to cut off the family lineage of sin and death that we are all a part of, so that the Father’s dream of adoption could be realized. 

Paul pleads with the Corinthians in 2 Corinthians 5:20, “we implore *you* on Christ’s behalf, be reconciled to God.” Yahweh says in Isaiah 59:2, 

“*Your* iniquities have separated *you* from your God, and *your* sins have hidden His face from *you*.”

God’s not hiding in the bushes, Adam’s hiding in the bushes. And sin does to you and I the same thing it did to Adam: it causes us to hide from God in fear and shame. But all the while, that burning flame of God’s love and mercy is chasing us down, ready to welcome us home…ready to put a ring on our finger and a robe on our back.

The cross does not restore God to us…the cross restores us to God. This isn't just semantics. If you don’t see what the difference is, imagine you’re a father whose child was abducted by traffickers. The child can't restore himself, but given the opportunity, you would move heaven and earth to pay whatever ransom was required to get your child back. We have all been abducted by sin. And for the joy set before Him, Jesus paid the ransom to restore us back to the Father.

## Cross Reference
Look at the parables Jesus tells in Luke 15; the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the lost son. All three parables were told on the heels of the Pharisees complaining that Jesus receives sinners…and *eats* with them (Luck 15:2). Each parable tells the story of something precious that was lost but then was restored. In telling these parables Jesus is telling the religious Pharisees, “You better believe I eat with sinners…I came to restore them back to My Father”
