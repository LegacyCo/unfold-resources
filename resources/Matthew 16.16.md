## Commentary
“You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.”

Christ is not Jesus’ last name. It’s His identity. *Christos* is the Greek translation of the Hebrew *Messiah*—the Anointed of God. It’s a royal title. When Peter says “You are the Christ,” he’s acknowledging that the King has come to establish His rule and reign among His people—the One Israel had been praying for, for hundreds of years.

But watch what Jesus says about how Peter got there: “Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but My Father who is in heaven” (==Matthew 16:17==). Peter had walked with Jesus, watched the miracles, heard every sermon—and Jesus says the confession didn’t come from any of that. It came from the Father.

Which tells us something essential: only God can reveal God. We cannot know who Jesus is apart from divine revelation. Proximity doesn’t produce it—the crowds were near Him and concluded He was John the Baptist or Elijah or Jeremiah (==Matthew 16:14==). Information doesn’t produce it—the scribes knew the texts and missed the Messiah standing in front of them. Flesh and blood, at its very best, gets you to an opinion about Jesus. Only the Father gets you to “You are the Christ.”

That should change how we pray—for ourselves and for the people we love. The unbelieving friend doesn’t primarily need a better argument; they need what Peter received (==2 Corinthians 4:6==). So before you explain, ask: “Father, reveal Your Son.”
