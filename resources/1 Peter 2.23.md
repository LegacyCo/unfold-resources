## Commentary
When Jesus was silent toward those who were beating and accusing Him, it wasn't that His suffering was not moving Him. He was silent because His peace was in the Father, not what was happening around Him. When He was insulted, He had no need to retaliate. When He suffered unjustly, He did not demand that everyone recognize His pain. He entrusted Himself to the One who judges righteously.

Samuel Chand writes, “Self-pity isn’t humility. It’s the opposite of humility.” Self-pity turns suffering inward until pain becomes the center of our identity. It continually asks others to notice what happened to us, acknowledge what it cost us, and affirm how deeply we have been wounded. But Jesus shows us another way to carry pain.

Humility does not deny suffering or pretend that injustice does not hurt. It simply refuses to use pain as a platform for attention or revenge. Jesus could entrust His suffering to the Father because He did not need human recognition to know who He was.

Pain will reveal what's beneath the surface. It can leave us angry, self-absorbed, and consumed with what others have done to us, or it can become the place where we meet Christ and are transformed into His likeness. The question is not only what we have suffered, but what our suffering is producing in us.
