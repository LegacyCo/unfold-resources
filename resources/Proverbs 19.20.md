## Commentary
One of the distinguishing marks of a wise person is that they remain teachable. They understand they do not see every angle, have every answer, or possess every perspective. Rather than protecting their opinions, they welcome godly counsel and receive correction with humility.

Notice that Proverbs says to _listen_ to counsel and _receive_ instruction. Listening alone is not enough. It is possible to hear wise words without allowing them to shape us. True wisdom begins when counsel moves beyond our ears and into our lives.

There is also a long-term perspective in this proverb. Solomon says that those who embrace counsel today will be wise "in your latter days." Wisdom is accumulated over a lifetime of humble responses to God's instruction. Every correction received, every blind spot exposed, and every lesson embraced becomes part of the foundation we stand on in the future.

The fool believes he has already arrived. The wise know they are still being formed.

To reject counsel is to limit your future to the boundaries of your present understanding. To receive counsel is to borrow from the wisdom God has cultivated in others and allow it to become part of your own life. Those who remain teachable never stop growing, and those who never stop growing often become the very people others seek for counsel in the years to come.
