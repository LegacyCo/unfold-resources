## Commentary
Peter calls Noah “a preacher of righteousness.” Here’s a question: how many of Noah’s sermons do we have on record?

Zero. Scripture doesn’t record a single sermon from the man God used to preserve humanity. Not one altar call. Not one recorded word of public proclamation. In fact, the only recorded words of Noah in all of Scripture are what he spoke to his sons *after* the flood (==Genesis 9:25-27==). His entire ministry as a preacher of righteousness unfolded without Scripture recording him saying anything at all. And yet the Holy Spirit, writing through Peter, gives Noah that title: *preacher of righteousness*.

So what was Noah's message? His life. For decades, Noah walked with God (==Genesis 6:9==) in front of a generation whose every intent was only evil continually (==Genesis 6:5==). Every plank of that ark was a proclamation. Every day of obedience in a culture of corruption was a message delivered to everyone watching. Hebrews says that by building the ark in holy fear, Noah “condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness which is according to faith” (==Hebrews 11:7==). His hammer preached. His holy fear preached. His obedience out-preached every voice in his generation.

This should recalibrate how we think about witness. We’ve largely limited “preaching the gospel” to what comes out of our mouths—and what we say matters; faith comes by hearing (==Romans 10:17==). But if the only recorded preacher of the pre-flood world never opened his mouth on record, then a transformed, obedient life is not the junior varsity version of witness. It’s preaching. Some of God’s most effective preachers have never held a microphone—they’ve held a hammer, a casserole dish, a checkbook opened for someone in need, a door held open for the lonely.

If you’ve believed you have nothing to contribute to the Great Commission because you’re not a talker, let Noah tell you otherwise. Build what God told you to build. Walk with God in front of the people He’s placed around you. That *is* proclamation.
