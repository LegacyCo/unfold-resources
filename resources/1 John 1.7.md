## Commentary
"...and the blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin."

Read that verb slowly. Not *cleansed*. *Cleanses*. In English it can pass for a past event, but John wrote it in the present tense—continuous, ongoing action. The blood of Jesus washed you, is washing you, and will still be washing you tomorrow morning. It happened, and it is *still happening*.

Take a stone out of the mud and hold it under a faucet, and you can truthfully say it's been washed. But put it back in the mud and it's dirty again. A one-time washing is only as good as the environment you return to. That is *not* the picture John gives of the blood of Jesus. In the new covenant, God doesn't lift us out of the miry clay, rinse us once, and set us back where He found us. He sets our feet on a rock (==Psalm 40:2==)—and then keeps us under a cleansing flow that washes and washes and washes.

That's what makes the Christian life livable. If cleansing were a single moment at an altar years ago, every failure since would be an argument that the washing didn't take. But the cleansing isn't a moment. It's a fountain, opened once for sin and uncleanness (==Zechariah 13:1==) and never shut off. So when John says "if we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us... and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (==1 John 1:9==), he's not describing a return trip to the faucet. He's describing what it looks like to keep walking in the light while the water keeps running.

The stone in the mud is the old covenant: repeat the sacrifice, get dirty, repeat it again (==Hebrews 10:1-3==). The fountain is the new. Same blood, one offering—cleansing that doesn't stop.

## Word Study
- *cleanses* : **katharizō** (Greek), present active indicative — to make clean, purify; the root of our word *catharsis*. It's the word for the leper Jesus touched: "I am willing; be *cleansed*" (==Matthew 8:3==), and for the vessels God has *cleansed* that no one may call common (==Acts 10:15==). The tense here is what preachers point to: not "has cleansed" (perfect) or "cleansed" (aorist), but *keeps on cleansing*.

Summary: the blood of Jesus is not a past-tense event in your life. Grammatically and actually, it is a present one.
