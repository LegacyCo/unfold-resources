## Commentary
When Israel crossed the Jordan on dry ground, God commanded twelve men to carry twelve stones out of the riverbed and stack them at Gilgal. Why? “That this may be a sign among you when your children ask in time to come, saying, ‘What do these stones mean to you?’” The stones existed to provoke a question—so that a generation who never saw the river part would hear the story from the mouths of those who walked through it (==Joshua 4:21-24==).

God builds memorials out of His acts, and He still does. Every time a child of Adam repents and surrenders to Jesus, it’s an announcement that the kingdom is here. Every healing, every answered prayer, every dream, every testimony of someone discovering that this physical world is not all there is—those become memorial stones for future generations to stumble over and ask, “What does this mean?” A testimony isn’t just a nice story about your past. It’s a stone pulled up from the riverbed of what God did, stacked where someone else can see it.

Which is why the enemy loves an untold testimony. A stone left in the river marks nothing. Scripture says the saints overcome the accuser “by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony” (==Revelation 12:11==)—the story has power when it’s *told*. So tell yours. Tell your kids the account of the day God saved you, the prayer He answered, the addiction He broke, the marriage He raised from the dead. Stack the stones where the next generation will trip over them and ask the question the stones were always meant to raise—and then tell them what the LORD did, “that all the peoples of the earth may know the hand of the LORD, that it is mighty” (==Joshua 4:24==).
