## Commentary
Paul prays “that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith; that you, being *rooted* and grounded in love, may be able to comprehend *with all the saints* what is the width and length and depth and height” of the love of Christ. Notice two details in that prayer. First, we are *rooted* in love. Second, we comprehend that love *with all the saints*. Paul is telling us something important: there are dimensions of God’s love you will never grasp by yourself.

Think about redwood trees. Fire is actually necessary for a redwood forest. The fire clears out the weeds and the bugs, and the heat causes the seed cones hanging high in the branches to open up and drop their seeds. Without fire, there is no next generation of trees. But the seeds can’t grow *in* the fire. Fire only prepares the ground—the seed still has to land in the soil and put down roots. And here’s the surprising part: redwoods are the tallest trees on earth, but their roots don’t grow down. They grow *out*. Each tree’s roots spread sideways and wrap around the roots of the trees next to it, and that’s what holds them up. A redwood doesn’t stand because its own roots go deep. It stands because its roots are joined to the trees around it. That’s how trees over 300 feet tall stay standing through centuries of storms.

Now think about church history the same way. I believe this is where many revivals went off the rails: the fire fell—genuinely fell—and gospel seeds burst open in thousands of hearts. But life doesn’t grow in fire. Fire serves its purpose: it burns away the impurity that would choke the seeds and prepares the soil. Then the seedlings need what only an interconnected community can give—family to nurture new life into maturity (==1 Thessalonians 2:7-8==; ==1 Thessalonians 2:11==). Where there was no forest of joined roots, the fire produced a moment instead of a generation.

So whether God sends revival to our generation or allows shaking, the assignment is the same: become a people whose roots are wrapped in one another. If revival comes, the babies born in the fire will need a family to grow up in. If persecution comes, a kingdom that cannot be shaken is displayed by a people who cannot be isolated (==Hebrews 12:28==). Either way, your strength will not be measured by how deep you go alone. It will be measured by how far your roots have grown out—into the saints beside you, grounded together in a love wide and long and deep and high enough to hold the tallest trees standing.
