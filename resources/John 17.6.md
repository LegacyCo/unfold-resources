## Commentary
“I have manifested Your name to the men whom You have given Me out of the world.”

Four times in this one prayer, Jesus returns to the Father's name. He has manifested the name (==John 17:6==); He asks the Father to keep the disciples through the name (==John 17:11==); He reminds the Father that while He was with them, He kept them in the name (==John 17:12==); and He has declared the name and will keep declaring it (==John 17:26==). In Scripture, a name reveals nature and character—so when Jesus says His mission was to manifest the Father's name, He is saying He came to show them who the Father actually *is*. And what He showed them was Father. That is the name purposefully revealed, intentionally manifested, jealously guarded, and deliberately declared over the disciples on the night that would test them more than any other.

Why does verse 26 add “and will declare it”? Because the manifestation of the Father's name was not finished at the Last Supper—and the reason: “that the love with which You loved Me may be in them, and I in them” (==John 17:26==). The name is declared so the Father's love can be received. A person can know God as Almighty and as LORD and still keep a distance. But no one keeps a wary distance from *Abba*. 

## Word Study
**Abba** (ἀββά) is Aramaic—the intimate household word a child used for his father. It appears three times in the New Testament, and the first is on this very night: hours after this prayer, Jesus falls on His face in Gethsemane and prays, “Abba, Father, all things are possible for You” (==Mark 14:36==). The other two occurrences put the same word in *our* mouths: “you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, ‘Abba, Father'” (==Romans 8:15==), and “God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying out, ‘Abba, Father!'” (==Galatians 4:6==). Summary: the name Jesus used at His darkest hour is the name the Spirit now cries from inside every believer—the manifestation of the Father's name did not stop with the disciples.
