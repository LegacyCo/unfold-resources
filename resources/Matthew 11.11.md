## Commentary
One of the most important things to understand about John the Baptist is that, although he announced the arrival of the new covenant, he himself ministered under the old covenant. John stood at the intersection of two ages. He was the final prophetic voice preparing Israel for the arrival of their Messiah, but he was not yet a minister of the new covenant that would be established through the death and resurrection of Jesus.

This helps us understand why Jesus could say, “Among those born of women there has not risen one greater than John the Baptist; but he who is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.”

Jesus was not diminishing John's faithfulness or character. He was making a distinction between two covenantal realities. John was the greatest among those who had come before, yet even the least in the kingdom would participate in something John could only announce from the threshold.

We can see this distinction in John's ministry. When the crowds came to him by the Jordan River asking, “What shall we do then?” John answered with instructions concerning their *conduct*. Those with two tunics were to share with those who had none. Tax collectors were to collect no more than what was appointed. Soldiers were to stop intimidating and falsely accusing people and to be content with their wages (==Luke 3:10–14==). These were righteous instructions, consistent with the moral demands of the Law and the prophets. John was calling Israel to demonstrate repentance through the way they lived.

But notice the difference when the crowds asked Jesus, “What shall we do, that we may work the works of God?” Jesus answered, “This is the work of God, that you believe in Him whom He sent” (==John 6:28–29==).

John called Israel to prepare for the coming King. Jesus announced that the King had come, and that entrance into His kingdom would be through faith in Him. The new covenant would not simply provide another set of instructions for people to follow. It would bring about a new birth, a new heart, and the indwelling presence of the Holy Spirit (==Ezekiel 36:26–27==; ==John 3:3–6==).

This does not mean John preached repentance while Jesus preached belief, as though the two were opposed. Both called people to repent, and both expected repentance to produce a transformed life. The distinction is that John was preparing people for what God was about to accomplish, while Jesus was revealing Himself as the One *through whom* that accomplishment would come. To repent in light of His arrival meant more than correcting one's behavior. It meant reorienting one's entire understanding of God, oneself, and the world around them because the promised King had come.

This is what makes Jesus' statement so remarkable. The least in the kingdom is not greater than John because of superior obedience, spiritual maturity, or personal devotion. They are greater because of what Christ has accomplished and the covenant into which they have been brought.
