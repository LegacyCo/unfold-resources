## Commentary
The psalmist declares, “Yes, the Lord will give what is good; and our land will yield its increase.” There is a beautiful connection between this promise and what God declares through Ezekiel: “Then the trees of the field shall yield their fruit, and the earth shall yield her increase. They shall be safe in their land; and they shall know that I am the Lord” (==Ezekiel 34:27==).

Both passages describe the restoration of God's people as something that extends beyond their individual lives to affect the very land they inhabit. When God restores His people to Himself, the land experiences the consequences of that restoration.

The context of Ezekiel's prophecy is particularly significant. God has condemned Israel's shepherds for exploiting His people rather than caring for them. He promises to intervene personally, gathering His scattered sheep, delivering them from oppression, and establishing a covenant of peace with them (==Ezekiel 34:25==). He also promises to establish one shepherd over them, His servant David (==Ezekiel 34:23==), a prophetic anticipation of Jesus Christ, the Good Shepherd.

Notice that the restoration of the land follows the restoration of God's people under the care of their Shepherd. The trees bear fruit, the earth produces its increase, and the people dwell securely. God's redemptive work brings restoration to both His people and their environment.

This reveals something important about the scope of salvation. God's intention is not just to rescue individuals from a fallen world, but ultimately to restore the world itself. This is the same hope Paul describes in ==Romans 8:19–21==, where creation eagerly awaits the revealing of the sons of God and its own deliverance from corruption.

We should not interpret these promises as a guarantee that every place inhabited by believers will immediately experience material prosperity. Both psalmist and prophet are looking toward a restoration whose fullness is ultimately realized through Christ. Nevertheless, they reveal God's heart for His creation and the relationship between the flourishing of His people and the flourishing of the earth.

As people united with Christ, we are invited to participate in this redemptive purpose even now. Our presence in a community should be a source of blessing, peace, justice, and flourishing. The places God has entrusted to us should be better cared for because His life is at work within us.
