## Commentary
Now death has come to Adam. If Adam had not sinned, Adam would not have died. Romans 5:12 says,

“Just as through one man, sin entered the world, and death entered through sin, death spread to all, because all have sinned.”

Matthew Henry writes, “God entrusted Adam with a spark of immortality, which he, by a patient continuance in doing well, might have blown up into an everlasting flame; but he foolishly blew it out by willful sin: and now death is the wage of sin, and sin is the sting of death.”

This is the judgment that has now come to each us; death is now our inheritance. Adam now becomes A-da-ma (dust/earth). Ecclesiastes 12:7 gives us a good picture of this: 

“The dust (man) will return to the earth as it was: and the spirit will return to God who gave it.”

When Adam ate, he didn’t immediately die…he didn’t die for another 900 years (see Genesis 5:5). So what does Scripture mean here by “death entered through sin”?

The moment he ate the fruit, Adam died in that he became separated from God, the source of life. That’s what death is…separation from life. He died spiritually the moment he disobeyed God. 

When Paul wrote to the Ephesians in Ephesians 2:1 that they were, “dead in their trespasses and sins,” he didn’t mean that they were dead physically, he meant that they were dead spiritually.

In the parable of the prodigal son in Luke 15, Jesus told a story about a boy who ran away from his father and squandered his inheritance on sinful living. When he finally returns, the father says, “My son was dead, and is now alive again…” (Luke 15:24). In his separation from the father, the son was dead because of sin.

Sin separates us from our Heavenly Father. If we are not born-again in Christ, we may appear alive on the outside to the rest of the world, but inside we are dead. We’re not connected to the source of life.


------
Jesus came to restore creation back to God's original design. He has redeemed us from the curse of Adam. We see this demonstrated in His life and ministry in many ways. One example is in Matthew 14, when He multiplies bread. The curse of Adam was that bread would only be multiplied by toil. Jesus, however, multiplies bread by blessing it,

"He commanded the multitudes to sit down on the grass. And He took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up to heaven, He blessed and broke and gave the loaves to the disciples; and the disciples gave to the multitudes" (Matthew 14:19).

## Application
In this verse we have the first mention of bread in Scripture,  “By the sweat of your brow you shall eat bread.” The curse of Adam was that he would now work the land and eat bread through toil and sweat. Yet no matter how hard he worked, and how much he sweat, Adam’s bread could never give him unending life. All it could do was push his expiration date a little further down the road.

But Jesus says something amazing in John 6,

“And Jesus said to them, ‘I am the bread of life. He who comes to Me shall never hunger, and he who believes in Me shall never thirst’" (John 6:35).

There is the bread of Adam, produced by toil and sweat, and there is the bread of God that comes down from heaven and gives life to the world. Whoever eats of *that* bread will not die..."he will live forever" (John 6:51). The bread that Jesus gives us is true food (John 6:55), because it doesn’t come up from the ground cursed by Adam, it comes down from heaven, blessed by God and holy (John 6:58). 

We know how hard we labor to bring bread up from the ground and onto our tables. But how hard should one labor to receive the bread of heaven? Is it through our own effort that we receive the bread of life? No. It’s a free gift of God’s grace…available to all who would simply believe that Jesus is exactly who He says He is.

When we stand on this side of the cross and read Genesis 3, we see how completely Jesus entered into our condition when He took on the form of sinful flesh.

Has travailing pain come to us because of sin? Isaiah 53:11 says that the Messiah entered into the pain and anguish of our sin. Acts 2:24 says that God raised Jesus from the dead, “putting an end to the agony of death.”

Were we subjected to a law of works and religion because of sin? Galatians 4:4 says that Christ, too, was born under the law to redeem those under the law. 

Did the curse come to us because of sin? Galatians 3:13 says that Christ became a curse for us, and has now redeemed us from the curse. 

Have thorns come to our land because of sin? Jesus was crowned with thorns for us. 

Did sweat come to us because of sin? With drops of blood, He sweat for us.

Does the pain of hunger consume the lives of our poor? Jesus entered into the hunger of the wilderness and refused to turn stones to bread so that He could become living bread for us all.

Did sorrow come to us because of sin? Jesus became the Man of sorrows for us, acquainted with our grief (Isaiah 53:3).

Has death come to us because of our sin? He was obedient unto death so that He could destroy the power of death forever from the inside out.

And because of His obedience, because of His death, because of His sorrow, because He sweat drops of blood, because He wore a crown of thorns, because He became a curse, because He subjected Himself to the law, because of His pain and His hunger and His thirst, which all came as a result of *our* sin—because of all this—Jesus has separated us from every one of those things, *and* the sin responsible for it, as far as the east is from the west. Praise Jesus forever!
