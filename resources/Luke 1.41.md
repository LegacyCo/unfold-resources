## Commentary
“And it happened, when Elizabeth heard the greeting of Mary, that the babe leaped in her womb; and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.”

Watch the order of events. Mary’s greeting reaches Elizabeth’s ears, the child leaps in her womb, Elizabeth is filled with the Holy Spirit—and the very next thing recorded is that “she spoke out with a loud voice and said, ‘Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb!’” (==Luke 1:42==). The filling produced speech. Elizabeth didn’t study her way to that blessing; no one had told her Mary was pregnant, let alone who the child was. The Spirit filled her, and she spoke what only God could have shown her.

This is a pattern Luke establishes deliberately and repeats. Zacharias is filled with the Holy Spirit and prophesies (==Luke 1:67==). The disciples at Pentecost are filled with the Spirit and begin to speak (==Acts 2:4==)—and Peter, standing up in that filling, preaches the sermon that opens the new covenant to three thousand people (==Acts 2:14==). Later, the praying church is filled with the Holy Spirit and speaks the word of God with boldness (==Acts 4:31==). Again and again, the sequence is the same: when the Spirit of God comes upon a person, that person speaks. God’s presence produces God’s voice through human mouths.

There is encouragement in the fact that Luke’s pattern begins here—not with an apostle or a prophet, but with an elderly woman in the hill country, months into an unexpected pregnancy, greeting a relative at her door. The Spirit is not waiting for a platform. He fills ordinary people in ordinary moments and gives them words that carry the knowledge of God. If the same Spirit lives in you (==Romans 8:9==), then the same pattern applies: the fullness of the Spirit will look for a voice.

## Application
Give the pattern a place to work this week. When you sense the nearness of God—in prayer, in worship, in an ordinary moment—don’t let the filling stay silent. Speak: bless someone out loud the way Elizabeth blessed Mary, with specific words about what God is doing in them. Encourage the believer who is carrying something God-given but hidden, the way Mary was. And pay attention to what rises in you around other people—sometimes the leap on the inside is the signal that God has something to say through you to the person in front of you. The Spirit fills people to give them words. Ask Him for yours, and then open your mouth.
