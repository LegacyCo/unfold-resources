## Commentary
“He answered and said to them, ‘Because it has been given to you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it has not been given.’”

The disciples have just asked Jesus why He speaks to the crowds in parables (==Matthew 13:10==). His answer tells them something about themselves. The mysteries of the kingdom of heaven have been *given* to them. A few verses later He goes further: “Blessed are your eyes for they see, and your ears for they hear; for assuredly, I say to you that many prophets and righteous men desired to see what you see, and did not see it” (==Matthew 13:16-17==).

That's an incredible thing to say to a group of fishermen and a tax collector.

God has always shared His secrets with His people. Before He judged Sodom, He asked, “Shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing?” (==Genesis 18:17==). He showed Joseph the meaning of Pharaoh's dreams, and Joseph was quick to say where it came from: “It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace” (==Genesis 41:16==). He revealed Nebuchadnezzar's dream to Daniel in a night vision (==Daniel 2:19==). Amos says the Lord “does nothing, unless He reveals His secret to His servants the prophets” (==Amos 3:7==).

Yet those prophets only saw part of the picture. Peter says they “inquired and searched carefully” about the salvation that was coming, and that even angels “desire to look into” it (==1 Peter 1:10-12==). The disciples were standing next to the One the prophets had been searching for.

Paul uses the same language in 1 Corinthians 2. He says we speak “the hidden wisdom which God ordained before the ages for our glory” (==1 Corinthians 2:7==). The rulers of this age missed it completely, “for had they known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory” (==1 Corinthians 2:8==). But God has revealed it to us “through His Spirit,” so “that we might know the things that have been freely given to us by God” (==1 Corinthians 2:10, 12==). Then Paul ends the chapter with this: “We have the mind of Christ” (==1 Corinthians 2:16==).

If you belong to Jesus, you're standing where the disciples stood. The secrets the prophets longed to see have been given to you.

## Word Study
**Mysteries** is the Greek *mystērion*. In English, a mystery is something we can't figure out. In Scripture, it's something God kept hidden until He chose to make it known. The same word appears in the Greek translation of Daniel 2. None of the wise men in Babylon could tell Nebuchadnezzar his dream, but Daniel told him, “There is a God in heaven who reveals secrets” (==Daniel 2:28==). And the secret God revealed was about a kingdom: “the God of heaven will set up a kingdom which shall never be destroyed” (==Daniel 2:44==).

So when Jesus talks about “the mysteries of the kingdom,” He's saying that kingdom has arrived in Him, though not in the way anyone expected. The parables in this chapter describe it: seed scattered on different soils, a mustard seed, leaven hidden in flour, treasure hidden in a field (==Matthew 13:31-33, 44==). It grows slowly, and most people walk right past it. Matthew says Jesus taught this way to fulfill the psalm: “I will utter things kept secret from the foundation of the world” (==Matthew 13:35==). Translation: the mysteries of the kingdom are God's long-kept plan, now made known in Jesus.

## Application
You're reading the Scriptures the prophets searched and the angels long to look into, and the Spirit who reveals the deep things of God lives in you (==1 Corinthians 2:10-12==). Boldly and confidently ask the Holy Spirit to reveal truth to you today.
