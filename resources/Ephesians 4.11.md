## Commentary
Jesus gave the 5-fold ministry to the church as a gift after He ascended...but also after He descended. Before the crown came the cross. Before He was exalted, He humbled Himself and took the form of a servant.

That pattern should shape every person called to lead in His church.

The fivefold ministries are not positions through which people rise above the body; they are gifts through which Christ stoops down to serve His body. Their purpose is not to build a platform, gather recognition, or establish personal importance. They exist to equip the saints, strengthen the church, and help the entire body grow into the maturity and fullness of Christ.

Those who represent the ascended Christ must also reflect the descended Christ.

Kingdom authority carries the nature of the Servant King. It doesn't force its way upward or demand to be recognized. It kneels, washes feet, carries burdens, and gives itself for the growth of others. The measure of ministry is not how many people serve the leader, but how faithfully the leader serves the people Jesus entrusted to them.

This is why Scripture tells us to clothe ourselves with humility and allow God to do the exalting (==1 Peter 5:5-6==). When a person attempts to exalt himself, he may gain visibility without receiving true spiritual authority. But when he willingly takes the lower place, God can entrust him with influence because that influence will be used to serve rather than control.

## Application
Do not chase elevation. Follow Jesus downward into humility, obedience, and service. Let God determine when—and whether—you should be lifted up. The safest place for a leader is not at the top of a platform, but low before God, carrying the nature of the King who descended before He ascended.
