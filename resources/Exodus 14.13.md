## Commentary
Israel was trapped. Pharaoh's army was closing in behind them while the sea blocked the path forward. Fear took over. They questioned God's goodness, doubted His leading, and even wished they had remained slaves in Egypt.

And into the crisis Moses delivers a surprising command:

> "Do not fear. Stand still... The LORD will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace" ([[Exodus 14.13-14]]).

The phrase _"hold your peace"_ (NASB: _"keep silent"_) comes from the Hebrew word haras. It certainly means _to be silent, to keep still,_ or _to remain inactive_. But elsewhere the same word is also used for plowing, engraving, and preparing.

A perfect word for that moment.

Plowing always comes before planting. Before new life can grow, hard ground must first be broken open. The soil is turned over, softened, and prepared to receive what it could never produce in its untouched condition.

That is exactly what God was doing on the shores of the Red Sea.

While Israel waited for whatever was coming next, God was plowing their hearts. Every question, every impossible scenario realized, every moment of waiting was exposing the hardened soil of unbelief. Israel was going through the process of learning who this God really was. Would He abandon them? Had He rescued them only to destroy them? Or would He prove Himself faithful once again?

Before God divided the sea in front of them, He was breaking up the ground within them.

The same is often true for us. The Lord uses seasons of delay to plow the fallow ground of our hearts. He engraves fresh revelations of His faithfulness through circumstances we could never have chosen for ourselves. Every act of surrendered obedience allows Him to turn over another layer of unbelief until, one day, we look back and can say, _I remember when God did what only God could do._
