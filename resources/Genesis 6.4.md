## Commentary
Whereas chapter 5 deals with the sons of Adam, chapter 6 begins by dealing with the daughters of Adam. V2 says that these “daughters of Adam” are being taken as wives by "the sons of God.” This phrase, “sons of God,” is very unique. It is the same one used in Job 1 which describes the heavenly counsel that would take place from time to time, “Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the Lord, and Satan also came among them” (Job 1:6).

This passage introduces a breed of creature referred to as the Nephilim. Nephilim literally means “fallen ones.” These “fallen ones” were “on the earth in those days, and also afterwards, when the sons of God came in to the daughters of mankind (Adam), and they (the daughters of Adam) bore children to them (the Nephilim). Those were the mighty men of old, men of renown (fame).”

Here are two reasons why I would say these Nephilim are fallen angels who were trying to crossbreed with the daughters of Adam in order to corrupt mankind — and, more importantly, why this matters when we get to the conquest stories of Israel taking the Promised Land. I could be wrong here, and I’m willing to be wrong, but I believe it does connect some dots for us on the larger story of Scripture.

Reason #1: Unlike the sons of Adam, these “fallen ones” were not affected by the flood. V4 says that these “fallen ones” were “on the earth in those days and also afterwards,” “afterwards” as in, after what is getting ready to happen (ie; the flood). Fast forward to when Israel has been freed from Egypt and they are in the wilderness. Moses sends out twelve spies to see the Promised Land where God was going to bring them. The twelve spies come back to Moses and tell the people of Israel what they saw in the Promised Land. This is what they say in Numbers 13:31-33, “We are not able to go up against the people of that land, for they are too strong for us…the land devours its inhabitants, and all the people we saw in it are giants. We also saw the Nephilim there…and we were like grasshoppers before them.” 

If the flood destroyed all humanity except for Noah and his family, how did the genetic freak show of the Nephilim show back up on the scene 1,000 years later? The conclusion is their origin is not on the earth, they are from a different realm, they have a different dwelling that isn’t affected by 40 days of rain flooding the earth.

Which brings me to the second reason why I would say the Nephilim are fallen angels. Jude writes something very interesting in his letter to the church. He tells us that there are fallen angels who have broken the cosmic order, inserted themselves into humanity, and are now suffering the consequences of their actions, "But I want to remind you, though you once knew this…angels who did not keep to their own domain (sphere of authority) but abandoned their proper dwelling place, these (angels) the Lord has kept in darkness, bound with eternal chains for judgment on the great Day, just as Sodom and Gomorrah…since they in the same way as these angels indulged in sexual perversion and went after strange flesh, they serve as an example of those who suffer the punishment of eternal fire" (Jude 5-7).

Jude is reminding the church that there are fallen angels who will be punished with eternal fire because they abandoned their assigned dwelling place within the cosmic order that is intended to separate the realm of the spirit from the realm of the flesh, but these fallen angels “indulged in sexual perversion” with “foreign flesh”…or to use the language of Genesis 6, they took wives for themselves from “daughters of Adam” who bore children to them. 

Jude helps us understand what is going on here in Genesis 6. There was a breed of creature from a different realm, called “fallen ones”, who saw that the daughters of Adam were beautiful and couldn’t resist them. So they left their assigned dwelling and “came in to” the daughters of Adam.

This is why God was so adamant that Israel not marry any of the tribes from the Promised land. It also puts their aggression toward the inhabitants of that land in a proper perspective. These fallen angels, the Nephilim, didn’t just indulge in sexual perversion with the daughters of Adam. Genesis 6 says that they reproduced. The daughters of Adam bore children for these fallen ones. Their offspring filled the earth in Genesis 6 and they once again filled the land that God intended his people to dwell in in Moses’ day (ie; the Promised Land). So, keep this in mind when you consider what seems like a very heavy handed command for Israel to completely annihilate the inhabitants of the Promised Land. That land wasn’t just filled with really tall people, it was the dwelling place of the offspring of fallen angels.

This *also* gives a new weight of importance to the standoff between King Saul’s army and that 9-foot tall Philistine giant named Goliath who, if you wanted to take the time to investigate (==Numbers 13:33==, ==1 Samuel 17:4==, ==Joshua 11:21-22==), would be found to be a descendant of the Nephilim. Goliath, the offspring of defiant fallen angels who rebelled against God’s created order, defiantly stands in front of God’s people and says, “If you can kill me then my people will be your servants. But if I kill your man (your *Adam*), then you will serve me and my people” (==1 Samuel 17:8-9==). The battle of David and Goliath isn’t just a Sunday school story, it’s a cosmic clash between two very different family lineages in the earth.
