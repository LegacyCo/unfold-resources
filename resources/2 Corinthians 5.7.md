## Commentary
“For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

Matthew Henry wrote: “Those that will deal with God must deal upon trust; we must quit the things that are seen for things that are not seen, and submit to the sufferings of this present time in hopes of a glory that is yet to be revealed.” That is the arrangement this verse describes. Dealing with God is dealing upon trust—Abraham went out not knowing where he was going (==Hebrews 11:8==), and everyone who follows the same call walks a road where what you see will sometimes lie to you and say there is nothing there. Faith is the capacity to keep seeing what sight denies (==2 Corinthians 4:18==).

Walking by faith belongs to this age alone. When the kingdom comes in its fullness and the saints are resurrected and glorified with Christ, there will be no more opportunity to live by faith—faith will have become sight (==1 Corinthians 13:12==). There will be no more worshiping in the midst of trial, no more maturing under tension, because there will be no trial and no tension. Those things are what this life affords us, and only this life. The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to be revealed (==Romans 8:18==), but the trust exercised in them is a treasure that can only be gathered now. This is not the time for comfort; this is the age of sojourning, seeking, pressing on, and laying hold (==Philippians 3:12-14==). The believer who understands that stops resenting the walk of faith and starts valuing it—it is the one thing we will never get to do again.
