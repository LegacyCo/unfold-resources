## Commentary
“But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord.”

Notice the mechanism Paul describes: beholding leads to becoming. You don’t grit your way into the image of Christ; you gaze your way into it. What you behold, you begin to reflect—the way a mirror can’t help but carry the image of whatever is set before it. This is God’s eternal intent working itself out in us: we are “predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son” (==Romans 8:29==), so that in the same way Jesus is the image of the invisible God (==Colossians 1:15==), the church becomes the image of Christ—“as He is, so are we in this world” (==1 John 4:17==).

But this is vital: the verse says we are *being* transformed—a process, from glory to glory—and that process has our participation built into it. Transformation doesn’t just happen. David didn’t only say, “One thing I have *desired* of the LORD”—he finished the sentence: “that will I *seek*” (==Psalm 27:4==). You can desire to bench press 275 pounds, but unless you actively train for it, you’ll never touch it. Desire without pursuit is just a wish. Plenty of believers deeply desire to look like Jesus and wonder why, year after year, they don’t—while never positioning themselves in front of the mirror.

So partner with the Holy Spirit. He does the transforming—“just as by the Spirit of the Lord”—but you do the beholding, and the practices of the faith are simply the places where beholding happens: prayer, fasting, Scripture, silence and solitude, fellowship, worship, self-control. Those disciplines have no power to change you in themselves; they’re not merit badges. They’re mirrors—positions you take up where the glory of the Lord can be seen steadily enough to shape you. If you are in Christ and you give yourself to this process, then your inner man is being renewed day by day (==2 Corinthians 4:16==)—which means every day that passes, you look more like the Man Christ Jesus than you did the day before.

## Application
Pick your mirrors for this season. Choose two practices—one that comes easily to you (maybe Scripture or worship) and one that doesn’t (maybe fasting, or silence and solitude)—and build them into the actual calendar, not the intentions list. Then check your aim: the goal of the practice is never the practice. It’s beholding. If you finish your reading and haven’t looked at *Him*, you’ve held the mirror backwards. And take the long view the verse takes: “from glory to glory” means the change is usually invisible day to day and unmistakable year to year. Surrender to the Spirit’s work, stay in front of the glass, and let Him do what only He does.
