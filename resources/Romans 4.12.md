## Word Study
Paul uses a fascinating word here. The word translated “walk” comes from the Greek verb stoicheō. It is related to the idea of moving in line, keeping in step, or ordering one’s life according to a pattern. The related noun *stoicheion* can refer to the vertical stick on a sundial that casts the shadow. Paul is emphasizing those who walk in line with Abraham’s faith.

The picture is that of ordered movement. To *stoicheō* is to keep step, like soldiers aligned in a row, moving in the same direction, in the same manner. Abraham’s true children are not those who claim connection to him outwardly, but those whose lives come into alignment with the faith Abraham had before circumcision.

Paul is showing us that Abraham was counted righteous *before* he received the covenant sign. Circumcision came afterward. It was a seal, not the source, of his righteousness. Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness. So the issue is not merely who bears the outward mark of Abraham’s family, but who walks in the same faith as Abraham.

This does not diminish obedience. It puts obedience in its proper order. Abraham’s obedience flowed from faith; it did not replace faith. The outward sign was important, but only as a witness to the inward reality. Paul is dismantling every attempt to make righteousness rest on anything external while the heart remains untouched by faith.

## Application
Romans 4:12 teaches us that faith has footsteps.

Abraham trusted God with his life. He left his country. He believed the promise. He waited for what he could not produce. He entrusted his future to the God who raises the dead and calls things that are not as though they were.

To walk in the footsteps of Abraham’s faith is to choose to live a life of faith even when the promise seems delayed and God hasn't told you why.
