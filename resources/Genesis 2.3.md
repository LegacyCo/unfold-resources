## Commentary
Theologians refer to the importance of the first time a word is used as the "law of first mentions." This means the initial occurrence of a word, doctrine, or theme in Scripture establishes a foundational meaning that continues throughout the Bible. Abraham Heschel points out in His book *Sabbath* that this is the first time the word "holy" (qadosh) is used in Scripture. "Holy" is a word that represents the mystery and majesty of the divine. And what does God first distinguish in creation as "holy" A mountain...an altar? 

No. A day.

Not an object. Not a sacred space. God first gives the quality of holiness to a moment in time.

Heschel goes on to point out that it was only *after* Israel had worshipped a *thing*, the golden calf, that the holiness of a space/thing was commanded; the tabernacle. The holiness of time came first, the holiness of man came second, the holiness of space last.
