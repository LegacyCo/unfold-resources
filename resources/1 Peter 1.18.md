## Commentary
“Knowing that you were not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold, *from your aimless conduct received by tradition from your fathers*, but with the precious blood of Christ” (==1 Peter 1:18-19==).

Look carefully at what Peter says the blood redeemed you *from*. Not just from guilt. Not just from judgment. From a way of living you *inherited*—handed down from your fathers. When each of us was born, we received more than hair color and height from our family line. We received patterns: ways of handling anger, money, conflict, and pain that got passed from one generation to the next like heirlooms nobody asked for.

You know how the script goes. “I can’t help yelling at my kids—that’s how it was when I was growing up.” “We’ve always been bad with money—that’s just how our family is made up.” We speak about inherited patterns as if they were genetic destiny. And apart from Christ, there’s truth in that: trace any family tree back far enough and you end up in a garden, where sin broke something in Adam that every generation since has passed down (==Romans 5:12==). But Peter says the blood of Jesus reached all the way back down your family line—and paid the ransom that gets you out of it.

This is what being born again actually means for your family tree: the lineage of addiction, divorce, poverty, depression, rage—whatever ran in your bloodline—*ends at your re-birth*. That may be the story of the family you were born into, but you’ve been born again into a different family, “not of corruptible seed but incorruptible” (==1 Peter 1:23==)—and there is no predisposition to those things in the new seed. You weren’t handed a patched-up version of the old inheritance. You were grafted into a new vine (==John 15:5==), given a new Father, a new bloodline, a new set of family traits: “if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away” (==2 Corinthians 5:17==).

## Application
Start by identifying the script. Write down the sentences that have run in your family—“we’re yellers,” “we always struggle with money,” “the men in our family leave.” Name them for what they are: aimless conduct received by tradition from your fathers, and specifically the thing the blood of Jesus redeemed you from. Then stop repeating them over your life, and replace them out loud with the truth of your new lineage: “That ran in my family. It doesn’t run in me. I’ve been born of incorruptible seed.” And take it one generation further—you’re not just the end of an old line; you’re the start of a new one. What your children and grandchildren inherit from you gets decided by which family tree you live from. Live from the new one.
