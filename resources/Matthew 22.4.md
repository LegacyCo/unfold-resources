## Commentary
“Again, he sent out other servants, saying, ‘Tell those who are invited, “See, I have prepared my dinner; my oxen and fatted cattle are killed, and all things are ready. Come to the wedding.”’”

The kingdom of heaven, Jesus says, is like a king who arranged a marriage for his son and prepared a feast (==Matthew 22:2==). Look at the message the king sends: not “start getting ready,” not “contribute a dish”—*all things are ready. Come.* The preparation is entirely the king’s. The oxen are killed, the tables are set, the cost has been paid. The only thing the invited can add to the feast is their presence.

And look at what kept them away. Busyness. “They made light of it and went their ways, one to his own farm, another to his business” (==Matthew 22:5==). Nobody in the parable argues that the feast isn’t worth attending. They simply treat it as less urgent than their fields and their commerce—ordinary, legitimate things, made deadly by what they displaced. The tragedy of the parable is not that the invited were refused entry; it’s that they refused a ready table for things that could have waited.

So the king widens the invitation: “Go into the highways, and as many as you find, invite to the wedding” (==Matthew 22:9==)—and the hall is filled with both bad and good (==Matthew 22:10==). This is the gospel’s shape: a feast fully prepared by God, an invitation that costs nothing to accept, and a guest list that reaches as far as the servants will carry it (==Isaiah 25:6==; ==Luke 14:17==).

## Application
Answer the invitation at the level the parable aims at. If you’ve been treating the things of God as something to get to after the farm and the business—after the season calms down, after the schedule clears—hear the king’s message: all things are *ready*, now, and the readiness is on His side, not yours. You don’t come to the feast prepared; you come to a feast that was prepared for you. And if you’ve already taken your seat, then take up the servants’ assignment: go find the people on the highways—the ones no one thought to invite—and tell them the same thing you were told. The dinner is ready. Come.
