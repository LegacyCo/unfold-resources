## Commentary
There is something striking about Matthew’s choice of words here. The man who comes to Jesus is a leper. Under the Law, his condition made him ceremonially unclean. He was required to live outside the camp, separated from the community in which God dwelt among His people (==Leviticus 13:45–46==; ==Numbers 5:1–4==).

In other words, this is a man accustomed to being told where he *cannot* go. He can't just enter the assembly at the temple. He can't approach God as everyone else approaches. His disease has placed distance between himself and the ordinary places of worship.

And yet Matthew says: “And behold, a leper came and *worshiped* Him, saying, ‘Lord, if You are willing, You can make me clean.’”

The word translated “worshiped” is _proskyneō_ (προσκυνέω). It literally carries the idea of bowing down, falling before someone, or paying homage. Depending on the context, it can describe reverence offered to a king or superidor, but Matthew also repeatedly uses it for worship directed toward Jesus (==Matthew 2:11==; ==Matthew 14:33==; ==Matthew 28:9==; ==Matthew 28:17==).

But we should not necessarily picture this man doing what we commonly call “worship”—singing, praising, or participating in a religious service. His worship looks like falling before Jesus and entrusting himself completely to Him.

In that moment, the man doesn't question Jesus’ ability to heal. His only question concerns Jesus’ willingness. _You can do this. I believe that. I simply don't know whether You will do it for me._

And Matthew calls that worship.

The man brings Jesus no song. He brings no sacrifice. He has no offering in his hands. He simply comes, bows himself before Jesus, and places the full weight of his need upon who he believes Jesus to be. And Jesus receives that man's *faith* as worship.

There is also a beautiful reversal happening in the story. Under the old purity system, the leper had to remain at a distance because his uncleanness could contaminate others. But when he comes to Jesus, Jesus does the unthinkable: “Jesus put out His hand and touched him” (==Matthew 8:3==). Uncleanness does not move from the leper into Jesus. Cleansing moves from Jesus into the leper.

The man who had been kept at a distance from the place of God’s presence discovers that the presence of God has come near to him.

Worship is not just something we offer when we have been made presentable enough to approach God. Sometimes worship is simply coming to Jesus exactly as we are, bowing before Him, and saying, “I believe You are able.”

## Application
The leper teaches us that worship can look like bringing our need to Jesus with confidence in who He is.

He did not come pretending to be clean. He came to Jesus because he knew he wasn’t. And he didn't allow his condition to keep him at a distance from the Healer. He bowed before Him and said, in effect, _“I know what is wrong with me, but I also know what is possible with You.”_

There may be places in your life where you feel unworthy to approach God. Your instinct may be to clean yourself up first, get your life together, overcome the struggle, and then come near. But the gospel moves in the opposite direction. We do not become clean so that we can come to Jesus; we come to Jesus because He is the One who makes us clean.

So come near. Bring Him what is broken. Bring Him what you cannot fix. Bring Him the thing that makes you want to keep your distance.

Sometimes the deepest expression of worship is simply falling before Jesus and saying, _“Lord, I know You are able.”_
