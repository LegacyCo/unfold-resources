## Commentary
Psalm 6 does not end where it begins. David begins in anguish. He is weak. His bones are troubled. His soul is greatly troubled. He asks, “But You, O LORD—how long?” (Psalm 6:3). Such an honest prayer. David is feeling the agony of delay.

Then he says, “I am weary with my groaning; all night I make my bed swim; I drench my couch with my tears” (Psalm 6:6).

The word translated “weary” is *yāgaʿ*, meaning to labor, toil, grow exhausted, or become worn out. David is exhausted from sorrow. Even his grieving has become labor.

The word translated “groaning” is ’*ănāḥâ*, which means sighing, groaning, or deep lament. These are not polished prayers. These are the sounds that come from a soul under pressure.

And yet David says something remarkable in verse 8: “The LORD has heard the voice of my weeping.”

David’s tears are not meaningless before God. They have a voice. 

Psalm 6 turns when David becomes confident that God has heard him.

“The LORD has heard my supplication; the LORD will receive my prayer.”

The circumstances may not have changed yet, but David’s confidence has. He moves from “How long?” to “The LORD has heard.” That's faith finding its footing again in the character of God.
