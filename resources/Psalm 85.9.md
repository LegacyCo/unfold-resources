## Commentary
The psalmist makes a remarkable statement in this verse: “Surely His salvation is near to those who fear Him, that glory may dwell in our land.”

His desire is not simply for Israel to experience prosperity, security, or national restoration. He longs for the glory of God to dwell among His people. The Hebrew word for glory is _kabod_, which speaks of weight, honor, and splendor. In this context, it points to the presence of God—the same glory that filled the tabernacle in ==Exodus 40:34–35== and later the temple in ==1 Kings 8:10–11==.

But notice where the psalmist desires this glory to dwell. Not simply in the temple, or even among the people, but _in the land_.

Throughout Scripture, there is a profound relationship between God's people and the land they inhabit. When God created Adam, He placed him in a garden with the responsibility to cultivate and keep it. Humanity was created in God's image and entrusted with the stewardship of His creation. The intention was that the earth would flourish under the care of those living in communion with its Creator. God's glory was never intended to be confined to a sanctuary. His desire has always been for the whole earth to be filled with His glory (==Habakkuk 2:14==).

Paul picks up on this relationship in ==Romans 8:19–21==, explaining that creation is eagerly waiting for the revealing of the sons of God. Creation itself was subjected to futility through humanity's fall, and it now groans in anticipation of its own liberation. Remarkably, Paul connects the restoration of creation to the glorification of God's children. The earth is awaiting the full unveiling of a redeemed humanity, restored to its intended identity and communion with God.

While the fullness of this restoration awaits the resurrection and renewal of all things, there is something of this reality we are invited to participate in now.

When God's people live in union with Christ, carrying His presence into the places He has entrusted to them, the effects of that union are not meant to remain confined to their private spiritual lives. The life of God within His people is intended to touch the world around them. Families, communities, cities, and even the land itself can experience the fruit of a people living in right relationship with their Creator.

This is part of the beauty of the psalmist's prayer. He longs for the restoration of God's presence among His people, knowing that the consequences of that restoration will extend beyond them. In fact, just a few verses later, he declares, “Yes, the Lord will give what is good; and our land will yield its increase” (==Psalm 85:12==).

There is glory _in_ the people, and there is glory that touches the land through them.

Our calling is to become a people through whom His presence is made known. We are called to live as the firstfruits of the coming restoration, bearing witness to the kingdom that will one day fill the earth.
