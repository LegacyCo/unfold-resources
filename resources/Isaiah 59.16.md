## Word Study
The word used to describe the Lord's reaction in this verse means to be moved deeply by the desolation of something, to be horrified by the state of ruin (see also Jeremiah 19:8).

Interestingly, this word is also used in Daniel 12:11 as the "abomination of desolation."

Our God is a God who is moved when He sees ruin and desolation, who sees the absence of justice and salvation, and He says, "This is not ok!" He is so deeply moved that He engages the desolation and brings life to it - "by His own arm He brought salvation." 

God does not stand back, aloof and detached from the injustice of the world. He enters in. He is Emmanuel, God *with* us. As the Psalmist writes, "He is intimately acquainted with all of our ways" (Psalm 139:3). 

God could have very easily remained where He was and sent salvation, and still that would have been more than we deserved. But that's not His nature. Instead, He wrapped Himself in zeal and flesh, *bringing* life to the world. He is the intervening God.
