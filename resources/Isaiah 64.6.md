## Commentary
“But we are all like an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are like filthy rags.”

Any righteousness I try to produce on my own is exactly this: filthy rags. But here’s what we have to get straight: *whose* righteousness is Isaiah describing, and under *which* covenant? Isaiah spoke as a man under the old covenant—a shadow of the good things to come (==Hebrews 10:1==). He knew nothing of the redemptive blood of Emmanuel. He didn’t yet understand that the same God he saw high and lifted up would wrap Himself in flesh, descend into the depths of our sin, and become unclean *for us* so that we could become the righteousness of God in Him (==2 Corinthians 5:21==).

The church has too often let old covenant language be spoken over a new covenant people. We hear Isaiah cry “Woe is me, for I am unclean” (==Isaiah 6:5==) and adopt his perspective as our own—as though the cross changed nothing about our condition. But for someone on this side of the cross to take on the self-assessment of a man under the old covenant is like a slave who has been legally freed continuing to live in his chains. “For freedom Christ has set us free… do not be entangled again with a yoke of bondage” (==Galatians 5:1==).
