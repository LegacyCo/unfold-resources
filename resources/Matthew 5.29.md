## Commentary
Tear out the eye. Cut off the hand. Jesus uses violent language because He is dealing with a severe issue—one whose implication is that it can carry a whole body into hell. Yet He is not advocating self-mutilation. The problem is not ultimately the eye or the hand but the heart. The eye and the hand represent something: the eye is access—what I allow in (==Matthew 6:22==). The hand is action—what I reach for.

Jesus commands ruthlessness with both. Deal ruthlessly with what has access to your eyes. Deal aggressively with what you keep reaching for. Make no provision for the flesh (==Romans 13:14==); put to death what belongs to the earthly nature (==Colossians 3:5==). Sin cannot be managed—only mortified. Assuming you can safely live with private sin is like assuming you can safely live with a rattlesnake in your bedroom. You may tiptoe around it for a while, but eventually you will step on it.

Practically, the amputation Jesus describes might look like deleting the app, blocking the account, ending the conversation, moving the phone out of the bedroom, confessing to a spouse, refusing to trust yourself in certain environments, or telling someone the truth so that what has been hidden in the dark can be overcome in the light. This is not weakness. It is wisdom. There is nothing mature about continuing to do things that destroy you.

Still, removal is not the destination. Jesus does not want merely empty hands and clean search histories. He removes what destroys so that He can heal what desires. The severity of the surgery is the measure of His intention to restore.

## Application
Identify your eye and your hand. The eye is your access point: the app, the account, the show, the feed, the environment where temptation reliably finds you. The hand is your repeated reach: the habit you keep returning to even after promising yourself you wouldn’t. Name them specifically—vague enemies are never defeated.

Then do the surgery today, not when it feels manageable. Delete the app. Block the account. Move the phone out of the bedroom. End the conversation. Tell someone the truth. Don’t negotiate with what Jesus says must be removed—you cannot manage a monster, and there is nothing mature about continuing to do things that destroy you. And remember why you’re cutting: not to impress God with what you’ve given up, but to clear the ground where He intends to heal your desires.
