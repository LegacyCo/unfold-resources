## Commentary
It is fair to say that this verse is one of the hardest verses in Genesis to translate and understand, so much so that Jewish tradition considered this to be one of the “indeterminate verses”, and some modern commentators have given up on discovering its meaning. One commentator says that “every attempt to extract a meaning from this verse is more or less a tour de force.”

However we translate the verse, the takeaway is two-fold: 

1) sin has a pervasive power that desires to enslave its victim and,

2) Cain has a choice. 

Somehow, even after the result of what his parents did, Cain is not so consumed by the power of sin that his future sin is inevitable. God says that Cain still has the ability to resist and rule over sin. 

Cain is not utterly depraved. Cain has a choice. 

When Cain faces the alternatives, he *is* capable of making the right choice. Otherwise, God’s words to him about “doing well” would be meaningless and cruel. Should he so desire, Cain is able to overcome the creature that crouches at the door. This becomes even more clear when you look at the original Hebrew text which uses an emphatic pronoun: “You, you are to master it.”

## Application
Sin is real. Its desire is real. Its power to enslave is real. God does not minimize any of that. He pictures sin like a beast crouching at the door, waiting for the moment when anger becomes action, bitterness becomes murder, and inward disorder becomes outward destruction.

But this passage shows us the sinner has a choice.

We cannot always control what rises up in us. Anger may rise. Jealousy may rise. Resentment may rise. The temptation to justify ourselves, punish others, or rehearse offense may rise. But the presence of temptation is not the same as surrender to it.

You do not have to give in to what is crouching at the door.

This is where many of us lose the battle. We treat temptation as though it has already decided the outcome. We say, “This is just how I am.” “This is what I always do.” “I can’t help it.” But Genesis 4:7 confronts that lie. Cain was not powerless in his anger, and neither are we powerless in ours.

When sin is crouching, don't negotiate with it. Don't entertain it. Bring it into the light before God. Name it. Resist it. Rule over it before it rules over you.
