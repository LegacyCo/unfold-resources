## Commentary
Israel understood God's promise, but they didn't understand His ways.

They knew God had promised them a land. They knew He had delivered them from Egypt. They had seen the Red Sea open, Pharaoh drown, manna fall, water come from the rock, and fire rest over the camp. The issue was not that they lacked evidence. The issue was that they interpreted every difficult situation through the lens of unbelief.

When they suffered, they complained. When they waited, they accused God. When they faced lack, they assumed abandonment. When the journey took longer than expected, they began to believe turning back to Egypt was a better option than remaining faithful to God.

The writer of Hebrews says the problem was that they did not know God’s ways. They didn't understand how God forms people through time. They misunderstand that the wilderness was the place where God was teaching them to trust, follow, and live by every word that came from His mouth.

Israel wanted promise without process. But Yahweh uses time differently than man does. We use time to measure delay. God uses time to form sons.

Israel's failure of unbelief is proof that they let suffering redefine God. 

And this is the warning for us: if we don't understand God's ways, we too will misread His process. As I heard Bob Gladstone once say, "If we don't understand God's ways, we'll be offended when He acts like Himself." And bringing us through the wilderness of trials and testing — before He seats us in the Promised Land — is just Him acting like Himself.
