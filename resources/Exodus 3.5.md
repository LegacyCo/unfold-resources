## Commentary
“Then He said, ‘Do not draw near this place. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you stand is holy ground.’”

Ordinary desert sand, the same dirt Moses had walked over for forty years, became holy ground the moment God was present in the bush. We read this story and accept that God is so holy, whatever He comes in contact with becomes holy. 

But here’s the question: do we believe that about dirt underneath Moses' feet and doubt it about ourselves? The same God who made a patch of sand holy by His presence now dwells *in* His people (==1 Corinthians 3:16==)—and many in the church still hesitate to agree with the Scriptures: that He has made *us* holy (==Hebrews 10:10==; ==Colossians 1:22==). As if His holiness were strong enough for sand but not for sinners.

Under the old covenant, uncleanness was contagious—touch a leper, touch a corpse, and *you* became unclean. Then Jesus came, and upended the entire flow of cleanliness. He touched the leper, and instead of Jesus becoming unclean, the leper became clean (==Mark 1:41-42==). That’s the scandal and the glory of His holiness: it doesn’t need protection from contamination. It *overwhelms* contamination. So when God takes dirty, broken, law-breaking, formerly-dead enemies and makes them holy, that doesn’t lower the standard of His holiness—it puts it on display. Anyone can keep clean things clean. Only God can make dirty things holy. And He intends for the world to see what He does with dirty people, not just patches of dirt—a people called by His name who actually know what His presence has made them.
