## Commentary
"Thus says the Lord GOD: 'Woe to the foolish prophets, who follow their own spirit and have seen nothing!'"

Ezekiel was one of many voices in Israel claiming to speak for God, and his was the least welcome. Some of the other voices were in Jerusalem, and some were living among the exiles in Babylon (==Jeremiah 29:8-9==). Their message was much easier to hear than Ezekiel's. Ezekiel said Jerusalem would fall. They said it wouldn't. They told the people "Peace!" when there was no peace (==Ezekiel 13:10==). So God sends Ezekiel to prophesy against "those who prophesy out of their own heart" (==Ezekiel 13:2==), and verse 3 tells us the source of their prophetic messaging. 

They followed their own spirit, and they had seen nothing.

The old name for a prophet was *seer* (==1 Samuel 9:9==), because a prophet was supposed to have seen something before he opened his mouth. Ezekiel's book begins, "the heavens were opened and I saw visions of God" (==Ezekiel 1:1==). But these false prophets had seen nothing. They spoke anyway, and they signed God's name to their message: "They have envisioned futility and false divination, saying, 'Thus says the LORD!' But the LORD has not sent them; yet they hope that the word may be confirmed" (==Ezekiel 13:6==). Translation: they said "God told me," then waited to see if it would come true.

Today someone stands up and says "The Lord told me" about a date, an election, a healing, a spouse, or a financial breakthrough—sometimes with an offering appeal attached—and it doesn't happen. It gets explained away, and the next word comes at the next conference. Others prophesy nothing but blessing over people the Spirit is calling to repentance. Peter warned it would happen inside the church: "by covetousness they will exploit you with deceptive words" (==2 Peter 2:3==). The damage looks the same as it did in Ezekiel's day. Sincere believers are left disillusioned, and some conclude that God doesn't speak at all. 

Jeremiah faced the same kind of prophets in Jerusalem, and through him God gives the test for a true word: "I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran. I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied. But if they had stood in My counsel, and had caused My people to hear My words, then they would have turned them from their evil way" (==Jeremiah 23:21-22==). A true prophet has stood in God's counsel. He has been with God before he speaks for Him, and the word he brings turns people toward God. He goes up into the gap and builds, the way Moses "stood before Him in the breach" for Israel (==Psalm 106:23==; ==Ezekiel 22:30==). True prophecy points to Jesus, "for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (==Revelation 19:10==). In the New Testament it builds up, exhorts, and comforts (==1 Corinthians 14:3==). It's spoken by people who know "we prophesy in part" (==1 Corinthians 13:9==), and it's submitted to others who weigh it (==1 Corinthians 14:29==). 

But the presence of false prophecy doesn't mean we throw prophecy away. In this very chapter, God's answer to false prophets is a true one. He exposes the counterfeit by speaking through Ezekiel. Paul handles Corinth the same way. That church was misusing tongues and prophecy so badly that their gatherings had turned into confusion (==1 Corinthians 14:23==; ==1 Corinthians 14:33==), and Paul's conclusion is, "desire earnestly to prophesy, and do not forbid to speak with tongues. Let all things be done decently and in order" (==1 Corinthians 14:39-40==). They had made a mess of the Lord's Supper too, so much so that some were sick, and even dying from mishandling the Body of Christ (==1 Corinthians 11:29-30==). But Paul didn't tell them to stop taking communion. He taught them *how* to take communion (==1 Corinthians 11:23-34==). To the Thessalonians he wrote, "Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies. Test all things; hold fast what is good" (==1 Thessalonians 5:19-21==). Paul expects some words to fail the test, and he still won't let the church despise prophecy. Scripture answers error with correction and instruction in the way God intended the gift to work.

Moses once said, "Oh, that all the LORD's people were prophets and that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!" (==Numbers 11:29==). At Pentecost God did exactly that: "your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" (==Acts 2:17==). He still wants a people who hear Him and speak what He gives them. A church that shuts down prophecy because of the counterfeits hasn't become safer. It has stopped listening.

## Word Study
**Foolish** is the Hebrew *nabal* (נָבָל). The word has less to do with intelligence than with moral and spiritual folly—living as though God doesn't need to be reckoned with. It's the word in "The fool has said in his heart, 'There is no God'" (==Psalm 14:1==). It's also the name of Abigail's husband, of whom she said, "as his name is, so is he: Nabal is his name, and folly is with him!" (==1 Samuel 25:25==). Isaiah says "the foolish person will speak foolishness, and his heart will work iniquity" (==Isaiah 32:6==). Summary: these prophets used God's name constantly, but they spoke like men who never expected God to hold hold them accountable for what they said in His name.

## Application
When someone gives you "a word," don't swallow it whole...and don't spit it out. Test it (==1 John 4:1==). Does it agree with Scripture, does it point you to Jesus, and does it call for repentance where repentance is needed instead of only telling you what you hoped to hear? Be cautious with any word that comes attached to money or to loyalty to the person giving it. Bring it to your pastor or a mature believer and weigh it together before you make a decision based on it. And when you're the one sharing what you sense God is saying, say no more than you've actually seen. "I think the Lord may be showing me..." is more honest than "Thus says the Lord" for something you received in prayer this morning.
