## Commentary
The condition of Israel could be traced through the condition of its priesthood. When the priests honored the presence of God, preserved His truth, and regarded their calling with holy fear, the people were drawn toward faithfulness. But when the priests departed from the way, they did not fall alone. They “caused many to stumble at the law.”

Israel’s decline did not begin when the nation rejected God; it began when those appointed to preserve the knowledge of God lost their heavenly respect for the calling. The priests became casual with holy things. They no longer trembled at His Word or guarded the distinction between what was clean and unclean. Once the priesthood ceased to reveal the holiness of God, the nation lost its bearings.

This is the weight carried by anyone entrusted with the knowledge of God. Our departure from His way never remains private. Those who look to us for direction will often follow the path our lives have made believable. A leader may continue to teach the right words while contradicting them through his conduct, and the contradiction eventually becomes a stumbling block to everyone watching.

This is why Scripture says, “Like people, like priest” (==Hosea 4:9==). The spiritual condition of those who lead, teach, and represent God inevitably works its way into the people. When they are consumed with zeal for God, that hunger spreads. When they become compromised, careless, or self-serving, the same corruption begins to reproduce itself in others.

Malachi’s charge is therefore more serious than personal failure. The priests had “departed from the way,” but their departure had created a false path for many others. They had used the authority of their calling to give credibility to a life God had not approved.

The people of God need more than accurate instruction; they need lives that make the way of God visible to them. The greatest safeguard against causing others to stumble is not greater talent, influence, or knowledge, but a continuing fear of the Lord. Those who remain near to God, tremble at His Word, and refuse to treat holy things casually become a steadying presence among His people.
