## Commentary
“Now the LORD had said to Abram: ‘Get out of your country, from your family and from your father's house, to a land that I will show you.’”

Abram was living in Ur of the Chaldeans, a pagan land, worshiping the gods of his fathers (==Joshua 24:2==). Then a God he had never heard of came to him there, revealed Himself, and called him out—out of his country, his family, his inheritance, his gods, his way of life; out of everything that defined him, onto a new path toward a home he had never seen. God spoke, God disrupted his life, and God called him out of all he had ever known.

This is always the first step on a person's journey with God. Search the Scriptures and the story repeats; read the biography of any saint and they say the same thing: “There I was, living my life like everyone else, when I became conscious of a God who was calling me—‘Come out from the way you are living and follow Me to a land I will show you.’” Abram had to come out before he could be brought in. Israel came out of Egypt so that they could go into the Promised Land. The pattern repeats for every believer: God calls us out of the life we were living so that He can bring us into His kingdom (==Colossians 1:13==; ==2 Corinthians 6:17==).

The name Scripture gives this is repentance. God comes to a person and, in His grace, invites him to think again about the kind of life he is living—“consider your ways” (==Haggai 1:5==). Repentance is a gift that opens the eyes: it shows a person that what he thought about God was wrong, and that the way he thought about life, family, money, heaven and hell, strangers, enemies, and the world was wrong as well. It is God's kindness that leads to it (==Romans 2:4==). He shows us that we are self-reliant and selfish, lets us see where that leads, gives us grace to confess that it is wrong according to His design, forgives the sin—and then, as He did for Abram, gives us a new name and a new family and leads us into a new land.

## Application
Ask what God is calling you to come out of. Most people mark their wedding day or the birth of a child as the most important moment of their lives; in truth, the most important moment is the one when God's disruption invites you to leave the life you have been living without Him, and the most important thing you will ever do is respond. If you have heard that call and are still in your own Ur, respond now. If you responded long ago, ask whether there is a country, a family pattern, or a way of life you were told to leave and have instead kept.
