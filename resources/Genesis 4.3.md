## Commentary
This passage raises an important question: where did Cain and Abel learn to bring offerings to the Lord? The text does not tell us directly, but it is very possible that the roots of sacrifice go back to Genesis 3:21, when the Lord clothed Adam and Eve with garments of skin. If animals were slain to cover their shame, then Adam and Eve may have learned, right there at the edge of Eden, that sin requires covering and that covering comes through the shedding of blood. If God taught them this, it would make sense that they passed that understanding down to their sons.

However Cain and Abel came to understand offerings, one thing is clear: they did not teach themselves how to worship. Fallen humanity does not naturally know what delights the heart of God. True worship must be revealed to us by God.

And this is the pattern throughout Scripture: God is not worshiped according to human preference or creativity. *He* shows us how to come near. *He* reveals what is pleasing to Him. Worship is not us deciding what God should accept. Worship is receiving from God the way He has chosen to be approached, and then offering back to Him what He first revealed.
