## Commentary
“Yet I say to you that in this place there is One greater than the temple.” Later, He announces that One greater than *Jonah* is present (==Matthew 12:41==), and then that One greater than *Solomon* has come (==Matthew 12:42==). These are extraordinary statements because Jesus is identifying Himself as greater than three of the most significant institutions and figures in Israel's history: the temple, the prophet, and the king.

Consider what each represented to the Jewish people.

The temple was the place of God's dwelling among His people, the center of Israel's worship, sacrifice, and priestly ministry. Yet Jesus declares that something greater than the temple is standing among them. It's difficult to overstate the gravity of what Jesus is saying here. The temple was the place where Israel encountered the presence of God, but in Jesus, God Himself had come to dwell among His people (==John 1:14==). He is the fulfillment of everything the temple was intended to reveal. This is why Jesus could later speak of His own body as the temple that would be raised in three days (==John 2:19–21==).

Jonah represented the prophetic ministry, through which God revealed His word and called people to repentance. The people of Nineveh repented when Jonah preached to them, yet Jesus declares that One greater than Jonah has come. Jesus is not just another prophet announcing the word of the Lord. He is the Word made flesh. Jonah's three days in the belly of the fish would become a prophetic sign pointing toward Jesus' own death and resurrection (==Matthew 12:39–40==).

Solomon represented the height of Israel's royal glory and wisdom. Under his reign, Israel experienced extraordinary prosperity, and people traveled from distant nations to hear the wisdom God had given him. Yet Jesus declares Himself greater than Solomon. He is the promised Son of David whose kingdom will have no end (==Luke 1:32–33==), and in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge (==Colossians 2:3==).

What makes these three comparisons so significant is that Jesus is not just claiming to be a better temple, a greater prophet, or a wiser king. He is announcing that the realities to which these institutions and individuals pointed have found their fulfillment in Him. The presence of God, the revelation of God, and the reign of God have all come together in the person of Jesus Christ.

And notice the tragedy of the religious leaders' response. The Ninevites repented at Jonah's preaching, and the queen of Sheba traveled a great distance to hear Solomon's wisdom. Yet the Pharisees, who possessed the Scriptures and understood the significance of the temple, the prophets, and the kingdom of David, were unable to recognize the One standing before them who was greater than them all.

The temple, the prophets, and the kings were never intended to be ends in themselves. They were preparing Israel for the arrival of the One in whom God's presence, His word, and His kingdom would be fully revealed.
