## Commentary
Outside the empty tomb, Mary recognizes her Teacher, and Jesus says something that has puzzled readers for centuries: “Do not cling to Me, for I have not yet ascended to My Father.” Why would the risen Lord refuse the embrace of the first witness of His resurrection?

Think about how a kingdom works. When a ruling king dies, there’s a span of time for the people to mourn before the new king ascends. Authority transferred to the new king the moment the old king died—but the coronation, the celebration of the new kings ascension, is when the *people* come out of mourning and acknowledge his lordship. Until the coronation, there’s a king on the throne with all authority in the land, but a people still living between two reigns.

Mary was caught in that gap—between the death, burial, and resurrection of the Savior and His ascension as King. Jesus was about to ascend to the throne (==Ephesians 1:20==), and it mattered that His disciples witness and celebrate that moment. So He tells Mary, in effect: don’t cling to Me yet—because you must cling to Me where I will reign as King, not just as resurrected Savior. Mary wasn’t wrong to reach for Him. She was just out of season.

“Go to My brethren and say to them, ‘I am ascending to My Father and your Father, and to My God and your God.’” Notice—He doesn’t send her to announce, “Tell them I’m alive.” They would see that for themselves within hours. The first message of the risen Christ is an *ascension* announcement: tell them I am ascending. Jesus wanted His people’s eyes lifted past the empty tomb to the throne. And tucked inside that message is something brand new: “My brethren… My Father and *your* Father.” This is the first time in John’s Gospel that Jesus calls the disciples His brothers. The ascension isn’t a departure that leaves us behind—it’s an enthronement that brings us in. 

The secondary question this verse asks is who are *you* clinging to? I’m not asking who your Savior is—I’m asking who your Lord is. Multitudes genuinely believe in the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus yet still live as if they were king: ruled by their passions, making whatever decisions seem right to them, responding to conflict out of personality and emotion rather than the Spirit who lives in them. Nobody would say it out loud, but plenty of us live seated on the throne or our own heart—and then wonder why there’s such internal chaos and lack of peace. You’re trying to live in a kingdom with two kings, and a house divided against itself cannot stand (==Mark 3:25==). Heaven has received Him as King (==Acts 2:36==). Have you?

I believe this is why so many walks with Jesus are a rollercoaster of high highs and low lows—why believers who are genuinely saved can’t seem to find peace, victory over sin, or authority against the enemy’s attacks. They’re clinging to the resurrected Savior, but they’ve never bowed their knee to the ascended King. 

Kingdom people are people of the ascension. They’ve celebrated the coronation of the new King, and they live surrendered to King Jesus—and no one else. Clinging to the resurrected Savior is a great place to start. Now see Him ascended, and cling to Him where He is: seated on the throne (==Colossians 3:1-2==). And here’s the wonder of clinging to Him *there*: you aren’t reaching up from a distance. God “raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus” (==Ephesians 2:6==). The people of the ascension don’t just acknowledge where the King sits—they live from where they’re seated with Him. That’s why they pray from victory instead of for it, worship before breakthrough instead of after it, and walk through storms like people who know how the story ends.

## Word Study
- *cling* : **haptomai** (Greek) — to fasten oneself to, hold on to, grasp. And the grammar matters: Jesus uses a present imperative with a negative, which doesn’t mean “don’t touch Me” but “*stop* holding on to Me”—stop an action already in progress. Mary had grabbed hold and wasn’t letting go.

How we know touch wasn’t the issue:

- ==Matthew 28:9== — that same morning, the women “held Him by the feet and worshiped Him,” and He didn’t stop them.
- ==John 20:27== — eight days later, Jesus *invites* Thomas to touch Him: “Reach your finger here.”

Summary: Jesus never refused to be touched—He refused to be *kept*. Mary was holding on to the Jesus of the season that was ending. He was loosening her grip so she could take hold of Him in the season that was beginning: not Rabboni back from the dead, but the King ascending to the throne.
