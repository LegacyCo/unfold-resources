## Commentary
Jesus is remarkably clear about what He attributes the woman’s healing to:

“Your faith has made you well.”

The power certainly came from Jesus. Faith was not an independent force operating apart from Him, nor was the woman healed because she possessed some extraordinary spiritual ability. Jesus was the healer.

But Jesus nevertheless draws attention to her faith.

According to Mark, this woman had suffered from a flow of blood for twelve years. She had “suffered many things from many physicians,” spent everything she had, and instead of improving had grown worse (==Mark 5:25–26==). By the time she reaches Jesus, every other source of hope has been exhausted.

Then she hears about Him.

Matthew tells us the woman's reasoning: “If only I may touch His garment, I shall be made well” (==Matthew 9:21==).

That is what Jesus calls faith.

It was not just believing that miracles were possible. It was confidence specifically placed in Jesus that believed there was something present in Him that could do for her what twelve years of effort had been unable to accomplish.

The Greek phrase for "Your faith has made you well" is _hē pistis sou sesōken se_—literally, “your faith has saved you.” The verb _sōzō_ can mean to save, rescue, preserve, or heal. In this context it clearly includes physical healing, but Matthew’s language is so much fuller than simply saying her symptoms disappeared. She has been rescued from the condition that had held her for twelve years.

The same language appears elsewhere when Jesus responds to faith. He tells the blind men, “According to your faith let it be to you” (==Matthew 9:29==). He tells the centurion, “As you have believed, so let it be done for you” (==Matthew 8:13==). Again and again Jesus acknowledges faith as the posture through which people receive what is present in Him.

This is important because Jesus could have said, “My power has healed you.” That would have been completely true. Instead He says, “*Your* faith has made *you* well.”

Jesus places unusual emphasis on the role of the person receiving. Faith does not manufacture the miracle. It does not create the power. Faith reaches toward the One in whom the power already resides. It pulls on the strings of healing that are found in God alone. Faith is the currency of heaven that allows a person to receive for *themself* what Jesus has purchased for all of humanity.

Hundreds of people could have brushed against Jesus that day. Mark describes a crowd pressing against Him from every side (==Mark 5:31==). Yet this woman's touch was different because there was faith inside it.

There is mystery here that Scripture does not attempt to remove. God is sovereign, and faith must never be turned into a formula by which we blame suffering people for not believing hard enough...or blame ourselves when what we are believing for has not come to pass.

But neither should we soften what Jesus says. He honors faith. He responds to faith. And in this moment, He explicitly attributes the woman's reception of healing to her faith.
