## Commentary
When our family served overseas, our kids assumed that “Christian” meant the kind of people we did life with: missionaries who had given their lives for the gospel, pastors and evangelists who embodied the messages they preached, new believers who had counted the cost, fathers in the faith who had stewarded revival and come out the other side as generals. That was normal Christianity to them. When they heard someone was a believer, that’s who they pictured.

Then we moved back to America, and our kids met a different kind of Christian—people who claimed Jesus as Savior while living lives that looked nothing like Him. He was Savior, but He clearly wasn’t Lord. And honestly, the greatest culture shock our kids experienced wasn’t going from life in the Middle East to life in America. It was going from life with kingdom people to life with church people.

That’s the condition Jesus confronts in Laodicea: “because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will vomit you out of My mouth.” Notice what made Laodicea lukewarm—not persecution, not poverty—comfort: “you say, ‘I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing’” (==Revelation 3:17==). Comfortable Christianity always drifts toward a faith that demands nothing—saved people who never become kingdom people. And Jesus loves His church too much to leave it there; the very next words are “as many as I love, I rebuke and chasten” (==Revelation 3:19==). The famous verse about Jesus knocking at the door wasn’t written to unbelievers—it was written to a lukewarm church He wanted back at the table (==Revelation 3:20==).

So here’s the invitation, and it isn’t condemnation—it’s a call higher. Let’s re-write the definition of what it means to be a Christian in our generation. Let’s re-write the script of what church looks like. Let’s re-introduce our communities to the kingdom of God—so that when a child hears the word “Christian,” the picture that comes to mind is a living demonstration of what the King is like.
