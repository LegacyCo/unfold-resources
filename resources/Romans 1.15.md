## Commentary
Consider that Paul is writing this letter to believers. He's not addressing people who have never heard the name of Jesus. He is writing to the saints in Rome, those who are “loved by God and called to be saints” (Romans 1:7). And yet Paul is eager to preach the gospel to them.

That reveals something essential about the apostolic perspective: We never graduate from the gospel.

Paul did not see the gospel as the doorway into Christianity and then something we leave behind once we become more mature. He did not treat the gospel as the beginner’s lesson before moving on to deeper things. For Paul, the gospel was the deep thing. It was the foundation and the continuing supply of the Christian life.

This is why Paul tells the Corinthians, “Now I would remind you, brothers, of the gospel I preached to you” (1 Corinthians 15:1). They had already received it. And still, Paul brings them back to it. They needed to remember again.

Peter writes something similar, “I intend always to remind you of these qualities, though you know them and are established in the truth that you have” (2 Peter 1:12). He says he will make “every effort” so that after his departure they may be able to recall these things (2 Peter 1:15). That is apostolic ministry. Returning the church again and again to the truths that form, anchor, correct, and sustain the people of God.

The gospel is the announcement that Jesus Christ has acted decisively in His death, burial, resurrection, ascension, and reign. It is the declaration that God has done for us in Christ what we could never do for ourselves. And because of that, it remains the answer to every area of life that needs correction and every situation around us that needs hope.

The church does not need less gospel as it matures. It needs the gospel pressed deeper into every corner of life. The gospel is the message we enter through, stand on, return to, and proclaim until Christ is all in all.
