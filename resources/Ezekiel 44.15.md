## Commentary
The first calling of the priest is not to minister to people, but to minister to God. Before the priest speaks, serves, teaches, or leads, he must first come near to the Lord and stand before Him.

As Art Katz said, “There is a requirement for the priest to minister unto God before one ministers to men. If we lack the sense of the sacredness of God, then there is going to be something brittle, something lacking, something plastic in the ministry we bring.”

Ministry becomes hollow when it is disconnected from the presence of God. We may still have skill, preparation, personality, and activity, but something essential will be missing. What we give to people will feel manufactured because it was not first formed in the secret place.

The priest could not treat the presence of God casually. Nearness required reverence. To minister unto the Lord meant recognizing His holiness, waiting before Him, and allowing everything in the priest to come under the weight of who God is.

We cannot give people what we have not received from God. We cannot carry the sacred into the lives of others if we have lost the sense of the sacred ourselves. The strength of public ministry is always determined by the depth of private worship.

Before we stand before people, we must stand before God.
