## Commentary
“Knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.”

Before you were born again, who sat on the throne of your kingdom? You did. And you weren’t alone up there. There were imposter kings ruling your world—maybe fear sat on the throne, and fear made your decisions. Maybe it was lust, or greed, or money, or success, or the endless hunt for the next adventure. Whatever ruled you, ruled you like a king.

Here’s the question: what has to happen for a king to lose his throne? He has to die. “Unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God” (==John 3:3==)—because before anyone enters God’s kingdom, there has to be a death to every other king on every other throne. You say, “I thought Jesus is the One who died.” He did. But look at Paul’s language: our old man was crucified *with Him*. Jesus didn’t die alone. When you were joined to Christ, everything that ruled you was dragged to that cross too—every imposter king, every anti-Christ way of living you inherited from Adam, nailed up with Him.

That’s why Galatians 2:20 can be personalized with surgical precision. “I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me.” It’s no longer *fear* who lives—but Christ who lives in me. It’s no longer *lust* who lives—but Christ who lives in me. It’s no longer bitterness, addiction, unforgiveness—the legs of their thrones have been kicked out from underneath them. And because you were united with Him in death, you were united with Him in resurrection (==Romans 6:5==)—but your old masters weren’t. You came out of that grave. They didn’t. Every king you used to serve is still buried in it.

## Word Study
- *done away with* : **katargeō** (Greek) — to render inoperative, powerless, out of a job; to nullify. Paul does not say the body of sin was merely wounded, and he doesn’t say you’ll spend your life managing it. He says its engine was disconnected.

Where else this word does its work:

- ==Hebrews 2:14== — through death Jesus “destroyed (*katargeō*) him who had the power of death, that is, the devil.” The devil still exists—but his power over you has been rendered inoperative.
- ==2 Timothy 1:10== — Christ “abolished (*katargeō*) death and brought life and immortality to light.”

Summary: sin isn’t a king you fight for your freedom. It’s a dethroned king that lies about still reigning. Katargeō means the throne room changed hands—for good.
