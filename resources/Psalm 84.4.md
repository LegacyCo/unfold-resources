## Commentary
“Blessed are those who dwell in Your house; they will still be praising You.”

The psalmist has just said that his soul longs and even faints for the courts of the Lord. Now he looks at those who actually live and serve there and calls them blessed.

Most likely, he is speaking first of the priests and Levites whose lives were centered around the temple. Unlike the ordinary Israelite who might travel to Jerusalem for appointed feasts and then return home, these men had been given the privilege of remaining near the house of God. Their vocation kept them continually around the place where God had chosen to make His presence known.

That is why the second half of the verse naturally follows: “They will still be praising You.” Nearness to God's presence produces worship. The people who continually dwell in His house continually have reason to praise Him.

There is also something here that reaches beyond the temple itself. The deepest desire of the psalmist is not simply to visit God occasionally, but to _dwell_ with Him. This is the same longing David expresses when he says, “One thing I have desired of the LORD... that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD” (==Psalm 27:4==). David was not asking to move into the temple complex. He was describing a life continually oriented toward the presence of God. A life in which God remains the center around which everything else is finds its orbit. To “dwell” in His house is to live with an ongoing awareness of the reality of God. David wanted his whole life to be lived before the Lord.

And now, in Christ, we have been made the dwelling place of God in the earth. The Spirit lives within us, and together God's people are being built into “a dwelling place of God in the Spirit” (==Ephesians 2:22==).

So “blessed are those who dwell in Your house” describes more than physical proximity to a sacred place. It describes the blessedness of a life that has made the presence of God its home.

## Application
Practicing the presence of God simply means learning to live with an awareness that He is with you.

You do not need a special feeling, a worship service, or a quiet room to do this. Start by acknowledging Him throughout the day. When you wake up, say, “Father, thank You that You are with me today.” Before a meeting, while driving, making dinner, or walking into a difficult conversation, turn your attention toward Him. You might pray something as simple as, “Lord, I know You are here. Help me recognize what You are doing.”

Build small pauses into your day. Turn your attention away from everything demanding it and become aware of God again. You are not trying to make Him come near. In Christ, He already has. You are simply remembering what is true.

Brother Lawrence was a seventeenth-century Carmelite whose conversations and letters were later collected in _The Practice of the Presence of God_. He learned to cultivate an awareness of God while doing ordinary work, even while serving in the monastery kitchen. He wrote, “The time of business does not with me differ from the time of prayer.”

That's the idea. Bring ordinary things into conversation with God. Thank Him when something is beautiful. Ask for wisdom when you are unsure. Tell Him when you are frustrated. Worship Him when something reminds you of His goodness. When you realize you have gone several hours without thinking about Him, do not feel guilty—just return your attention to Him.

Over time, these small moments begin to form a way of life. Prayer becomes less like something you start and stop and more like an ongoing conversation. You begin to live *with* God.
