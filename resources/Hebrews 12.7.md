## Commentary
“If you endure chastening, God deals with you as with sons; for what son is there whom a father does not chasten?”

This verse reframes the hardest seasons of the Christian life as evidence of real relationship. Chastening—the training, discipline, and delay a father imposes—is not proof that God is displeased with you; it is proof that He is parenting you. A father does not train strangers. So when God closes a door and will not open the next one on your schedule, His restraint means you are a son, you are a daughter—He is treating you as one of His own children. The writer presses the point from the other direction: if you were without discipline, “then you are illegitimate and not sons” (==Hebrews 12:8==).

This changes how a believer can respond at every stage of a changing season. When a door closes, the child of God can respond with hope: *my Father is on the move in my life*. When the wait stretches between one door and the next, the child can respond with faith: *my Father is forming endurance in me* (==James 1:3-4==). And when the new door finally opens, the child can walk through with assurance: *He has formed the character in me that can carry what is on the other side*. The discipline is never the destination—“afterward it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it” (==Hebrews 12:11==). This is how Paul could say he had learned contentment in every circumstance: “I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me” (==Philippians 4:11-13==)—including endure with patience the space between two closed doors.

## Word Study
**Chastening** is the Greek *paideia* (παιδεία), from *pais*, “child”—the whole upbringing and training of a child, not merely punishment. Paul uses it for raising children “in the training and admonition of the Lord” (==Ephesians 6:4==) and for Scripture's work of “instruction in righteousness” (==2 Timothy 3:16==). Summary: *paideia* is a father's formation of a child he intends to mature—when God chastens, He is not paying you back; He is bringing you up.
