## Commentary
“You have sown much, and bring in little; you eat, but do not have enough; you drink, but you are not filled with drink; you clothe yourselves, but no one is warm; and he who earns wages, earns wages to put into a bag with holes.”

The people had returned from Babylon to rebuild the house of the LORD—and then stalled. Their explanation sounded reasonable: “The time has not come” (==Haggai 1:2==). But God points out what they *had* found time for: “Is it time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, and this temple to lie in ruins?” (==Haggai 1:4==). Not just houses—*paneled* houses. Finished, upgraded, comfortable. The problem was never capacity. It was priority. Each of them was running to his own house while God’s house sat desolate (==Haggai 1:9==).

And look at the diagnosis God gives for their economy. They worked hard—sowing much, earning wages—and it all kept evaporating. Enough to eat but never satisfied. Clothes but no warmth. Income disappearing like coins through a torn bag. God doesn’t attribute this to bad luck or bad markets: “You looked for much, and behold, it came to little; and when you brought it home, I blew it away. Why? … Because of My house that is in ruins” (==Haggai 1:9==). A self-first economy leaks. When God’s house comes last, there is no bag that can hold what you earn, because the One who sends the dew and the harvest has withheld His blessing from a backwards set of priorities (==Haggai 1:10-11==).

The remedy wasn’t more effort—it was reordered priority: “Go up to the mountains and bring wood and build the temple, that I may take pleasure in it and be glorified” (==Haggai 1:8==). Put His house first, and the leak stops. Jesus preached the same economics on a hillside: “Seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you” (==Matthew 6:33==).
