## Commentary
"Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or else make the tree bad and its fruit bad; for a tree is known by its fruit."

Ask most of us what the fruit of our lives is, and we'll start listing what we *do*—our serving, our giving, our work, our ministry. But watch what Jesus does with this image. He says a tree is known by its fruit, and then He immediately starts talking about *words*: "Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks" (==Matthew 12:34==). In this passage, the fruit Jesus inspects is not what the Pharisees had done. It's what they had just *said*.

What we do matters—Scripture makes that clear (==James 2:17==). But Jesus teaches here that what we say reveals who we are. Our words are the audible expression of the song our hearts are singing. You can discipline your schedule, your habits, and your public behavior, and still be exposed in an unguarded sentence. Fruit doesn't lie about the tree it came from.

Jesus says make the *tree* good, and the fruit follows. If the words coming out of us have been harsh, cynical, faithless, or careless, the solution is not to tighten our lips—it's to bring the tree back to the Gardener and let Him deal with the root (==Luke 6:45==, ==Proverbs 4:23==).
