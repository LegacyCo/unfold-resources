## Commentary
“What then? Israel has not obtained what it seeks; but the elect have obtained it, and the rest were blinded.”

Israel was not necessarily indifferent toward God. Paul had already testified that they possessed a zeal for God (==Romans 10:2==). They were pursuing righteousness, observing the Law, maintaining their religious traditions, and seeking to establish themselves as acceptable before God. But despite all their religious effort, they failed to obtain the very thing they were pursuing.

Why? Paul answers this in ==Romans 9:31–32==. Israel pursued the law of righteousness but did not attain it because they sought it “not by faith, but as it were, by the works of the law.” Their problem was not the absence of devotion, but the foundation upon which that devotion rested.

It is possible to be sincerely devoted to the pursuit of God and yet resist the very means by which God has chosen to reveal Himself. Israel's confidence in its religious identity and obedience became an obstacle to receiving the righteousness that could only come through faith in Jesus Christ.

Paul then makes an even more difficult statement: “The rest were blinded.”

The Greek word translated “blinded” is *pōroō*, which means to harden or render insensitive. It was used to describe something becoming calloused. In this context, Paul is describing a spiritual condition in which people become increasingly unresponsive to the revelation of God.

Paul is not describing intellectual confusion or a lack of information. Israel had been given extraordinary revelation. The problem was that their hearts had become resistant to the very truth they claimed to be seeking.

In the following verses, Paul draws upon ==Deuteronomy 29:4==, ==Isaiah 29:10==, and ==Psalm 69:22–23== to explain this hardening. He understands it as a judgment of God, not just the natural consequence of religious stubbornness.

It's easy to assume that greater exposure to spiritual truth, revelation, encounters with God, etc. will produce greater spiritual understanding. But the story of Israel shows us that revelation is not guarantee. It can either be received with faith or resisted through unbelief. The same Scriptures that should have prepared them to recognize Jesus became, through their misunderstanding of those Scriptures, part of the framework by which they rejected Him.

Jesus confronted this very problem when He told the religious leaders, “You search the Scriptures, for in them you think you have eternal life; and these are they which testify of Me. But you are not willing to come to Me that you may have life” (==John 5:39–40==).

They knew the Scriptures, but they resisted the One to whom the Scriptures pointed.

We, too, can become so committed to our theological systems, religious traditions, and established ways of understanding God that we lose the humility necessary to receive correction from His Word. We can mistake familiarity with spiritual things for genuine knowledge of God.

Of course, this doesn't mean we should abandon sound doctrine or treat every new spiritual idea as a revelation from God. Rather, it means recognizing that our understanding must always remain submitted to the Eternal God who has revealed Himself in Jesus Christ.

## Application
We must never allow our religious accomplishments, theological knowledge, denominational history, or our own history with God to become a substitute for present dependence upon Him. The moment we begin trusting in what we know, what we have done, or what we have experienced rather than in Christ Himself, we are moving toward the very condition Paul is describing.

The answer to spiritual hardness is humility before God, repentance, and a renewed willingness to receive what He has provided in His Son.

The elect obtained what Israel was seeking, not because they were more deserving, but because they received by grace what others were attempting to establish through their own righteousness. And this is the great dividing line between man-made religion and the true gospel of Jesus Christ: whether righteousness is something we seek to establish for ourselves or something we receive through faith in Jesus.
