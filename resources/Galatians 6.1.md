## Commentary
“Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted.”

Notice what Paul does *not* tell the church to do with a brother overtaken in sin. Not expose him in shame. Not distance himself from him. Not turn his failure into a cautionary tale. One word: *restore*. When a believer acts contrary to their nature and falls, the family’s assignment is to do everything it can to bring that brother or sister back to life—the way a community rallies around a beached whale, nursing it, protecting it, working together to get it back to the water where it belongs. Conviction from the Holy Spirit, yes. Correction, when needed, yes. But never condemnation—“there is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus” (==Romans 8:1==). Condemnation always has its origin in the accuser of the brethren (==Revelation 12:10==), and the church must never do the accuser’s work for him.

And Paul attaches a posture to the assignment: “in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted.” Restorers come low. The moment I forget that I’m made of the same material as the one who fell, I’ve stopped restoring and started judging.

## Word Study
- *restore* : **katartizō** — to mend, repair, put back into proper working condition; to fit something back together. It’s the word used for the fishermen “*mending* their nets” (==Matthew 4:21==)—patiently repairing torn cords so the net can do again what it was made to do.

The word’s other work in the New Testament:

- ==1 Corinthians 1:10== — Paul urges the divided Corinthians to be “perfectly *joined together* (katartizō)”—a torn church mended into one.
- ==Hebrews 13:21== — the God of peace “make you *complete* (katartizō) in every good work.” Restoration is ultimately His craft; we just get to assist.

Summary: a fisherman doesn’t scold a torn net, and he doesn’t throw it away—he sits down with it and mends it, because he knows what it’s for. That’s the church’s posture toward a fallen believer: this is not garbage; this is a net made for catching, a life made for righteousness, temporarily torn. Handle it gently, mend it, and get it back in the water.
