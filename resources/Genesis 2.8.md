## Commentary
There is, of course, speculation on the location of the Garden of Eden. We don't know where the garden was for sure, but scholars typically zero in on two locations; a northern location putting the garden in modern-day Armenia, and a southern location putting the garden in modern-day Iraq, where the Euphrates and Tigris Rivers flow into the Persian Gulf. 

My opinion is the southern location, putting the garden in Iraq. Here are three reasons for this opinion: 

-the location of the Euphrates and the Tigris Rivers, 
-where Abraham lived when God called him, and 
-the location of the Promised Land, which is where God brought Abraham.

If you look at a map of southern Iraq, you will see the Euphrates and the Tigris come together in the very southern tip of the country just before they feed into the Persian Gulf. The area between these two rivers was known as the fertile crescent in the ancient world. 

One of the cities that developed in this area by the rivers was Ur of the Chaldees. Abraham was from the city of Ur…the area where most scholars believe the garden of eden to have been.

This is what I find interesting; if you look at a map of the Middle East and move from southern Iraq, literally, directly west you end up in Jerusalem. In fact, Jerusalem and the land where we believe the Garden of Eden and Abraham were from are both on the same latitude line (31st parallel). 

This may be interesting, but what makes this significant? Genesis 2:7 says that God formed man from the dust of the earth and then God places man in the garden "toward the east." Translation: Adam wasn't created in Eden, he was created from the ground *west* of Eden.

As we move through God’s story in Scripture, you will notice multiple instances of God’s people moving west. For example; 
	-Abraham came from the east and moved west into the land of Canaan. 
	-When God brought Israel into the Promised Land they didn't come from the south, which would’ve made sense practically. They came from the east moving west across the Jordan. 
	-When Jesus came riding into Jerusalem on a donkey He came from the Mount of Olives...which is east of the city. The triumphal entry of the Messiah into Jerusalem was a move westward. 
	-After His resurrection when Jesus ascended, He was standing on the Mount of Olives. Angels appear to the disciples and tell them that He will come back the same way He left…by way of the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem…once again making a triumphal entry into the city from the east. Zechariah actually prophecies this in Zechariah 14:4,
		"And in that day His feet will stand on the Mount of Olives, which faces Jerusalem on the east. And the Mount of Olives shall be split in two, from east to west, making a very large valley; half of the mountain shall move toward the north and half of it toward the south." 

Follow my train of thought here; man was not formed in the garden, he was formed west of the garden, and then brought eastward into the garden. Could it be that man was formed from the dust of the Promised Land and brought eastward to a garden where he would learn what it meant to bear God's image…so that he could return west to the land he was formed from and fill that land with God's image? 

That sure sounds like the storyline of Israel, beginning with Abraham and culminating with Jesus. Could it be that the garden was never intended to be Adam’s final destination, or ours, but simply the training ground for the main assignment, which was a city called Jerusalem? In fact, the end of the story is not man in a *garden*…it’s man in a *city*…a city called Holy Jerusalem. Listen to Revelation 21:9-11,
	 “Then one of the seven angels…came to me and said, ‘Come, I will show you the bride, the Lamb’s wife.’ And he carried me away in the Spirit to a great and high mountain, and showed me the great city, the holy Jerusalem, descending out of heaven from God, having the glory of God. Her light was like a most precious stone, like a jasper stone, clear as crystal.”

Then, for the next 21 verses John describes in detail what this end-times city is like…the place where God Himself dwells with man on earth.

In the parable of the talents (Luke 19), the reward that the master gave the faithful stewards was not more money…he gave them cities. 
	“You are the light of the world,” Jesus says in Matthew 5:14, “and a CITY that is set on a hill cannot be hidden.”

## Application
I believe the garden was intended to be the place where Adam matured and learned God’s ways SO THAT he could return to the land where he was formed and build a heavenly city…the place where the throne of God would rest among man. Adam may have failed, but I believe God is still writing that story…and the original assignment of Adam has been given to the church.

Our heavenly destination is not a paradise where we do nothing, it’s actually a city where we rule and reign as kings. This is a massive paradigm shift for most believers. Salvation doesn’t stamp our heavenly passport while we sit back waiting to go to heaven some day, doing our best to make it through life. Salvation restores us immediately, in this life, back to the garden, the source of life, so that we can mature and learn God's ways. And as we learn what it means to bear God’s image in the earth, we are equipped to go and transform the desolate places into vibrant cities for the King.

The church has been tasked with expanding the boundary of God’s authority in the earth until the knowledge of the glory of God covers the earth like the waters cover the sea.
