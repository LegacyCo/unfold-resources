## Commentary
John names three currents that run through the fallen world: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life—“not of the Father but of the world.” The lust of the eyes is the impulse to consume with sight what God has not given.

Notice how temptation presented itself the very first time. The tree of knowledge was not covered in warnings. “The woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree desirable to make one wise” (==Genesis 3:6==). Sin presented itself as something beautiful. The Greeks, who knew they could not take Troy by storming its gates, gave the city a gift that appealed to its senses—and the enemy was carried inside willingly. That is how sin usually enters: not as an obvious invader, but as something desirable, seemingly harmless, apparently manageable.

There is nothing wrong with beauty. God created it. But there are beautiful things created by God that do not belong to you. When we consume something with our eyes that God has not given us, we take ownership of what belongs to someone else. David saw before he inquired, and inquired before he took (==2 Samuel 11:2==)—the eyes signed the contract before the hands did.

The remedy is not to un-see beauty but to have our vision healed: to become people who can acknowledge beauty and honor it without possessing it. What is of the Father teaches the eyes reverence; what is of the world teaches them appetite.

## Application
Audit what your eyes consume. Scroll through your feeds, your follows, your watch history, and ask of each thing: is this teaching my eyes reverence or appetite? Trojan horses never look like enemies—they look desirable, harmless, manageable. If you find yourself insisting something is “not that big of a deal,” that insistence itself may be the warning.

Then practice a new reflex with beauty. When you see something beautiful that does not belong to you, let the admiration travel upward instead of inward: thank the God who made it, pray for the person instead of consuming them, and move on without taking ownership. Beauty acknowledged becomes worship. Beauty consumed becomes theft. The difference is not in what you see—it is in what your heart does next.
