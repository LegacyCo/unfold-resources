## Commentary
In the preceding verses, Jesus warns His disciples that following Him will eventually bring them into conflict with religious and political authorities. They will be delivered to councils, beaten in synagogues, and brought before governors and kings because of their association with Him. But rather than instructing them to prepare an elaborate defense, Jesus tells them not to worry about what they will say when that moment comes. He assures them that the words they need will be given to them.

Then He explains why: "For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you."

This is a remarkable promise. Jesus is telling His disciples that when they find themselves standing before those who oppose them, they will not be standing there alone. The Holy Spirit will be present with them, supplying the wisdom, courage, and words necessary to bear witness to Christ.

Notice that Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as "the Spirit of your Father." This language is significant because it emphasizes the relationship between God and those who belong to Him. The disciples are children of a Father who will be actively involved in what He has called them to do. The One who sends them is also the One who will sustain them.

Jesus says that the Spirit Himself will speak _in them_. God intends to accomplish His purposes through ordinary people who have yielded themselves to Him. Their weakness, lack of education, and inability to anticipate every situation will not prevent Him from speaking through them.

We see this promise fulfilled in ==Acts 4:8–13==, when Peter and John are brought before the Jewish religious authorities. Peter, who had previously denied Jesus when questioned about his association with Him, now stands before the most powerful religious leaders in Jerusalem and boldly proclaims the resurrection of Christ. Luke specifically tells us that Peter was "filled with the Holy Spirit" as he began to speak. The authorities were astonished by the boldness of these men, especially because they were uneducated and untrained. What they recognized, however, was that these men had been with Jesus.

It is important to understand that Jesus is not discouraging thoughtful preparation, careful study of Scripture, or the development of our ability to communicate the gospel. The immediate context concerns persecution and the unexpected moments when His followers are called upon to defend their faith. His instruction is not an excuse for neglecting preparation, but an invitation to trust the Spirit rather than being consumed by anxiety over our own inadequacy.

There is a broader principle here that extends beyond persecution. God has never intended for us to accomplish His purposes through human ability alone. The Christian life is not simply a matter of receiving instructions from Jesus and then attempting to carry them out through our own strength. We have been given the Holy Spirit, who dwells within us and enables us to participate in what God is doing.

This should give us tremendous confidence as we follow Jesus into situations where we feel inadequate or unprepared. 

Jesus does not promise His disciples that they will avoid difficult circumstances. He promises that when those circumstances come, the Spirit of their Father will be present and active within them. Their confidence is not to be found in their ability to speak for God, but in God's willingness to speak through them.
