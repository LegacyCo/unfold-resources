## Commentary
“How is it then, brethren? Whenever you come together, *each of you* has a psalm, has a teaching, has a tongue, has a revelation, has an interpretation. Let all things be done for edification.”

Paul’s assumption is that "each of you" *has* something. Not “the professionals up front have something and the rest of you have seats.” In Paul’s picture of the gathered church, every believer arrives carrying something from the Spirit for the rest of the family—and the gathering is designed so it can be given: “you can all prophesy one by one, that all may learn and all may be encouraged” (==1 Corinthians 14:31==). Peter says the same thing: “As each one has received a gift, minister it to one another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God” (==1 Peter 4:10==). Each one has received. Each one ministers.

Be honest: that’s nearly impossible in a large room on a Sunday morning—and that’s okay, because that’s not the setting Paul is describing. These letters were written to churches that met in living rooms and around tables, house to house (==Acts 2:46==). The smaller setting is where the gifts of the Spirit get shared with one another, activated in one another, and practiced in safety. Nobody learns to prophesy in front of five hundred people. They learn in a room of twelve who love them—where a wrong word can be weighed graciously (==1 Corinthians 14:29==), where the shy get drawn out, and where the family celebrates a first step of faith like a baby’s first step.

A church that only ever gathers in rows will produce believers who only ever receive. But the manifold grace of God is distributed across the whole Body—which means there are psalms, teachings, revelations, and encouragements sitting dormant in the people around you, waiting for a room small enough to give them in.

## Application
Get yourself into a room small enough for your gift to come out of you. If your church family gathers house to house, be there this week—and come *expecting to carry something in*: a scripture that’s been alive to you, a song, an encouragement, a prayer for someone specific. Before you walk in, ask the Holy Spirit, “What do You want to give this family through me tonight?” Then take the risk and give it. And if you host: ask the quiet ones what God’s been showing them. You may be the safe room where someone’s gift gets activated for the first time.
