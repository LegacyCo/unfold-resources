## Commentary
“For how then will it be known that Your people and I have found grace in Your sight, except You go with us? So we shall be separate, Your people and I, from all the people who are upon the face of the earth.”

After the golden calf, God offered Israel the land with an angel to lead them—the promise fulfilled, but without His own presence in their midst (==Exodus 33:2-3==). Moses refused the arrangement: “If Your Presence does not go with us, do not bring us up from here” (==Exodus 33:15==). And this verse gives his reason. Without the presence, there is nothing that distinguishes God's people from every other people on the face of the earth. Not their law, not their history, not their destination—the presence is the distinguishing mark. Moses understood this under an inferior covenant, sight unseen of what Pentecost would make available.

The same is true of the church. It is His presence that defines the church—not a building, not a charismatic leader, not great music, not denominational heritage. All of those can exist in a congregation the presence has left. When those inferior things become ultimate, God does what He has always done when His people choose the inferior way: He gives them over to the idols of their hearts (==Psalm 81:11-12==)—and a community is left with a religious institution rather than a lampstand burning in its midst (==Revelation 2:5==). Moses' question is the church's question in every generation: if Your presence does not go with us, what will separate us from the world? A church that cannot answer it with His presence cannot answer it at all.
