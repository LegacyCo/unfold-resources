## Commentary
“I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore. Amen. And I have the keys of Hades and of Death.”

Keys mean ownership. Whoever holds the keys to a house decides who’s locked in and who goes free. For every generation since Adam, death held its keys with an unbroken grip—every son and daughter of Adam eventually walked through its door, and no one walked back out (==Hebrews 9:27==).

Then the Man from Galilee died with “Father, into Your hands I commit My spirit” on His lips (==Luke 23:46==)—entered death’s house as its apparent victim—and walked out three days later as its new owner. I love to imagine the moment behind this verse: the risen Son standing before His Father—*Abba, we did it. Now give Me the keys, so I can set the captives free.* Because that’s exactly what He does with them. He didn’t take the keys of Hades and Death as trophies for a shelf. Keys exist to open doors. Through death He destroyed him who had the power of death, and released those who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage (==Hebrews 2:14-15==).

So notice who’s speaking in this verse: not a theology, but the living Jesus, introducing Himself to a terrified John with the credentials that end all fear—“Do not be afraid… I was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore.” The doors that used to hold humanity captive now answer to nail-scarred hands. Death still exists, but it’s been reduced to a doorway whose lock was changed—and the One who holds the key is the One who loves you.

## Word Study
- *keys* : **kleis** (Greek) — the key as a symbol of authority to open and shut, admit and release. Watch what Jesus does with this word across the New Testament:

- ==Matthew 16:19== — “I will give *you* the keys of the kingdom of heaven.” He takes death’s keys and hands kingdom keys to His church.
- ==Revelation 3:7== — He holds “the key of David”: “He who opens and no one shuts, and shuts and no one opens.”

Summary: every key in Scripture ends up on Jesus’ keyring—and the astonishing part is how freely He shares them. He kept the keys of Death and Hades for Himself, so you’d never have to fear that door; He gave the keys of the kingdom to His people, so we could go unlock everything else.
