## Commentary
Doors protect. Doors separate. But there’s a third thing doors do that completes the picture: doors *connect*. A door is the one place in a wall where two worlds meet.

Jesus says, “I am the door. If anyone enters by Me, he will be saved, and will go in and out and find pasture.” He is the door that connects His world with ours—the meeting point between heaven and earth, the one opening in the wall that sin built (==John 1:51==). And unlike the ark’s door, this door stands wide open, inviting everyone to come and find their place in Him. The thief comes to steal, kill, and destroy; the Door came that you may have life, and have it more abundantly (==John 10:10==).

And here’s the security of entering by Him: the same Jesus who is the door is also the One who seals you inside Himself. He’s the author *and* the finisher of your faith (==Hebrews 12:2==)—He doesn’t start what He won’t complete, and nothing that happens outside of Him can steal you out of Him: “neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand” (==John 10:28==). So remain in Him. Go deeper into Him. And watch as He leads you through open doors, closes doors behind you, and gives you grace to rest and wait in the hallways—so that endurance and faith and character can be formed in you.

## Word Study
- *go in and out* : a Hebrew covenant idiom for living in complete security and freedom. In a world of walled cities and locked gates, only the safest people could “go in and out” at will. Scripture uses the phrase everywhere:

- ==Numbers 27:17== — Israel needed a shepherd “who may go out before them and go in before them… that the congregation of the LORD may not be like sheep which have no shepherd.”
- ==Deuteronomy 28:6== — the covenant blessing: “Blessed shall you be when you come in, and blessed shall you be when you go out.”
- ==Psalm 121:8== — “The LORD shall preserve your going out and your coming in from this time forth, and even forevermore.”

Summary: when Jesus promises that those who enter by Him “will go in and out and find pasture,” He’s not describing a door that locks behind you like a cell. He’s describing the freest life possible—secured people moving in covenant safety, at home in the sheepfold and unafraid in the field, because the Door Himself watches every crossing.
