## Commentary
“…according to my earnest expectation and hope that in nothing I shall be ashamed, but with all boldness, as always, so now also Christ will be magnified in my body, whether by life or by death.”

Paul writes this from prison, genuinely uncertain whether he will be released or executed—and his hope is untouched by the uncertainty, because both outcomes serve the same end. Christ will be magnified in his body either way. This is the gospel: come out of your old life and follow Jesus wherever He wants to go, and the journey will be one of both life and death. Life to the dead things He touches along the way, and death to whatever He says cannot come on the journey. The believer does not get to choose which of the two any given season will hold; the believer only settles the question the seasons will test—that Jesus is magnified in this body, whichever comes.

The next verse tells us what makes such a hope possible: “For to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain” (==Philippians 1:21==). A person whose life *is* Christ cannot lose—living means more of Him here, and dying means all of Him there. And Paul is not speaking only in figures. Following Jesus can mean literal life and literal death for the glory of His name; the faithful “did not love their lives to the death” (==Revelation 12:11==). Jesus is magnified whenever someone hears the call, comes out of their life, and follows Him on those terms—terms with no exit clause, no reserved outcomes, and no condition under which He is not glorified (==Romans 14:8==).
