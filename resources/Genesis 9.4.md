## Commentary
“But you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood.”

The verse before this one is all freedom: “Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you. I have given you all things” (==Genesis 9:3==). This verse is the boundary. It is the same pattern God set with Adam—eat freely of every tree, except one (==Genesis 2:16-17==). Covenant brings freedom, but true freedom includes restriction. The covenant family of God understands the tension between the two and does not resent it.

Restriction is not a cage; it is structure that produces flow. A garden thirty feet from the house cannot be watered by turning on the spigot—the water spills everywhere and the plants stay dry. What gets the water to the garden is a hose: a structure that restricts the flow so the water can go freely where it is needed. Without the restriction the water is chaos, unproductive. The most beautiful things ever made were made inside this tension. Music is composed within the boundaries of key and time. Sculpture is carved within the boundaries of the block. A child given paint and the freedom to make anything is also given a piece of paper. When God restricts, He is increasing the pressure that hems a life in safely and produces more flow through it. What God designed you for is too big for you; the restrictions are how He gets you somewhere you could not go on your own. Israel learned this at the level of daily life: “all of God's paths drip with abundance” (==Psalm 65:11==)—the freedom is to walk wherever His path leads; the restriction is to stay on it.

## Word Study
**Life** here is the Hebrew *nephesh* (נֶפֶשׁ)—soul, life, the living being itself. The reason for the boundary is stated later in the law: “the life (*nephesh*) of the flesh is in the blood… it is the blood that makes atonement for the soul” (==Leviticus 17:11==). Blood was withheld from man because life belongs to God, and blood was reserved for the altar—which is why the blood of Jesus, “the blood of the new covenant” (==Matthew 26:28==), carries the weight it does. Summary: the first restriction of the Noahic covenant protected the one thing that would one day be poured out for atonement.

## Application
When you begin to feel restricted by God—a boundary in your finances, your relationships, your ambitions, your schedule—consider that He may be building the structure required to carry you somewhere you do not yet know how to reach. Ask what the restriction is producing rather than what it is withholding. A boundary that comes from God will always serve to bless you and mature you.
