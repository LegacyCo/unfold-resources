## Commentary
There is an interesting backstory to the mighty men of David. When David was fleeing from Saul and hiding in the cave of Adullam, Scripture tells us that “everyone who was in distress, everyone who was in debt, and everyone who was discontented gathered to him. So he became captain over them” (==1 Samuel 22:1–2==). About four hundred men initially joined him. These were not the celebrated warriors of Israel. They were troubled, discontented, and marginalized men who were looking for a different future.

David had already established a reputation as a courageous warrior and successful leader (==1 Samuel 18:5–16==). Yet he was also an outsider, rejected and pursued by Saul. Those who felt marginalized or mistreated under Saul's reign may have recognized something of their own situation in David. He offered them something they were not finding under Saul: a leader worth following and the possibility of a better future.

There was also a spiritual dimension. Some recognized that God's favor was upon David and that he was destined to become king. When another group of warriors joined him, Amasai declared, “We are yours, O David; we are on your side, O son of Jesse! Peace, peace to you, and peace to your helpers! For your God helps you” (==1 Chronicles 12:18==).

It is important to recognize that these four hundred men were not necessarily wicked or morally questionable. They were distressed and dissatisfied, and Scripture does not tell us that every one of them became one of David's mighty men. Nevertheless, some of the men who gathered around David would become courageous, loyal warriors whose extraordinary exploits were eventually recorded in Scripture (==2 Samuel 23:8–39==).

There is a beautiful picture here of what happens when struggling people come under the influence of godly leadership. These men initially came to David because they needed something, but many would eventually become men willing to give everything for him. They came seeking refuge and became participants in establishing his kingdom.

In a similar way, Jesus did not surround Himself with the most accomplished or respected men of His generation. He called fishermen, a tax collector, and other ordinary men who, through their relationship with Him, would become foundational leaders in His kingdom. Many of us first come to Christ because we are desperate for help, but as we walk with Him, He transforms us into people who are willing to give our lives for His purposes.

## Application
Who you are when you come to Christ does not determine who you can become through walking with Him. God delights in taking those the world overlooks and transforming them into people through whom He accomplishes extraordinary things (==1 Corinthians 1:26–29==).
