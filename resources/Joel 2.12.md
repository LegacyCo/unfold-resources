## Commentary
“‘Now, therefore,’ says the LORD, ‘turn to Me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning.’”

When God calls His people back to Himself, fasting is part of the summons: “rend your heart, and not your garments” (==Joel 2:13==). The outward act is only worth what the heart is doing inside it. But notice that God does call for the outward act. Wholehearted return involves the body: fasting, weeping, mourning. When Nineveh believed God's warning through Jonah, they proclaimed a fast from the greatest to the least of them—and God relented (==Jonah 3:5-10==).

Fasting has always been connected to repentance. And this is fitting, because repentance is the moment you stop feeding yourself on what is killing you. The fast enacts that turning: the mouth that stops taking in bread is a picture of the soul that stops taking in what it has been living on apart from God. Sin always involves appetite—something consumed, indulged, reached for in place of Him—so the return to God fittingly involves appetite too, denied and redirected. And the ground for such a return is God's own character, which Joel gives in the same breath: “He is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness” (==Joel 2:13==). The fast of repentance is not an attempt to earn His welcome. It is the whole self—heart and body together—turning around to receive it.
