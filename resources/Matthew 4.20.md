## Commentary
“They immediately left their nets and followed Him.” Peter and Andrew’s leaving was not a career change. It was a full surrender of the old life brought under the authority of the King. 

A culture where following Jesus costs little can deceive us into thinking the call is supplemental: a few adjustments, church attendance, cleaner language. But believers in much of the world know what the West forgets—that following Jesus means a complete end to the life you have been living. Before the gospel says “come and live,” it first says “come and die”: deny yourself and take up your cross daily (==Luke 9:23==); crucified with Christ, so that it is no longer I who live (==Galatians 2:20==); no longer living for ourselves (==2 Corinthians 5:15==); not our own, bought at a price (==1 Corinthians 6:19==).

The kingdom demands everything—not because God is greedy, but because He knows how He designed us. Being born again implies a death to everything we brought with us, and the King determines what walks out of the tomb carrying resurrection life and what stays buried. Because He is wonderful, He is exactly the One you want making that determination.

## Application
Identify your nets—the thing your hands reflexively return to, the identity that has fed you, the security you would grieve to leave on the shore. The nets weren’t evil; they were simply the old life. The question is whether they now come under the King’s authority or compete with it.
