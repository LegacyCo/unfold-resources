## Commentary
Jesus quoted this verse in Matthew 19 when He was questioned by the Pharisees about marriage. They tried to test Him by asking if it was lawful for a man to divorce his wife for any reason. Jesus responds, 

“Have you not read that He who made them at the beginning made them male and female, and said ‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh’? So then, they are no longer two but one flesh. Therefore, what God has joined together, let no man separate” (Matthew 19:4-6).

The Pharisees then try to trap Jesus and say, 

“Why then did Moses allow for a certificate of divorce?” (Matthew 19:7)

Jesus responds,

“Because of the hardness of your hearts, Moses permitted you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so” (Matthew 19:8).

My point is not to comment on divorce…there is room at the cross for every situation that we could bring to it. We don’t pick and choose what God extends grace to. My point is to show that Jesus looks to the Garden as the source of God’s design for marriage. Jesus doesn’t point to the law to defend His position on marriage, He goes back to the very beginning.

And the Genesis story shows us that God intends marriage to be between one man and one woman who leave the families they were born into so that they can live in a monogamous covenant with one another. Monogamous, heterosexual marriage is God's bedrock cornerstone for the family which He set in place in the very beginning of His story. So when you hear culture looking for alternative definitions and paradigms for family, know that “from the beginning it was not so.”

God defines family, and the Garden shows us what His definition of family is.

From the beginning God intended that a man and a woman would become one flesh by way of their covenant marriage. And that union of two becoming one, which would be the most recognizable relationship in any culture of the world at any time in history, was going to serve as a metaphor of the relationship that the Messiah would one day have with His bride, the church.

As we’ve seen, God is intentional in everything that He does. There is a reason the sun is the sun, the moon is the moon, the ocean is the ocean, the land is the land…and there is a reason why He made man to be man and woman to be woman. Blending identities and erasing boundary lines is not God, it’s anti-God. It’s the boundary lines that define the beauty of His creation. Separation gives definition. 

When I go to the beach I’m very thankful that He has defined what is ocean and what is land. 

When I breathe I’m very thankful that He has defined what is air and what is not air. 

And when I look at my parents I’m very thankful that He said one of you is man and one of you is woman…or else I wouldn’t be here.
