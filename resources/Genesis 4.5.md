## Commentary
To say that God accepted Abel’s offering because Abel brought a firstborn animal and Cain did not is too simplistic. It’s not completely wrong, but it’s doesn’t take the time to examine a consistent theme that Scripture wants to teach us.

An offering of grain is neither superior nor inferior to an animal offering. For example; when the Lord gives Israel the law and the sacrificial system in Leviticus, He includes both the offering of animals and the offering of grain as acceptable (see ==Leviticus 2:9==).

The text does not give us a detailed explanation of what was visibly wrong with Cain’s offering. And perhaps that's the point. From the outside, the two brothers both appear to be worshiping. Both bring an offering. Both approach God. Both participate in the same act of devotion. But God sees what we cannot see.

The difference in their worship was internal.

The consistent witness of Scripture is that God does not examine the gift apart from the giver. Worship is never separated from the worshipper. The offering may be extravagant or simple. It may be a lamb, a bull, a goat, grain from the field, a large sum of money, or a widow’s mite. But the true weight of the worship is not measured by the amount, the sound, the species, or the outward form. It is measured by the heart from which it comes.

Cain was not rejected because of his offering. His offering was rejected because of him.

His heart was not right before God, and what happens next proves it. When God confronts him, Cain does not repent. He becomes angry. His countenance falls. Sin is already crouching at the door. The rejected offering exposed what was already alive in him.

Genesis 4:5 reminds us that God is not impressed by religious activity when the heart is resistant to Him. He receives worship that comes from faith, humility, and surrender. He rejects worship that is outwardly correct but inwardly corrupt. The question is never only, “What did I bring?” The deeper question is, “What kind of heart did it come from?”

## Application
Cain’s offering to the Lord was the fruit of his own sweat and toil. He brought to God what he had worked to produce from the ground. Cain thought his labor could make up for the absence of heart devotion, and all it did was expose his lack of faith.

Most people read the story of Cain as a story about murder. And because they have never taken a life, they think the lesson of Genesis 4 is not for them. But the murder was not the beginning of Cain’s problem. It was the fruit of something already wrong beneath the surface.

Before Cain killed his brother, he tried to approach God on his own terms. And untold multitudes are still doing the same thing. They bring God their effort, their discipline, their giving, their service, their religious activity, their moral improvement, and they hope all of it will make them righteous and acceptable before Him. But no amount of sweat can cleanse the human heart. No offering produced by self-effort can repair what sin has broken.

True righteousness can never come from you. It can only come to you.

And it comes through faith in the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

This is the great difference between religion and the gospel. Religion brings God the fruit of human effort and asks Him to accept us because of what we have done. The gospel brings us to God empty-handed, trusting that we are accepted because of what Christ has done.
