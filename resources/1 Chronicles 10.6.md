## Commentary
There is a tragic irony in how Saul's life comes to an end. Before Saul was ever anointed king, God spoke to Samuel concerning him, saying, “You shall anoint him commander over My people Israel, that he may save My people from the hand of the Philistines” (==1 Samuel 9:16==).

One of the clearest purposes for Saul's anointing was that he would deliver Israel from the Philistines. Yet here, at the end of his life, Saul and three of his sons are killed in battle against the very enemy he was appointed to defeat.

The man who was anointed to deliver Israel from the Philistines was ultimately defeated by them.

This doesn't mean Saul never experienced victory over the Philistines. He certainly did (==1 Samuel 14:47, 52==). But there is something deeply sobering about the contrast between the calling Saul received at the beginning of his life and the circumstances surrounding his death.

What makes this even more significant is that the Chronicler tells us exactly why Saul's life ended this way. In ==1 Chronicles 10:13–14==, we read that Saul died because of his unfaithfulness to the Lord. He did not keep the word of the Lord, sought guidance from a medium, and failed to inquire of God.

Saul's downfall was not ultimately because the Philistines were too powerful. It was because he had become increasingly unwilling to submit himself to the God who had called and anointed him. As a result, the Lord withdrew His Spirit from Saul. We read in ==1 Samuel 16:14==, “But the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and a distressing spirit from the LORD troubled him.” The same Spirit who had come upon Saul and empowered him for his calling had now departed from him. Saul continued to occupy the throne, but he no longer enjoyed the divine empowerment that had accompanied his anointing.

Consider how his story began. Saul was chosen by God, anointed by Samuel, and empowered by the Spirit of God (==1 Samuel 10:6–10==). He had everything he needed to fulfill the assignment God had given him. Yet throughout his reign, we repeatedly see Saul allowing fear, insecurity, pride, and the desire to preserve his position to influence his decisions. Rather than trusting God and obeying His word, Saul increasingly relied upon his own understanding.

The very calling Saul was entrusted with would eventually be fulfilled through someone else. David would rise to the throne and accomplish significant victories over the Philistines (==2 Samuel 5:17–25==).

It is possible to receive a genuine calling from God, experience His power, and even see significant victories along the way, while gradually drifting away from the obedience and dependence upon Him that the calling requires.

We should never mistake having been called by God for continuing to walk faithfully with Him. Our past encounters, victories, and experiences of His power don't remove our present responsibility to listen to His voice and obey His word.
