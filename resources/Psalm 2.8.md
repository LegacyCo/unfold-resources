## Commentary
The Scriptural promise is not that God's people will one day "go to heaven" as disembodied souls, but that God's new creation will define _this_ creation. The expansion of the inheritance from promised land to "the ends of the earth" is a sign and a symbol of a kingdom that spreads and fills like leaven (see ==Matthew 13:33==). This theme is repeated at length in passages like ==Psalm 72:1-20== and ==Isaiah 11:1-16==. These passages paint a picture of a renewed creation. Scripture insists that God's purpose is to eventually bring the whole world under the redemptive power of the cross.

## Application
Have you ever experienced a miracle? What seemed like a breaking in of the supernatural world, the "crossing over" of God's heaven into your moment, is about more than your healing or your rescue. God intends for those miracles to serve as signposts that the ends of the earth are now the possession of His Son, that the kingdom of God has come and the King is ruling and reigning. We often think of miracles as an invasion by some external, heavenly force. But that wasn't how the early church saw it. As N.T. Wright points out in his book *Paul*, "For them, dramatic and otherwise inexplicable healings were seen as evidence of new creation, of the Creator Himself at work in a fresh way."
