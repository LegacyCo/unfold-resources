## Commentary
This is an enormous statement for Jesus to make. He identifies John the Baptist as the messenger promised in ==Malachi 3:1==: “Behold, I send My messenger, and he will prepare the way before Me.”

What makes this so significant is the identity of the One speaking in Malachi. It is Yahweh Himself. The verse concludes, “says the LORD of hosts.” God promises that He will send a messenger ahead of Him to prepare the way for *His own coming*. Malachi then says that “the Lord, whom you seek, will suddenly come to *His* temple.”

Jesus takes that prophecy and says John is the messenger: “This is he of whom it is written, ‘Behold, I send My messenger before Your face, who will prepare Your way before You.’” If John is the promised messenger, then who is Jesus claiming to be? He is the One whose way John was sent to prepare.

This is much more than Jesus saying that John predicted the arrival of the Messiah. Jesus is placing Himself inside a prophecy concerning the coming of Yahweh. In Malachi, the messenger prepares the way for the LORD. In Matthew, John prepares the way for Jesus.

That identification is especially striking because John's ministry had already been described in exactly these terms. ==Matthew 3:3== applies ==Isaiah 40:3== to John: “Prepare the way of the LORD.” Isaiah's Hebrew text uses the covenant name Yahweh. Once again, John prepares the way for Yahweh, and the One who appears behind him is Jesus.

Jesus does not merely claim to be someone sent by God. He takes Old Testament passages describing the arrival of Yahweh among His people and deliberately places Himself in the position occupied by Yahweh. For those who knew the Scriptures, the implication was difficult to miss, and the significance of this cannot be overstated. As the Pulpit Commentary observes, “Christ does not hesitate to apply to himself a prophecy of the coming of God.” Jesus is not merely claiming to represent Yahweh or to speak on His behalf. He is identifying Himself as the One whose arrival the prophets had announced.

The biblical scholar Johann Albrecht Bengel recognized the importance of this kind of Old Testament fulfillment, observing that one of the strongest arguments for the divinity of Christ is that the New Testament attributes to Jesus things the Old Testament attributes exclusively to God.

The One Israel had been waiting for was standing in front of them. Yahweh had come to His people in the person of Jesus Christ.
