## Commentary
This verse pulls the curtain back on a strategy of the enemy: the accuser attempts to change appointed times and laws (decrees). 

This is from a teaching I heard from Dutch Sheets on this passage: Satan tries to alter God's appointed times, decrees, and plans. He does so by attacking us, by wearing down the saints. The Hebrew word used in this verse is never used to describe physical fatigue...only mental and emotional weariness. Satan attempts to wear us down in our souls, causing us to grow weary, lose heart in our efforts. Discouragement, hope deferred, fear, anxiety, lack of endurance and other emotional responses attack our faith...and if not guarded against, can cause us to lose heart. 

If this type of weariness occurs, it can alter our God-appointed times and decrees. 

This strategy succeeded against the remnant sent to rebuild the walls of Jerusalem and the temple. Satan's method was to work through other people in the region (Ezra 4:4-5). This discouragement lasted 15 years. The Israelites yielded to fear and discouragement. The result was that the rebuilding of the temple stopped for 16 years. All they had was a foundation.

The existence of opposition should never determine what we believe to be God's will or whether it is His appointed time to do something. 

Eventually, God raised up a prophetic voice in Haggai to deliver His message to His people; Haggai 1:1-11. The problem was not the enemy. The problem was God's people who had succumbed to fear and unbelief and weariness.

One month later, when they had shaken off their fear and began building again, the Lord told them that He was with them, the nations would bring wealth into that house, and it would be better than it had been in the past (Haggai 2:1-9).
