## Commentary
In Scripture, divine visitation is always purposeful. God doesn't just casually drop by. When God visits, He comes to act. He comes in remembrance of His covenant. He comes to intervene where human strength has run out.

Naomi had left Bethlehem (which literally means "house of bread) because there was no bread. Famine had emptied the place that was supposed to speak of provision. But now the rumor reaches Moab: God has visited His people. Bread has returned. The land that seemed abandoned is being touched again by the mercy of God.

God’s visitation had a tangible effect. Hungry people were fed. Empty fields began to bear fruit again. The goodness of God became tangible enough to be heard about in another country.

So Naomi returns. She begins the journey home because she hears that God is moving among His people again.

## Application
Sometimes, there are places we leave because they have become too painful to stay. Naomi left Bethlehem because the house of bread had become the place of famine. So she went to Moab, and for a time, Moab must have seemed like the right decision.

But Ruth 1:6 shows us that famine is not the final word for the people of God. God can bring provision back to the very place we assumed had nothing left for us.

Naomi didn't know how her story would eventually contribute to the coming of the Messiah. She simply heard that God had visited His people with bread, and that was enough to begin the journey home. 

You may not know how your story is going to unfold, either. It may seem like everything around you has dried up. But like Naomi, you can trust that famine is never the end of the story for the people of God.
