## Commentary
Watch the sequence at Caesarea Philippi. Peter receives the revelation of who Jesus is: “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God” (==Matthew 16:16==). And Jesus responds to the revelation of the King by giving Peter a revelation of the *church*: “On this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it. And I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven” (==Matthew 16:19==). The two revelations belong together. You can’t rightly see the King without also seeing what the King is building.

Notice three things in what Jesus says. First, it’s *His* church—“I will build *My* church.” The church doesn’t belong to us; we belong to her Builder. Second, the promise of victory is given to the church: the gates of Hades will not prevail against *her*. And third, the keys of the kingdom are placed in the church’s hands. That doesn’t mean individuals have no assignments or protection—but the principle is unmistakable: God’s eternal plan of establishing heaven on earth runs through the church. The keys hang on her belt.

Now let’s be honest about why many of us resist this. We’ve been hurt by the church. Disappointed by her. Somewhere along the way we told ourselves we can’t trust her anymore—so we’ll follow Jesus on our own. I’ve felt that sting too, and I want to tread carefully here: some of us received our deepest wounds from people who claimed to love God. So did Jesus. He weeps with those who weep, and He will expose every injustice done in His name. But hear this: walking away from the church because a church wounded you is like a man whose first girlfriend broke his heart vowing never to associate with women again. He only hurts himself. The problem was the girlfriend, not all girls. And what wounded you may have called itself church—but it was not representative of the church Jesus loves and died for (==Ephesians 5:25==). The solution to broken church isn’t separation, and it isn’t becoming non-committal. It’s becoming the answer to your own prayers *for* the church—taking your seat at the table and being for someone else what you once prayed someone would be for you. When we respond to bad church by isolating, who wins? The gates of Hades.

I heard an old preacher's story that makes the point. When Jesus returned to heaven, the angels gathered around Him, amazed at His wounds. Gabriel asked, “Lord, do they all know what You did for them?” “Not yet,” Jesus said. “Only a handful in Jerusalem.” “Then what’s Your plan to tell the world?” “I’ve asked Peter, James, and John and the others to spread the news—they’ll tell others, until the message reaches the ends of the earth.” Gabriel, knowing human nature, pressed: “So what’s Plan B?” And Jesus answered: “There is no Plan B. They are My Body. They are My church.” 

The church—gathered, blemished, and beautiful—is still His one and only plan. She’s the one who prevails. Devote yourself to what Jesus is devoted to.
