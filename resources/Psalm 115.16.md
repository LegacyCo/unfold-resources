## Commentary
“The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD’s; but the earth He has given to the children of men.”

Have you ever read the Bible—or watched the news—and wondered, “Why doesn’t God just fix the problem? That sure would be a lot easier.” This verse is a big part of the answer. In creation, God drew a line of delegation: the heavens are His, but the earth He *gave* to mankind. He assigned the children of Adam to rule here on His behalf (==Genesis 1:26==; ==Psalm 8:6==). And God does not violate His own delegation. He doesn’t take back what He has given just because we manage it badly.

So how does God act in the earth? He finds a person. Watch the pattern. When His people were enslaved in Egypt, God didn’t vaporize Pharaoh from heaven. He said: “Moses—go, and I will deliver My people through you” (==Exodus 3:10==). Who set Israel free, God or Moses? God—through the obedience of a man. When wickedness filled the earth in the days of the flood, God didn’t scrap the project and concede that the devil had ruined creation. He found a righteous man named Noah and rebuilt the future through his family (==Genesis 6:8-9==). When Israel served the idols of the land, God didn’t shout from heaven—He put His Spirit on a man named Othniel and delivered Israel through him (==Judges 3:9-10==). The fact is, God *does* fix things—when He finds someone He can work through.

That’s why the pivotal sentence of the Old Testament might be this one: “I sought for a man among them who would make a wall, and stand in the gap before Me on behalf of the land, that I should not destroy it; *but I found no one*” (==Ezekiel 22:30==). Not that God wouldn’t act—He couldn’t find the human partner His own order required. And it’s why, when heaven finally moved to fix the deepest problem of all, God didn’t bend the rule even then. He submitted to it: He wrapped Himself in flesh and entered the earth *as a Man* (==1 Timothy 2:5==)—because earth’s authority had been given to man, lost by a man, and had to be reclaimed by a Man.

Understand this verse and prayer stops being a religious formality. If the earth is mankind’s delegated domain, then prayer is how the children of men invite the God of the heavens into the affairs of earth. “Your kingdom come, Your will be done, *on earth* as it is in heaven” (==Matthew 6:10==) isn’t poetry—it’s the delegation being exercised in the right direction. God is still looking for people He can work through (==2 Chronicles 16:9==). The question was never whether He’s willing to fix things. The question is whether He can find someone who will stand in the gap.
