## Commentary
At the end of his life, David gives Solomon one charge above all the others: “Know the God of your father… If you seek Him, He will be found by you.” It’s the same promise God later speaks through Jeremiah: “You will seek Me and find Me, when you search for Me with all your heart” (==Jeremiah 29:13==).

Now be careful not to shrink that promise. Scripture doesn’t say *salvation* will be found by anyone who seeks it. It says *God* will be found by anyone who seeks Him. Yes, that includes salvation—He is salvation. But He isn’t only salvation. He is abundant life. He is joy. He is peace that surpasses understanding. He is beauty and power and healing and hope and purpose, and the assurance that a day is coming when every tear is wiped away and wickedness is buried forever. The door of salvation is real—but it opens into a house with more doors than you could walk through in a lifetime.

A friend of mine was once shown a vision of an endless hallway. As far as he could see in both directions, doors lined both walls. He understood it was the Lord’s heart, and that each door opened into an aspect of God’s nature. Then the Lord spoke: “No one has ever been that way, and no one has ever been that way—yet every door is available to anyone who would open it.”

That’s the inheritance David was pressing into Solomon’s hands: not a religion to maintain, but a God to explore. And the same charge falls to us. As you discover more of the mystery of who He is, you become able to show the world who He is. You weren’t born to be saved. You were born to be possessed by God, passionately in love with God, showing the world what happens when a lost, broken, empty vessel becomes filled with Him. Seek Him—He will let you find Him. The hallway has no end.
