## Commentary
“Therefore ‘Come out from among them and be separate, says the Lord. Do not touch what is unclean, and I will receive you.’”

This verse sits at the end of Paul's charge not to be “unequally yoked together with unbelievers” (==2 Corinthians 6:14==). We hear that phrase and assume it applies only to marriage. It doesn't—that is not what Paul is talking about in this passage at all. He is admonishing the church to live holy lives in absolute surrender to the gospel, and his questions make the scope of his argument clear: what fellowship has righteousness with lawlessness? What communion has light with darkness? What agreement has the temple of God with idols? “For you are the temple of the living God” (==2 Corinthians 6:16==)—and the conclusion he draws in the very next sentence is the point of the whole passage: “let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God” (==2 Corinthians 7:1==).

The call is Abraham's call. Come out from anything that keeps you connected to the world, to old habits, to the flesh—the life of faith demands a total separation from the way we used to do things, so that we can be devoted to a life of holiness that shows the world who God is. Jesus paid for you to be free from the old life of sin and all its ripple effects, so that you can follow Him into a promised land. Don't settle for Haran when Canaan is your inheritance (==Genesis 11:31==). Don't fight Lot's battles when peace is now your environment (==Genesis 14:12-16==).

## Word Study
**Be separate** is the Greek *aphorizō* (ἀφορίζω)—to mark off by a boundary, to set apart. The word cuts both ways in Paul: the believer is to be separated *from* the unclean, and Paul himself was “separated (*aphorizō*) to the gospel of God” (==Romans 1:1==), set apart from his mother's womb to preach Christ (==Galatians 1:15==). Summary: biblical separation is never merely a leaving; everything God calls a person out of, He calls them out *unto* Himself—“I will receive you.”

## Application
Name what still yokes you to the old life—a habit, an entertainment, a business practice, a relationship pattern—and treat this verse as permission and command to walk away from it. Separation is not isolation from people (Paul says plainly the church cannot leave the world, ==1 Corinthians 5:9-10==); it is refusing agreement with what defiles. Come out, and let the promise attached to the command carry you: “I will receive you, and I will be a Father to you” (==2 Corinthians 6:18==).
