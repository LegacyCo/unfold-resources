## Commentary
“‘They shall be Mine,’ says the LORD of hosts, ‘on the day that I make them My jewels. And I will spare them as a man spares his own son who serves him.’”

God calls His people His jewels—His treasured possession—and He is preparing them for a day when they are set in place. That preparation involves shaping. He wants each believer to become a living stone that fits together seamlessly with the other living stones in His house (==1 Peter 2:5==; ==Ephesians 2:21==), and stones that fit have been cut. The process of having our own rough edges chipped away is difficult, which is why God first brings us to the end of ourselves: so that we do not resist when He begins to shape.

Consider a diamond. The raw stone that comes out of the ground looks nothing like the diamond in a king's crown. It has the potential to shine, but first it must give itself to the craftsman's blade. He sets it on his bench and examines it for the rough edges that keep light from passing through, then cuts them away. What he removes is genuine diamond—but it is what keeps the stone from being priceless. Only after the craftsman has finished can the jewel be set in the crown. Spurgeon, writing on this verse, applies it directly: “You, Christian, may venture to compare yourself to such a diamond, for you are one of God's jewels, and this is the time of the cutting process. You must endure it. Be of good courage. Let faith and patience do their perfect work. In the day when the crown shall be set upon the head of the King of kings, rays of His glory shall stream through you, for you belong to Him.”

## Word Study
**Jewels** translates the Hebrew *segullah* (סְגֻלָּה)—a treasured, personal possession; a king's private treasure as distinct from the wealth of the realm. God used the word at Sinai: “you shall be a special treasure to Me above all people” (==Exodus 19:5==), and again of Israel as “a people for Himself, a special treasure” (==Deuteronomy 7:6==; ==Psalm 135:4==). Peter carries it into the church: “His own special people” (==1 Peter 2:9==). Summary: *segullah* is what a king keeps for himself—and what he keeps, he cuts, polishes, and sets where his glory can be seen through it.
