## Commentary
At the end of John 2, many believed in Jesus’ name because they saw His signs—but Jesus “had no need that anyone should testify of man, for He knew what was in man.” They professed faith; He didn’t accept the profession. He knew their hearts. Warren Wiersbe calls them “unsaved believers”—people genuinely *with* Jesus who were never *in* Him.

Being with Jesus has real benefits. Hanging around royalty always does. Nobody in a prince’s entourage rides in economy—there are good seats, good meals, good moments, and stories to tell. But you can’t assume that a ride in the royal Rolls-Royce is the same thing as the King being your father. The entourage enjoys the benefits of proximity. But only sons carry the bloodline. And the born-again reality doesn’t come from proximity to the King—it comes from being born again, reborn as a child of the King (==John 1:12-13==).

This is the mistake of the crowds in John 2. Millions of people experience what it’s like to be in the vicinity of God once a week—and the presence is real, and the benefits are real. We can’t assume that because we show up on Sunday and our playlists picked up some worship music, that a born-again transformation has taken place. So here’s the question that separates the entourage from the family: is there an internal flame inside you, or are you content to warm yourself beside someone else’s fire? Are you gathering to be near His presence and enjoy His benefits—or is the Spirit of Jesus *in* you, drawing you together with others who carry Him too, living stones being built into a dwelling place for His glory (==1 Peter 2:5==)?

Jesus knew what was in man. He still does. And what He’s looking for in you isn’t proximity—it’s Himself. “Christ in you, the hope of glory” (==Colossians 1:27==). Don’t settle for the entourage when you’ve been offered adoption (==Galatians 4:6-7==).

## Word Study
Two little Greek words carry the whole difference between the entourage and the family:

- *with* : **meta** (Greek) — with, in the company of; the language of proximity and association. Being meta Jesus is where everyone starts—He appointed the twelve first of all “that they might be *with* (meta) Him” (==Mark 3:14==). But meta by itself guarantees nothing: a servant girl said of Peter, “this fellow was *with* (meta) Jesus,” in the very hour he was denying Him (==Matthew 26:69==).
- *in* : **en** (Greek) — in, within; the language of union. “At that day you will know that I am in My Father, and you *in* (en) Me, and I *in* you” (==John 14:20==).

Think of the two criminals crucified beside Jesus. Both of them began that day *with* Him—same hill, same hour, equal proximity. But only one, through faith, ended it hearing, “today you will be with Me in Paradise” (==Luke 23:43==)—because his believing had moved him from proximity to belonging.

Summary: meta is where everyone starts; en is where the gospel is taking you. Proximity puts you near the King. Only new birth puts you in Him.
