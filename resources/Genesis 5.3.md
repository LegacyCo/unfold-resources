## Commentary
We have here in chapter 5 something we didn’t have in chapter 4; “fathering” language. When chapter 4 tells the story of Cain, it says, “Eve conceived and she gave birth to a son” (==Genesis 4.1==). 

But here in chapter 5, a father has a son who is born in his own image and likeness.

Genesis 5 is picking up the language of a better day, before sin and death, before disobedience and deception and fig-leaves. Genesis 5 is using the language of Genesis 1 as a clue. This “fathering” language, where a son is born in the image and likeness of the one who came before him, is a declaration that the family lineage that all future generations can put their hope in is this one. It’s through the lineage of a boy named Seth, son of Adam. Genesis 5 is saying, “Watch the family of this son, because the Redeemer will be born through his lineage.”

## Application
The story of the Bible is often the story of a people who are on the verge of defeat. And when all hope seems to be lost…a child is born. And that one life, lived in faithfulness to God, changes the whole story. 

God’s people are in slavery in Egypt…and Moses is born.

500 years later, when God’s word was rare in Israel and the priesthood was ungodly…Samuel is born.

One generation later, when God’s people are ruled by an insecure, selfish king…David is born.

1,000 years after David, when God’s people are burdened and weary in their bondage to religion and sin…Jesus is born.

We could go on, but the point is clear; we may look around and see sin, rebellion, injustice, bad leaders, churches that hurt us, pastors who fail, and news outlets who lie — and it may seem hopeless. But we must remember, this is God’s story. He will accomplish His purpose in the earth. He just needs one life lived in surrender to Him
