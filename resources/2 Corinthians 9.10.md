## Commentary
“Now may He who supplies seed to the sower, and bread for food, supply and multiply the seed you have sown and increase the fruits of your righteousness.”

God supplies two different things in this verse, and knowing which is which changes how you handle what’s in your hands. He gives *bread for food*—provision meant to be consumed. Eat it, enjoy it, be thankful. And He gives *seed to the sower*—provision meant to be planted. Same grain, two assignments. The farmer who eats his seed corn has a good meal and no future; the wisdom is in recognizing which portion is dinner and which portion is next year’s field.

Now watch what God promises to multiply: not the seed in the barn—the seed *you have sown*. “The one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and he who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully” (==2 Corinthians 9:6==). Multiplication happens in the ground, never in storage. “There is one who scatters, yet increases more” (==Proverbs 11:24==). And notice who does the multiplying. Not the sower—the Supplier. Your job is to plant; increase is His department. He’s the One “who supplies seed,” and Paul stacks the verbs on God’s side of the ledger: He will *supply* and *multiply* and *increase*.

One more thing, because it keeps this from becoming a money formula: what God says He’ll increase is “the fruits of your *righteousness*.” Paul is quoting the psalm he just cited—the generous man who “has dispersed abroad, he has given to the poor; his righteousness endures forever” (==2 Corinthians 9:9==; ==Psalm 112:9==). The harvest God is growing through your generosity isn’t primarily a bigger bank account; it’s a bigger *you*—a life increasingly shaped like His, with more grace flowing through it toward every good work (==2 Corinthians 9:8==). So take inventory of what’s in your hands. Some of it is bread: eat with gladness. And some of it is seed: it was never meant for the barn.
