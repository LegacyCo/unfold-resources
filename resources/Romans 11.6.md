## Commentary
To understand the significance of this statement, we need to remember what Paul has been explaining about Israel. Although much of the nation had rejected Jesus as the Messiah, God had preserved a remnant for Himself. But Paul is careful to explain how that remnant came to exist. It wasn't because these individuals had maintained stricter obedience to the Law or somehow earned God's favor. They were, as Paul says in the preceding verse, “a remnant according to the election of grace” (==Romans 11:5==).

If our standing with God is secured by what we accomplish, then it is no longer grace. And if it is truly grace, then our works cannot be the basis upon which we receive it.

Consider the implications of this for Israel. The Jewish people had been entrusted with the Law, the covenants, the promises, and the temple worship (==Romans 9:4–5==). These were extraordinary privileges, but none of them could establish righteousness before God. Israel's failure was that they sought to establish their own righteousness through the law rather than submit to the righteousness God provided through Christ (==Romans
10:3–4==).
