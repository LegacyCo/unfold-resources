## Commentary
On the night He was betrayed, Jesus prayed, “I do not pray for these alone, but also for those who will believe in Me *through their word*.”

Not “through My word”—through *their* word. The word of eleven ordinary men. Hours before the cross, Jesus stakes the future of the gospel on the testimony of His people. Every believer since—including you—came to faith downstream of that sentence, through an unbroken chain of Jesus People opening their mouths.

That’s the mystery of the gospel’s delivery system. The gospel is the power of God unto salvation (==Romans 1:16==)—and God has determined that it be declared by former enemies who have themselves been transformed by it. The message of reconciliation is carried by the reconciled (==2 Corinthians 5:18-19==).

Jesus did what only He could do. His death on the cross was completely effective in accomplishing salvation for the world—nothing can be added to it. But now that He has done what only He can do, it’s the responsibility of the people of the cross to do what only *they* can do: leave the starting line of salvation and show the world a crucified life. A life of surrender to Jesus, of first love for Jesus, of death to the flesh and life in the Spirit. The finished work doesn’t make our witness unnecessary. It makes our witness possible.

So when you pray for the lost—your kids, your coworkers, your city—remember that Jesus prayed for them too, and He prayed with a mechanism in view: they will believe *through your word*. Someone in your world is on the far end of the prayer Jesus prayed in John 17. Say something.
