## Commentary
This passage gives us a vivid picture of discontentment.

Israel has been delivered from Egypt. They have seen the Red Sea split, water come from a rock, and heavenly bread appear on the ground every morning. 

And somehow, it wasn't enough.

The “mixed multitude” among them, Egyptians who left with Israel after seeing what God did to their Pharaoh, began craving meat. And soon Israel is weeping with them, “Who will give us meat to eat?” Then they remember Egypt: “We remember the fish which we ate freely…”

But they *didn't* eat freely in Egypt. They were slaves there. They were oppressed. Their sons were murdered there. Their backs were bent under Pharaoh’s burden.

Discontentment has a way of editing the past. It remembers the fish and forgets the chains. It gladly distributes rose-colored glasses.

That is what makes Numbers 11 so sobering. Israel looks at literal bread from heaven and says, “There is nothing at all except this manna.” What was once a miracle has become monotonous. What was once mercy now feels like something to complain about.

The issue was not simply that they desired meat. Desire wasn't the problem. The problem was that desire became a demand. They were no longer receiving from God with trust. They were accusing God because He had not arranged the wilderness according to their appetite.

And the cost was severe.

While the meat was still between their teeth, the judgment of the Lord fell. The place was called Kibroth-hattaavah — “the graves of craving” — because there they buried the people who gave into their carnal cravings.

And their discontentment did not only affect them. It crushed Moses. The burden of leading a complaining people became so heavy that he asked the Lord to take his life.

That is what discontentment does. It spreads. It wears people down. It turns provision into accusation. It turns leaders into targets. It turns the wilderness, which was meant to be a place of formation, into a place of resentment.

God had brought Israel out of Egypt, but He was also teaching them how to live free. And free people have to learn dependence. 

## Application
Numbers 11 warns us that we can be delivered from Egypt and still crave its menu.

We can receive daily mercy and still call it “nothing” because it is not what our flesh wanted.

Contentment is not pretending the wilderness is easy. It's learning to trust that if God has brought me here, and God is sustaining me here, then His portion is enough.
