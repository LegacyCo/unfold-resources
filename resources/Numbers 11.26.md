## Commentary
“But two men had remained in the camp: the name of one was Eldad, and the name of the other Medad. And the Spirit rested upon them… but they had not gone out to the tabernacle; yet they prophesied in the camp.”

God told Moses to gather seventy elders at the tent of meeting, where He would take of the Spirit that was on Moses and put it on them (==Numbers 11:16-17==). Sixty-eight showed up. Eldad and Medad were still out in the camp somewhere—and the Spirit of God rested on them right where they were. Among the tents, where the ordinary life of the people was happening, two men who never made it to the meeting began to prophesy.

Notice that the Spirit was not confined to the sanctioned location. The tent of meeting was real, the gathering was commanded, and God honored it—but His Spirit was not limited to it. When a young man ran to report what was happening, Joshua wanted it stopped: “Moses, my lord, forbid them!” (==Numbers 11:28==). Joshua’s instinct is still alive in the church: the assumption that God’s voice belongs at the official gathering, through the recognized channels, and that prophecy in the camp is a problem to be managed. Moses corrected him with the desire that the new covenant would one day fulfill: “Oh, that all the LORD’s people were prophets and that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!” (==Numbers 11:29==).

This is the difference between a prophetic movement and a prophetic people. A movement gathers around the *one* who hears—an event you attend, detached from the rhythm of real life. A prophetic people is a family...who hears: mothers and fathers, children and friends, accountability and maturity, the voice of God present in conversation around dinner tables. Under the new covenant, the Spirit has been poured out on all flesh (==Joel 2:28==; ==Acts 2:17==), which means the camp is now full of Eldads and Medads. God gave the church prophets (==Ephesians 4:11==), but there is a difference between a gift and a gate—He gave prophets to the church; He never appointed them gatekeepers of His voice. The Spirit still rests on people who never made it to the meeting.
