## Commentary
“…let us make a name for ourselves…”

This phrase is repeated almost word for word in the next chapter, with one (big) difference. To Abram God says, “I will bless you and make your name great” (==Genesis 12:2==). The people of Babel tried to make a name for themselves; God would make Abram's name for him. The striking difference between the two stories is not whether a name is made but *who makes it*. Using their own effort, their own ideas, and their own manufactured materials, the builders of Babel were not just constructing a tower—they were building a reputation. They wanted to be known in the earth. God had told Noah's family to fill the earth with image-bearers; these people wanted to fill it with the fame of their name.

Scripture does not record a single name from the people of Babel. Their reputation is the one thing the story leaves out. This is the outcome the rest of Scripture predicts. Making a name for yourself is pride, and “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble” (==James 4:6==)—which is exactly what the builders came up against on the plain of Shinar. The opposite path is the one Jesus walked: He emptied Himself and humbled Himself, “therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name” (==Philippians 2:9==). When a believer humbles himself, resists the pull to make his own name great, and seeks to exalt the name of Jesus, he comes into alignment with God's purpose in the earth—and the resources of heaven that were withheld from Babel are placed at his disposal.

## Word Study
**Name** is the Hebrew *shem* (שֵׁם)—not merely a label but a reputation, renown, and memorial; what a person is known for. God makes Abram's *shem* great (==Genesis 12:2==), David is given “a great name, like the name of the great men who are on the earth” (==2 Samuel 7:9==), and the LORD's own *shem* is “excellent in all the earth” (==Psalm 8:1==). Summary: a *shem* worth having is one God gives; the name Babel made for itself is the one no one remembers.
