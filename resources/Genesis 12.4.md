## Commentary
“So Abram departed as the LORD had spoken to him, and Lot went with him. And Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran.”

This verse records an obedience and a compromise in the same sentence. Abram departed as the LORD had spoken—and Lot went with him. God had told Abram to leave his country, his family, and his father's house (==Genesis 12:1==). But Abram thought it would be all right to bring his father, and his father's presence cost him years in Haran, a place God had not told him to settle (==Genesis 11:31-32==; ==Acts 7:4==). He also brought his nephew Lot, and Lot brought strife over the land (==Genesis 13:7==), a war to rescue him from captivity (==Genesis 14:12-16==), and finally the anguish of Sodom (==Genesis 19:29==). Abram was not perfect. He tried to carry pieces of his old life into the new one.

But Abram never turned back, and that is what matters most. Obedience is important; so is total surrender and commitment to the path God has called you to. What Abram brought from Ur did not disqualify him—he is still the father of the faithful—but it slowed him, distracted him, and at times endangered him. The pattern is instructive precisely because it is so common. Many who have answered God's call have also brought things from the old life into the new: friends from before, business practices, hobbies and habits, even family entanglements. These things do not cancel the call, but they will cost something along the way.

## Application
Take an inventory of what came with you out of your own Ur. Name the relationships, practices, or attachments you carried into your life with Jesus because it seemed harmless to bring them—and ask whether they have slowed you down, pulled your attention, or put you in harm's way, as Lot did for Abram. Then do what Abram eventually had to do with Lot: separate from what God told you to leave (==Genesis 13:9==). Learn from Abram's successes, and learn from his mistakes; respond to God's call the way he did, with surrender and commitment, and refuse to turn back.
