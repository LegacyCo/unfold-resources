## Commentary
Moses had asked God, “Please, show me Your glory” (==Exodus 33:18==). God answered that request by descending in the cloud, standing with Moses, and proclaiming His *name*. The glory Moses desired to see was revealed through the declaration of who God is.

In Scripture, the name of God is more than a title by which He is addressed. His name reveals His nature and character. When the Lord proclaimed His name over Moses, He declared Himself to be merciful and gracious, patient, abounding in goodness and truth, forgiving sin, and unwavering in justice. God’s glory is the revelation of God as He truly is.

We often think of glory primarily in terms of visible splendor, supernatural power, or an overwhelming spiritual experience. But when God showed Moses His glory, He spoke of His character. The weight of His glory is found in the perfect union of His mercy and holiness, His forgiveness and justice, His tenderness and truth. None of these attributes compete with the others. God is not merciful at the expense of being holy, nor just at the expense of being compassionate. Everything He is exists in perfect fullness and agreement.

This means that to know His name is to know His ways. We cannot claim to desire the glory of God while remaining uninterested in the character of God. His glory is not merely something to be witnessed; it is something intended to transform those before whom it is revealed. Moses came down from the mountain carrying upon his face the evidence that he had been with God. In the same way, those who truly behold the Lord should begin to bear the likeness of the One whose name has been proclaimed over them.
