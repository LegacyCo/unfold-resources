## Commentary
“And as for Me, behold, I establish My covenant with you and with your descendants after you.”

This is the first time the word *covenant* appears in Scripture. It is one of the biggest words in the Bible and in the life of the church, and one of the least used in ordinary conversation—but a people building God's house according to His design has to be familiar with it and comfortable with it. Covenant is the framework inside which God relates to His people from this verse forward.

“As for Me”—God is the initiator and the guarantor. Noah does not negotiate the terms or contribute to the guarantee. God binds Himself, and He binds Himself to Noah's descendants after him, to every living creature, and for perpetual generations (==Genesis 9:12==). The covenant is God's idea, God's promise, and God's responsibility to keep. And the terms are entirely in Noah's favor. God commits His own resources to protect Noah's family—the fear of them is placed on every creature (==Genesis 9:2==); He commits to provide for them—“I have given you all things” (==Genesis 9:3==); He commits to their children and their children's children (==Genesis 9:9==); and He commits never to bring the flood again (==Genesis 9:11==). Nothing is asked of Noah beyond trust. A relationship like this, offered by One who can honor every term of it, would be foolish to refuse.

The word “establish” also carries weight. God had already spoken of this covenant before the flood: “But I will establish My covenant with you, and you shall go into the ark” (==Genesis 6:18==). What was promised before the judgment is confirmed after it. The flood did not interrupt God's commitment; the same word given on the far side of the waters is now made to stand on this side. This is what distinguishes a covenant from a contract. A contract is an exchange of goods with conditions and exit clauses; a covenant is persons bound to one another by oath, for life—“where you die, I will die” (==Ruth 1:16-17==). This is the pattern for every covenant that follows, up to the one made in the blood of Jesus (==Luke 22:20==): God comes to His people and says, in effect, “I want to be with you, and I will do whatever it takes.”

## Word Study
**Covenant** is the Hebrew *berit* (בְּרִית)—a binding agreement, a bond between parties sealed with an oath. The idiom for making one is “to cut a covenant” (*karat berit*), from the sacrifice that accompanied it (==Genesis 15:18==). God cuts a covenant with Abraham (==Genesis 17:7==), with Israel at Sinai (==Exodus 24:8==), with David (==2 Samuel 7:16==), and promises a new covenant written on the heart (==Jeremiah 31:31-33==). **Establish** is *qum* in its causative form (הֵקִים, *heqim*)—to cause to stand, to make firm, to carry out what was spoken; it is the verb used when God “confirms” His word to Abraham (==Genesis 17:19==) and “performs” His promise to David (==1 Kings 8:20==). Summary: *berit* is the word for God binding Himself by oath to a people, and *heqim* is the word for Him making that oath stand—the first time He uses them together, He is the one taking on all the obligation.

## Application
Two questions follow from this verse. Are you in covenant with God—not merely acquainted with Him, but bound to Him through the blood of Jesus? And if you are, are you honoring that covenant? Being in covenant with a spouse and honoring that covenant are two different things. Take stock of both: whether you have entered, and whether you are living like someone who has.
