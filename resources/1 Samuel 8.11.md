## Commentary
When Israel demanded a king so they could be like the other nations, God told Samuel to warn them exactly what they were asking for: “This will be the behavior of the king who will reign over you: he will *take* your sons and appoint them for his own chariots… he will *take* your daughters to be perfumers and cooks and bakers… he will *take* the best of your fields, your vineyards, and your olive groves… he will *take* your servants and your donkeys… he will *take* a tenth of your sheep. And you will be his servants” (==1 Samuel 8:11-17==). Read the passage and count the verb: take, take, take, take. That’s what kings of this world do. They take whatever they want.

Now hold that language against the language of the ultimate King, “Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will *give* you rest… for I am gentle and lowly in heart” (==Matthew 11:28-29==). “The bread that I shall *give* is My flesh, which I shall *give* for the life of the world” (==John 6:51==). To him who overcomes I will *give* the tree of life, *give* the hidden manna, *give* power over the nations, *give* the morning star, *give* the right to sit with Me on My throne (==Revelation 2:7==; ==Revelation 3:21==). He gives salvation by grace (==Ephesians 2:8==). He gives the Holy Spirit (==Acts 2:38==). And in the most sobering passages of Romans, even His judgment takes the form of giving—He *gives* people over to what they insist on choosing (==Romans 1:24==). The whole gospel starts with this incredible verb: “For God so loved the world that He *gave*” (==John 3:16==).

This is why Jesus will never seize the throne of your heart, kick out the imposters by force, and install Himself uninvited. That might be what other kings do. But that isn’t the kind of King He is. Kings who take, demand. The King who gives, invites. In this age His kingdom comes with persuasion, not irresistible power; like leaven that spreads rather than a bomb that goes off (==Matthew 13:33==). The King is enthroned—and astonishingly, we can still ignore Him. He must be received by a willing heart and a surrendered will.

So examine the kings you’ve served. Every one of them—fear, money, success, approval—took from you. Your peace, your years, your sons and daughters, the best of your fields. There has only ever been one King who, instead of taking your life, gave you His.
