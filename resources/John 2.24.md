## Commentary
Many people in Jerusalem saw the signs Jesus performed and “believed in His name.” Then John says something unsettling: “But Jesus did not entrust Himself to them.”

The same Greek word is used on both sides of the sentence. They _believed_ in Jesus, but Jesus did not _believe_ their belief. They entrusted themselves to Him at the level of fascination, but He would not entrust Himself to them because He knew what was in them....or, we could say, what was *not* in them.

Their faith had been produced by what they saw Jesus do. They were impressed by miracles, convinced that something divine was happening, and willing to associate themselves with Him while the bread multiplied and the sick were healed. But Jesus knew how quickly admiration disappears when following Him becomes costly. A person can believe that Jesus is powerful while remaining unwilling to surrender to Him as Lord.

Nicodemus appears immediately after this. He has seen the same signs and reaches the same conclusion: “Rabbi, we know that You have come from God as a teacher” (==John 3:2==). Jesus does not congratulate him for believing. He tells him he must be born again.

That is the difference between belief that observes Jesus and faith that gives itself to Jesus. Demons believe that God exists and tremble (==James 2:19==). Crowds can believe in a miracle worker and abandon Him when He speaks of a cross. Saving faith moves beyond agreeing with facts about Jesus. It joins itself to Jesus—His death, His resurrection, His life, and His lordship.

Jesus was not rejecting people who had too little theological understanding. He was exposing a profession of faith that still left the old life untouched. His refusal to entrust Himself to them was mercy. He would not allow enthusiasm to masquerade as surrender.

## Application
Ask yourself the difficult question: do you believe in Jesus because of what you hope He will do for you, or have you entrusted yourself to Him because of who He is?

Faith is often revealed when Jesus stops multiplying bread and shifts to conversation to surrender. 

## Word Study

- _believed / entrust_ : **pisteuō** — to believe, trust, rely upon, or entrust something to another. John uses the same word to describe what the crowd did toward Jesus and what Jesus refused to do toward the crowd.

The repetition creates the force of the passage: they trusted themselves to Him, but He did not trust Himself to them. Their mouths professed faith, but Jesus knew what remained within their hearts.

Summary: Jesus does not measure faith by the strength of our profession. He looks for a surrendered life that the profession of faith produces.
