## Commentary
Mark points us back to a previous miracle to show that proximity to Jesus did not guarantee understanding. Jesus had fed the five thousand (v33-44), walked on water, and calmed the storm, yet there still was a measure of bewilderment in the disciples. “They did not understand about the loaves, because their heart was hardened.” That is a striking statement. 

The issue was not that they had failed to see a miracle. They saw it. The issue was that they had not discerned the mystery being revealed through it. They saw bread multiplied, but they did not yet see what the bread was saying. They received the provision, but missed the revelation. 

The miracle of the loaves was never just about Jesus’ ability to feed a crowd. It was a sign pointing beyond itself. The bread in His hands was revealing the One who stood before them. He was not merely a miracle worker handing out food. He is the true bread from heaven. He is the living bread of God who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world (John 6:51). And when He later says, “Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you,” (John 6:53) He is inviting us to grab hold of a mystery by faith.

This is where we stumble. We are tempted to reduce mystery down to something manageable, something symbolic, something that fits neatly inside our preferred categories. We want a faith that can be diagramed and defended without remainder. But the kingdom of God doesn't submit itself to the limits of natural reasoning. There are mysteries that must be received before they can ever be understood — and some that may never arrive at sufficient understanding in this life. There are realities in God that are of another age, revealed by the Spirit, entered by faith, and known through participation.

Communion is one such spiritual reality. We can't relegate the mystery of bread and wine to mere symbolism, as though the table were only a visual aid to help us remember a crucified Christ. No. The table is mystery. By saying that, we are not saying the elements cease to be bread and wine, but neither dare we flatten the meal into a bare mental exercise. Jesus did not say, “Think about Me.” He definitely did not say, "This *represents* My body." He said, “This *is* My body. Take, eat.” He gave Himself to His people in a form that must be received, not merely understood. 

The logic of communion is the logic of the incarnation itself. God comes near. God gives Himself. God makes the invisible tangible. The eternal Word took on flesh. The Son of God became touchable, receivable, eatable in the deepest spiritual sense. This is offensive to the proud mind because it refuses to let us keep God at a safe distance. It requires faith. It requires us to admit that we do not master divine things by explanation, but receive them by faith.

The tragedy of Mark 6 is that the disciples stood in the presence of mystery and still did not understand the loaves. Let that not be said of us. Let us be a people who ask the Holy Spirit to open our eyes to the mystery of the bread. I pray those words often when I take communion, "Holy Spirit, call me up into the mystery: where bread is flesh and wine is blood, where the shadows heal the sick and kings carry crosses." Let us be a people who refuse to dull down what God has made holy simply because we cannot fully explain it. Let us be a people who believe that when Jesus tells us to eat of Him, He intends to bring us into a real participation in His life.

## Application
Ask the Lord to deliver you from a hardened heart that can witness holy things without discerning what they mean. It is possible to handle bread and still miss the mystery. It is possible to be near the table and yet live untouched by it.

So come to communion with reverence, faith, and hunger. Don't come merely to remember a historical incident. Come to receive a living Person. Ask the Holy Spirit to awaken your heart to the mystery of Christ, the living bread of God. Refuse the hardened heart that insists everything must be reduced to logic before it can be believed. "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed" (John 20:29).
