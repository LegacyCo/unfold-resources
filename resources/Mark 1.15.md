## Commentary
This is from *Pentecostal Fire* by Larry Sparks: The present Kingdom reign and rule of Jesus Christ, on the earth, through and in His people, begins now. He made it clear: "the kingdom of heaven is at hand." This means it's within reach and accessible to our spiritual grasp. He also said, "the kingdom of God is within you" (==Luke 17:21==).

The Kingdom simplified is the rule and reign of God on the earth. This is not a political or governmental overthrow in the natural realm; it is a violent dismantling of demonic principalities and powers in the unseen heavenly dimensions. This is accomplished through a people (the Church, Jesus's ekklesia) filled with the Holy Spirit who are confident in what God says (the Scriptures) about the circumstances they are called to challenge and change through the supernatural power He has given to them. Theologically speaking, I understand that we will not experience the full measure of God's Kingdom rule this side of eternity. I confidently and enthusiastically believe in the Second Coming of Jesus and His glorious appearing. But Jesus did not give us safe perimeters for how much "on earth as it is in Heaven" we could expect to see in the here and now. He just gave us that model to pray, and then presented all of this radical language about a Kingdom that was present, near, and dwelling within us. So, how much of Heaven can we experience on earth? Perhaps as much as we are willing to press in for.
  
A. W. Tozer in The Fire of God's Presence offers this great illustration: "Many people have the kind of faith Martha had. She said 'Yes, I know, Lord. He'll (Lazarus) arise in the last day.' And the Lord said, 'That's not the kind of faith that works. I want you to believe he'll rise now; right now. I don't want Martha faith; I want Mary faith. Martha did not grasp that the living embodiment of Resurrection and Life stood right before her, Jesus the Great Awakener. Thus, she did what many Christians do today with the Bible prophecies of Holy Spirit outpouring and revival: discard who is standing before us, and instead, settle on comfortable theology that does not demand anything of me right now.
