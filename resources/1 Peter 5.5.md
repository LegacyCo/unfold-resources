## Commentary
In our journey with God, we may have experienced seasons of great humbling and brokenness, but that is very different from being clothed with humility and possessing a truly humble spirit.

Many can point to seasons when they were broken, corrected, brought low, or made deeply aware of their weakness. Those experiences may be painful and necessary, but they do not automatically produce humility. A person can be humbled by life and still remain proud in spirit. 

To be clothed with humility means humility becomes the spirit in which we live. It shapes the way we relate to one another, the way we receive correction, the way we serve, and the way we carry whatever authority or influence God gives us. Humility is no longer an occasional response forced upon us by hardship; it becomes the daily garment of the heart.

This is why Peter connects humility with submission to one another. True humility is revealed relationally. It does not insist on being seen, heard, praised, or preferred. It is willing to take the lower place because it trusts God with honor, recognition, and promotion.

God may use moments of humbling to strip away our pride, but His purpose is greater than a broken moment. He intends to form in us a humble spirit. As Andrew Murray wrote, "May God teach us that our thoughts and words and feelings concerning our fellowmen are His test of our humility toward Him. May He teach us that our humility before Him is the only power that can enable us to be always humble with our fellowmen. Our humility must be the life of Christ, the Lamb of God, within us."
