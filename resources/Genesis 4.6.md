## Commentary
We see here in chapter 4 that sin had not cut off interaction between God and man. Adam and Eve had been driven out of the garden, but they had not been driven beyond the reach of God. The expulsion from Eden was not because God wanted to remove Himself from humanity. Genesis 3 tells us the reason: access to the tree of life had to be guarded. In mercy, God would not allow fallen humanity to eat from that tree and live forever in a corrupted state.

Cain is now outside the garden. He is part of a fallen world. Sin is already working in the human heart. And yet, God comes near. God speaks to Cain, “Why are you angry? And why has your countenance fallen?” This is not the voice of a distant God who has abandoned man to his ruin. This is the voice of a God who still humbles Himself to engage fallen humanity.

Before Cain murders Abel, God confronts the condition of his heart. He meets him in the place of disorder and offers a moment of mercy before sin becomes action.

The garden is no longer accessible, but God is still speaking. He is still warning. He is still coming near. Genesis 4 shows us that the fall did not make God passive toward humanity. It revealed the depth of His mercy. Even east of Eden, God is still pursuing man.
