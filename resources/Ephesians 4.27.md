## Commentary
“Nor give place to the devil.” The word translated “place” is *topos*—ground, territory, a foothold. Paul’s assumption is this: the devil cannot simply take ground in a believer’s life. He has to be *given* it. As Warren Wiersbe put it, “Satan cannot harm the believer who will not harm himself.”

Look at the context. Paul isn’t writing about witchcraft or the occult—he’s writing about ordinary anger: “Do not let the sun go down on your wrath, nor give place to the devil” (==Ephesians 4:26==). The anger itself isn’t the foothold. Anger *kept overnight* is. Emotion becomes territory when it’s harbored instead of dealt with. That distinction runs through the whole subject: sin does not give the devil legal access to you—*living* in sin does.

This matters in both directions. Some believers are tormented by the lie that every stumble hands the enemy the deed to their peace. It doesn’t. A sin brought to the cross is a canceled debt—the enemy gains nothing from a failure you’ve confessed and turned from (==1 John 1:9==; ==Colossians 2:14==). But others have grown comfortable in areas of compromise—the habit no longer fought, the accusation spoken over themselves daily, the agreements made with shame and fear. That’s not a stumble; that’s a lease. Unrepentant sin is an open door, and what stays unchallenged long enough begins to claim a right to remain (==Psalm 1:1==).

The enemy doesn’t come and kick the door in. He has no right to do that. He just knocks until he finds someone who wants to come out and go for a walk.

## Application
Take inventory of your open doors—not your failures, your *doors*. The sin you confessed last week isn’t one. The one you’ve renegotiated with, renamed, or scheduled around is. So is the anger you’ve been carrying past sundown for weeks, and every sentence that starts with “I’ll always be…” or “I’ll never get free of…”—those are agreements, and agreements are territory.

Then close them the way doors actually close: repent specifically, break the agreement out loud in Jesus’ name, and where anger is the ground, go make it right with the person. The stewardship of the life Jesus purchased—at enormous cost to Himself—either closes the door to the enemy or opens it.
