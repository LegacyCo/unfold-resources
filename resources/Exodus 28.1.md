## Commentary
God commanded Moses to bring Aaron and his sons near “that he may minister to Me as priest.” The priesthood began with a divine summons into intimacy with God. Aaron was not first separated from the people; he was first separated unto the Lord. Big difference.

This is the highest purpose of consecration. We often think of being set apart in terms of what God will ask us to do, but Scripture first describes it in terms of whom we belong to. The priest was consecrated to God before he was entrusted with responsibility among men (==Numbers 18:7==).

The phrase “minister to Me” corrects our tendency to make all service man-centered. God certainly cares for people, and the priest was given responsibilities toward the nation. But service to men was to flow out of service to God. The priest first attended to the presence, the altar, and the desires of the Lord (==Ezekiel 44:15–16==).

When this order is reversed, our activity may continue while its spiritual life slowly disappears. We may remain busy doing things in God’s name while no longer doing them from God’s presence. Eventually, even service becomes a way of expressing ourselves rather than ministering to Him.

The first calling of every priest is not to accomplish something impressive, but to belong wholly to the Lord and attend faithfully to Him (see ==Acts 13:2==).
