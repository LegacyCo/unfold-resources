## Commentary
The wilderness is a place of covenant. It was there that God revealed Himself to Israel as a bridegroom and called them His own. Jeremiah remembers it as the love of their betrothal, when Israel followed Him into a land where there was nothing else to depend on (==Jeremiah 2:2==). Ezekiel describes it as the moment God covered their nakedness, entered into covenant with them, and said, “You became Mine” (==Ezekie 16:8==).

In the wilderness God removes distractions. In the noise of abundance, His voice can become one voice among many. But in the wilderness, the lesser loves grow quiet and the heart becomes able to hear Him again.

The wilderness may feel empty, but it is anything but empty. God extends us the most extravagant gift He can give: Himself. What He forms there is a communion with Him that often could not have been forged anywhere else.

As Samuel Whitefield wrote in *Will You Choose the Wilderness*, "God gathered Israel in the wilderness to ask them the question, 'Am I enough for you?' The wilderness is a crucible that tests unmet expectations with that burning question 'is God enough?' We all think that He is...until He is all that we have left. The wilderness forces us to confront the real answer to that question, the answer that is only exposed by pressure. Our deepest desires are revealed in the wilderness because the wilderness seems to have only one thing to offer: deep communion with God. Will that be enough?...Crowds are seducing, but the wilderness is where the fire burns, the burning bush appears, and God speaks…The wilderness offers something you cannot find anywhere else: the potential of undistracted communion with God. There are simply some encounters with God that are found only in the wilderness."
