## Commentary
Dr. Robert Gladstone writes, “This statement points to the quality of Jesus’ leadership. How effective would His years of disciple-making have been if He had to remain to lead His disciples once they received the Holy Spirit?”

Jesus told His disciples that His departure would be to their advantage. That must have sounded impossible. How could losing the physical presence of Jesus possibly be better than having Him beside them? Yet Jesus had not spent those years forming disciples who could function only while He was in the room. He was preparing them to live by the indwelling presence and leadership of the Holy Spirit.

This is one of the clearest marks of healthy spiritual leadership. Immature leadership makes people increasingly dependent upon the leader. Jesus formed people who would become increasingly dependent upon God. His goal was never to make Himself unnecessary—He would remain their Lord—but to move them from following His external presence to knowing His internal presence through the Spirit.

Jesus’ departure revealed the success of His disciple-making. He could entrust the mission to ordinary people because He was entrusting those people to the Holy Spirit. Genuine leadership does not build a ministry around the leader’s constant presence. It prepares people to hear God, obey God, and carry the mission forward through the power of the Spirit.
