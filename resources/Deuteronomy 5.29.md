## Commentary
Israel has just heard the voice of God speak the Ten Commandments directly to them — and they're terrified. The mountain is on fire. There's thunder, lightning, thick darkness. And God's voice is coming out of all of it.

So the people turn to Moses and say, "You go talk to Him. We'll listen to whatever you say, but if we hear His voice again, we're going to die." And here's what's striking: God doesn't rebuke them for being afraid. He actually affirms their fear. He says to Moses, "They are right in what they have spoken." 

Their fear was appropriate. The holiness of God is not a theological concept to be studied in a classroom — it's an eternal flame that reveals how far we actually are from Him.

But then God says something heartbreaking. He says, "Oh that they had such a heart in them, that they would fear Me and keep all My commandments always, that it may be well with them and with their sons forever!" Do you hear the longing in that? God wanted more than their fear. He wanted their hearts. He wanted a people who would walk with Him, not just tremble before Him.

This is the Old Covenant in a snapshot. God gives the law. The people agree to obey. But there's a problem — they don't have the capacity to do what they're promising. The law exposes the sin, but it can't remove it (==Romans 3:20==). It can tell you what's wrong, but it cant fix what's broken. Israel needed more than commands written on stone tablets. They needed new hearts.

And that's exactly what Jesus came to give us.
The cross didn't just forgive the failure to keep the law — it ended the entire system that required our performance to access God. When you were baptized into Christ, you were immersed into His death (==Romans 6:3-4==). The old you — the one born under the law, the one defined by the flesh, the one who could never measure up — that person was crucified with Him. And when He was raised, you were raised as a new creation with a new heart and a new capacity to actually walk in obedience — not out of terror, but out of love (==Ezekiel 36:26-27==, ==2 Corinthians 5:17==).

Israel heard God's voice and said, "Stay back there, or we'll die." But now, because of the cross, we can come boldly into His presence (==Hebrews 4:16==). The veil has been torn. The fire that once consumed is now the Spirit who indwells. What Deuteronomy 5 reveals is our need. What the gospel reveals is God's answer.

## Application
Are you still relating to God from the posture of Mt. Sinai — trying to keep enough rules to stay in His good graces — or have you crossed over into the New Covenant, where obedience flows from union, not obligation?
