## Commentary
Judgement here is not simply a legal/courtroom term, it means to rule. The dominion God gave to man to bring into order that which is not reflecting the image of God is still intended to belong to man. Jesus embodies this. He judges (rules) perfectly *and* brings salvation, peace, deliverance...especially to the persecuted and oppressed (which is what Jesus did for the lame man in the beginning of the chapter which then led to this statement in v27). Jesus executes judgement on the lame man's infirmity, bringing the man's health back into order. Rather than resembling a courtroom, God's judgement looks like Jesus saying "Rise, take up your bed and walk" (John 5:8). "For judgement I have come into the world, that those who do not see may see" (John 9:39). 

The great judges in Israel's history are the deliverers, helpers, saviors raised up by God to obtain justice for Israel in the face of their enemies/oppressors and, in turn, bring salvation, rest, and peace to the land (see Judges 3:9)

God's judgement is motivated by love and its goal is salvation.
