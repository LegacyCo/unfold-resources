## Commentary
“The soul of a lazy man desires, and has nothing; but the soul of the diligent shall be made rich.”

Both souls in this proverb desire. The difference is not in the wanting; it is in what the wanting becomes. The lazy man's desire stays desire—and has nothing. You can tell yourself and everyone around you that you want more of God, but until you put action to the desire, you will never have what you desire. Desire without seeking is just curiosity, and curiosity never accomplished anything. The promise of Scripture is attached to the verb: “*seek*, and you will find” (==Matthew 7:7==). Desire is a gift, but at some point desire must give way to intentional seeking—“One thing I have desired of the LORD, *that will I seek*” (==Psalm 27:4==).

And where there is fire, there will be evidence. Real seeking touches everything: how you spend your time, your money, your mental capacity; what you listen to; who your friends are. How much you desire of God will be seen in how much you seek Him. One guardrail here: this is not working to earn something from Him—not love, approval, acceptance, or rank in the kingdom. Servants work for a wage; sons receive an inheritance, and He Himself is the inheritance (==Lamentations 3:24==). The diligence this proverb rewards is the diligence of a son pursuing his Father, not an employee earning his keep. But Scripture leaves room for human responsibility: it is the hungry who are fed, and the ones who seek who find. If you are not satisfied in your walk with Jesus, there are only two options to consider: either He is not who He says He is, or you have not sought Him with your whole heart. The only thing holding you back from more of God is you.

## Application
Take an honest inventory of whether your seeking matches your desire. Look at the actual evidence—your calendar, your spending, your inputs, your friends—and ask what they say about what you really want. Then build your seeking on three certainties: He is kind, and a rewarder of those who seek Him (==Hebrews 11:6==); He loves you with the same love He has for Jesus (==John 17:23==); and you are a son or daughter, so what He gives is an inheritance, not a wage. From that place of knowing who He is and who you are, seek Him with your whole heart. 

And if you don't desire Him the way you once did, or the way you know you are capable of, ask Him for hunger—desperation is something He is glad to give.
