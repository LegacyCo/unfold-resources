## Commentary
Paul’s language in 1 Corinthians about the temple of the Holy Spirit is often reduced to something merely private and personal, as if his only concern were individual morality in isolation. That is too small. Paul is certainly speaking to individuals, but he is speaking to them as members of a people, a body, a house. The temple image is corporate. It belongs to the reality that God is forming for Himself a dwelling place in the earth through a redeemed community.

Temples are not built with one stone. A house is not a house because of a single brick. It becomes a house because many parts are joined together in right relation. So when Paul says, “your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit,” and, “you are God’s temple,” he is not severing personal holiness from the life of the church. He is showing that what we do in our bodies affects something larger than ourselves. Our bodies are not private property. They belong to the Lord, and they exist within a fellowship that also belongs to the Lord.

This is especially important in 1 Corinthians because Paul is addressing sins that were tearing at the fabric of the church—sexual immorality, division, pride, misuse of freedom, dishonor at the Lord’s table. These were not merely personal struggles with private consequences. They were violations of the holiness of God’s dwelling. They brought disorder into His house. Sin is never just individual. It always has a corporate echo. It always touches the fellowship. 

That's the weight of Paul's use of temple language. God does not merely save isolated persons; He builds a habitation. He is after a people among whom He can dwell, a house that reflects His character, His order, His purity, and His glory. Holiness is not just about “me and Jesus.” Holiness is about whether the house is fit for His presence. 

## Application
Paul shatters the lie of modern Christianity—that sin is private, discipleship is private, purity is private, worship is private. If I belong to Christ, and if the Spirit dwells in His people, then my choices are never mine alone. My obedience strengthens the house. My compromise weakens it. My purity honors the presence of God in the midst of His people. My sin brings contradiction into the very place where His holiness is meant to be seen.

So I cannot think individualistically about consecration. There's no such thing as private compromise in a holy temple. The entire house feels it. The presence of God is either honored or misrepresented by the way I live.

Live with an awareness of the whole house.
