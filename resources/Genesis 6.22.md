## Commentary
This sentence is easy to pass over in the midst of the larger story, but it may be one of the most staggering descriptions of obedience in Scripture.

Noah did *all* that God commanded him.

He did not obey partially. He did not revise the instructions. He did not wait until the command made sense to the people around him. He did not require public affirmation before he began. He heard the word of the Lord, and he moved.

Matthew Henry wrote, “The grace of God crowns the obedient.” That's not saying obedience earns grace, but because grace produces obedience. Noah had already found grace in the eyes of Yahweh. Now that grace was taking form in sawdust, labor, endurance, and faithfulness. The grace that rested on Noah became obedience through Noah.

This is what it means to walk with God. Those who walk with Him listen to Him. And those who listen to Him long to obey Him. David gives language to this kind of heart in Psalm 119: “Blessed are the blameless in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord… Oh that my ways may be steadfast in keeping Your statutes!” (Psalm 119:1, 5).

Noah’s obedience would cost him time, energy, reputation, comfort, and likely years of his life. He was building something no one had ever seen, in preparation for something no one around him believed was coming.

That is the nature of faith.

Hebrews 11:7 says, “By faith Noah, being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen, in reverent fear constructed an ark for the saving of his household.”

Noah obeyed God concerning things “as yet unseen.” That means his obedience had no visible evidence to lean on. There was no flood on the horizon or natural explanation that made his labor seem reasonable. All he had was the word of God.

And that was enough.

Had Noah consulted flesh and blood, he likely would have been talked out of obedience. People would have called him foolish. Extreme. Irrational. But Noah’s obedience was not up for committee review.

There are moments when obedience to God will make you look foolish to people who cannot see what God has spoken.

Faith does not need forty-seven confirmations when God has already spoken clearly. Faith does not make obedience dependent on consensus. Faith moves. Faith builds. Faith says, “This may cost me everything, and it may look ridiculous to everyone around me, but if God has spoken, then obedience is the only sane response.”

Noah did all that God commanded him.

Praise God he did.
