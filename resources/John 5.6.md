## Commentary
“When Jesus saw him lying there, and knew that he already had been in that condition a long time, He said to him, ‘Do you want to be made well?’”

Bethesda was a sea of need. A great multitude of sick people lay around the pool—blind, lame, paralyzed—waiting for the stirring of the water (==John 5:3==). Jesus walked into that multitude with the resources to heal every one of them. He healed one. Why? Because He did not see a need and assume the response. He was moved by what He saw the Father doing: “the Son can do nothing of Himself, but what He sees the Father do” (==John 5:19==). Jesus was never moved by need; He was moved by vision—and that made Him a man at rest in the middle of more need than any of His people will ever face. Everywhere He went He was bumping up against the demon-possessed, the sick, the blind, the leprous, and He faithfully walked in the lane the Father gave Him without hurry and without guilt.

Need is one of the greatest motivators there is. It provokes action, draws attention to the loudest crisis of the moment, and always justifies itself—and need will always war against your rest. In a sea of need, which need do you serve and which do you pass by? Without vision, a servant of God becomes reactionary, adopting whatever crisis is nearest as a calling and asking God to bless it. Vision is what guides a person through the sea of need. And a man with vision is a man at rest—which is exactly what a world in chaos is looking for. The demoniac with a legion inside him ran to Jesus (==Mark 5:6==), because he knew the answer to the chaos inside of him was the rest inside of Jesus. The answer for a world in chaos is a man at rest.

## Application
Examine what moves you. When you feel the pull to act, ask whether you are responding to what the Father is doing or reacting to the loudest need in view—the two can point in different directions, as they did at Bethesda. Practice the vertical gaze before the horizontal response: seek what He is doing first, and let action be the consequence of what you see rather than the pressure of what you feel. The needs around you are real; so is the lane the Father has given you.
