## Commentary
Paul had been entrusted with the gospel, sent to the nations, used to establish churches, and given revelation that would shape the faith of generations. Yet when he described his deepest ambition, he did not say, “That I may accomplish more,” but, “That I may know Him.”

This was not the desire of a young believer who had not yet encountered Christ. Paul had seen the risen Lord and walked with Him for years. Still, the great pursuit of his life remained the knowledge of Jesus (==Jeremiah 9:24==).

We often assume that knowing God is preparation for our real assignment. We seek Him until we receive direction, strength, or opportunity, and then move on to the work. Paul understood that knowing Christ was not preparation for something greater. It was the greatest thing (==John 17:3==).

Everything else flowed from this pursuit. Paul could endure suffering because he knew the One for whom he suffered. He could relinquish reputation because he had found surpassing worth in Christ. His ministry carried the life of Jesus because his heart was continually reaching toward deeper participation in Him.

The measure of a life is not simply what it accomplishes in the name of Christ, but how fully it comes to know Christ. Our works will eventually be completed, but the knowledge of Him will remain the eternal occupation of His people (see ==2 Peter 3:18==).
