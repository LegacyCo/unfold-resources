## Commentary
“This girl followed Paul and us, and cried out, saying, ‘These men are the servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to us the way of salvation.’”

Every word the slave girl said was accurate. Paul and his companions were servants of the Most High God, and they were proclaiming the way of salvation. Yet the spirit behind her words was demonic—a spirit of divination (==Acts 16:16==)—and after many days Paul, greatly annoyed, turned and cast it out (==Acts 16:18==). True content from a wrong source, generating attention and confusion rather than revealing Christ.

Accuracy is not the test of a prophetic word. “The testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy” (==Revelation 19:10==)—the animating spirit of everything genuinely prophetic is the unveiling of Christ. A word can be dramatic, supernatural, even correct, and still fail that test. Scripture gives us three examples. Here in Philippi: accurate words, demonic source. In ==Matthew 7:22-23==: “Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in Your name?”—Jesus doesn’t dispute that they prophesied; He says “I never knew you.” Prophecy without relationship. And in ==Matthew 24:24==: false prophets who produce great signs and wonders—not false because their signs fail, but because the spirit behind them is antichrist. In all three cases, the things that impress a crowd—accuracy, supernatural information, results—are present, and Jesus is either absent or distorted.

So the new covenant test for a prophetic word is not only “did it come to pass?” Deuteronomy asked that question, and it's still an important one (==Deuteronomy 18:22==)—but a word can pass the accuracy test and fail the testimony test. Accuracy measures the information; only the testimony of Jesus verifies the source. When you weigh a word—one you’ve received or one you’re about to give—ask the deeper question: does this word reveal a measure of Jesus? Does it bring the hearer closer to Him, or does it merely generate attention? The girl in Philippi drew a crowd with true words for days, and nobody was drawn to Jesus by them. Paul silenced a technically accurate word because prophecy that doesn’t testify of Jesus isn’t prophecy at all.
