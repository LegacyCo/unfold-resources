## Commentary
Isaiah gives us a sobering picture of divine justice: those who oppose God’s people will be handed over to themselves. “I will feed those who oppress you with their own flesh…they shall be drunk with their own blood.” This is what happens when a life turns inward and feeds on itself.

Sin has a trajectory. Left unchecked, it collapses inward. It consumes its own strength, its own desires, its own identity. What began as rebellion against God ends as self-devouring. The oppressor doesn’t just harm others—they eventually become the object of their own destruction. God’s judgment, in part, is giving people over to what they’ve chosen (see Romans 1:24-28). A life cut off from Him has nothing left to draw from but itself.

And into that depravity, Jesus steps in and offers a life radically different. He turns humanity from feeding on its own brokenness and offers His own flesh. His own blood. 

Humanity will “eat” and “drink” from somewhere. We will either draw life from Christ or attempt to sustain ourselves from within. One leads to life. The other, inevitably, leads to decay.

We all take communion, but only those who receive Christ by faith partake unto life. The table of the Lord is a declaration of dependence. To receive His body and blood is to say: _I will not live off myself anymore. I will not draw from my own strength, my own righteousness, my own resources. I receive life from Another._
