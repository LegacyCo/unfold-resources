## Commentary
“For what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, on account of sin: He condemned sin *in the flesh*.”

Notice where sin got condemned: in the flesh. Not from a distance. Not by decree from heaven. Sin reigned in the realm of flesh—that’s where it entered through Adam, and that’s where it held humanity captive (==Romans 5:12==). And in the order God established at creation, that’s where it had to be defeated. God doesn’t break His own rules. So the Son of God did the unthinkable: He came *into* the realm where sin ruled—“in the likeness of sinful flesh”—and took the fight to sin on its own ground. He looked sin, death, and the devil in the eye *as a Man*, and destroyed their power from the inside (==Hebrews 2:14==).

God didn’t send salvation. Jesus *brought* it. He didn’t mail deliverance into enemy territory; He walked in carrying it. He lived a whole life as a son of Adam—in the flesh, tempted in all points, yet without sin (==Hebrews 4:15==)—and then offered that sinless life so that sin would be condemned in the very realm where it had always won. Which means there now stands a Man bridging the two realms—like an emergency exit cut through the wall between the kingdom of the flesh and the kingdom of the Spirit. He could only become that door by coming into our realm.

And verse 4 tells us why He did it: “that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled *in us*, who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.” Every son and daughter of Adam can now find the exit—joining ourselves to Christ, receiving His death to the flesh as our own, His burial as our own, and His resurrection by the Spirit as our own (==Romans 6:4-5==). The law demanded from the outside what flesh could never produce. The Spirit now fulfills from the inside what the law could only demand. Sin was condemned in the flesh so that you would never again be condemned in it: “there is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus” (==Romans 8:1==).
