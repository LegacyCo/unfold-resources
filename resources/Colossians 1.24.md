## Commentary
“I now rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up in my flesh what is lacking in the afflictions of Christ, for the sake of His body, which is the church.”

Lacking? What could possibly be *lacking* in what Jesus suffered on the cross? Nothing. His death was complete and effective—it can never be added to or repeated (==Hebrews 10:14==). So what is Paul saying?

Hold the two halves apart and the verse opens up. The death of Christ is finished; the afflictions of His *Body* are not. When Jesus died and rose, He inaugurated a kingdom that spreads like leaven—buried in the dough, slowly filling everything with itself (==Matthew 13:33==). And then He handed the completion of that process to His church. The task changed hands, but the *way* the kingdom comes never changed: it still comes through a people of the cross. So when the Body of Christ suffers as a result of obediently following the King—when it forgives, serves, bleeds, and refuses to retaliate—it fills up what is still necessary for the leaven to fill the earth. That’s why Paul could rejoice in his sufferings without a trace of martyr complex (==Philippians 3:10==). He understood his trial was not just about him. It was about a kingdom that has come and is still coming.

The opposite is also true, and it should sober us: if Jesus People pursue the same lifestyle, in the same ways, as the world around them, the kingdom loses its witness. The church is God’s testimony in the earth, and the God we represent is the God who suffers. We can’t afford to misrepresent Him.

Now, we may never die physically for this kingdom—but all of us are called to die for it. Ever tried to actually forgive someone who really hurt you? It feels like a death—to your sense of justice, to your right to defend yourself. Humility, meekness, serving, honesty, hospitality, radical generosity: every Christ-like quality is costly, and choosing the Jesus way of life will leave a mark on your soul. Much of the American Dream is built around stockpiling resources that protect us from the very trials that would make us more like Jesus. But power is perfected in weakness (==2 Corinthians 12:9==)—and every costly obedience, however small it looks, is a thread in the kingdom filling the earth. Your cross-shaped choices are never wasted. They're how the leaven spreads.
