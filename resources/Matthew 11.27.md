## Commentary
This is one of the most significant statements Jesus makes concerning His identity and His relationship with the Father. “All things have been delivered to Me by My Father, and no one knows the Son except the Father. Nor does anyone know the Father except the Son, and the one to whom the Son wills to reveal Him.”

Consider the magnitude of what Jesus is claiming. Throughout Israel's history, God revealed Himself through prophets, priests, visions, dreams, and the Scriptures. Men like Moses, Abraham, and David experienced extraordinary encounters with God, yet their knowledge of Him remained partial. But Jesus makes a claim that places Him in an entirely different category. He declares that no one truly knows the Father except the Son, and no one truly knows the Son except the Father.

The Greek word translated "knows" is *epiginōskō*, which carries the sense of recognizing or knowing fully by way of the senses and experience. Jesus is describing a depth of mutual knowledge between Himself and the Father that is unique to their relationship. He is not claiming to be another prophet with a greater revelation of God. He is claiming to possess a knowledge of God that belongs uniquely to the Son by way of their fellowship together.

And then He makes an equally remarkable statement: the Father is known by those to whom the Son chooses to reveal Him. Jesus is the One through whom God makes Himself known. John expresses this same truth when he writes, “No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, He has declared Him” (==John 1:18==). Later, when Philip asks Jesus to show them the Father, Jesus responds, “He who has seen Me has seen the Father” (==John 14:9==).

This is fundamental to understanding the revelation of God in Christ. We do not begin with our assumptions about what God must be like and then attempt to make Jesus fit those assumptions. We begin with Jesus, because He is the definitive revelation of the Father's nature and character. As ==Hebrews 1:3== explains, He is “the brightness of His glory and the express image of His person.”

There is also something remarkable about the context of this statement. Just two verses earlier, Jesus thanks the Father for hiding these things from the wise and prudent while revealing them to little children (==Matthew 11:25==). The knowledge of God is not something we achieve through human effort. It is something given to us through revelation. This is not a dismissal of studying Scripture or pursuing understanding, but a recognition that knowing God ultimately depends upon God making Himself known. As ==Psalm 119:130== says, "The entrance of Your words gives light; it gives understanding to the simple."

We have been invited into the knowledge of the Father through the Son, who has come to reveal Him. If we want to know what the Father is like, we look to Jesus. If we want to understand how the Father feels about sinners, the broken, the rejected, and the weary, we look to Jesus.
