## Commentary
“For I am the LORD. I speak, and the word which I speak will come to pass; it will no more be postponed; for in your days, O rebellious house, I will say the word and perform it,” says the Lord GOD.

God is answering two sayings that were going around Israel. Both were ways the people were ignoring His word.

The first was a proverb: “The days are prolonged, and every vision fails” (==Ezekiel 12:22==). In other words, the prophets keep warning us and nothing ever happens. There was some history behind that. Micah had prophesied Jerusalem's destruction more than a century earlier (==Jeremiah 26:18==). Jeremiah had been preaching judgment for over thirty years. But the city was still standing. People concluded the warnings were hollow.

The second saying sounded more respectful: “The vision that he sees is for many days from now, and he prophesies of times far off” (==Ezekiel 12:27==). These people didn't call Ezekiel a liar. They agreed it would happen someday. They just didn't think it had anything to do with them.

God gives both groups the same answer. “The days are at hand” (==Ezekiel 12:23==). “In your days, O rebellious house, I will say the word and perform it.” “None of My words will be postponed any more” (==Ezekiel 12:28==). Within a few years of this prophecy Jerusalem was under siege, and in 586 BC it was burned.

What Israel had read as God failing to act was actually God being patient. Scripture says people often make that mistake. “Because the sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil” (==Ecclesiastes 8:11==). Peter says scoffers in the last days will ask, “Where is the promise of His coming?” because everything seems to continue as it always has. His answer is, “The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is longsuffering toward us, not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance” (==2 Peter 3:4==; ==2 Peter 3:9==). The delay is mercy, and it's meant to be used for repentance. 

The second saying is the one religious people are more likely to use. In Jesus' parable, the servant who starts mistreating the others says in his heart, “My master is delaying his coming” (==Matthew 24:48==). He believes the master is coming back. He has only decided it won't be soon. Felix did the same thing with Paul. He heard about “righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come,” was afraid, and said, “Go away for now; when I have a convenient time I will call for you” (==Acts 24:25==). As far as Scripture tells us, the convenient time never came.

The God who doesn't put off judgment forever doesn't put off what He has promised His people either. “Though it tarries, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry” (==Habakkuk 2:3==).

## Word Study
**Postponed** is the Hebrew *mashak* (מָשַׁךְ)—to draw, to pull, to stretch something out. When it's used of time, it means to drag out or prolong. It's the word in “Hope deferred makes the heart sick” (==Proverbs 13:12==). It's also used for God drawing people to Himself: “with lovingkindness I have drawn you” (==Jeremiah 31:3==). Summary: God had been stretching out the time before judgment, and Israel took that merciful delay to mean it would never come. Here He says the stretching has an end.

## Application
“Someday” is the most common way believers avoid obeying God. We agree with what He says but fail see to see the urgency of "today." Is there something you already know God has told you to do that you've been putting off? It might be a sin to confess, a person to forgive, a conversation to have, or a step of obedience you keep scheduling for later. Do it today. “Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation” (==2 Corinthians 6:2==).
