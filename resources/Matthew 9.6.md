## Commentary
“But that you may know that the Son of Man has power on earth to forgive sins…”

The word translated “power” is _exousia_ (ἐξουσία). It means far more than raw strength or ability. It speaks of authority, right, jurisdiction, and freedom of action—the legitimate right to act without needing permission from another. The word can carry the idea of an _unrestricted possibility of action_, or the full authority to do what one has determined to do.

The scribes have just accused Jesus of blasphemy because He told the paralytic, “Your sins are forgiven you” (==Matthew 9:2–3==). Their reasoning was understandable: forgiveness ultimately belongs to God. Sin is first and finally an offense against Him, and therefore no ordinary man can simply announce its removal.

Jesus does not back away from their conclusion about the seriousness of His claim. Instead, He proves that He possesses the authority they believe belongs to God alone.

“But that you may know that the Son of Man has _exousia_ on earth to forgive sins…”

The healing is the visible evidence of an invisible authority. Anyone could pronounce the words, “Your sins are forgiven,” because forgiveness cannot be seen with the eyes. So Jesus does something they _can_ see in order to demonstrate the reality of something they cannot see:

“Arise, take up your bed, and go to your house” (==Matthew 9:6==).

And the man rises.

The miracle is Jesus’ public demonstration that His word carries heaven’s authority. The same voice that commands crippled limbs to function has authority to command guilt to leave.

There is also something important in the phrase _“on earth.”_ The authority of God to forgive sins is no longer a distant reality associated with heaven, temple, sacrifice, or priesthood. In Jesus, that authority has stepped into the human world. Forgiveness is standing in the room with the sinner.

This is part of what Jesus means by calling Himself “the Son of Man.” The title reaches back to ==Daniel 7:13–14==, where “One like the Son of Man” approaches the Ancient of Days and is given dominion, glory, and a kingdom:

“Then to Him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom…”

The Greek Old Testament uses _exousia_ language in this passage to describe the dominion given to this coming figure. Jesus is announcing that the authority Daniel saw has arrived.

The first display of the Son of Man’s authority is not destruction but forgiveness. Jesus possesses unrestricted authority, and He uses it to remove sin, restore a broken man, and send him home whole.

This becomes one of the great revelations of Jesus throughout the Gospels. He teaches with authority (==Matthew 7:29==). He has authority over sickness (==Matthew 8:8–13==). He commands winds and waves (==Matthew 8:26–27==). Demons obey Him (==Matthew 8:28–32==). And here He reveals that His authority reaches deeper still: He has authority over the thing beneath all human brokenness—sin itself.

And what does Jesus choose to do with His authority? He uses it to forgive, heal, restore, and set people free.
