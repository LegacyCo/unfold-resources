## Commentary
“But you, when you fast, anoint your head and wash your face, so that you do not appear to men to be fasting, but to your Father who is in the secret place; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly.”

The hypocrites disfigured their faces so their fasting would be noticed by people—and Jesus says they received their reward in full: the noticing was the payment, and there is nothing further owed (==Matthew 6:16==). His instruction runs the opposite direction. Anoint your head, wash your face—look like a man on an ordinary day—so that the fast is transacted entirely between you and “your Father who is in the secret place.” Fasting belongs to the same secret economy as giving and prayer in this chapter (==Matthew 6:4==; ==Matthew 6:6==): what is done for an audience is spent; what is done for the Father is rewarded. This is also the only instruction Jesus gives about *how* to fast. Everything else is wisdom drawn from Scripture and the church's experience.

## Application
Four practicals for the fast itself. First, set the length before you start: Esther's three days (==Esther 4:16==), Daniel's three weeks (==Daniel 10:2-3==), Jesus' forty days (==Matthew 4:2==)—every biblical fast has an end, and a fast without a defined end becomes a negotiation with yourself that will finish earlier than it needs to. Second, give the fast to Him, not just the meal: skipping food and filling the space with your phone is dieting with a Bible verse attached—dedicate the hours to the Lord. Third, choose the kind of fast honestly: a full fast, a partial fast like Daniel's “no tasty food, no meat or wine” (==Daniel 10:3==), a liquid fast, a sunup-to-sundown fast—choose what fits your body and your season, and don't compare yourself with others. Fourth, expect the flesh to complain: the temptation to quit, to cheat, or to be proud is normal, and none of it disqualifies you. If you break the fast early, there is grace. God is not keeping score.
