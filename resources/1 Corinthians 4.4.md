## Commentary
Paul says something pretty shocking here in v3-4, "It is of little importance that I would be evaluated/judged/examined by you...for I don't even evaluate/judge/examine myself." 

For one thing, this is a staggering statement on its own. However, it also seems to contradict other passages where Paul himself, and Scripture as a whole, encourages us to examine ourselves (For example: 1 Corinthians 11:28 - "But let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of the bread and drink of the cup" and 2 Corinthians 13:5 - "Examine yourselves as to whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not know yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you?—unless indeed you are disqualified.").

So what is Paul saying here in 1 Corinthians 4?

It's not that Paul is free from examination/testing. Not at all. The point is Paul knows where his true evaluation comes from. The Corinthian's evaluation of Paul is biased. Paul's own evaluation of himself is biased. However, the Lord's judgment is true and perfect, unbiased and actual. That's the only judgment that matters to the mature saint. Jesus is the truth (John 14:6). His Spirit is the Spirit of truth (John 16:13). Paul's concern is not what others, or even himself, determine, but what is actually true. Paul is determined to know what is factual about his walk with Jesus and love towards those around him.

Indeed, Paul writes this to the Thessalonians, "Just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the gospel, so we speak, not intending to please people, but to please God, who examines our hearts" (1 Thessalonians 2:4). Similarly, John writes this in 1 John 3:19-20, "We will know by this that we are of the truth, and will set our heart at ease before Him, that if our heart condemns us, that God is greater than our heart, and He knows all things."

## Application
Most people live their lives getting pulled in two directions—by what others think of them and by what they think of themselves. One inflates you. The other condemns you. Neither tells the truth.

Paul refuses both. He’s not moved by human opinion. And he’s not paralyzed by self-analysis. He’s anchored in the only evaluation that matters.

If you can do the same, it frees you from living for inferior approval. You don’t have to build your identity on affirmation or defend yourself against criticism. People are inconsistent. Their judgments shift with mood, preference (bias), and perception.

It also frees you from being trapped inside your own head. Your self-assessment isn’t reliable either. You will either justify what should be confronted or fixate on what God has already dealt with.

The apostolic teaching of Scripture is this: What does God say about me? This requires maturity. An immature son rejects the correction of the father. It would rather be affirmed than corrected. It filters out anything that confronts it. Like a spoiled child, it hears discipline as rejection.

But the mature son will receive the discipline of the father. He knows even the father's discipline is an extension of his love. He can receive correction without being crushed by it. He can be confronted without becoming defensive. Because he knows the character of the father behind the correction.

That means when God speaks—whether it affirms or corrects—don’t argue, deflect, or explain it away. Receive it as a true evaluation of who you currently are.

Don’t live under the instability of the opinions of those around you. And don’t live under the bias of your own. Live before the One who sees clearly, speaks truthfully, and loves you enough to tell you the truth. That’s the place of true freedom.
