## Commentary
“I was alive once without the law, but when the commandment came, sin revived and I died.”

Scholars have long discussed who the “I” in this passage is, and the main readings are worth knowing. The oldest takes it as straightforward autobiography—Paul describing his own encounter with the law, a reading Augustine wrestled with and commentators like C. E. B. Cranfield have defended in various forms. Others hear Adam's story being retold in the first person: Adam was truly “alive once without the law,” one commandment came to him in the garden, sin seized its opportunity through that very commandment, deceived him, and he died—and Paul's language in verse 11, “sin… deceived me, and by it killed me,” echoes Eve's words almost exactly, “the serpent deceived me” (==Genesis 3:13==). James Dunn is among those who hear these Adamic overtones. A third reading, argued by Douglas Moo and N. T. Wright in different ways, hears Israel's story: Israel was “alive” before Sinai, the commandment came at the mountain, and sin revived immediately—within weeks the golden calf stood at the foot of the mountain where the law had just been given (==Exodus 32:1-6==). On this reading Paul speaks as a son of Israel, telling his people's history from the inside.

These readings are not as far apart as they first appear, and that may be the point of Paul's word choice. Adam's story, Israel's story, and Paul's story are similar: the commandment arrives, sin seizes it, and death follows. Paul is telling a story that belongs to Adam, to Israel, and to himself all at once—and anyone who reads it honestly will recognize that it belongs to them too. A man can feel perfectly healthy right up to the moment the scan comes back. He walks into the doctor's office alive, as far as he knows, and walks out dying—though nothing in his body changed between those two moments. The scan didn't give him the disease; it revealed what was already there. That's what the commandment does—Paul calls the law's work “the ministry of death” (==2 Corinthians 3:7==), and Scripture confines everyone under sin so that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given (==Galatians 3:22-24==). The death Paul died in this verse was a severe mercy. A man who has never died under the law's diagnosis still believes his condition can be managed with self-improvement; a man who *has* died is finally ready for what only God can do with the dead—make them alive (==Ephesians 2:5==).
