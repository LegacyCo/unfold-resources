## Commentary
This is from my booklet *Righteous, Holy, & Whole*: Contrary to what most of us think, you can't grow in righteousness. _Righteous_ is something you either are or are not. And if you are in Christ, then the righteousness of God has been transferred to you.

We can grow in our understanding of righteousness...and we should. But you can't become more of something that God says you already are. Righteousness is not a process, it’s a transfer. God’s righteousness is transferred to us on the basis of faith.

For example, Paul says about Abraham in Romans 4:3; "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness."

Faith was the gate that opened up the transfer of righteousness from God to Abraham.

I’ll give you an example; if a billionaire tells you, “I'm going to give you a million dollars, give me your banking information," what are you expecting to see when you look in your account? 

A million dollars.

If you found that the billionaire had only transferred $100, you would be a little disappointed. So you get a hold of the billionaire and you say, “Thank you for the $100, but you said you would give me $1million.”

To which the billionaire responds, “O, I’m giving you a million dollars. See, I’m giving you $100 every day for the rest of your life. And at the end of your life you will have $1million." 

That's how most of us think of righteousness. We think we received the *seed* of righteousness which then grows over a lifetime into the *oak* of righteousness as we successfully walk out a process theologians call sanctification. That may be an accurate picture of growing in *maturity*, which is very important, but that is not how righteousness works. 
It was faith that put righteousness in Abraham's account in one transaction, not a succession of days where he did more good stuff than bad stuff.
