## Commentary
This is the heartbeat of Proverbs. If we are to become fully human—people who truly reflect the image and character of God as He intended—we must become wise. Wisdom is not an optional virtue for a select few. It is central to the life God intended for every one of His children.

Throughout Proverbs, wisdom is personified as a woman calling aloud in the streets, inviting anyone who will listen to come and learn her ways (see ==Proverbs 1:20-23==, ==Proverbs 8:1-11==, ==Proverbs 8:32-36==, ==Proverbs 9:1-6==). The invitation is freely given, but it still requires a *response*. Wisdom is available to all, yet she is only possessed by those who pursue her.

That's why Solomon doesn't just admire wisdom; he commands us to _get_ wisdom. The language is intentional. Wisdom does not simply appear with age, experience, the right genetics, or education. Many grow older without growing wiser. Wisdom must be sought, valued, and pursued. It is cultivated through humility, obedience, meditation on God's Word, and a life that remains teachable.

Notice also that Solomon pairs wisdom with understanding. Wisdom is not just knowing what is true; it is seeing life from God's perspective and living accordingly. Understanding enables us to discern what God is doing, while wisdom teaches us how to respond.

The invitation of Proverbs is therefore an invitation into the life of God Himself. Every time we choose His perspective over our own, every time we submit our thinking to His Word, and every time we respond to Wisdom's call, we become a little more like the One in whose image we were created.
