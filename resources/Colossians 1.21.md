## Commentary
“And you, who once were alienated and enemies in your mind by wicked works, yet now He has reconciled.”

Look at where Paul locates the alienation: *in your mind*. Not in God’s heart—in Adam’s head, and in every head descended from his. Trace it back to the garden. When Adam sinned, something broke inside him. For the first time he felt fear, shame, and anxiety—and he took those awful things and projected them onto God, creating a mythological god fashioned in the image of his own brokenness. Then he hid from the god he had invented (==Genesis 3:8-10==). But sin didn’t change God—“I am the LORD, I do not change” (==Malachi 3:6==). Sin changed *Adam*. And we inherited his fallen mind: a lens that reads every move God makes through brokenness, so that when the Father says “I love you exactly as I love Jesus” (==John 17:23==), we hear “I love you when you’re doing more good than bad.”

On the cross, Jesus entered the full depth of Adam’s sin—which meant He also took on Adam’s alienated mind, the estrangement itself, the crushing sense of a Father turned away. He felt the full weight of the lie: “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?” (==Matthew 27:46==). But that was not His last word, because Jesus did what Adam never did—He refused to believe the lie about His Father. The struggle resolves in His final breath: “*Father*, into Your hands I commit My spirit” (==Luke 23:46==). Not to an absent judge—into hands He knew were still there. He stepped into Adam’s deception about who the Father is and, by refusing it, broke its power forever. The Father never turned away; “God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself” (==2 Corinthians 5:19==). A.W. Tozer put it plainly in *The Radical Cross*: “We have not been redeemed by one person of the Trinity putting Himself against another, but by the three Persons of the Godhead working in glorious harmony.”

So the reconciliation this verse announces isn’t God coming around on you—it’s your mind coming home to God as He actually is. Jesus carried Adam’s broken ideas about the Father to the grave and left them there. If you're still seeing God through fear, distance, and suspicion, you're carrying something the blood already cleansed you from. It's time to know Abba as He truly is.
