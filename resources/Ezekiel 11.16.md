## Commentary
The people who remained in Jerusalem believed that those carried into exile were the ones God had rejected. They essentially tell the exiles that they have been removed from the Lord and that the land now belongs to those who remain. They assumed that because they still possessed the land and had access to the sanctuary, they were the ones enjoying God's favor.

But God sets them straight. Although He acknowledges that He was the one who scattered His people among the nations, He says: “Yet I shall be a little sanctuary for them in the countries where they have gone.”

The Hebrew expression translated “a little sanctuary” is *miqdash* *me'at*. It can be understood as either a small sanctuary or a sanctuary for a little while, referring to God's presence with His people during their exile. The people had been removed from the place where God had chosen to establish His dwelling, but they had not been removed from God Himself. The Lord would personally become their sanctuary in a foreign land. 

This is particularly significant within the larger context of Ezekiel's vision. The glory of the Lord is departing from the temple in Jerusalem (==Ezekiel 10:18–19==; ==Ezekiel 11:22–23==), yet that same glory had already appeared to Ezekiel among the exiles in Babylon (==Ezekiel 1:1–28==). The people in Jerusalem possessed the temple but were losing the presence of God, while those who had lost access to the temple were discovering that God had not abandoned them.

Exile was not an unfortunate interruption of Israel's history. It was the consequence of their disobedience, just as God had warned through Moses (==Deuteronomy 28:64==). Yet even in judgment, God remained committed to His people. In ==Leviticus 26:44==, the Lord had promised that even when Israel was scattered among the nations, He would not utterly reject them or break His covenant with them.

God's discipline was real, but His discipline did not mean the cancellation of His promises.

And we must be careful not to interpret God's promise to become their sanctuary in exile as though He were abandoning His purposes for Israel or the land. The very next verses promise that He will gather them from the nations, restore them to their land, and give them a new heart and spirit (==Ezekiel 11:17–20==). His presence with them in exile was not a replacement for those promises but an expression of His faithfulness to fulfill them.

## Application
God's people may find themselves in circumstances they never would have chosen, including circumstances that are the consequence of their own failures. Yet even there, the Lord can be their sanctuary. We should never confuse the loss of familiar places, circumstances, or even privileges with the loss of God's presence. The God who disciplines His people is also the God who meets them in the place of their discipline.
