## Commentary
"And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, returned, and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at His feet, giving Him thanks."

Ten lepers were healed. One came back. And in that gap—between the ten and the one—you can see the difference between the kingdom *demonstrated* and the kingdom *established*.

The kingdom demonstrated is Jesus healing ten lepers. The kingdom established is one leper returning and falling on his face before the King. The kingdom demonstrated is a legion of demons cast out of a man (==Mark 5:13==). The kingdom established is that man begging to be *with* Jesus (==Mark 5:18==). The kingdom demonstrated is bones rattling together in the valley. The kingdom established is an army standing on its feet, in rank, under a King (==Ezekiel 37:7-10==).

Don't hear this as a knock on demonstration. Kingdom demonstrated is glorious. Jesus demonstrated the kingdom everywhere He went, and revival is a concentrated season of exactly that. God loves to show what His reign looks like. But demonstration is not the goal; it's the invitation. Nine men got the demonstration and kept walking. One man let the demonstration become a *relationship*—he came back, he worshiped, he stayed at the feet of the King. That's establishment: the rule of God grounded in a person, in a place, and not just passing through.

This is the apostolic way of seeing. It recognizes when the kingdom is being demonstrated around us—healings, outpourings, seasons of unusual grace—and asks the next question: *how does this become established?* How does a miracle become a disciple? How does a revival become a people? Every revival in history has demonstrated the kingdom. Very few have managed to establish it—to ground it into the soil of a community so deeply that it gets handed to the next generation.

Ten were healed. One was changed. Be the one who comes back.
