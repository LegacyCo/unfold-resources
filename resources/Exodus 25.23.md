## Commentary
God has always had a table in His house.

When God gave Moses the instructions for building the tabernacle, He first tells Moses how to build the ark of His presence. The second thing that God tells Moses to build is a table, "You shall also make a table of acacia wood..."
	
Before God speaks to Moses about the lamp stand or the inner & outer veil, even before the altar, He tells him to build a table.

Fast forward to the time of Jesus. Jesus could have said, “I am the fulfillment of all that stuff in the tabernacle...it’s no longer necessary.” But He didn’t. Instead, on the night He was betrayed, Jesus sat around a table with the twelve men He had done life with for the past threes years, gave them bread, gave them wine, and said, “Do this…” Rather than doing away with the table, Jesus firmly established the table of the Old Covenant in the age of the New. He is the Living Bread of God that has come down from heaven. He removed the veil of separation between God and man in His Father’s house, but He never removed the table.
	
For the first 1,500 years of the church, the broken body of Christ and His shed blood were the center piece in God’s house, the anchor that continually proclaimed the Lord’s death, burial, resurrection, ascension, and enthronement. There was a table in the heart of God’s house. Communion was the gravitational center of the church gathering, until the reformation of the 1500’s when the church replaced the table with the pulpit. Now when we go to house of the Lord, it's not a table that we gather around, it's a lectern, an announcement that we have not come together to feast and celebrate, we have come together to listen. 

God wants His table back.

This is a quote by William Willimon, “When people look at the church and ask ‘Are you Christ’s body or shall we look for another?’…we have to point them to our table, to that gathering of sick, hurting people, with the nobodies up at the head table eating like somebodies, with the outcasts invited in and filled with the good things.”

## Application
Real life happens at the table. Drinks spill. Dishes break. Hard conversations happen. Hands are held and prayers are prayed. Tears fall. Glasses are raised. Stories are told. Laughter echoes. If the kitchen is the heart of the home, the table is where the family expresses that love.

A pulpit may instruct us, but a table reminds us who we are.
