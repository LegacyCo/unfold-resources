## Commentary
There is something remarkable about the accusation made against Jesus in this verse. His critics call Him “a glutton and a winebibber, a friend of tax collectors and sinners.” The first two accusations were distortions of His behavior, intended to discredit Him. But the third, although spoken as an insult, revealed something wonderfully true about His ministry. Jesus really was a friend of sinners.

In the religious culture of first-century Judaism, sharing a meal with someone was more than an opportunity to eat together. Table fellowship communicated acceptance, relationship, and a degree of social identification. This is why the religious leaders were so offended by Jesus eating with tax collectors and sinners. They understood that He was doing more than tolerating their presence. He was intentionally drawing near to people they believed should be kept at a distance.

Jesus had already explained His reasoning in ==Matthew 9:12–13==: “Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. ... For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance.” Jesus was not indifferent to sin, nor was His friendship an endorsement of everything these people did. He drew near to them because He had come to save them. He refused to allow their sin to become a barrier to the mercy He had come to reveal. And in His presence, those who were open to receive the remedy only He could give were transformed.

There is also significance in Jesus' concluding statement: “But wisdom is justified by her children.” Some translations render this as “wisdom is justified by her deeds.” Both expressions communicate a similar idea: the wisdom of God will ultimately be vindicated by what it produces.

The religious leaders judged John and Jesus according to their own expectations of what godliness should look like. They rejected John's separation from sinners and condemned Jesus' fellowship with them. But Jesus points to the fruit of what God was doing. Consider Zacchaeus, a wealthy tax collector whose encounter with Jesus resulted in repentance, generosity, and restitution (==Luke 19:1–10==). The very people the religious leaders despised were being transformed through their encounters with Christ. The wisdom of the God who "shows sinners the way" (==Psalm 25:8==) was being demonstrated in the lives of those who responded to Jesus.
