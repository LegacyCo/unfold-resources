## Commentary
Ezekiel 34 opens with God confronting the shepherds of Israel—leaders who fed themselves instead of the flock, who grew fat while the sheep were scattered, broken, and lost (==Ezekiel 34:2-4==). And then God makes an announcement that should still shape how every church on earth understands leadership: “Indeed *I Myself* will search for My sheep and seek them out.”

I Myself. God doesn’t say, “I’ll recruit better shepherds.” He says, “I’m taking the shepherd's staff.” And listen to His job description: “I will feed My flock, and I will make them lie down… I will seek what was lost and bring back what was driven away, bind up the broken and strengthen what was sick” (==Ezekiel 34:15-16==). Every line of Psalm 23 is in that promise—green pastures, restored souls, a Shepherd personally present with His sheep.

When Jesus said, “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep” (==John 10:11==), that was Him announcing that Ezekiel 34 has come true. The “I Myself” of Ezekiel is standing in front of the crowd. God kept His promise personally, in person.

Jesus is the Chief Shepherd over every under-shepherd (==1 Peter 5:4==), the great Shepherd of the sheep (==Hebrews 13:20==), the Shepherd and Overseer of your soul (==1 Peter 2:25==). Which means the lead pastor of every local church—big C global church and little c local congregation—is Jesus Himself. Human leaders matter; God gives them as gifts (==Ephesians 4:11==). But they serve *under* a Shepherd who never delegated His flock away. If leaders ever made you feel like scattered sheep, take heart in the two most personal words of Ezekiel 34: not “I will send someone.” *I Myself.*

Maybe that’s you. Maybe a shepherd fed himself at your expense—used your gifts, your money, or your trust to build his own house while you went hungry. Notice two things in Ezekiel 34 that God wants you to know. First, He saw it. He names exactly what those shepherds did, and He holds them responsible: “I will require My flock at their hand” (==Ezekiel 34:10==). Second, He doesn’t wait for wounded sheep to find their own way home—wounded sheep rarely can. He goes out after them: “As a shepherd seeks out his flock… so will I seek out My sheep and deliver them from all the places where they were scattered in the cloudy and dark day” (==Ezekiel 34:12==). That’s the same Shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine, searches until He finds the one, and carries it home on His shoulders—rejoicing, not scolding (==Luke 15:4-5==).

So if a bad experience with leaders has kept you at a distance from church, hear this: you may have left a congregation, but you never left His flock. He knows where you are. He knows what scattered you. And He’s not sending a committee to retrieve you—He’s coming Himself, to bind up what was broken and strengthen what got sick along the way (==Ezekiel 34:16==). Let Him. And when He carries you back to His people—because He will, His sheep belong in a flock—you’ll come back under a different banner: not the shepherd who failed you, but the Shepherd who came for you.
