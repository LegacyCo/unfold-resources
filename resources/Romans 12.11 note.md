## Word Study
“…not lagging in diligence, fervent in spirit, serving the Lord.”

- *fervent* : **zeō** — to boil; to bubble over with heat, like water at a rolling boil or metal glowing in the forge. Paul drops this word into the middle of his most practical list—hate evil, love good, show brotherly affection, give preference to one another (==Romans 12:9-10==)—as if a boiling spirit were as basic to the Christian life as honesty. To Paul, it is. Fire in the spirit isn’t the trait of a special class of intense believers; it’s row-three of normal Christianity.

The word family’s other notable appearance:

- ==Acts 18:25== — Apollos, “being *fervent* (zeōn) in spirit, spoke and taught accurately the things of the Lord.” Boiling and accurate—passion and precision were never enemies.

And its close cousin:

- ==2 Timothy 1:6== — “stir up (*anazōpyreō* — to rekindle, fan into flame) the gift of God which is in you.” Paul’s word to Timothy assumes the fire can burn low—and that tending it is Timothy’s job, not God’s alone.

Summary: water doesn’t boil by itself, and it doesn’t stay boiling by itself—someone keeps it on the heat. That’s the command hiding in this participle: keep your spirit on the flame. Stay near the fire of the Spirit in prayer, in the word, at the cross, and don’t come off the burner. Lukewarm is what boiling becomes when it’s left unattended (==Revelation 3:16==).
