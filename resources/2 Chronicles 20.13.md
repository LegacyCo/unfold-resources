## Commentary
“Now all Judah, with their little ones, their wives, and their children, stood before the LORD.”

Set this verse next to the prayer that was just prayed. Jehoshaphat has confessed before God, “we have no power against this great multitude… nor do we know what to do, but our eyes are upon You” (==2 Chronicles 20:12==)—and this verse tells us who was listening. Not a room of strangers. All of Judah. The king's sons and daughters heard him. His wife heard him. Men who were about to be asked to march out toward three armies heard their king say, in effect, “I'm empty.”

Most leaders would rather die than say that in front of the people they lead—especially their family. We assume a man who admits he doesn't know is a man who has lost the room. The rest of the chapter says otherwise. God did not respond to the strength, charisma, or fame of the king; He responded to the king's emptiness. The Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel in the midst of that same assembly, with the answer the king had just confessed he didn't have (==2 Chronicles 20:14-15==). By emptying himself before God and his people, Jehoshaphat made room for God to be God in the situation—and the children standing there watched heaven answer a leader who told the truth about his own insufficiency. “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble” (==James 4:6==), and “My strength is made perfect in weakness” (==2 Corinthians 12:9==). A godly leader's confessed emptiness does not cost him his authority. It fills him with the authority only God can give.
