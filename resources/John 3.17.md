## Commentary
“God did not send His Son into the world to condemn the world, but that the world through Him might be saved.”

Religion is always looking for an opportunity to condemn. It gathers evidence, draws lines, establishes the correct procedure, and then waits for someone to cross it. Jesus entered the world carrying an entirely different assignment.

He came to save.

This does not mean that Jesus ignored sin. No one spoke more seriously about sin, judgment, and the necessity of repentance. But He confronted sin for the purpose of freeing people from it—not to simply prove they were guilty.

The Pharisees repeatedly turned God’s gifts into barricades. Practices meant to lead people into covenant life became measurements used to determine who was acceptable. The same thing can happen with baptism, communion, spiritual gifts, or denominational traditions. Gifts intended to celebrate God’s grace become religious guards posted outside its door.

Meanwhile, the world remains trapped outside while believers argue about which human procedure gives a person permission to enter the salvation Jesus purchased with His blood.

The church must never become more difficult to approach than Jesus was.

There will be a day when Christ judges the living and the dead. But this is still the day of salvation (==2 Corinthians 6:2==). The church has not been entrusted with the ministry of suspicion. We have been entrusted with the ministry of reconciliation, announcing that God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself (==2 Corinthians 5:19-20==).

We cannot faithfully represent a Savior who came to open the way while becoming the reason people believe the way is closed.

## Application
Consider what someone burdened by sin would conclude about God after encountering you.

Would they see a Father eager to restore them, or a religious system eager to evaluate them? Truth without mercy misrepresents Jesus. Mercy without truth cannot lead anyone into freedom. In Christ, both meet—and the door opens.
