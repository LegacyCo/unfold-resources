## Commentary
At the last supper, with the cross hours away, Jesus tells His disciples, “The ruler of this world is coming, and he has nothing in Me.”

What a thing to be able to say. Jesus was born a son of Adam, but He never sinned the sin of Adam. There was no hook in Him the enemy could pull on. And because He knew the enemy had no legal ground in Him, He could rest—even walking into the greatest suffering in history. He belonged to the Father, so whatever came, He was in the Father’s hands.

Now here’s what it means to be *in Christ*: you can say the same thing. When Christ is in you, you have the authority to stand before your family, your business, your neighborhood, your city and say, “I know the enemy is coming for us—but don’t worry. He has nothing in me.”

Some of us can’t imagine talking like that. We’ve been discipled by a false humility that says, “Well, I’m just a sinner saved by grace.” It sounds modest, but it isn’t true—and there’s no expectation of victory in it. That mindset measures God’s word against the reality of your imperfections, and because you know you still sin, it concludes the enemy must still have hooks in you. Present sin gets overvalued; the victory of the cross gets undervalued.

But your standing before the enemy was never based on your perfection. It’s based on Christ’s (==Colossians 2:10==; ==1 John 4:17==). Sin that’s confessed and repented of gives the devil nothing to hold (==1 John 1:9==). You aren’t “just a sinner.” You’re a son or daughter in whom Christ dwells—and the ruler of this world has nothing in Him.
