## Commentary
This chapter is a reminder that God had made a covenant with David. The Lord promised that David’s house, kingdom, and throne would be established forever (==2 Samuel 7:12–16==; ==Psalm 89:3–4==, ==Psalm 89:34–37==). So when the Chronicler lists David’s sons and then follows the royal line through Solomon, the kings of Judah, the exile, and beyond, he is communicating something vital to God's people: their exile did not erase His promise.

The family line continues.

That doesn't mean the line is clean. David’s household was marked by devastating failure. Amnon violated Tamar. Absalom murdered Amnon and later rebelled against David. Adonijah tried to seize the throne. Solomon began well but later turned his heart toward other gods (2 Samuel 13; 2 Samuel 15; 1 Kings 1; 1 Kings 11:1–13).

And yet, through this complicated family line, God continued to carry His covenant.

This chapter teaches us something about the faithfulness of God. God’s promises are not held together by the perfection of the people who receive them. The kings of Judah failed again and again. Some were faithful. Many were not. Eventually the nation was carried into exile. But even then, the promise was not dead.

The genealogy moves all the way through the exile and names descendants after the exile. That means God was still preserving the line of David even when there was no visible throne.

And ultimately, this line points us to Jesus.

Matthew opens his Gospel by tracing Jesus as “the son of David, the son of Abraham” (==Matthew 1:1==). Luke also traces the line of Jesus through David (==Luke 3:23–38==). Jesus is the true Son of David, the King whose throne will not fail, whose kingdom will not end, and whose reign is not dependent on the strength of earthly institutions (==Luke 1:32–33==; ==Revelation 22:16==).

## Application
There are seasons when the promise of God looks buried under failure and loss. That's how David’s line must have looked to God's people after the exile. No throne. No king. No visible glory.

But 1 Chronicles 3 reminds us that God knows how to preserve His covenant.

The absence of visible evidence does not mean the absence of God's faithfulness. God can carry His promise through broken families, failed leaders, national collapse, and long seasons where nothing appears to be happening. It was true for Israel, and the same is true for you.
