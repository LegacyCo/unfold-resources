## Commentary
At the climax of his teaching on marriage, Paul quotes Scripture: “For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.” Then he says something astonishing: “This is a great mystery, but I speak concerning Christ and the church.”

Notice *where* Paul reached for that quote. It’s Genesis 2:24—and sin doesn’t enter the story until Genesis 3. Paul is reaching all the way back to God’s original design of marriage, before the fall, before the curse, before fear and shame ever entered the story, and telling us what it was *for*. Marriage was never a social arrangement that the gospel later borrowed as an illustration. From the beginning, it was designed to declare the story of the gospel: a Son who leaves His home to seek out a bride and be joined to her in covenant (==Genesis 2:24==; ==Revelation 19:7==).

This is why the family carries such weight as a witness. Your unbelieving neighbors may never come see the church worship—but they can see your family. The Christian family is like a front porch light on a dark street, the front window of the house the neighborhood looks into to see what the kingdom of God looks like. And what God wants them to see isn’t just love, commitment, and trust—though all of that—but the restoration of an eternal plan that sin hijacked: two distinct persons living as one in self-giving love, each releasing the other to flourish, together showing an accurate image of who God is.

So Paul hasn’t descended from the heights of Ephesians 1–3 into rule-book ethics. He’s showing us that even the most fundamental relational ecosystem on the planet—the one everyone understands because everyone has one—was intentionally designed to declare Christ and His kingdom. Your family isn’t a private world behind closed doors. It’s a stage for the gospel. You may never preach from a pulpit, but you’re declaring a message to everyone around you. Your family is your sermon.
