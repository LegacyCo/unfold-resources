## Commentary
Jesus’ invitation comes in a deliberate order: “If anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink. He who believes in Me…out of his heart will flow rivers of living water.”

First we come. Then we drink. Only then does the river flow.

We are often preoccupied with what should flow out of us—teaching, service, compassion, power, wisdom, etc. Jesus begins with what must be received into us (==Isaiah 55:1==). We cannot give what we have not received, and we cannot release the life of the Spirit while neglecting the One from whom that life comes.

The river is not produced by human effort. It flows from inward fullness. Our responsibility is to remain near Christ and drink deeply of Him.

This is the priestly pattern: come to Him, minister to Him, receive from Him, and then carry His life into the world. Anything else eventually becomes imitation. We may retain spiritual language and outward form while having no living water within us.

The nations do not need a better performance of Christianity. They need the life of God (==Ezekiel 47:9==). That life flows through people who have allowed their thirst to drive them continually back to Jesus (see ==Revelation 22:1–2==; ==Revelation 22:17==).
