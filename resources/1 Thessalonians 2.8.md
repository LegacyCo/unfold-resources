## Commentary
Paul had come to Thessalonica as a stranger, but became like family. His words to them in this letter show his approach to developing that covenant relationship, and how it was as much a part of sharing the gospel with them as the words that he used. He adds in v11, "we exhorted, and comforted, and charged every one of you, as a father does his own children."

Paul saw his apostolic role in building the church through the lens of family and covenant. Once the gospel was received, discipleship looked like life together; so much so, that he could say that he shared/gave not only the gospel, but his own life to the church.

This is no mere leadership strategy. This is no method of evangelism. This is a man filled with the love of God who can't help but love the way Jesus loves. The hope was that they, in turn, would love the same,
	"May the Lord cause you to increase and abound in love to one another and to all, just as we do to you" (1 Thessalonians 3:12).

## Application
The church is not built by transferring information, but by shared life. Paul didn’t just preach sermons and move on; he loved people deeply, walked with them personally, and related to them like family. Rather than an event we attend, church is intended to be a life of covenantal relationship lived with the family of God. This is one of the many things that makes the gospel visible.
