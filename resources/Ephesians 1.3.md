## Commentary
A woman once became known as “America’s greatest miser.” Her name was Hetty Green. When she died in 1916, she left behind an estate worth more than $100 million — equivalent to almost $5 billion today. Yet for all her wealth, she lived like a pauper. It is said she ate cold oatmeal because she did not want to spend the money to heat it. When her son needed medical care, she delayed so long trying to find a free clinic that his condition worsened, and he eventually lost his leg. She had extraordinary wealth at her disposal, yet refused to live from it.

That is a picture of far too many Christians.

Paul says that God “has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ.” Not a few blessings. *Every* spiritual blessing. There are no shortages in heaven. There's no poverty of resource in the life of the believer. Heaven has opened its account to those who are in Christ.

And yet many believers still live like paupers. They live timid, anxious, and spiritually deprived, as though God has withheld what He has already given. They beg for crumbs while seated at a table of abundance. They live below their inheritance because they are unaware of what is already rightfully theirs.

## Application
Paul confronts the spiritual pauper in this incredible letter. He opens our eyes to the riches of grace, the wealth of our inheritance, and the fullness that belongs to us in Christ, so that we would stop living like beggars and start living like sons and daughters of the King.
