## Commentary
Between the cross and the empty tomb, every follower of Jesus had to decide how to wait—and the Gospels preserve three very different postures.

Thomas waited as the wounded skeptic. His confusion hardened into unbelief, and he drew a line: “Unless I see in His hands the print of the nails… I will not believe” (==John 20:25==). He wouldn’t trust again until he could put his hands on the promise.

The two on the road to Emmaus waited by leaving. When the story didn’t turn out as they expected—“we were hoping that it was He who was going to redeem Israel” (==Luke 24:21==)—they turned their backs on Jerusalem and walked. Disappointment became their compass, and they set out to find their own way into the next season.

And then there’s Mary Magdalene. Grieving, but still faithful. She came to the tomb where everything she hoped in lay buried—came in mourning, yes, but she also came in worship. She refused to let her disappointment steal her devotion. And it was Mary—not Peter, not John, not Thomas—who first heard the resurrected King say her name: “Jesus said to her, ‘Mary!’” The new season was announced first to the one who stayed near in the dark.

All three loved Jesus. All three were devastated. The difference wasn’t the depth of their pain—it was what they did with it while they waited. And that’s the question for every in-between place we walk through, every stretch where it looks like the story has died: the waiting is not just about what God is preparing on the other side of the closed door. It’s about what we allow Him to do on the inside of us while we wait.

You don’t get to choose whether the three-day journeys come. You do get to choose your posture in them. Choose Mary’s: grieve if you must—but bring your grief to where He was last seen, and keep worshiping. The ones who stay near in the dark are the first to hear their name when the stone moves.
