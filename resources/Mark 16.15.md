## Commentary
“And He said to them, ‘Go into all the world and preach the gospel to every creature.’”

Kingdom people are on mission. They are not like the Dead Sea—only taking in, receiving the life-giving nutrients of the gospel with no outlet. They are a river: “out of his heart will flow rivers of living water” (==John 7:38==). What comes in goes out, and life follows wherever it flows.

And notice the scope Jesus assigns to the mission: *all* the world, *every* creature. In the 1970s, an advertisement ran in the back of a Polynesian magazine seeking missionary workers for the unreached jungles. The qualifications: “Ability to mix with people, wade rivers, write articles, mix concrete, love one’s neighbor, deliver babies, sit cross-legged, conduct meetings, drain swamps, digest questionable dishes, patch human weaknesses, suffer fools gladly, and burn midnight oil.” It went on to warn that persons allergic to ants, babies, beggars, cockroaches, humidity, indifference, mud, poverty, and sweat “had better think twice before applying.” That’s the job description—because the assignment is all creation, in all situations. Some are called to carry the gospel across the world. Others are called to carry it across the street. Jesus handles the where; guiding your steps is His responsibility (==Proverbs 3:6==). Your part is willingness—to the neighbor, the barista, the coworker, the difficult relative. When you trust that God takes you where He wants you, then the person standing in front of you is no accident.

## Word Study
- *preach* : **kēryssō** — to herald; to make a public, authoritative announcement as the official messenger of a king. The kēryx (herald) didn’t invent the message, soften it, or negotiate over it—he announced what the king had already decreed and done.

Watch the word in action elsewhere:

- ==Luke 4:18-19== — Jesus, announcing His own commission: anointed “to *proclaim* (kēryssō) liberty to the captives… to *proclaim* the acceptable year of the LORD.”
- ==Romans 10:14== — “How shall they hear without a *preacher* (kēryssōn)?” The lost aren’t waiting for a better argument; they’re waiting for a herald.
- ==2 Timothy 1:11== — Paul: “I was appointed a *preacher* (kēryx), an apostle, and a teacher.” Herald comes first.

Summary: a salesman persuades you to make a purchase; a herald announces something that has already happened. The gospel is not a product pitch—it’s a royal announcement: the King has died for sinners, risen, and taken His throne. Our job isn’t to close the deal. It’s to make the announcement to every creature, and let the news do what only news of this King can do.
