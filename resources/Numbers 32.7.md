## Commentary
This is Moses’ response when the tribes of Reuben and Gad saw the land east of the Jordan and wanted to settle there. On the surface, it may have looked practical. The land was good for livestock. It seemed like a reasonable decision. But Moses saw something beneath the surface of the two tribe's decision. Their willingness to stop short of the promise had the power to discourage everyone else who still needed to cross over.

How you steward your promise impacts my ability to steward mine. Your obedience is never just personal. Your courage strengthens the body. Your compromise burdens the body. When one part of the people of God decides to settle for less than what God said, it does not remain isolated. It sends a message into the camp.

This is why testimony matters so much. For example, when I hear stories of missionaries crossing borders, leaving comfort, enduring hardship, and carrying the gospel into difficult places, it stirs something in me. Their obedience becomes a prophetic confrontation to my excuses. Their willingness to cross over makes me ask what might be possible if I would cross over too.

The same is true in the body of Christ. When I see discipline in the life of my brother, it provokes me. When I see hunger, sacrifice, prayer, obedience, and endurance in someone else, it reminds me that more is possible. But when I see a falling away, when I see someone settling east of the Jordan, it burdens me. Instead of moving together into the promise with momentum, I now have to stop and care for the one who is losing heart.

Of course, we do that because we love one another. But we should not pretend our choices have no effect on the people around us.

Moses understood that discouragement can travel through a community. So can courage. One person’s refusal to cross over can weaken the heart of many. But one person’s obedience can also put strength back into the bones of the body.

The question is not only, “Am I entering what God has promised me?”

The question is, “Is the way I am stewarding my promise giving courage to others to enter theirs?”
