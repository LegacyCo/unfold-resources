## Commentary
“For a day in Your courts is better than a thousand. I would rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God than dwell in the tents of wickedness.”

This is the conclusion the psalmist has been moving toward from the beginning. He has longed for the courts of the Lord (==Psalm 84:2==), envied those who dwell in God’s house (==Psalm 84:4==), and described the blessedness of those whose hearts are set on the journey toward His presence (==Psalm 84:5==). Now he tells us why: one day near God is worth more to him than a thousand days anywhere else.

The comparison is intentionally extreme. He is not saying that life outside the temple has no value. He is saying that no amount of time, pleasure, comfort, or opportunity can compensate for the absence of God. If he had to choose between one day in the presence of the Lord and a thousand days somewhere else, the decision would be easy.

The second half of the verse pushes the contrast even further: “I would rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God than dwell in the tents of wickedness.”

The idea behind “doorkeeper” can carry the sense of standing at the threshold. The psalmist is willing to occupy the lowest place at the edge of God’s house rather than have a permanent home somewhere God is not honored. He does not need status in the presence of God. He simply wants to be near Him.

That is the heart of a lover.

There are places this world can offer us that may look more impressive or exciting than the place obedience has put us. But the psalmist has settled the question of what makes a place good: _Is God there?_

The presence of God is not one blessing among many. It is the blessing that gives every other blessing its meaning.
