## Commentary
A famous concert pianist used to say, “If I quit the piano one day, I notice it. If I quit two days, my friends notice it. If I quit three days, the public notices it.” Only by giving himself to the fundamentals of daily practice could he preserve his skill.

The same is true of the secret place. If I don’t spend time with Jesus one day, I notice it. Two days, and those closest to me notice it. Three days without touching the hem of His garment, and my church suffers—my community suffers. We never graduate from the fundamentals of the faith, and communion with God in prayer is the most overlooked fundamental of them all.

Look at the company this verse keeps: crowds are multiplying, sick people are coming from everywhere—and Luke tells us Jesus “often withdrew into the wilderness and prayed.” *Often*. If the sinless Son of God ordered His life around withdrawing to pray, what does it say about us when we don’t? Prayerlessness is really an indicator of pride. It communicates to God, “I’ve got this under control.” But prayer assumes a posture of dependence—and God gives grace to the humble (==James 4:6==). Humility moves Him; it reminds Him of Himself.

Church history says the same thing from every direction. Martin Luther: “I have so much to do today, I must pray three hours.” John Wesley: “God does nothing but in answer to prayer.” Study anyone who ever did anything for the kingdom and you’ll find the common thread: they prayed. Our God has humbled Himself to work in cooperation with a praying people—which is why our programs, sermons, and strategies carry divine authority only to the degree they are *preceded* by prayer. God is only pleased with what God Himself does, and what He does, He does through a praying people.

Could it be that the world is not waiting for God to move—so much as it’s waiting for His church to move Him in prayer?
