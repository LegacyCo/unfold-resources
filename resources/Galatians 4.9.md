## Commentary
This if from "Paul" by N.T. Wright: Every Jew knew the story of the Exodus: slavery in Egypt; divine victory over Pharaoh; Israel (as "God's firstborn son") redeemed and brought through the Red Sea; the gift of the Torah on Sinai; the glorious divine presence coming to dwell in the Tabernacle; Abraham's children heading home to the "inheritance" of the promised land. Paul retells that story here with Jesus and the Spirit at the heart of it. The whole world is enslaved; God sent his son to redeem and his Spirit to indwell; Abraham's children are assured of their "inheritance." There is a sting in the tail, however. Paul warns the Galatians that they are now in danger of behaving like those Israelites in the wilderness who wanted to go back to Egypt. If they get circumcised, they will be saying that they prefer the old slavery to the new freedom. 

## Application
Paul’s warning to the Galatians is that it is possible to be brought out of slavery and still crave it. God had acted decisively in Christ to redeem them, adopt them, and fill them with His Spirit, but they were now tempted to go backward—to return to a system of externals, regulations, and religious bondage that could never give life. That is the tragedy of the church of Galatia: not that they were ignorant of freedom, but that having tasted freedom, they were in danger of preferring chains. Like Israel longing for Egypt, they were romanticizing the place God had already delivered them from.

We must be careful not to go back to old yokes once Christ has made us sons and daughters. That may not look like circumcision for us, but it can look like returning to performance, legalism, fear, self-effort, or any religious system that makes us feel in control while pulling us away from dependence on the Spirit. Whenever we start relating to God through duty without intimacy, rule-keeping without trust, or ritual without life, we are drifting back toward Egypt. The Christian life does not begin by grace and then mature through bondage. It begins with Christ, continues with Christ, and is sustained by the Spirit.
