## Commentary
“For the love of Christ compels us, because we judge thus: that if One died for all, then all died; and He died for all, that those who live should live no longer for themselves, but for Him who died for them and rose again” (==2 Corinthians 5:14-15==).

This is born-again language. If you believe that Jesus is who He says He is, and He died for all, then you have died. You died to everything that used to control you—the fears that ran your decisions, the appetites that set your schedule, the opinions of others that steered your life. All of it went to the cross with Him (==Romans 6:6==).

But nobody lives uncontrolled. Paul isn’t describing an empty driver’s seat—he’s describing a change of drivers. Notice that verse 15 doesn’t say Jesus died for all so that when we die we can go to heaven. He died so that we would *live*—and live for Him. And to live for Him means you are now compelled by the very thing that carried Him to the cross in the first place: love. It was love that seized Him, surrounded Him, and pressed Him up Calvary’s hill (==Galatians 2:20==). And Paul says that same love has now seized *us*. The old masters are dead, and the new Master rules by love.

That’s the difference between religious duty and the life Paul is describing. Duty pushes from behind with guilt; love grips from within. Paul didn’t endure shipwrecks, beatings, and prisons because he was disciplined. He tells us plainly what was driving him: the love of Christ. When that love takes hold of a person, living for yourself stops making sense—not because someone told you it should, but because you died, and the life you’re living now belongs to Someone else (==1 Corinthians 6:19-20==).

## Word Study
- *compels* : **synechō** (Greek) — to seize, to press in on every side, to hold together, to dominate. This is not gentle influence; it’s the word for being gripped so completely that you have no choice about your direction.

Watch the word work elsewhere:

- ==Luke 8:45== — the crowds “press” (synechō) against Jesus—bodies hemming Him in on every side.
- ==Luke 12:50== — Jesus says of the cross, “I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how *distressed* (synechō) I am till it is accomplished!” Love had Him in its grip, pressing Him toward Calvary.
- ==Acts 18:5== — Paul was “compelled by the Spirit” to testify that Jesus is the Christ.

Summary: the same word that describes Jesus being gripped and driven toward the cross now describes the believer being gripped and driven by His love. You don’t muster a life lived for Him. You get seized into it.
