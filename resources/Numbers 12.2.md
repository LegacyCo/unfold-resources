## Commentary
“So they said, ‘Has the LORD indeed spoken only through Moses? Has He not spoken through us also?’ And the LORD heard it.”

What makes this complaint dangerous is that part of it is true. God *had* spoken through them. Miriam led Israel in prophetic song at the Red Sea (==Exodus 15:20-21==). Aaron was appointed as Moses' spokesman, and God called him Moses' “prophet” (==Exodus 4:16==; ==Exodus 7:1==). Nobody was denying their gifts.

The problem is the word “only,” and what they were doing with it. They took something real—God has used us too—and used it to pull Moses down to their level. That's how envy usually works. It rarely needs to lie. It just takes a fact and aims it at someone with the intent of tearing them down. A few chapters later Korah makes the same argument against Moses: “all the congregation is holy, every one of them… Why then do you exalt yourselves above the assembly of the LORD?” (==Numbers 16:3==). Both arguments sound right. But both are an attempt to grab for a position that only God can give. James says, “where envy and self-seeking exist, confusion and every evil thing are there” (==James 3:16==).

“And the LORD heard it.” There’s no sign that Moses heard what Miriam and Aaron were saying, or that he answered if he did hear it. The conversation may well have happened in private, between a brother and sister who assumed it would stay between them. It didn’t matter. The LORD heard it. He’s the One who suddenly calls all three of them out to the tabernacle (==Numbers 12:4==), and He’s the One who responds to the complaint (==Numbers 12:6-8==). The same phrase shows up a chapter earlier, when the people complained and “the LORD heard it” (==Numbers 11:1==). It works the other way too: when those who feared the LORD spoke to one another, “the LORD listened and heard them” (==Malachi 3:16==). God listens to what’s said about His servants, including what’s said when they aren’t in the room. No conversation about another person is ever fully private. Jesus said we’ll give account for “every idle word” (==Matthew 12:36==).
