## Commentary
"...in whom you also are being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit."

A *dwelling place*. Not a place He visits. A place He lives.

There's a difference between the two, and the church often settles for the lesser one without noticing. Revival is God visiting the houses we build. Revolution is God dwelling in the house *He* builds. Visitation is glorious—no one who has experienced it would say otherwise. But being grateful that Dad can come by on the weekends is a subtle way of announcing that we're okay with separation the rest of the week. God never intended visitation rights. He intended to move in.

Look at what Paul says the dwelling place is made of. Not "you are being built *into* a dwelling place" as individuals, but "you are being built *together*." The whole building, fitted together, grows into a holy temple (==Ephesians 2:21==). God's house is a *people*—living stones, joined to each other on the Chief Cornerstone. Which means the question of whether God dwells among us is inseparable from the question of how we are joined to one another. A pile of stones is not a house. It's a quarry.

And this is why God's house, built His way, looks like family. God *is* family—Father, Son, and Spirit—and He is building a family (==Ephesians 3:14-15==). Until His house looks like that, we may experience His glorious visitation, but we will not experience Him dwelling. Paul is so sure of this that the moment he finishes describing the dwelling place, he drops to his knees and prays that Christ would *dwell* in their hearts through faith, that they'd be rooted and grounded in *love* (==Ephesians 3:17==). Dwelling and love are the same prayer.

So when we pray for God to come, we should be honest about what we're asking for. Are we asking Him to visit what we've built and endorse it? Or are we willing to be built *together*—fitted to people we didn't choose, in covenant we can't walk away from—so that He has a place to actually live?

## Word Study
- *dwelling place* : **katoikētērion** (Greek) — a permanent habitation, a settled residence; from *katoikeō*, to settle down and make one's home. This is not *paroikeō*—to sojourn, to stay as a temporary resident (==Hebrews 11:9==). It's the word for putting down roots. It appears only one other time in the New Testament: fallen Babylon becoming a *dwelling place* of demons (==Revelation 18:2==)—a settled residence.

Summary: God is not looking for a place to stop by. He's looking for a people to settle down in.
