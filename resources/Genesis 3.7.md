## Commentary
Two things to point out about this verse:

*First*, the fig leaves may have concealed, but they could never completely *cover*. Rather than coming to God and confessing what they had done, Adam and Eve tried to cover it up. They didn't know how to deal with their guilt, so they manufactured something to hide it. 

If Adam telling Eve not to touch the fruit was the first instance of religion, it could be said that Adam and Eve trying to cover their own guilt is the second.

Man-made religion promotes a message that says if you go through the right motions then that religious activity will cover your sinful condition. In an attempt to produce self-righteousness, self-effort is the present day fig leaf. Of course, religion apart from relationship is useless…our righteousness is as filthy rags, as Isaiah says in ==Isaiah 64:6==. Relationship with God, and the righteousness of God, can only come to us by faith in Jesus. Religious activity might make the guilty feel better, and it may convince others who are on the same religious hamster wheel that everything is fine, but deep inside we all know that our best efforts are no more a solution for sin than a fig leaf is a solution for nakedness.

The second thing to point out about this verse is it’s interesting that in the entire story of Scripture to this point, *this* tree is the only tree we know for sure what kind of fruit actually grew from it—it was a *fig* tree. We don’t know what fruit grew from the tree of knowledge or the tree of life, but Scripture does tells us what kind of tree Adam used to cover himself. 

I can't help but think of the story of Jesus walking past the fig tree in Mark 11. Beginning in verse 12, Mark tells the story like this,

"Now the next day, when they had come out from Bethany, He (Jesus) was hungry. And seeing from afar a fig tree having leaves, He went to see if perhaps He would find anything on it. When He came to it, He found nothing but leaves, for it was not the season for figs. In response Jesus said to it, 'Let no one eat fruit from you ever again'” (==Mark 11:12-14==).

Jesus curses the tree that man originally used to cover his guilt and shame. Jesus says, “I’ve come to make sure that man never uses anything but My blood to cover their shame again.” Jesus didn’t come to start a new religion, He came to show us the Father and expose the bankruptcy of all other attempts to get to Him.

Right after this story of Jesus and the fig tree, Mark says that Jesus went to the temple and denounces the religious activity that was taking place in His Father’s house, overturning the tables and and throwing out the money changers who had made His Father's house a "den of thieves." Cursed is anything that man could use to try to attain righteousness apart from the free gift of grace that God freely gives us in the death of His Son.

## Cross Reference
See ==Luke 24:31== for another example of someone eating and their eyes are opened. It's fascinating that Luke uses the same language found here in Genesis 3:7. Continue this train of thought and meditate on the importance of communion and how our "eyes are opened" when we eat from the Lord's Table.
