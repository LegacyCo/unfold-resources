## Commentary
One of the marks of wisdom is restraint. The wise have learned that every thought does not need to be spoken, every feeling does not need to be vented, and every opinion does not need to be expressed.

The more we speak, the more opportunity we create for exaggeration, gossip, slander, anger, pride, and careless words. A multitude of words may feel honest or expressive, but Scripture warns that sin is rarely far behind (see ==Proverbs 10.19==). Unrestrained speech can damage relationships, destroy trust, create conflict, and even bring practical ruin.

The fool speaks from impulse. He empties his heart through his mouth without first allowing wisdom to examine what is inside. His words become his destruction and the snare of his soul (==Proverbs 18.7==). What he calls “speaking his mind” often reveals that his mind has never been brought under discipline.

The wise guard their mouths. They spare their words, hold back what does not need to be said, and consider the effect their speech will have before releasing it. 

There is peace in learning not to say everything. There is rest in no longer feeling responsible to explain yourself, defend yourself, correct everyone, or respond to every provocation. Silence often protects what many words would destroy.

Wisdom does not just teach us what to say. It teaches us when to be quiet.
