## Commentary
"To Him who loved us and washed us from our sins in His own blood..."

Before John describes anything Jesus *did*, he tells us who Jesus *is*: the One who loves us. Older translations put it in the past—"who loved us"—but the Greek is a present participle. He *loves* us. Right now. Ongoing. The release from sin happened once, at the cross; the love that produced it never stopped.

Everything in this verse flows out of that first phrase. Why did He release us from our sins? Because He loves us. Why the blood? Because He loves us. Why make us a kingdom and priests to His God and Father (==Revelation 1:6==)? Because He loves us. *He* is the initiator—of life, of salvation, of repentance, of the sacrifice that made every one of those things a reality. Everything is from Him, through Him, and to Him (==Romans 11:36==).

We tend to tell the story backwards. We start with our sin, move to His death, and arrive at forgiveness—as if the cross were mainly a response to a problem. John starts somewhere else. He starts with love. Sin didn't drive Jesus to the cross; love did. He created because of love. He came because of love (==John 3:16==). He died because of love (==Galatians 2:20==). Victory over sin and death was the *result*, but love was the *reason*.

Which is why the verse ends in worship: "to Him be glory and dominion forever and ever" (==Revelation 1:6==). John doesn't work up to a doxology by listing his sins. He arrives there by remembering that he is loved.

## Word Study
- *loves* : **agapaō** (Greek), here in the present tense — "the One *loving* us." Not a completed act but a continuing state. Compare the next verb:
- *washed / released* : **lyō** (Greek), here in the aorist tense — a single, decisive act in the past. He *loosed* us once, and it's done.

Summary: one verb never ends and one verb never needs repeating. His love is ongoing; His release is finished. Kingdom people live in the overlap—permanently freed, continually loved.
