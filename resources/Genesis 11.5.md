## Commentary
“But the LORD came down to see the city and the tower which the sons of men had built.”

The verse reveals something about the nature of God. He keeps the world in order, and He ties the hands of those who will not be kept in order by His law. Men were building a tower to reach heaven; heaven had to come *down* just to look at it. At times it can appear that evil is winning and darkness is overcoming—that the builders of Babel will finish their tower. But the God of Scripture humbles Himself to come down and see, and He does not only see—He acts. He acts through His own judgment, as He does here; He acts through His church; He acts in response to the prayers of His saints. He is present, and the world is held together by the power of His word (==Hebrews 1:3==). This is the believer's rest: God is in control, and no civilization is permitted to build itself without Him.

The same is true of the individual. The people of Babel were planners; they had the specifications of the city worked out. Every person plans a life—a career, a future, a course mapped out in detail. The question the chapter presses is whether the plan is made under God or apart from Him. As one preacher put it: “If you plan your life without God at the centre, it will come to nothing, nothing at all. It will be as futile and as fatuous as the Tower of Babel… The human race is not allowed to build a civilization without God, and you are not allowed to build your life without God.” The Lord who came down to see Babel will one day sit on His throne and gather all nations before Him (==Matthew 25:31-32==). Nothing built apart from Him will survive that inspection.

## Word Study
**Came down** is the Hebrew *yarad* (יָרַד)—to descend, to go down. It is the verb of God's intervention: He comes down to Babel in judgment here, comes down to Sinai to give the law (==Exodus 19:20==), and comes down to deliver Israel from Egypt—“I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians” (==Exodus 3:8==). The pattern reaches its fullness in the Son, who “came down from heaven” to do the Father's will (==John 6:38==). Summary: man's towers cannot go up to God; the whole of Scripture is the record of God coming down to man.
