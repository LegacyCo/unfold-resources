## Commentary
Genealogies may not be the most exciting passages of Scripture to read, but there is intention behind these family trees. Without a genealogy, a person had no way of proving their tribal membership or their right to an inheritance. Tragically, when the Romans destroyed the temple and razed Jerusalem in 70 AD, the records of family lineage were lost. This means Jesus is the only Jew who can actually prove His claims to the throne of David.

It's also worth noting who appears in the family tree of the Messiah; specifically, the women who are included in this genealogy. It's very unusual to find the names of women in Jewish genealogies, since names and inheritances came through the fathers. And not only that, to go out of the way to include a prostitute (Rahab) and a woman connected to one of David’s greatest failures (Bathsheba) is incredible. If mentioning these names wasn't necessary, why would Matthew do it? Two reasons:

- To prove these words are not inspired by man, they are God-breathed.

- To show the relentless pursuit of God's grace.

When we lived in Iraq, I had a conversation with a young Muslim man about this very topic. He asked how I could be so certain the Bible was the Word of God. I pointed to this genealogy as part of my reason. Man would never include these scandals in his holy book. Man would erase the record of what David did to Uriah and Bathsheba. Man would conveniently fail to mention that his Messiah was the descendant of a prostitute. But where man blushes, God declares. The story didn't end with prostitution or murder. Those situations were redeemed, so much so that they are now recorded for all of eternity by God Himself as a trophy of His grace.

## Application
Do you have a story from the past that you'd rather keep hidden? What if God wanted to bring your lowest moment into the light of redemption—and turn the mistake into a testimony? That's who He is. He gives beauty for ashes. He makes all things new. No story is too broken for His grace to redeem.
