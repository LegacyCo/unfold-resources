## Commentary
I remember traveling overseas years ago with the director of our Bible school for an outdoor crusade. I asked him what he was going to preach. He said, “The kingdom of God.” And I remember thinking that sounded vague. I had been around church my whole life, heard hundreds of sermons—and I had never once heard anyone preach on the kingdom of God. Yet the kingdom is *the* message Jesus preached (==Mark 1:15==).

If that’s you, you’re in good company. After His resurrection, Jesus spent forty days with His disciples “speaking of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God” (==Acts 1:3==). Forty days of Bible study with the risen Lord Himself, on one subject. And here’s their response at the end of it: “Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?” After three years of walking with Jesus and forty days of kingdom teaching, they were still seeing the kingdom through the lens of nationalism—a better Israel, a defeated Rome, their nation back on top. 

Most of us make the disciples’ mistake. We hear “kingdom of God” and assume it must be a better version of the kingdoms we're familiar with. But saying the kingdom of God is a better version of other kingdoms is like saying salvation makes you a better version of your unsaved self. Jesus didn’t come to make you better—He came to make you new (==2 Corinthians 5:17==), and His kingdom isn’t an upgrade of the old order. It’s the end of it.

Just look at how differently this kingdom works. Its King defeats His enemies by laying down His life. The greatest aren’t the ones being served but the ones serving (==Matthew 20:26-28==). The first aren’t first—the last are first. The rich don’t inherit the land—the meek do (==Matthew 5:5==). A widow’s two small coins outweigh the offerings of the wealthy (==Luke 21:2-4==). In this kingdom, bread is flesh and wine is blood, two become one, faith moves mountains, leaves heal nations (==Revelation 22:2==), shadows heal the sick (==Acts 5:15==), kings carry crosses, and power is perfected in weakness (==2 Corinthians 12:9==). There is nothing like this kingdom. Trying to explain the kingdom of God by comparing it to earthly ones is like explaining flight to a starfish—the categories don’t exist at the bottom of the ocean. Which is exactly why Jesus told Nicodemus, “Unless one is born again, he cannot *see* the kingdom of God” (==John 3:3==). You don’t need a comparison. You need new eyes.

And take heart from how the disciples’ story ends. Their confused question in Acts 1 didn’t disqualify them—ten days later the Holy Spirit fell, and the same men who were still thinking in terms of national restoration began preaching the kingdom with clarity and power (==Acts 2:4==; ==Acts 8:12==). In fact, the book of Acts closes with Paul in Rome “preaching the kingdom of God… with all confidence” (==Acts 28:31==). The book that opens with a kingdom misunderstanding ends with a kingdom proclamation. That’s the journey God wants to take every one of us on. So become a student of this kingdom—it’s the responsibility of every citizen to learn its ways (==Matthew 6:33==). Ask the Holy Spirit to do for you what He did for them: replace every borrowed picture of the kingdom with the real one.
