## Commentary
Here’s the strange thing about the disciples’ failure with the demonized boy: they shouldn’t have failed. Seven chapters earlier, Jesus had given them “authority over unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal every disease” (==Matthew 10:1==). The authority was already theirs. So when they ask Jesus why they couldn’t help the boy, He doesn’t say, “You lacked authority.” He says, “Because of your unbelief.” The problem was never their credentials. It was their faith.

And then comes the sentence most of us get wrong: “If you have faith as a mustard seed, you will say to this mountain, ‘Move from here to there,’ and it will move.” We hear that and think *size*—and some translations push us there with “faith the *size of* a mustard seed” or “faith as *small as* a mustard seed.” But Jesus isn’t running a measuring contest. Try it at home: tell your wife she’s the size of the Pacific Ocean, and see how the evening goes. Tell her she’s deep and awe-inspiring *like* the Pacific Ocean, and you’ll have a different night entirely. The comparison isn’t about dimensions. It’s about nature.

Jesus is describing the *quality* of faith He wants His people to possess—faith with the nature of a seed. And what is the nature of a seed? It’s designed to grow under pressure. No apple seed ever produced apples sitting comfortably in the packet; it has to be buried, pressed, and cut off from light before it breaks through (==James 1:3==). Faith like a mustard seed isn’t impressive in the palm of your hand. Its glory is what it becomes in the dark, under the soil, when everything is pressing against it.

So the question this verse asks isn’t “How much faith do you have?” It’s “What kind?” Is your faith the decorative kind that sits in the package—or the buried kind, alive under pressure, on its way to becoming something that moves mountains? Because to *that* faith, Jesus attaches the staggering promise: “nothing will be impossible for you.”

## Word Study
- *as* : **hōs** — a comparative particle: “as, like, in the same way as.” It compares *nature and kind*, not measurement. Jesus isn’t quantifying faith; He’s characterizing it.

Other places *hōs* frames a comparison of nature:

- ==Matthew 10:16== — “Be wise *as* serpents and harmless *as* doves.” Not the size of a serpent—the shrewdness of one.
- ==1 Peter 2:2== — “*As* newborn babes, desire the pure milk of the word.” Not the dimensions of an infant—the appetite of one.

Summary: translations that render this “the size of a mustard seed” answer a question Jesus wasn’t asking. The mountain doesn’t move for a certain *amount* of faith. It moves for a certain *kind*—seed-natured faith that treats burial as the beginning of growth. Ours is a faith designed for pressure.
