## Commentary
Paul quotes ==Isaiah 65:1== to explain something remarkable about the way God has revealed Himself to the Gentiles: “I was found by those who did not seek Me; I was made manifest to those who did not ask for Me.”

To appreciate what Paul is saying, we need to understand the contrast he is making between Israel and the Gentiles. Israel had been entrusted with the Law, the covenants, the promises, and the prophetic Scriptures. For generations, they had pursued righteousness through their observance of the Law, yet when the Messiah came, many failed to recognize Him. Meanwhile, the Gentiles, who had not been given these same privileges and were not pursuing righteousness through Israel's covenant, were now discovering the God of Israel through faith in Jesus Christ.

Paul has already described this surprising reversal in ==Romans 9:30==, explaining that the Gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, attained righteousness through faith, while Israel, which pursued a law of righteousness, did not attain it.

God has announced that He will be found by people who were not even looking for Him.

In its original context, ==Isaiah 65:1–2== describes God's willingness to reveal Himself alongside the persistent rebellion of those who refuse to respond to Him. Paul recognizes in these words a prophetic picture of what was happening in his day through the proclamation of the gospel. The Gentiles, who had lived outside Israel's covenant relationship with God, were now being brought near through Christ.

But notice the language God uses: “I was found” and “I was made manifest.” The emphasis is upon God's initiative. These people did not discover God because they had finally become sufficiently religious or had succeeded in their spiritual pursuit of Him. They encountered Him because He graciously chose to reveal Himself to them.

Before humanity ever began searching for God, God was already moving toward humanity. The story of Scripture is not about mankind's successful pursuit of God, but about God's determination to reveal Himself and reconcile humanity to Himself.

God did not remain distant, waiting for humanity to discover a way into His presence. John writes that “the Word became flesh and dwelt among us” (==John 1:14==). He entered our world, took on our humanity, and made Himself known to us. Jesus is the ultimate revelation of the God who desires to be found.

Yet there is something else significant about Paul's use of this prophecy. In the very next verse, he quotes God's words concerning Israel: “All day long I have stretched out My hands to a disobedient and contrary people” (==Romans 10:21==).

Consider the contrast. To the Gentiles, God reveals Himself to those who were not seeking Him. To Israel, God stretches out His hands toward those who continually resist Him. In both cases, we see a God who takes the initiative in making Himself known. The difference is not in God's willingness to reveal Himself, but in how people respond to that revelation.

## Application
There is an important implication in this verse for how we understand evangelism and mission. Earlier in this chapter, Paul emphasized the necessity of preaching the gospel so that people might hear, believe, and call upon the Lord. Now he reminds us that behind the proclamation of the gospel is a God who is actively making Himself known. We are not attempting to persuade an unwilling God to save people. We are participating in the mission of a God who has already demonstrated His desire to reconcile the world to Himself.

This should give us tremendous confidence as we share the gospel. The people we encounter may not be searching for God. They may have little interest in spiritual things or no understanding of who Jesus is. But neither did many of the Gentiles who first heard the gospel. Through the faithful witness of His people, God made Himself known to those who had not been looking for Him.

We should never assume that someone's apparent lack of interest in God means they are beyond the reach of His grace. After all, the testimony of countless believers is that they were not looking for God when they discovered that He had been pursuing them.
