## Commentary
“But when you do a charitable deed, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing.”

We quote this often, but stop and think about what Jesus is actually asking. Your hands share one body and one nervous system. No one has ever kept their left hand from knowing what their right hand is doing. Jesus isn’t handing us a technique for discreet giving—He’s handing us another impossibility, in the same category as “unless your righteousness exceeds the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees” (==Matthew 5:20==). And impossibilities are how Jesus points past self-effort to the work only God can do in us.

Notice the progression. Verse 2 dealt with your second-favorite audience: other people—don’t sound the trumpet. Verse 3 deals with your favorite audience: *you*. Because you can give in total anonymity and still keep a personal record. You can make sure no one ever finds out—and then replay the whole thing in your head for the rest of the week. That’s a version of self-righteousness where nobody has to clap for you, because you’re clapping for yourself.

So what is Jesus after? Not better hiding—self-forgetfulness. True righteousness is so absorbed in the person in front of it, and the need they have, that the giver can’t possibly think of himself. Picture a man holding his newborn for the first time. He’s not wondering how he looks holding the baby. He’s not composing the story he’ll tell later. He’s gone—completely taken up in somebody else. In that moment, his left hand has no idea what his right hand is doing. That’s how God gives: so enamored with the ones in front of Him that He gave everything He had—“For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son” (==John 3:16==).

And this is exactly what we find at the end of the age. When the King commends the sheep—“I was hungry and you fed Me”—they’re baffled: “Lord, when did we see You hungry and feed You?” (==Matthew 25:37==). The people Jesus commends have no memory of the thing He’s commending them for. Nobody was working to keep their giving secret. They simply weren’t looking at themselves. They were looking at a hungry man. That’s the quality of the born-again—the Holy Spirit forming in Jesus People the self-forgetful generosity of God Himself. Lose yourself in righteousness.
