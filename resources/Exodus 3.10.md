## Commentary
Seeing a need does not automatically mean we have been sent to meet it. Need may awaken compassion, but compassion alone is not a commission. Some of the greatest hindrances to the purposes of God are the well-intentioned actions of people who move out of human concern and religious zeal without first being sent by Him.

Moses had seen Israel’s suffering forty years earlier, and he attempted to correct it in his own strength. He recognized the injustice, but he had not yet received God’s timing, God’s way, or God’s commission. The need was real, but the need itself was not enough to authorize him.

Now, in Exodus 3, everything begins with God: “I have surely seen the oppression of My people… I have heard their cry… I know their sorrows.” God saw. God heard. God knew. Then God said to Moses, “Come now, therefore, and I will send you.”

Moses was not sent because he had finally become sufficiently moved by Israel’s pain. He was sent because God had heard their cry and chosen him as the vessel through whom He would respond. The deliverance originated in God’s heart before it ever became Moses’ assignment.

We must learn the difference between recognizing a need and receiving a commission. Otherwise, we may spend our strength correcting things God has not asked us to touch. Those who are truly sent do not move merely because something is wrong; they move because God has spoken. As I heard Art Katz once say, "If it's not sent of God, it will not go in the authority of God."
