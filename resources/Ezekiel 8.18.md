## Commentary
“Therefore I also will act in fury. My eye will not spare nor will I have pity; and though they cry in My ears with a loud voice, I will not hear them.”

After everything Ezekiel has been shown in this chapter, the severity of these words begins to make sense. The coming judgment is not impulsive or unexplained. Ezekiel has been taken room by room, layer by layer, through the corruption of Jerusalem: the image of jealousy, the hidden idolatry of the elders, the women mourning for Tammuz, and finally men turning their backs on the temple of the Lord to worship the sun. God is showing His prophet why judgment has become necessary.

And there is mercy even in this.

God did not owe Ezekiel an explanation. He could simply have judged Jerusalem. Instead, He brought His prophet near and showed him what He saw. He exposed what was happening behind closed doors and allowed Ezekiel to understand something of the heart of God in the matter.

This reflects a pattern seen throughout Scripture. Before judging Sodom, the Lord said, “Shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing?” (==Genesis 18:17==). Amos later writes, “Surely the Lord GOD does nothing, unless He reveals His secret to His servants the prophets” (==Amos 3:7==).

The word translated “secret” in ==Amos 3:7== is _sod_, a word that can describe confidential counsel—the kind of conversation shared within an intimate circle. The prophets were not just messengers receiving orders from a distant King; they were invited into the counsel of God. They were allowed to hear what He was saying, understand what He was doing, and then carry that revelation to His people (==Jeremiah 23:18, 22==).

That is what is happening with Ezekiel. Before the glory departs and judgment falls, God shares His heart with His prophet.

Even God's warnings are an expression of mercy. Revelation creates an opportunity to see, understand, repent, and return. Judgment is coming, but it does not come without witness. God has spoken. God has warned. God has revealed what is hidden.

This is one of the remarkable privileges of friendship with God: He is a God who shares His secrets.
