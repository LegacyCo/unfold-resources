## Commentary
“He who corrects a scoffer gets shame for himself, and he who rebukes a wicked man only harms himself.”

Wisdom includes knowing when correction will do no good. That’s an uncomfortable proverb for people who believe every error must be confronted—but Solomon is direct: correct a scoffer and you won’t improve him; you’ll be shamed and harmed for the effort. The next verse gives the diagnostic pair: “Do not correct a scoffer, lest he hate you; rebuke a wise man, and he will love you” (==Proverbs 9:8==). The same rebuke, delivered the same way, produces hatred in one hearer and love in the other. The variable was never your delivery. It’s the heart on the receiving end.

What makes the scoffer uncorrectable? “A fool has no delight in understanding, but in expressing his own heart” (==Proverbs 18:2==). He isn’t in the conversation to learn; he’s in it to broadcast. “A scoffer does not love one who corrects him, nor will he go to the wise” (==Proverbs 15:12==). So Solomon’s counsel is to save your breath: “Do not speak in the hearing of a fool, for he will despise the wisdom of your words” (==Proverbs 23:9==). This isn’t contempt for the person—it’s stewardship of the word. Jesus taught the same discernment: “Do not give what is holy to the dogs; nor cast your pearls before swine” (==Matthew 7:6==). And He practiced it: before Herod, who wanted entertainment rather than truth, the Word made flesh said nothing at all (==Luke 23:9==).

So before you correct, read the room the way wisdom does. Is this a wise heart that will love you for the rebuke, or a scoffer collecting material? Your words are seed, and seed is too valuable to scatter on pavement. Pray for the scoffer—God can plow ground you can’t. But spend your correction where Proverbs says it compounds: “Rebuke is more effective for a wise man than a hundred blows on a fool” (==Proverbs 17:10==).

## Cross References
The new covenant carries the same discernment: ==2 Timothy 2:23-25== (avoid foolish disputes, yet correct “in humility… if God perhaps will grant them repentance”—firmness about truth, realism about hearts), ==Titus 3:10== (a divisive man gets two admonitions, then distance), ==Matthew 10:14== (Jesus told the disciples when to shake the dust off and move on), and ==1 Peter 3:15== (when the hearer is genuinely asking, answer—with meekness and fear).
