## Commentary
David did not fall into sin with Bathsheba in a moment. He descended by degrees. The chapter opens “at the time when kings go out to battle” with David at home, idle on his rooftop. From there the verbs mark the steps down: he saw her, then he inquired about her, then he took her.

That is usually how sin works. It does not walk through the front door fully grown. It begins as something accidental or seemingly innocent that is entertained too long. It gets rehearsed in the mind. And the longer a thought, an image, or a person is given internal real estate, the more normalized it becomes—until we have convinced ourselves that what we are harboring is not that big of a deal because it has not yet bitten us.

James traces the same anatomy: desire conceives, conception gives birth to sin, and sin, when full-grown, brings forth death (==James 1:14-15==). Between the first glance and the final act stand multiple exit ramps—and each one passed makes the next harder to take.

This is why Jesus locates adultery at the first verb rather than the last (==Matthew 5:28==). He is not being needlessly severe; He is fighting the battle where it can still be easily won. David—a man after God’s own heart—shows us that no one is beyond the descent. The easiest place to stop sin is at the first step: refuse the first glance, and the rest of the descent never begins.

## Application
Locate yourself honestly on the staircase. Is there something right now at the “saw” stage—a glance you keep repeating? At the “inquired” stage—someone or something you’ve started researching, following, circling back to? Whatever step you’re on, the nearest exit ramp is the one to take, and it is always wider than the next one down.

Guard the rooftop too. David’s descent began before he saw anything—he was home, idle, at the time when kings go out to battle. Notice when you are most vulnerable: bored, alone, unaccountable, out of your calling. Fill those spaces on purpose. And build accountability at the first verb, not the last: tell someone about the glance. Nobody confesses a fall who wouldn’t have been rescued faster by confessing a look.
