## Commentary
“The grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all people.”

Paul speaks about grace as though it walked into the room.

Grace is often reduced to a theological idea, a favorable attitude in God, or the cancellation of a punishment. Paul says grace _appeared_. It became visible. It took on flesh and entered the helplessness of the human condition in the person of Jesus.

Every religious system offers some version of advice to the drowning man: swim harder, learn from the experience, suffer what you deserve, or follow the correct path back to shore. The gospel announces something no system of human effort could announce: God entered the water.

Jesus didn't stand on the shore explaining how we could save ourselves. He came to seek and save the lost (==Luke 19:10==). The stronger came to the weaker. The living One came to people dead in sin. The Holy One came to those who could never make themselves holy.

That's grace.

Grace is God intervening in our lives to save, strengthen, lead, comfort, and transform a people who could never in a million lifetimes do those things for themselves.

## Application
Where are you still struggling to accomplish what only grace can do?

Take His hand. The admission that you cannot save, change, heal, or sustain yourself is where salvation begins.

## Word Study

- _grace_ : **charis** — favor, kindness, or a freely given gift; help extended without being earned. It carries the picture of one who possesses strength or resources giving help to one who lacks them.

Grace can never become something we possess independently of God. It is always received *from* Him because He alone is its source.

Summary: grace is not humanity reaching high enough to find God. It is God coming down to rescue humanity.
