## Commentary
"Let no corrupt word proceed out of your mouth, but what is good for necessary edification, that it may impart grace to the hearers."

Paul gives our words one job description in this verse: build. Not vent, not impress, not win—*build up according to the need of the moment*. A carpenter doesn't grab just any board; he measures the need and cuts for it. Edifying speech is like that. It pays attention to the actual person in front of us—what they're carrying, where they're weak, what this moment requires—and offers the word that fits *there*. We can talk to be heard, or we can talk to build up.

And look at what Paul says our words can do at their best: *impart grace*. Your mouth can be a means of grace to another person. An ordinary sentence at a kitchen table or in a hospital hallway can deliver the kindness of God into someone's need. That is an astonishing dignity to give to speech.

## Word Study
- *corrupt* : **sapros** (Greek) — rotten, spoiled, decayed. It's the word used for *rotten* fish thrown out of the net (==Matthew 13:48==) and the *bad* tree that cannot bear good fruit (==Matthew 7:17-18==, ==Luke 6:43==).

Summary: a "corrupt" word is not only profanity. It's any word that's begun to rot—and rot spreads. Gossip, cynicism, mockery, constant negativity: these decay whatever they touch, the way one spoiled fish ruins the basket. Paul's contrast is total—nothing rotting out of the mouth, only what builds and delivers grace.
