## Commentary
“Now I say this, that each of you says, ‘I am of Paul,’ or ‘I am of Apollos,’ or ‘I am of Cephas,’ or ‘I am of Christ.’ Is Christ divided?”

Notice something about the Corinthian division: it wasn’t caused by bad leaders. Paul, Apollos, and Peter were all genuine, anointed, gifted men. The problem was what the people *did* with good leaders—they became enamored with the gifts and organized themselves around them. And here’s the tension every gifted leader has to carry: gifting is attractive, and it will always gather people. That’s how the Giver designed the gift of leadership to work (==Ephesians 4:11-12==). But the gift exists to bring people to the *Giver*—never to itself. The moment people camp around the gift, Christ gets divided into fan clubs.

Paul modeled the safeguard in one sentence: “Imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ” (==1 Corinthians 11:1==). Follow me *as far as* I’m following Him—and the moment my path and His diverge, follow Him. That’s a leader keeping himself in the courier position: carrying people toward Jesus, never collecting them. You see the same heart in Paul’s farewell at Ephesus—three years of teaching publicly and house to house, night and day with tears, precisely so he could leave the church standing without him, in the hands of elders under God (==Acts 20:31-32==). The goal of biblical leadership is a people who don’t need *you*—they need Jesus, and you got them closer to Him. Contrast the counterfeit: wolves who rise up “to draw away the disciples *after themselves*” (==Acts 20:30==). Same gathering power. Opposite destination.

So what do kingdom leaders look like? Not brand CEOs building empires on their own gifting, but anointed servants who do what the Holy Spirit does—“He will glorify Me” (==John 16:14==). Doorkeepers in the house of the Lord (==Psalm 84:10==): they open the door, point inside, and stay out of the frame.

## Application
Two quick examinations. If you lead: watch where people land when your gift gathers them. If they leave your teaching talking about you, recalibrate; if they leave talking about Jesus, keep going. Build like Paul—work yourself out of a job by making disciples of Him, not you. And if you follow: thank God for the leaders who feed you, but check whose name is forming your identity. “I am of” anyone but Christ is a fan club, not a faith. Be built up by the gift. Belong to the Giver.
