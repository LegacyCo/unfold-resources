## Commentary
God fed Israel with bread from heaven every morning—but with one strange rule: don’t keep any of it overnight. Some tried anyway, “and it bred worms and stank.” The manna had a built-in expiration date, and it was always the same: today.

That was by design, and the design hasn’t changed. You can’t live off yesterday’s bread. God has built us to need Him *fresh*—the way you need fresh breath. You can’t live this afternoon on the breath you took this morning; breathing is designed to keep you returning to the air. In the same way, God designed us to constantly breathe in the fresh breath of His Spirit—to remain in Him the way we remain in oxygen (==John 15:4==). Yesterday’s encounter was real. Yesterday’s word was true. But stored-up manna was never meant to feed you today; it was meant to teach you where the Bread comes from, every single morning (==Deuteronomy 8:3==).

This is why Jesus taught us to pray, “Give us this day our *daily* bread” (==Matthew 6:11==)—not “give us this month’s supply.” The provision is daily because the relationship is daily. His mercies are new every morning (==Lamentations 3:22-23==)—so stop trying to reheat last year’s revival. Go get today’s.

## Word Study
- *manna* : **man hu** (Hebrew) — literally, “What is it?” (==Exodus 16:15==). Israel named the bread after their own question, because they’d never seen anything like it. God’s daily provision often arrives the same way—unrecognized at first, not looking like what we expected, fresh enough to make us ask.

Summary: the bread that fed a nation for forty years was named “What is it?”—and Jesus answered the question: “I am the bread of life… the bread of God is He who comes down from heaven” (==John 6:33-35==). The manna was always pointing at a Person, and the Person is available fresh, daily.
