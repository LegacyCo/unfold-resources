## Commentary
“So Moses cried out to the LORD, saying, ‘Please heal her, O God, I pray!’”

This is one of the shortest prayers in the Bible. In Hebrew it's five small words: *El na, refa na lah*. “God, please. Heal her, please.” 

The person he's praying for is the one who just attacked him. Miriam challenged his calling and criticized his marriage, and God Himself took Moses' side. If anyone ever had the right to stand back and let the judgment run its course, it was Moses in this moment. But he prays for her instead, and he does it immediately. This is what the humility of verse 3 looks like in practice. A humble man doesn't defend himself when he's wronged, and he takes no pleasure when the person who wronged him gets what's coming.

There's some irony in how the prayer comes about. Aaron is the high priest. Interceding for the people is his job. But he doesn't pray. He turns to Moses, calls him “my lord,” and begs him to do something (==Numbers 12:11==). A few verses earlier Aaron was asking whether Moses was really any different from the rest of them. Now he knows that Moses is.

And the chapter ends with an act of mercy: “the people did not journey till Miriam was brought in again” (==Numbers 12:15==). The whole nation waited for her. 

Moses does this again and again. He intercedes after the golden calf (==Exodus 32:11-14==), after the spies' report (==Numbers 14:13-19==), and after Korah's rebellion (==Numbers 16:22==). Each time, he stands between God's judgment and people who have sinned against God and against him. In that, he points ahead to Jesus, who prayed for the men crucifying Him, “Father, forgive them” (==Luke 23:34==), and who “always lives to make intercession” for us (==Hebrews 7:25==).

## Application
Is there someone who has spoken against you? Pray for them by name this week, and pray for their good. Ask God to heal what's broken in them and to restore them. It doesn't have to be long. Moses' prayer was five words. Jesus told us to “pray for those who spitefully use you” (==Matthew 5:44==), and it's very hard to stay bitter toward someone you're honestly asking God to bless.
