## Commentary
“Rejoice with those who rejoice, and weep with those who weep.”

Paul has said that believers are “members of one another” (==Romans 12:5==). This verse is what that looks like on an ordinary day. When one part of a body is hurt, the whole body reacts, and when one part is honored, the whole body shares in it: “if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; or if one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it” (==1 Corinthians 12:26==).

Paul's instruction is to join people where they are. He doesn't say to advise those who weep, or to explain to them why it happened, or to cheer them up. He says to weep with them. Job's friends did their best work in the first week, when “they sat down with him on the ground seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his grief was very great” (==Job 2:13==). They went wrong when they started trying to help Job out with their own ideas. Proverbs says forced cheerfulness makes grief worse: singing songs to a heavy heart is like taking away someone's coat in cold weather (==Proverbs 25:20==). And Jesus stood at Lazarus's tomb knowing He was about to raise him, and He still wept with Mary and Martha first (==John 11:33-35==).

Rejoicing with people sounds like the easier half. Often it's the harder one. John Chrysostom, preaching on this verse in the fourth century, said it takes more maturity to rejoice with those who rejoice than to weep with those who weep, because sharing someone's joy requires being free of envy. When a friend gets the promotion, the engagement, the baby, or the open door you've been praying for yourself, their good news can feel like your loss. The older brother in Jesus' parable heard music and dancing for his brother, and “he was angry and would not go in” (==Luke 15:28==). John the Baptist shows us the opposite. As the crowds left him for Jesus, he called himself the friend of the bridegroom, who “rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom's voice,” and he said, “this joy of mine is fulfilled” (==John 3:29==).

Both halves of what Paul is saying here require the same thing of you: taking your attention off yourself long enough to feel what someone else is feeling. Jesus did this for us. We do not have a High Priest “who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses” (==Hebrews 4:15==).

## Application
Think of one person who has good news right now and one who is grieving. For the first, congratulate them without bringing up yourself, and thank God for their news out loud in prayer, especially if it's something you wanted. For the second, go and be with them. You don't need to have anything to say. Sit with them, listen, and let their sorrow be yours for a while.
