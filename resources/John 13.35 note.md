## Commentary
“By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another.” Jesus staked His reputation on our love. Not on our apologetics, our traditions, or our events—on whether His people actually love each other. And because the Spirit of God now lives in us, it is finally *possible* for us to be a genuinely unified family. That's the good news. The bad news is it's now *required* that God's people be a unified family. The prayers Jesus prayed on the night He was betrayed demand it (==John 17:21==).

Let me tell you where this verse stopped being theory for our family. One of the reasons the Lord took us to the Middle East was to show us the necessity of His people being family in the midst of suffering. The invitation we were extending to our Muslim friends was, of course, an invitation to receive Jesus—but it came with a staggering cost. Following Jesus could cost them their job, their home, their friends, sometimes their lives—and almost certainly their family. Feel the weight of that: we were inviting people to lose their family to join ours. Which meant the family we were inviting them into had to be *real*—or the family didn’t match the Jesus we were preaching.

One young man who became dear to us paid that price. When he believed, his parents kicked him out—then kidnapped him and held him in the house. His mother was so heartbroken that her son had become a Christian that she threw herself from the roof. She survived, with a shattered hip. And in the weeks that followed, she watched something she couldn’t explain: the love her son still had for her, and the love this new family had for him—and for *her*. They reconciled. In time, that young man led his own brother to Christ. Today he leads others, discipling new believers into the same family that held him. He encountered Jesus—and the family of Jesus was real enough to love him to maturity, and real enough to love the mother who had opposed him.

That’s what Jesus meant. “By this all will know.” Not by this all will be impressed—all will *know*. A watching world, a hostile family, a heartbroken mother on a shattered hip—love among the disciples is the evidence that ends the argument. So the question for every church is not whether our message is true. It’s whether the family we’re inviting people into matches the Jesus we’re telling them about.
