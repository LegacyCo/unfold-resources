## Commentary
“How then shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed? And how shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard? And how shall they hear without a preacher?”

There’s an old story attributed to St. Francis of Assisi. One day Francis said to a young monk, “Brother, let us go into the town and preach!” Delighted to be chosen, the young man followed as they walked the main streets, turned down the alleys, passed through the suburbs, and at last circled back to the monastery gate. Confused, he said, “You have forgotten, Father—we went to the town to preach!” Francis replied, “My son, we have preached. We were preaching while we were walking. We have been seen by many; our behavior has been closely watched… It is of no use to walk anywhere to preach unless we preach everywhere as we walk.” From that story comes the famous saying: “Preach the gospel at all times, and if necessary, use words.”

There’s real truth in the heart of that statement. The most effective gospel witness is a life that looks like Jesus—“the one who says he abides in Him ought himself also to walk just as He walked” (==1 John 2:6==). When the life being lived matches the word being spoken, the world loses its argument; when it doesn’t, the most polished presentation rings hollow. The world is watching to see if we are who we say Jesus is.

But Paul’s question in this verse won’t let the saying stand as written—because words are *always* necessary. How shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard? Your neighbor can watch your kindness for twenty years and never learn from it that Jesus died for their sins and rose again. Sin, repentance, forgiveness, the cross—these have to be explained, and explained by those who have received them. Faith comes by hearing (==Romans 10:17==). So let’s tweak St. Francis: *Preach the gospel at all times—with your life and your words.* Live a life that’s undeniably Christ-like, and then open your mouth and make the announcement your life has been backing up. The lost don’t need us to pick between demonstration and declaration. They need both barrels.
