## Commentary
In Ephesus, Paul meets a group of disciples and something seems missing, so he asks about their baptism. Their answer: “Into John’s baptism.” Paul explains: “John indeed baptized with a baptism of repentance, saying to the people that they should believe on Him who would come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus.” And when they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus (==Acts 19:5==).

Notice that Paul honors John’s baptism—it was authentic, real, and necessary. A baptism of repentance is nothing to dismiss; John’s whole ministry prepared the way for the Lord (==Matthew 3:11==). But notice also that it wasn’t enough. John’s baptism celebrated the *removal* of something—sin confessed, an old life renounced. The baptism of Jesus goes further: it is a baptism *into* something. Into His name. Into His death and resurrection (==Romans 6:3-4==). Into His body (==1 Corinthians 12:13==). Repentance empties the house; union fills it.

That’s a picture of two ways to live the Christian life, and plenty of believers camp at the first one. Their faith is defined by what they’ve left: I quit this, I renounced that, I’m not who I was. Thank God for all of it—but a faith defined only by subtraction eventually runs out of things to remove. The gospel’s destination was never a cleaned-out life; it was an occupied one (==Colossians 1:27==). So take the Ephesian upgrade: don’t settle for celebrating what you’ve been washed *from*. Step into everything you’ve been baptized *into*.
