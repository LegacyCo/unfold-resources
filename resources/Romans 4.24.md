## Commentary
Paul is referring back to ==Genesis 15:6==, where Abraham “believed the LORD, and he counted it to him as righteousness.” But now Paul makes a crucial point: that statement was not written for Abraham’s sake alone. Abraham’s story was preserved in Scripture for *us*.

This means Genesis 15 is not a historical record of one man’s faith. It's a pattern. Abraham becomes a living witness of how God justifies the ungodly: not by works, not by circumcision, not by law-keeping, not by religious achievement, but by *faith*.

Paul then applies that same logic to believers in Christ. Just as Abraham believed the God who gives life to the dead and calls things into existence, we believe in the God who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. Abraham trusted God for life from a barren womb. We trust God for life from an empty tomb.

Paul also shows us that the story of Scripture is unified. Abraham’s faith was looking forward to the promise of God. Our faith looks back to the finished work of Christ and the resurrection that vindicated Him. Different moments in redemptive history, but the same foundation: God speaks, God promises, God raises the dead, and God counts faith as righteousness.

## Application
Do you believe in Him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead? Then *His* righteousness is now *your* righteousness. And contrary to popular belief, righteousness is not something you grow in. You either are righteous or you're not.

Think of it this way; when you believed in Jesus, did you receive a *little* salvation? Of course not. You’re either saved from sin or you're not. 

When I was born I didn't receive a *little* sonship from my parents. The moment I was born, I was a son. And as I grew, I grew in my understanding of what it means to be a son in my father’s house. I learned the culture of his home — what he likes, what he dislikes, what is permissible and what isn’t. But that's a result of growing in *understanding*, not growing in sonship. 

Learning the culture of my father’s family doesn't make me more of a son. It just means I’ve matured and learned how to act in alignment with my identity as a son. 

It’s the same with our Heavenly Father. When we know who we are in Christ — righteous — then we will show the world the beauty of His family and the power of a faith-filled life.
