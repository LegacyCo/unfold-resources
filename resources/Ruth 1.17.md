## Commentary
Ruth’s words to Naomi have become memorialized in wedding ceremonies around the world. Indeed, they are beautiful words of covenantal commitment. But there is nothing sentimental about what Ruth is saying here. She is binding her future to a widow with no visible prospects.

From the outside, Ruth’s decision makes very little sense.

Naomi has nothing to offer her. No husband. No inheritance. No guarantee of security. In fact, Naomi has already tried to release Ruth from obligation. She has told her to go back and made it clear that following her will likely mean a harder road.

But Ruth stays.

And this is where her commitment becomes a lesson for us. Ruth is not attaching herself to Naomi because Naomi represents opportunity. She is attaching herself to Naomi because love has made a claim on her life. Her loyalty is not transactional. It is covenantal.

“Your people shall be my people, and your God my God.”

Ruth is not only choosing Naomi. She is turning toward the God of Israel. She is leaving behind Moab, its gods, its future, and its security, and placing herself under the covering of the Lord. What begins as loyalty to Naomi becomes an act of faith in Naomi’s God.

Ruth’s commitment leads her into blessing and life, though she could not have seen it at the time. Her choice brings her to Bethlehem, to Boaz, to redemption, and eventually into the lineage of David and the Messiah Himself.

But none of that could be seen from Moab. All she had in front of her was a widow and a rumor about Bethlehem.

## Application
This is how faith works. We can't see the end of the story, yet we take the step of faith anyway. We don't always understand the story God is weaving through our decisions of loyalty, sacrifice, and obedience. Ruth simply chose to stay faithful to the person God had placed in front of her, and that faithfulness became the doorway into a future she never could have manufactured for herself.
