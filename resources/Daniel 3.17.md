## Commentary
“If that is the case, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and He will deliver us from your hand, O king. But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we do not serve your gods, nor will we worship the gold image which you have set up.”

Nebuchadnezzar has just taunt the three young men: “who is the god who will deliver you from my hands?” (==Daniel 3:15==). The three men answer him in three statements. First: He is *able* to deliver us. Before anything else, they settle the question of God's capacity—the furnace is not a problem for the God they serve, no matter how many times it is heated (==Jeremiah 32:17==). Second: He *will* deliver us. This is bolder than the first. No recorded promise had been given to them about this furnace; they are not quoting a guarantee, they are declaring their confidence in God's character. They knew their God well enough to expect deliverance from His hand. And then the third statement, the one that turns the confession into worship: *but if not*—we still will not bow (==Daniel 3:18==).

That third clause is where their faith shows its depth. They deliberately unhook their obedience from the outcome. They are not bargaining—obedience in exchange for rescue—and they are not hedging, as if unsure of God. They are acknowledging that the God who is able and willing is also free: He owes them no particular outcome, and their allegiance is not conditioned on receiving one. Their surrender does not depend on whether the furnace stays lit. Habakkuk prayed in a similar way: though the fig tree does not blossom and the fields yield no food, “yet I will rejoice in the LORD” (==Habakkuk 3:17-18==). That's true allegiance to God. If loyalty holds only while the deliverance comes, then the real god was the deliverance, not the Deliverer—and the worship was a transaction, not a surrender. The three men settled their answer before they knew the outcome, which is why they could tell the most powerful man on earth, “we have no need to answer you in this matter” (==Daniel 3:16==). 

This is the consistency that marks a person fully surrendered to the lordship of Jesus. Paul had it—content whether abased or abounding (==Philippians 4:11-12==), because Jesus was King over every city he entered, every ship he sailed on whether it reached harbor or broke apart on the rocks, and every synagogue he spoke in whether it received him or stoned him. When Jesus is King of a life, whatever happens to that life is happening under His rule—so the high places come down, the valleys come up, and the individual stays consistent through both (==Job 13:15==).
