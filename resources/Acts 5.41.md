## Commentary
“So they departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for His name.”

The apostles had just been beaten. And notice what’s completely absent from their response: anger at the men who beat them, surprise that it happened, and any trace of seeing themselves as victims. Instead—rejoicing. Why? Because their Lord had told them the terms up front: “A servant is not greater than his master. If they persecuted Me, they will also persecute you” (==John 15:20==). So when the blows came, the disciples read them correctly—not as evidence that something had gone wrong, but as proof that they belonged to the Suffering King. Jesus had even told them how to respond: “Rejoice and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven, for so they persecuted the prophets who were before you” (==Matthew 5:11-12==).

Now, be honest: the testing of our faith isn’t typically at the hands of a hostile council. It’s at the hands of the people around us. And if our faith is shallow, those pressures will move us into self-pity—we’ll see ourselves as victims of the sins of others. But mature saints know that God uses the people around us to shape us into the image of His Son. Watchman Nee writes in *The Release of the Spirit*, “How often when the Lord deals with us we see only the outward hand of man. This is entirely wrong. We must remember that it is God who is behind all dealings, not our brother or sister or any other visible person… He has predetermined everything; its time, its boundary, and its intensity in order to break our hard-to-deal-with characteristics.”

So the person who’s treating you unfairly? If you can’t handle that, you won’t handle the kind of suffering the gospel is designed to produce in your life. They may actually be God’s grace to you—pressure that exposes weakness so you become more like Jesus and less like yourself. It’s one thing to give your neck to the terrorist for the sake of the gospel. But where are the saints who can wash the feet of Judas—who can serve him the bread and the cup, knowing he betrays with a kiss? That’s the company the apostles walked out of that council chamber to join.
