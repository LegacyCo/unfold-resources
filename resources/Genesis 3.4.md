## Commentary
The serpent insinuates that the fruit won’t hurt Eve like she thinks, it will actually have a *positive* effect…she will become like God. The serpent has brought into question God's character. He caused Eve to doubt the goodness of God, 
	"He's holding out on you. He's mean, obsessively jealous, selfish, and deceptive. 
	He actually has something good for you but He’s refusing to give it to you 
	because He doesn't want you to be like Him” (even though they were made in 
	God’s image and likeness!).

So, again, we see the enemy’s strategy for drawing us in to sin:
	deny there is any danger in it,
	suggest there is actually an advantage to it,
	and continue to misrepresent God.
