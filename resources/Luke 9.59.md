## Commentary
“Then He said to another, ‘Follow Me.’ But he said, ‘Lord, let me first go and bury my father.’”

Listen to the two phrases sitting next to each other in this man’s answer: “Lord” and “let me first.” They cannot both be true. If He is Lord, He is first; if something else comes first, He is not functioning as Lord, whatever we call Him (==Luke 6:46==). The man wasn’t refusing Jesus—he was scheduling Him. And the next volunteer does the same thing with softer words: “Lord, I will follow You, but let me first go and bid them farewell who are at my house” (==Luke 9:61==). Neither man says no. Both say *later*.

Notice that the requests sound completely reasonable. Burying a father was among the most sacred duties of a son; saying goodbye to family is simple courtesy—Elisha was granted exactly that request (==1 Kings 19:20==). Jesus’ replies seem severe (“Let the dead bury their own dead… No one, having put his hand to the plow, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God”) until you see what He’s exposing: not the duties themselves, but the word *first*. The call of God had come *today*, and both men were negotiating for a more suitable tomorrow. Delay dressed as duty is still delay—and Jesus treats it as a kingdom-fitness issue, because a plowman who keeps looking back cuts crooked rows (==Luke 9:62==).

Don’t wait for the conditions to be more suitable in hopes that obedience will be easier tomorrow. It won’t be. Don’t wait for feelings to change so the sacrifice will hurt less. They rarely do. The word that came today was made for today (==Hebrews 3:15==).

## Application
Put your own “let me first” on the table. What has God told you to do that you’ve agreed to in principle and deferred in practice? Some places to look: the phone call He’s asked you to make—to forgive, or to ask forgiveness; the place He’s told you to stop going; the distraction He’s asked you to remove; the part of your calendar He’s asked you to clear; the friend He’s told you to check on. Pick the one you just thought of—that’s usually the one—and do it today, before the day is over. Obedience delayed until conditions improve is a heart quietly learning to say “later” to its Lord.
