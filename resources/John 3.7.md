## Commentary
Nicodemus came to Jesus convinced that the signs proved God was with Him. Jesus responded by telling him that recognizing the signs was insufficient. The kingdom could not merely be observed, studied, or admired. To see it, Nicodemus would need an entirely new birth.

The phrase translated “born again” also means “born from above.” Nicodemus hears Jesus speaking about a second beginning, but Jesus is speaking about a new origin. The life required for God’s kingdom cannot be produced from below. It must come from above.

Our first birth gave us life in Adam’s family. We inherited human nature through our parents, along with a world disordered by sin, fear, death, and separation from God. No amount of education, religious discipline, or behavior management can make that life suitable for the kingdom. Flesh can become more religious, more respectable, and more controlled, but it remains flesh (==John 3:6==).

This is why Jesus does not tell Nicodemus to improve. He tells him to be born.

The gospel does not offer Adam a better version of Adam. It brings Adam’s life to the cross and gives us the life of Christ. By faith, we are joined to Jesus in His death—“I have been crucified with Christ”—and the Spirit who raised Him from the dead gives resurrection life to us (==Galatians 2:20==; ==Romans 8:11==).

Being born again means that God becomes the source of a new kind of life within us. We are born “not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God” (==John 1:13==). We receive a new Father, a new family, a new inheritance, and a new likeness.

Christianity begins with more than believing that Jesus came from God. It begins when the life that came from God is born in us.

## Application
You cannot disciple the old life into becoming the new one. Bring it to the cross.

Stop asking God to make the life you inherited from Adam more manageable. Surrender it, and receive the life that comes from above. The kingdom becomes visible when you begin seeing God, yourself, and the world through the life of Christ rather than through the brokenness of the first birth.

## Word Study
- _born_ : **gennaō** — to beget, give birth to, or bring forth life. Jesus is describing an actual change of origin, not the adoption of a new religious outlook.
- _again / from above_ : **anōthen** — again, anew, or from above. John uses the same word for what comes “from above” in ==John 3:31== and for authority given “from above” in ==John 19:11==.

Nicodemus concentrates on the idea of beginning again. Jesus concentrates on where the new beginning comes from.

Summary: the new birth is not another attempt at the old life. It is life from above being born within us.
