## Commentary
“And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive him, that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses.”

Notice where this verse sits. Jesus has just made one of the most staggering promises about prayer in all of Scripture: speak to the mountain, don’t doubt, believe you receive, and “whatever things you ask when you pray… you will have them” (==Mark 11:23-24==). And then, in the very next breath—before the ink of the promise is dry—He says: *and when you stand praying, forgive.* This is no accident. His other great teaching on prayer works the same way: the model prayer of Matthew 6 is followed immediately by “for if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you” (==Matthew 6:14-15==). Twice Jesus teaches prayer; twice He fastens forgiveness to it. In the economy of heaven, prayers either soar on the wings of forgiveness or they’re weighed down by the chains of offense.

So read the whole passage—don’t cherry-pick the mountain-moving half and skip the forgiveness half. The same Jesus who promised that faith-filled prayer receives also warned that unforgiveness obstructs. Peter says harshness in a marriage does the same thing: it causes prayers to be *hindered* (==1 Peter 3:7==). If you’ve been contending for breakthrough that never comes, it’s worth asking: is unforgiveness jamming the lines? Ask the Holy Spirit to search you. He’s kind, and He’s specific.

Unforgiveness doesn’t only weigh down prayers—it weighs down bodies. Even secular medicine has caught up to this: chronic anger keeps the body in fight-or-flight, elevating blood pressure and stress hormones and raising the risk of depression and heart disease, while forgiveness measurably calms it. Now, maturity is required here: never look at a sick saint and assume unforgiveness—healing is often a mystery, and that assumption is not love. But if you carry both a chronic burden and an old offense, it costs nothing to ask the Holy Spirit whether the two are acquainted. Then stand praying, forgive—and let the prayer go up with nothing tied to its feet.
