## Commentary
“Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump?”

Paul asks this in the middle of confronting a church that was tolerating open, unrepentant sin in its midst—and even feeling broad-minded about it (==1 Corinthians 5:1-2==). His argument isn’t “rules are rules.” His argument is bakery science: leaven doesn’t stay where you put it. Work a little into one corner of the dough and it spreads through the whole lump. That’s how sin behaves in a body of believers.

We lie to ourselves when we think our sin can be self-contained—“it’s my business, it only affects me.” Ask Adam. Why did Eve’s sin reach him? Because the two had become one (==Genesis 2:24==). And the church is one body: “if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it” (==1 Corinthians 12:26==). Unrepentant sin in a member isn’t a private matter; it’s an injury to the body—an infection. And when a body gets an infection, you address it quickly, not because you hate the limb, but because you love the body and infections travel.

That’s the heart behind Paul’s severity here. He’s not policing rule-breakers; he’s protecting a family. And it’s the heart behind how we’re taught to handle situations with each other: restore the one overtaken in a trespass “in a spirit of gentleness” (==Galatians 6:1==)—gentle with the person, serious about the leaven. So deal with your sin quickly, and let others help you deal with it—not because God is waiting to condemn you (there is no condemnation for those in Christ, ==Romans 8:1==), but because people you love are joined to you. In a body, nobody bleeds alone.
