## Commentary
Consider the difference between that Friday and Saturday. All the chaos and activity of Friday; the slaughtering of the lambs (the *Lamb*) throughout the city, the noise, the violence, the blood, the spiritual activity over the city reaching its apex, the earth literally shook and split open, the mid-day sun was darkened, the graves of ancient saints were ripped open, as was the temple veil...it was a city turned upside down. 

And then...rest. 

The city rested. The earth rested. Creation rested.

All four gospels are strangely silent about this particular Sabbath. They reflect the tone of that day. 

Stillness. Silence. Rest.

God could've died on any day, but He chose the day of preparation. He chose to bookend the Sabbath with death and resurrection. The Sabbath is the link and the touchpoint between the cross and the empty tomb. It's where striving and effort not only die, but become entombed. 

The Sabbath is blessed. It has the ability to give life. It is holy. God is in that day.

That particular Sabbath is perhaps the most mysterious day in all of history. It is as if all of creation entered into an ultimate stopping. One giant collective exhale. A massive pause for reflection on what just happened. One giant question; is faith still present or were we deceived?

## Application
God often does His deepest work in us between the disappointment of Friday and the celebration of Sunday. We know how to mourn on Friday, and we all know how to celebrate the victory of Sunday, but very few of us know how to live in Saturday—that day where everything we hoped in has died, but resurrection hasn't yet appeared. 

That space between is where faith is tested. 

*Can I find rest when I don't yet understand?* 
*Can I be still when heaven seems quiet?* 

That Saturday in Scripture, when even Scripture is silent, reminds us that God is always at work even when we can't see it.
