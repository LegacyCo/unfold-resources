## Commentary
Jesus has just described the crowds as “weary and scattered, like sheep having no shepherd” (==Matthew 9:36==), and then tells His disciples, “The harvest truly is plentiful, but the laborers are few” (==Matthew 9:37==). His instruction is to pray that God would send more workers into the harvest.

The word translated “send out” is _ekballō_ (ἐκβάλλω). It is a strong word that can mean to cast out, drive out, expel, or send forth. Matthew frequently uses the same word for the casting out of demons (==Matthew 8:16==; ==Matthew 8:31==; ==Matthew 9:33==). In this verse, it carries the idea of God actively moving laborers out into the work of restoration that needs to be done in the earth.

This language emphasizes that the harvest belongs to God. Jesus calls Him “the Lord of the harvest.” The people being reached belong to Him, the work belongs to Him, and He is the One who calls and sends the workers.

The placement of this command is also significant. Jesus tells the disciples to pray for laborers at the end of Matthew 9, and then immediately afterward He calls the twelve, gives them authority, and sends them out (==Matthew 10:1==; ==Matthew 10:5==).

In other words, the disciples become part of the answer to the prayer Jesus has just told them to pray.

To pray for laborers means we are asking God to call, prepare, and send people into the places where there is need. But it is also a prayer that we ourselves might become the answer to. Those who pray for the harvest should be prepared for the possibility that God may send them.
