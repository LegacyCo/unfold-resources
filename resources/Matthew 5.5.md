## Commentary
“Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth.”

Meekness and humility are often said in the same breath, and they are related—but the best way to distinguish them is this: humility is an attitude; meekness is an action. Humility is loving your enemies; meekness is turning the other cheek. Meekness is strength under control. A horse is meek in that he has enormous strength and power but yields to the authority of the rider. Humility is the horse’s attitude toward the rider; meekness is the horse choosing to restrain his strength for the rider’s sake.

This is why meekness is one of the most challenging qualities of God we will ever demonstrate: it means having the authority and the means to get what you want, and refusing to get it your way. God Himself works in meekness. Meekness was God letting Adam exercise the authority he had been given, even when his decisions corrupted a perfect creation (==Genesis 1:28==). Meekness was God letting Israel’s unbelief delay the promise forty years (==Numbers 14:34==). Meekness was the Son letting soldiers bind Him and lead Him away when twelve legions of angels were a breath from that garden (==Matthew 26:53==). And meekness was Abram giving Lot the first choice of the land when Abram was the one with every right to choose (==Genesis 13:9==).

The promise Jesus attaches is the one Scripture has always attached: “the meek shall inherit the earth” echoes “the meek shall inherit the land” (==Psalm 37:11==). Rather than exalting themselves and demanding their way, the meek wait on the Lord and let Him do the exalting (==Psalm 37:34==). The meek inherit what the world strives for.

## Word Study
**Meek** translates the Greek *praus* (πραΰς)—gentle, mild, strength brought under discipline; the word was used of a powerful animal trained to the bridle. Jesus uses it of Himself: “I am gentle (*praus*) and lowly in heart” (==Matthew 11:29==), and Matthew applies it to the King riding into Jerusalem on a donkey rather than a war horse (==Matthew 21:5==). Peter calls a gentle (*praus*) and quiet spirit “very precious in the sight of God” (==1 Peter 3:4==). Summary: *praus* is not weakness or timidity; it is power that has voluntarily taken the yoke.

## Application
Locate the place in your life where you have both the power and the right to force an outcome—an argument you can win, a position you can leverage, a claim you can press. That place is where meekness is tested. Choosing not to seize what you could take, and trusting God to give what He has promised, is not passivity; it is Abram at the moment of Lot’s choice.
