## Commentary
The Pharisees had just attributed the work of the Holy Spirit to Satan, and Jesus refuses to treat their words as an isolated slip. He traces them to their source: “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” Words are never free-floating. The mouth can only draw from the heart’s supply.

This means no word is truly careless—including the “idle words” for which Jesus says every person will give account (==Matthew 12:36==). Picture the heart as a cup filled to the brim. When life bumps against it—criticism, pressure, an interruption at the wrong moment—whatever fills the cup is what spills over. A cup of water cannot spill orange juice (==Luke 6:45==). Our most unguarded words, then, are not accidents. They are evidence of what is inside.

This is also why speech can never be reformed at the mouth alone. Vocabulary can be managed for a season—the tone softened, the words filtered, the language made to sound more spiritual—but a hard enough bump will always reveal the cup’s true contents. Scripture therefore directs its attention deeper than the lips: “Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life” (==Proverbs 4:23==).

The words we speak when no one important is listening are the most honest report of our inner life. If we want different words to spill out, we do not need a tighter lid. We need a different filling.
