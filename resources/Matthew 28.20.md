## Commentary
“…teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age.”

The Great Commission ends the way every commission in Scripture ends. When God calls a person out and sends them somewhere new, the command to go is joined to the promise of His presence. God tells Joshua, “Go over this Jordan, you and all this people, to the land which I am giving to them… do not be afraid… for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go” (==Joshua 1:2==; ==Joshua 1:9==). He tells Gideon, “Go in this might of yours… surely I will be with you” (==Judges 6:14==; ==Judges 6:16==). He tells Moses, “I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring My people out of Egypt… I will certainly be with you” (==Exodus 3:10-12==). He tells Israel, “I have called you from the farthest regions of the earth… fear not, for I am with you… I will strengthen you, yes, I will help you, I will uphold you with My righteous right hand” (==Isaiah 41:9-10==). He tells Isaac, “Sojourn in this land, and I will be with you” (==Genesis 26:3==). The pattern is the same: every *go* comes with a *with*.

So when Jesus sends His disciples—and everyone who would believe through their word—to make disciples of all nations, He attaches the same promise: “lo, I am with you always.” An old preacher put it provocatively: “You don't get ‘lo, I am with you' without ‘go ye therefore.'” That is not a doctrine to build on—His presence is not earned by activity—but the point is made all the same: it is in the obedient response to the call, and in the ongoing surrender to keep following, that His presence with us becomes *evident*. The ones who go are the ones who find out how true “with you always” is.
