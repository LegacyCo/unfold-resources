## Commentary
This psalm is written during one of the lowest moments of David’s life. He's not running from a foreign army. He is fleeing from his own son, Absalom, who has betrayed him and stolen the throne and, even more heartbreaking, the hearts of the people of Israel. David is leaving Jerusalem in shame, barefoot, weeping, and surrounded by the consequences of both personal sin and family devastation (you can find this story in 2 Samuel 15-18).

David is writing this psalm from the pain of betrayal, humiliation, and danger. He first acknowledges the reality of his pain. But v. 3 brings the shift: “But You, O LORD…”

Those three words change everything. David’s circumstances are real, but they are not ultimate. His enemies are many, but they are not in control. The reality of his situation is a fact, but it doesn't determine the truth of who David's God is...or what David's God will do next.

David calls the Lord his shield. A shield doesn't remove the battle, it stands between the servant of God and what seeks to destroy him. David is not abandoned.

Then David says the Lord is “my glory.” David’s *visible* glory had been stripped from him. He had lost the throne, the city, the approval of the people, and the loyalty of his own household. Everything that once gave him honor was being taken away. Yet David says, “My glory is not in those things. My glory is the Lord Himself.”

And then he says God is “the One who lifts up my head.” Shame lowers the head. Grief lowers the head. Betrayal lowers the head. But *God* restores dignity to His people when circumstances have bent them low.

This doesn't mean David’s situation instantly changes. But it does mean David knows who is ultimately in charge of his situation. 

## Application
There are seasons when what once gave you confidence is stripped away. And in those moments, the question is: where is your glory? What is lifting your head? Is there still a shield in front of you?

If your glory is your job, your relationships, your bank account, you will collapse when those things are taken away. If your glory is people’s opinion, you'll be crushed when their opinion turns. If your glory is control, you'll panic when life happens.

But if the Lord is your glory, then even in the wilderness, your head can be lifted.

David teaches us that faith doesn't deny the presence of difficult situations. Faith declares the greater reality of a sovereign and present God.
