## Commentary
When Peter began to waiver, Jesus said, "O you of little faith, why did you doubt?" The word for doubt here means to have two thoughts, to believe only a little, to question one's heart.

"To believe only a little" is interesting. It's saying there is faith present, which is what Jesus said ("You of little faith"), but it's not *enough* faith. We hear this and think "size," as if Peter's faith needed to be bigger — if only it was the size of a mustard seed, right?! But "mustard seed faith" isn't about the quantity of faith, it's about the quality of faith (see the note on Matthew 17:20).

Mustard seed faith is faith that is ever increasing. Peter's faith began strong, strong enough to get out of the boat in a fierce storm, strong enough to walk on water, but it didn't continue to grow...it stagnated and became mixed with other thoughts. Peter moved from the realm of faith to reason. He took in the information the circumstance around him was communicating. It was a circumstance he had never been in before, that no man had been in before. He was walking on water! As soon as he took that information in, he now had "two thoughts"...the definition of doubt.

This new information built a wall around the faith that got him out of the boat which prevented his faith from growing.

Faith is single-minded. Faith is undivided. Faith is ruthlesslessy narcissitic, thriving when it is the only thing present and detrimentally weakened by the presence of anything that questions its supremacy. Faith doesn't play nice with others. Faith feeds on itself like yeast; growing, expanding, filling all the space around it with itself as long as it has itself to feed on.

## Cross Reference
Other examples of doubt destroying faith:

In Acts 10:20, when Gentiles showed up at his house, Jesus told Peter to go with them with "no doubt." He wasn't to permit this new information that his circumstance had introduced into his life (it suddenly being okay to go with Gentiles to their Gentile home) to stop the growth of faith in his heart that God had "cleansed" what was previously unclean for the Jew.

In Luke 24:38, when Jesus appeared to His disciples after the resurrection and they were afraid, He told them, "Why do doubts arise in your hearts?" Rather than the presence of Jesus increasing their faith, it caused fear to settle in them. They had "two thoughts" about the presence of Jesus now instead of one.

James writes in James 1:6 that when we ask for anything we should ask "in faith, with no doubting, for he who doubts is like a wave of the sea driven and tossed by the wind." If the wind is blowing in the right direction it can help the wave grow and maintain its shape. But if its blowing in the wrong direction the wind will make the wave choppy, and unpredictable. Wind is like the new information introduced to the man of faith. It can be something that grows the man's faith or stunts it.

James says that man who doubts should not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord, "for he is a double-minded man, unstable in all of his ways" (James 1:7-8). Double-minded...literally "a doubter." There is mixture present. Faith can't grow in the presence of another.
