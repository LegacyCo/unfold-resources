## Commentary
You already know this verse. You’ve probably decreed it, declared it, and amen-ed it: “No weapon formed against you shall prosper.” But most of us have only ever used half of it. Listen to Isaiah 54:17 in its entirety: “No weapon formed against you shall prosper, and every tongue which rises against you in judgment you shall condemn. *This is the heritage of the servants of the LORD, and their righteousness is from Me*, says the LORD.”

Their righteousness is from Me. The most quoted spiritual-warfare verse in the Bible ends with a declaration of imputed righteousness—and that ending is not a footnote. It’s the foundation the first half stands on.

Religion loves to quote Isaiah against you: “Your righteousness is as filthy rags” (==Isaiah 64:6==). And to that I say—amen. Any righteousness I try to produce on my own *is* filthy rags. But ten chapters earlier, God had already spoken a better word: the *heritage*—the hereditary possession—of the servants of the LORD is a righteousness that comes from Him. In Christ, I've received the righteousness of God as a free gift of grace (==2 Corinthians 5:21==). Same prophet. Two righteousnesses. One is rags; the other is inheritance.

Now, here’s why the two halves of the verse can’t be separated. The enemy will happily let you shout “no weapon formed against me shall prosper” all day long—as long as he can keep you from knowing you're righteous. Because words declared with the lips that lack an internal witness of faith may sound right, but they carry no authority. Just ask the seven sons of Sceva (==Acts 19:15==).

So could it be that weapons formed against you have prospered because you don't know who you are? Could it be that the tongue rising against you hasn't been silenced because you've resisted the very declaration of righteousness God speaks over you? He wants us to become a people who hear *Him* tell us who we are, not religion, not the world, not our mistakes. “No weapon formed against me shall prosper” is the declaration of the righteous. It works because of who's speaking it.
