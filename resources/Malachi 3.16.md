## Commentary
"Then those who feared the LORD spoke to one another, and the LORD listened and heard them; so a book of remembrance was written before Him for those who fear the LORD and who meditate on His name."

Notice the sequence in this verse. These people feared the Lord and *meditated* on His name—but the book of remembrance wasn't written while they meditated. It was written when they *spoke*. While the meditation stayed inside them, heaven had nothing to record. The moment they opened their mouths to one another, the Lord listened, heard, and had it written down.

There is a connection between what we meditate on and what eventually comes out of our mouths. A thought is only a thought until we give it language. When we speak it, it takes shape—it enters time and space, and according to Malachi, it gets recorded. God pays attention to the conversations of His people. Not just the prayers we aim at Him directly, but the things we say *to one another* when we assume no one important is listening.

What are we saying when we come together? What are we declaring at our dinner tables? What are we speaking to our children and our spouses? What are we saying over our city and church?

Also note what the book is called. It's not a book of evidence. It's a book of *remembrance*—kept by a God who treasures the words of those who fear Him and doesn't want to forget them (==Psalm 56:8==, ==Revelation 5:8==). Our words are on record. For those who fear the Lord, that is not a threat. It's an honor.
