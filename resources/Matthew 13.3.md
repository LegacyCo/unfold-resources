## Commentary
“Then He spoke many things to them in parables, saying: ‘Behold, a sower went out to sow.’”

This is the first parable Jesus tells in Matthew 13, and the timing is significant. Matthew says it happened “on the same day” (==Matthew 13:1==) as the events of chapter 12. And chapter 12 was a hard day. The Pharisees had walked out plotting how to destroy Him (==Matthew 12:14==). They'd accused Him of working miracles by the power of Beelzebub. Before that, whole towns had watched His miracles and still didn't repent (==Matthew 11:20==).

Then Jesus sat down in a boat and told the crowd a story about a farmer whose seed didn't always take root. He knew what that felt like.

Years ago I got frustrated with how little fruit I was seeing when I would share my faith with others. I was sharing the gospel with people, and it seemed like so few were coming to faith. I talked about it with a good friend of mine, a very wise man, and he pointed me to this verse. “Neil,” he said, “the sower sows the seed.”

His point was simple. We share our faith. We sow the word of God into the people around us. What happens next is up to them and God. Our responsibility is simply to sow.

Paul saw it the same way: “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase” (==1 Corinthians 3:6==). The harvest is never ours to produce.

## Application
Think of the people God has put in front of you this week. Confidently tell them what Jesus has done in your life, and leave the results to Him. Your part is to sow.
