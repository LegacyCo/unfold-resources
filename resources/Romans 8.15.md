## Commentary
“You did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, ‘Abba, Father.’”

Imagine a child who has spent years in foster care, moving from home to home. Everywhere he goes he tries to prove he is good enough to stay, always fearing that one wrong move will send him away again. Then one day a family adopts him. They go before a judge, sign the papers, and give him their name: “You are our son now.” Legally, everything has changed.

But that boy may still wake up the next morning feeling like an orphan. He may hide food in his room because he is afraid there won’t be enough tomorrow. He may wonder how long before they decide they don’t want him. His identity has changed; now his mind must learn to believe what his identity already says. That is what repentance is—thinking changed at such a core level that it changes the way we live (==Romans 12:2==).

Notice what the boy does not need to do: he does not become more adopted by following the rules of the house. And as his identity settles in his heart, maturity appears. He unpacks his bags. He stops hiding food. He begins to ask for what he needs. He starts to reflect the culture of his new family. He learns to live as the son he already is (==Galatians 4:6-7==).

The law exposes the orphan mentality in all of us—the fear, the striving, the belief that we could earn our place in the house. But the Father does not stand over His children saying, “Prove that you belong here.” He says, “You are My child. You bear My name. Everything that belongs to My Son has been given to you.”

## Application
Look for the hidden food in your room. Orphan habits survive long after adoption: hoarding (believing God’s provision won’t come through tomorrow), performing (believing acceptance must be re-earned each morning), and flinching (believing one wrong move will get you sent away). Name the one you default to under pressure.

Then practice the adopted child’s reflexes instead. Ask for what you need—orphans scavenge, but children ask (==Matthew 7:11==). Unpack your bags—stop living as if your place in God’s house is provisional. And when fear whispers that you are one failure from the door, cry out what the Spirit within you was given to say: “Abba, Father.” You do not become more adopted by keeping the house rules. You simply learn, day by day, to live like the child the papers already declare you to be.
