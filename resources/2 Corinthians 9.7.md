## Commentary
“So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, not grudgingly or of necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver.”

Notice everything Paul refuses to do in this verse. He doesn’t set an amount. He doesn’t apply pressure. In fact, he rules pressure out entirely—not grudgingly, not of necessity, not under compulsion. Kingdom giving is decided in the heart, between the giver and God, and then given freely. Why is God so careful to protect the *manner* of our giving? Because He isn’t after your money—He’s after givers who share His nature. And His nature is to give with joy: the Father who “so loved the world that He *gave*” (==John 3:16==), the Son who endured the cross “for the joy that was set before Him” (==Hebrews 12:2==). Grudging obedience can move money. Only love can move it cheerfully—and cheerful is the part God loves.

## Word Study
- *cheerful* : **hilaros** — glad, joyous, merry; the word behind our English word *hilarious*. This is its only appearance in the New Testament, and Paul saves it for giving: God loves a *hilarious* giver—one whose giving bubbles up with gladness rather than being squeezed out under pressure.

The word family appears one other place:

- ==Romans 12:8== — “he who shows mercy, with *cheerfulness* (hilarotēs).” Mercy delivered with a long face isn’t the gift God designed; the joy is part of the mercy.

Summary: reluctance can write the same check that cheerfulness writes, but heaven reads the two gifts differently. God’s question about your giving is never just “how much?” It’s “how?”—because the hilarity is evidence that the giver has actually tasted the generosity of God and is passing it on rather than parting with it.
