## Commentary
“If you love Me, keep My commandments.”

That order is important. Love comes first; obedience follows as evidence. John says it plainly in his letter: “For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome” (==1 John 5:3==). Obedience is the proof that we’re authentic lovers of God—people who don’t love in word only, but with the whole heart.

True kingdom obedience is only possible where love is present. If your obedience isn’t undergirded by love, you might still do what God asks—but you’re obeying out of fear of punishment, not because you believe He is good. And perfect love casts out fear (==1 John 4:18==)—not perfectly toeing the line of rule-keeping. This is why religion is so dangerous: it demands obedience without supplying the love that keeps the obedient safe. Religion hands you a set of rules without ever showing you a God worth loving—the god of religion is only a god to be feared.

But when I believe that God is good and kind—that He is exactly as good as Jesus shows Him to be, even when I don’t understand—then obedience changes character entirely. It stops being an occasion for dread and becomes an opportunity for worship. Every act of obedience becomes a way of saying, “I love You, and I trust You.” My obedience becomes the occasion to *express* love, not *earn* it. That’s the difference between a servant of religion and a son in the house—and it’s the only kind of obedience Jesus is asking for in this verse. He doesn’t say, “If you fear Me, keep My commandments.” He says, *if you love Me.*
