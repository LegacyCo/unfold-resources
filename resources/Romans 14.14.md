## Commentary
For the born-again believer, something is either clean or unclean based on the thoughts the believer has about it. What we "consider" about something has the power to render what God has cleansed unclean to us. 

Paul writes in Titus 1:15, "To the pure all things are pure, but to those who are defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure." The individual determines the purity of of what is received, not the thing itself. The unrighteousness of the individual who consumes transforms something pure into something evil.

Is this not Adam's dilemma after the fall? What God says and does is pure, but in Adam's now fallen state, it's filtered through a lens of broken consideration. God is not evil...Adam's consideration doesn't have the power to change God. But Adam's reality is changed, and to Adam, the truth of that new reality is to him as if God himself has become something that He is not. So Adam hides in his sin and shame from a loving God coming to cover him.

Peter experienced this tension in Acts 10:15. The Lord tells him, "kill and eat." Peter responds that he has never eaten anything unclean or common. Jesus tells him, "What God has cleansed you must not call common."
