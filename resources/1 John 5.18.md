## Commentary
“We know that whoever is born of God does not sin; but he who has been born of God keeps himself, and the wicked one does not touch him.”

This is the fifth time John has said this in one short letter. Count them: “I write these things to you so that you may not sin” (==1 John 2:1==). “Whoever abides in Him does not sin” (==1 John 3:6==). “Whoever has been born of God does not sin,” and again in the same verse, “he *cannot* sin, because he has been born of God” (==1 John 3:9==). And now here, closing the letter. Five times. John clearly believed something about the born-again that much of the church has stopped believing: that living free from sin is our inheritance in Christ, not a fantasy for the super-spiritual.

Compare that with the language our traditions have handed us. William Carey—called the father of modern missions—had it carved on his tombstone: “Here lies a wretched, poor, and helpless worm.” Martin Luther described the believer as “snow-covered dung”—righteous on the outside, still filth underneath. These were great men whom God used mightily, but hold their language up against John’s and the two don’t match. One tradition speaks a present-tense wretchedness over the redeemed; Scripture speaks a present-tense new birth. And what we speak over ourselves shapes how we live before God. If you’ve believed for years that you’ll always struggle, that real victory isn’t available until heaven—could it be that the lie has become a self-fulfilling prophecy in your walk with God?

Notice John is no idealist. He put the safety net in the same letter: “If anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous” (==1 John 2:1==). Falling is possible; it just isn’t your nature anymore. The born-of-God person who sins has acted *out of* character, not *in* character—and there is One beside the Father saying so. So here’s the question this verse leaves us with: whose epitaph are you living under—the wretched worm, or the word of the living God? You are born of God. The wicked one does not touch you. Try believing what God says about you, and let *that* become your identity.
