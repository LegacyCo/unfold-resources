## Commentary
“It happened in the spring of the year, at the time when kings go out to battle, that David sent Joab and his servants with him, and all Israel… But David remained at Jerusalem.”

Before Bathsheba is ever mentioned, the text signals that something is wrong in David's decision-making. It was the time when kings go out to battle. Leading the army was one of a king's primary responsibilities, and David sent someone else to fight his battle. The next verse finds him where a man ends up when he has stepped out of his assignment: wandering the rooftop in the evening, looking around (==2 Samuel 11:2==). With no battle to fight, David became idle, and an idle man is a vulnerable man. Within a chapter, the man after God's own heart has committed adultery, arranged the death of one of his most loyal soldiers, and pulled his family into a judgment that followed him to his grave (==2 Samuel 12:10==).

The enemy knows he cannot beat a man on the battlefield who has his eyes on God. So he waits for the man to come home, get comfortable, and wander onto the rooftop. We all have a rooftop—the idle place we drift to when we've stepped out of the battle God has given us. The rooftop is the business trip with the empty hotel room. It's the phone at eleven o'clock at night. And we don't guard our rooftop by building fences around it, but by staying engaged in the battle the Lord assigned us: “Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life” (==1 Timothy 6:12==). A man laying hold of God has no evenings free for the rooftop. David's fall did not begin with a woman bathing. It began in the spring, when the king stayed home from his war.

## Word Study
**Lay hold** in Paul's charge to Timothy is the Greek *epilambanomai* (ἐπιλαμβάνομαι)—a violent apprehension that is, at the same time, the trustful grip of a hand. It's the word Matthew uses when Peter is sinking and Jesus “stretched out His hand and caught him” (==Matthew 14:31==), and the word Paul uses of his own pursuit: “I press on, that I may lay hold of that for which Christ Jesus has also laid hold of me” (==Philippians 3:12==). Hebrews uses it of Christ taking hold of Abraham's seed to help them (==Hebrews 2:16==). Summary: *epilambanomai* is the grip that keeps a man off the rooftop—aggressively grabbing hold of God, because God has already grabbed hold of him.
