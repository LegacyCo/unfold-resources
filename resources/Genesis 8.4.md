## Commentary
There is an important word hidden in this verse.

“The ark rested in the seventh month, the seventeenth day of the month, on the mountains of Ararat” (==Genesis 8:4==).

The Hebrew word translated “rested” is _nuach_ (נוּחַ), meaning to rest, settle, or come to repose. It comes from the same Hebrew root associated with Noah’s name. When Noah was born, his father named him Noah, saying, “This one will give us comfort (*rest*) from our work and the toil of our hands” (==Genesis 5:29==).

Now, after the judgment of the flood, the ark carrying Noah _rests._

This is more than a boat finally running aground. Genesis is telling the story of creation beginning again.

The first creation moved toward rest. God completed His work and rested on the seventh day (==Genesis 2:2–3==). Now the flood has returned the earth to something resembling the watery condition of ==Genesis 1:2==, and God begins bringing order out of the waters again. The waters recede, dry land appears, living creatures emerge, and humanity steps into a renewed world.

And at the center of that movement is _rest._

The world outside the ark is not yet restored. Noah cannot even leave the boat yet. But the ark has stopped drifting. Sometimes rest begins before restoration is complete.
