## Commentary
“Now to Him who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that works in us, *to Him be glory in the church* by Christ Jesus to all generations, forever and ever.”

We love the first half of that doxology—the exceedingly-abundantly God. But look where Paul says the glory shows up: *in the church*. A few verses earlier he said the same thing about God’s wisdom: “the manifold wisdom of God might be made known *by the church* to the principalities and powers” (==Ephesians 3:10==). In this age, the church is the address where God has chosen to put His glory on display. She is the wineskin that holds the wine of heaven.

That has a profound impact on how we pray. We can’t ask the Lord for salvation and miracles and revival to touch our community while staying undevoted to the instrument He extends those blessings through. That’s like expecting my car to drive when it has no gas tank to hold the fuel. Our prayers for God to do new-covenant miracles ring hollow if we aren’t devoted to new-covenant ways—and His way, from Pentecost forward, has been a devoted, gathered people through whom He adds, heals, and reveals (==Acts 2:42==; ==Acts 2:46-47==).

There’s a personal side to this too. My experience has been that I can only go so far in my walk with Jesus before I hit the ceiling of what I can reach on my own. I can’t encourage myself in the middle of a storm—not the way a brother can. Watchman Nee said it plainly in *Further Talks on the Church Life*: “When a person seeks heavenly things as an individual, he cannot obtain them all. This does not mean that an individual has no blessing, but it does mean there are certain things of God that he cannot obtain by individual seeking. God’s riches today are in the church.” An individual can attain real spiritual heights—but God has committed the ultimate things of this age to His church. When I join myself to the Body, the fullness of the Head flows to me freely (==Ephesians 4:16==).

## Application
Line your devotion up with your prayers. Make a short list of what you’re asking God for right now—revival, healing, breakthrough for your community, growth in your own walk. Then ask the honest question: am I devoted to the instrument He pours those things through? If you’ve been seeking as an individual and hitting a ceiling, the next step isn’t a better quiet time—it’s the Body. Show up. Join yourself to a church family, take your place in her rhythm of teaching, table, fellowship, and prayer (==Acts 2:42==), and let what you’re asking God to do in your city start with what He wants to do through a people you actually belong to.
