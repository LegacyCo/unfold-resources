## Commentary
This is from "Spiritual Authority" by Watchman Nee: It is seen then that the failure of Noah became a test to Shem, Ham, Japheth, and Ham's son, Canaan. It revealed who was obedient and who was rebellious. Noah's fall unveiled Ham's rebellion. 

## Application
The failure of another will often reveal the condition of your own heart.

Noah’s fall didn't create Ham’s rebellion. It exposed it.

When an authority figure stumbles, hidden things rise to the surface in those who are watching. One man uses another man’s weakness as an occasion for dishonor, exposure, and self-exaltation. Another responds with grief, sobriety, and covering. The same failure is seen by all, but it doesn't produce the same fruit. It reveals what was already there.

This is why the failures of others are such serious tests. We often think the real issue is the one who fell — a fallen pastor, a hypocritical parent, a leader who didn't finish well. But what you do next speaks volumes about your own character. It reveals what's already living inside of you. Does it reveal the fear of God? Does it produce trembling, mercy, and restraint? Or does it stir something carnal—something eager to expose, eager to speak, eager to lift itself by standing over another man’s shame?

Ham saw nakedness, but the greater tragedy is that he had no instinct to cover it.

When someone ahead of you fails, be careful. Your response to their weakness may reveal more about you than their failure reveals about them. Heaven takes note of the ones who know how to walk backward with a garment in their hands.

A fall in authority is tragic.  
But rebellion in the observer is tragic too.  
And sometimes the second sin is worse than the first.
