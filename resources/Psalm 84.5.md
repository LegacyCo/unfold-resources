## Commentary
“Blessed is the man whose strength is in You, whose heart is set on pilgrimage.”

The image here is of a worshiper whose heart is already turned toward Jerusalem.

Three times each year, the men of Israel were required to appear before the Lord in the place He had chosen to dwell: at Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Tabernacles (==Deuteronomy 16:16==). Once Jerusalem became the established center of worship, these feasts became regular pilgrimages to Zion. Families would leave their towns and villages and make the journey toward the temple, often traveling long distances through difficult terrain in order to worship before the Lord.

So when the psalmist speaks of someone “whose heart is set on pilgrimage,” this was not abstract language. Israel knew what it meant to pack for the journey, leave home behind, and begin climbing toward Jerusalem again.

The Hebrew behind the phrase carries the idea of highways or paths being in the heart. The road to Zion was not just under the worshippers feet; it was already inside him. 

That is what makes pilgrimage such a powerful picture of the life of faith. The blessed person of ==Psalm 84:5== is someone whose inner life has been oriented toward God. His heart has a direction. He may still be far from Jerusalem. The road may still be difficult. He may pass through the Valley of Baca in the next verse (==Psalm 84:6==). But difficulty does not determine his direction. His heart is already set toward the presence of God.

Pilgrimage was not a once-in-a-lifetime experience. Returning to the presence of God was built into the rhythm of their year. Again and again, they turned their faces toward Zion.

The movement of this psalm is beautiful. The psalmist longs for the courts of the Lord (==Psalm 84:2==), calls those who dwell there blessed (==Psalm 84:4==), and now calls blessed those who are still on the way there. Some are dwelling in the house; others are walking toward it. Both are defined by the same desire: they want to be where God is.

A heart set on pilgrimage is a heart that has settled the direction of its life: _I am moving toward God._
