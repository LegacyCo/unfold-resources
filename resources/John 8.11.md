## Commentary
“Neither do I condemn you; go and sin no more.”

Pay attention to the order.

Jesus doesn't say, “Go and sin no more, and then you won't be condemned.” He removes condemnation first. Then, from the ground of mercy, He calls her into a different life.

Religion reverses the sentence. It tells the sinner to change so that God might receive them. Jesus receives the sinner and, within that encounter, gives them the possibility of change.

This is not Jesus becoming soft on adultery. He names her life as sin and commands her to leave it. But condemnation would never have given her the power to become different. Shame can expose what someone has done, but it cannot create a new heart. Only grace can do that.

The religious leaders brought her to Jesus as evidence in an argument. They reduced her to the worst thing she had done and used her humiliation to test Him. Jesus restored her dignity before addressing her behavior: “Woman, where are those accusers of yours?”

He saw a woman where they saw a case.

Then the only One present who had the right to throw the first stone refused to do it. “Neither do I condemn you.”

This is the same order Paul later announces: “There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus” (==Romans 8:1==). Freedom from condemnation does not give us permission to remain in sin. It gives us solid ground from which to walk away from it.

Jesus does not condemn you into holiness. He releases you from condemnation and calls you to follow Him out.

## Application
Some people continue condemning themselves because they believe shame will keep them from sinning again. It will not. Shame keeps your eyes fixed on the person you *were*. Grace shows you who you can now become in Christ.

Hear both parts of what Jesus says. Receive “Neither do I condemn you,” and then obey “Go and sin no more.”

Grace speaks first, but grace never leaves you where it found you.
