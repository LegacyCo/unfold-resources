## Commentary
“Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, were born as many as the stars of the sky in multitude—innumerable as the sand which is by the seashore.”

This verse pairs the promise with the impossibility. God had said Abraham's descendants would be as the stars (==Genesis 15:5==), and the man He said it to was “as good as dead”—a hundred years old, with a wife whose womb was dead (==Romans 4:19==). Faith did not deny the reality of the situation; it considered God faithful in spite of it, which is precisely what the previous verse says of Sarah: “she judged Him faithful who had promised” (==Hebrews 11:11==). God chose barrenness as the starting point so that the multitude could only be explained by Him.

This is the pattern of the God who called Abraham. He calls people out of what they have into what they cannot see, and He brings life out of what is as good as dead. The question for anyone walking Abraham's journey is where God wants to take them, and what area of barrenness He wants to give life to. Whatever a person lets go of in order to take hold of Him is not a loss; it is leaving behind a cheap, temporary substitute for what God has—which is always eternal, authentic, supernatural, and glorious. The life God brings a person into is not guaranteed to be easy, but it is guaranteed to be full of Him.

## Application
Name the barren place—the area of your life where, by every natural measure, nothing more can come: a relationship, a calling, a prayer prayed for years. Instead of concluding that it is finished, do what Sarah did and judge Him faithful who promised. Bring that specific barrenness to God and ask Him for the multitude He alone can produce from it. The father of the faithful was as good as dead when the promise came true.
