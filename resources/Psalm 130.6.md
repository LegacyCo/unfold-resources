## Commentary
“My soul waits for the Lord more than those who watch for the morning—yes, more than those who watch for the morning.”

A watchman on the wall in the last hours of the night is not passive—he is at his post, alert, eyes on the horizon. And he is not uncertain. Of all the things a watchman wonders about, the morning is not one of them; dawn has never once failed to come. His waiting is expectation with a guarantee. That's the posture the psalmist claims toward the Lord, and then doubles for emphasis: *more* than watchmen for the morning. More alert. More certain.

Waiting is one of the least practiced forms of prayer, and one of the most commended. The Psalms return to it more than twenty times: “On You I wait all the day” (==Psalm 25:5==); “I wait for the LORD, my soul waits, and in His word I do hope” (==Psalm 130:5==); and the promise attached to it—“those who wait on the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles” (==Isaiah 40:31==). Waiting is prayer defined by faith rather than volume: stillness, silence, patience, attention fixed on the Lord for His direction rather than working through a list. It believes that even when there is no response in the moment, no tangible experience, the heart of God has been touched—because He has been waited on.

It is also genuinely difficult, which is why so few practice it. Art Katz writes in *Apostolic Foundations*: “In priestly waiting, every fleshly thing, every human contrivance and device… every lazy, fearful tendency to take the easy and cheap way out, will rise to the surface. A mind battle takes place… To patiently dismiss those thoughts, and bring our minds to a place of rest in God, free from distracting thoughts, does not come readily… The greatest exploits of the last days will come from those who make waiting on God a habitual aspect of their priestly life.” The watchman’s advantage is a settled certainty: morning is coming. The one who waits on the Lord holds the same certainty about Him—and stays at the post until He comes.
