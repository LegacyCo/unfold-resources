## Commentary
“When He ascended on high, He led captivity captive, and *gave gifts* to men.”

Here’s a way to discern the difference between the kingdom of God and the kingdoms of this world: the world gives itself titles; Jesus gives gifts. The world hands out titles to tell people what we do—I’m an electrician, I’m a worship leader, I’m a youth pastor. And a self-given title lasts exactly as long as it benefits us. The minute I stop enjoying being a hair dresser, I stop calling myself one and do something else. Titles are ours to pick up and ours to put down.

Gifts don’t work that way. When Jesus gives a gift, the gift defines you—and it holds you until He says otherwise. Listen to how Paul introduces himself: “Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ *by the will of God*” (==2 Corinthians 1:1==). You want to say, “Paul, look at yourself—scarred, shipwrecked, hunted, in and out of prison. Why don’t you do something different with your life?” And Paul would answer the same way every time: I’m an apostle by the will of God. He didn’t give himself the role, so hardship couldn’t talk him out of it (==Galatians 1:1==). A title quits when serving it stops paying. A gift keeps serving when everything else quits.

Now notice *when* the gifts are given: “when He *ascended*.” The gifts of the kingdom flow wherever the King is enthroned. When Jesus is actually exalted as Lord in a local church family—not just named in the songs, but given the keys—He entrusts that house with gifts: apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, teachers, for the equipping of the saints (==Ephesians 4:11-12==). But where something else sits on the throne of a church—a brand, a personality, a program—the titles multiply and the gifts run dry, because kingdom authority only comes from the King.

## Application
Run the diagnostic on yourself. Ask: which of the roles in my life did I take because they benefit me, and which did Jesus put on me? A simple test separates them: what happens when it gets hard? The self-given title starts polishing its résumé; the God-given gift plants its feet, because it answers to the will of God and not to job satisfaction. Then check your appetite for recognition. If you find yourself wanting people to know your position, do what kingdom leaders do: put the title down and pick up a towel (==John 13:14==). In this kingdom, nobody needs to be told who the leaders are. They’re the ones washing feet.
