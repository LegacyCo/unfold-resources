## Commentary
The works of God were never meant to be hidden from the next generation. Israel was commanded to tell their children what the Lord had done: how He delivered them, provided for them, judged rebellion, showed mercy, and remained faithful when His people were not.

Psalm 78 says these things were to be told so the next generation would “set their hope in God,” remember His works, and keep His commandments (==Psalm 78:5–7==). In other words, testimony was meant to become formation. The stories of God’s faithfulness were supposed to shape the faith of those who had not yet seen those works for themselves.

This same command appears in Deuteronomy 6, where Israel is told to teach the words of God diligently to their children in the ordinary rhythms of life (==Deuteronomy 6:4–9==). It also appears in Joshua 4, when stones from the Jordan River became a memorial so future children would ask, “What do these stones mean?” (==Joshua 4:6–7==, ==Joshua 4:20–24==).

What one generation forgets, the next generation may never know.

## Application
We are responsible to tell the next generation who God is, what He has done, what He has spoken, and why He can be trusted. If we do not pass on the testimony of the Lord, we should not be surprised when the next generation has no category for His faithfulness.

Faith is not inherited automatically. It must be taught, modeled, remembered, and declared.
