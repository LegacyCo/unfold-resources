## Commentary
Overwhelmed by what he’s seen, John falls at the feet of the angel to worship. The angel refuses: “See that you do not do that! I am your fellow servant… Worship God! For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.”

That last sentence defines prophecy at its core. The animating spirit of all true prophecy is the testimony of Jesus. Prophecy isn’t fundamentally about predicting events or reading the headlines; it’s about revealing a Person. This is exactly what Jesus said the Spirit would do: “He will glorify Me, for He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you” (==John 16:14==). So here’s a reliable test for any prophetic word: does it unveil Jesus? A word can be dramatic, detailed, even accurate—but if it doesn’t ultimately bring you closer to Him, it has lost the very spirit of prophecy.

And notice what this verse means for *you*. If you hold the testimony of Jesus—if you believe He is who He says He is—then you have the spirit of prophecy. Access to the voice of God is not reserved for a gifted elite; Paul told an entire congregation, “you can all prophesy one by one, that all may learn and all may be encouraged” (==1 Corinthians 14:31==). The doorway into prophetic things is not a platform or a following. It’s the testimony of Jesus.

The scene itself preaches one more thing. Even this glorious messenger—fresh from delivering the revelation of heaven—refuses to be the center of attention: *I’m a fellow servant. Worship God.* That’s the posture of everything genuinely prophetic. When the messenger becomes the attraction, something has gone wrong with the message. The true prophetic voice does what the angel did: it deflects every spotlight to the throne.
