## Commentary
“Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her.” Whatever biblical headship means, Paul locks its definition to a single reference point: the way Christ loved the church. And how did He love her? He gave Himself.

Watch how John describes the night Jesus washed feet: “Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands, and that He had come from God and was going to God, rose from supper and laid aside His garments, took a towel and girded Himself” (==John 13:3-5==). The sentence hinges on the word *knowing*. Because Jesus knew exactly who He was—where He’d come from, where He was going, what the Father had placed in His hands—He didn’t need to prove anything. He could go low. Identity settled is what makes self-giving possible.

That’s the freedom the gospel hands a husband. When I don’t know who I am, I have to work by the sweat of my brow to prove to you that I’m not who my insecurities say I am—and men who lead from insecurity dominate, defend, and demand. But the gospel speaks identity over insecurity. I know who I am as a son of God, so I don’t have to prove it to my family. I get to *show* it to them. The new covenant doesn’t give a husband permission to be a dictator, and it doesn’t ask a wife to become invisible. A man can undermine the very cross that freed him from bondage by placing his wife and children in bondage to his personal power trip.

You want to know God’s design for authority? Real simple: the one with the authority is the first one to the cross. Men, you want the anointing of authority? Grab a towel and learn to wash the feet of your family. The Christian husband, rightly seated in his God-given place in the home, will have scuff marks on the knees of his pants.

## Application
Husbands, audit your leadership by the towel, not the title. Where in your home are you demanding what Christ would have absorbed, defending what He would have laid down? Pick one concrete act of self-giving this week that costs you something real—your evening, your preference, your being right—and do it without announcing it. Headship isn’t proven from the head of the table. It’s proven on the floor, at their feet.
