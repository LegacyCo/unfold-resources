## Commentary
“Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

The order of Paul's verbs in this chapter is intentional: *knowing* (==Romans 6:6==; ==Romans 6:9==), then *consider* (==Romans 6:11==), and only then *present* (==Romans 6:13==). Paul does not ask believers to do a single thing until they know who they are. And because of union with Christ, whatever is true of the risen Christ in relation to sin is now true of you: He died to sin once for all, He lives to God (==Romans 6:10==)—so conisder the same of yourself.

Most of us get identity backwards. We think what we do determines who we are: I cook food, so I'm a chef; I drive trucks, so I'm a truck driver; I sin, so I'm a sinner. So when Scripture says you are dead to sin, alive to God, a saint, a son, something in us says, “That can't be about me”—not because God's word is unclear, but because we already decided who we are based on our behavior, and then we read the Bible through that name. If you are in Christ, your identity is no more “sinner” than a whale washed up on the beach has become a sunbather. The whale has acted contrary to its nature; the Creator still calls it a whale. When you act contrary to your new nature, God does not revoke your identity. You are still a son—a son still maturing (==1 Corinthians 1:2==). Righteousness came the way it came to Abraham: not by growth but by transfer, credited in one transaction on the basis of faith (==Romans 4:3==; ==2 Corinthians 5:21==). You don't grow *into* righteousness. You grow *from* it.

## Word Study
**Reckon** is the Greek *logizomai* (λογίζομαι)—an accounting word: to put to one's account, to enter on the books. It's the same word Paul uses of Abraham: “Abraham believed God, and it was accounted (*logizomai*) to him for righteousness” (==Romans 4:3==), and of the blessed man “to whom the Lord shall not impute (*logizomai*) sin” (==Romans 4:8==). In chapter 4, God does the crediting; in chapter 6, the believer does—counting as true what God has already entered in the ledger. Summary: *logizomai* is not pretending or positive thinking; it is agreeing with God about who you are.
