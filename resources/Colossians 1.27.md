## Commentary
Paul says God willed to make known “what are the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles: which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.” Hidden for ages and generations—and now revealed. Not Christ *near* you. Not Christ *for* you. Christ *in* you.

Think of a lightbulb. Why was the lightbulb created? To be filled with light. That’s its entire purpose—a bulb that’s never filled with light is a failure at the only thing it was made for. Now: why were you created? To be filled with God. You weren’t created just to be *with* God; you were created to be filled with Him. “He who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him” (==1 Corinthians 6:17==).

When you look at a glowing lightbulb, do you see the lightbulb or do you see the light? When *can* you see the lightbulb? When the light is off.

We were designed to be so filled with God that the world no longer notices us—only the Light in us. That’s not the erasure of your personality; it’s the fulfillment of your purpose. Jesus said it plainly: “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven” (==Matthew 5:16==). They see the works; they glorify the Father. The bulb shines; the Light gets the glory.

And He is in you for both reasons at once. He dwells in you because He loves you and wants to be with you—union was always the point. But He also dwells in you to shine His life on everyone around you. He wants to demonstrate His divine life through your ordinary one. Because the world doesn’t need more lightbulbs. The world needs light. The world needs the living God who lives inside of you.

## Word Study
- *mystery* : **mystērion** (Greek) — not a puzzle to be solved but a secret once hidden that has now been revealed. In Scripture, a mystērion isn’t something unknowable; it’s something no one could figure out on their own until God unveiled it (==Ephesians 3:3-5==).

Other places Paul uses the word:

- ==Ephesians 5:32== — marriage as a great mystērion “concerning Christ and the church”
- ==1 Timothy 3:16== — “great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifested in the flesh”
- ==Colossians 2:2-3== — the mystery of God, “in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge”

Summary: you don’t have to figure out God’s mysteries—He reveals them to His people. Which means the mystery of union isn’t for scholars. It’s for lovers. It’s for those with faith like a child.
