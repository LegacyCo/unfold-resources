## Commentary
Paul was not merely separated *from* something. He was separated *to* something. His life had been redirected by the gospel of God.

Before Christ met him on the road to Damascus (see Acts 9:3-9), Paul was already a separated man. As a Pharisee, he lived set apart according to the strictest traditions of Israel. He was separated by law, by zeal, and by religious identity. But after his encounter with the Messiah, everything changed. He remained separated, but what he was separated by, and to, changed.

He was no longer separated by his own righteousness. He was separated to the gospel of grace. The message that once offended him and drove him to endorse murder became the message that took possession of him ("The love of Christ compels me" - 2 Corinthians 5:14). The Christ he once persecuted became the Lord he now served.

This is the power of the gospel. It lays claim to the man and reorders his ambitions. The gospel had become the defining reality of Paul's life.

And notice whose gospel it was: “the gospel of God.”

It wasn't Paul's gospel. It belonged to God. It was from Him, revealed Him, and returned all glory to Him. Paul was simply the servant who had been taken captive by the gospel of grace (see Galatians 1:15-16 and 1 Corinthians 9:16).

To be separated to the gospel of God means grace has become the governing announcement over your life. You belong to the God who saves sinners, raises the dead, keeps His promises, and gives righteousness as a gift through Jesus Christ.

Paul was set apart by grace and set apart for grace. His life would now be spent announcing the message that had remade him: that God has acted in Jesus Christ to do for sinners what they could never do for themselves.
