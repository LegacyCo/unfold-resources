## Commentary
"But I say to you that for every idle word men may speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment."

Jesus doesn't say we will give account for every idle *thought*. It's the idle *word* that has consequences. This is both a mercy and a responsibility.

Here's the mercy. A thought is only a thought. The mind is the place where transformation happens—the workshop where ideas about God are tested, where thoughts are measured against the truth of His word and either renewed or rejected (==Romans 12:2==, ==2 Corinthians 10:5==). While a thought is still in that process, it is not yet my testimony. I am not judged for the raw material that passes through my mind.

Here's the responsibility. The moment I give that thought language, it leaves the workshop as a finished product. It takes shape, enters time and space, and goes on record as the fruit of my heart. I am responsible for it now. That's why kingdom people learn to let their thoughts finish the renewal process *before* releasing them through the tongue.

## Word Study
- *idle* : **argos** (Greek) — literally "without work" (*a* + *ergon*). An unemployed word. A word with no job to do.

Where the word shows up elsewhere:

- ==Matthew 20:3, 6== — laborers standing *idle* in the marketplace, hired by no one
- ==1 Timothy 5:13== — those who learn to be *idle*, wandering from house to house as gossips and busybodies
- ==2 Peter 1:8== — the qualities of Christ keep us from being *barren* (argos) and unfruitful in our knowledge of Him

Summary: an idle word is not necessarily a vulgar or vicious word. It's a word doing nothing useful—carrying no truth, building no one up, accomplishing no purpose. Jesus says even those words are on record. If our unemployed words will be reviewed, how much more should we put our words to work—giving them jobs like blessing, encouragement, truth, and life.
