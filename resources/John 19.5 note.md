## Commentary
Jesus has been arrested and scourged. The soldiers have twisted together a crown of thorns and thrown a purple robe over His shredded back. And Pilate—a Roman politician trying to wash his hands of a Jewish dispute—leads Him out to the crowd and says three words: “Behold the Man!”

Pilate had no idea what he was saying. But hidden inside that Roman governor’s announcement is a thread that runs through the entire Bible—because for centuries, heaven had been searching for exactly what Pilate was unknowingly presenting. “I sought for a man among them who would make a wall, and stand in the gap before Me on behalf of the land… but I found no one” (==Ezekiel 22:30==). That’s the story of the whole Old Testament: God looking for a man. A man to deliver His people. A man to show the world what He is like. A man righteous enough to take back the authority that the first man handed to the enemy in the garden (==Luke 4:6==). Moses, Noah, David, Othniel—each one a partial answer, none of them sufficient. God kept looking, and the gap stayed open.

Then one Friday morning, in front of a screaming crowd, a pagan governor stands next to a bleeding Galilean and announces the end of the search: *Behold, the Man.* Here He is. The Man God had been seeking since Eden. The Man who would stand in the gap on behalf of the land—and stay there, arms stretched out, until the wall was rebuilt with His own body. Put it this way: if the Old Testament is the story of God looking for a man in the earth, the New Testament is the story of God revealing that Man to the earth.

And the Man is still the point. God “has appointed a day on which He will judge the world in righteousness by *the Man* whom He has ordained,” and He gave assurance of it by raising Him from the dead (==Acts 17:31==). There is one Mediator between God and men—“the *Man* Christ Jesus” (==1 Timothy 2:5==). Because of what the Last Adam did in another garden (==1 Corinthians 15:45==), there is a Man seated on the throne of heaven right now, scars and all. The scandal of the incarnation isn’t only that God became man. It’s that the victory of that Man placed humanity inside the circle of the Trinity.

Pilate thought he was displaying a defeated prisoner. He was unveiling the answer to heaven’s oldest search. Behold the Man.
