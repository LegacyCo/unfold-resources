## Commentary
“But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under the law.”

There are two ways to live on the far side of repentance, and Paul’s sentence draws the line between them.

People of the wilderness stay stuck in the cycle of guilt and confession. They’re always trying to fix themselves. They’re often bitter—it’s hard to forgive others when you can’t forgive yourself. They relate to God through distance and effort, forever trying to get *back* to Him.

People of the Spirit are aware of sin but not defined by it. They’re free, not just forgiven. They’re led by the Spirit, not by their mistakes. They know they’re in process, and they receive grace when they fall short. They’re walking *with* God, not campaigning to be readmitted to Him.

The difference comes down to where the eyes are. One is always looking at themselves. The other is too fascinated with the beauty of Jesus to be worried about themselves.

That’s why Paul could write, “I do not even judge myself. For I know of nothing against myself… He who judges me is the Lord” (==1 Corinthians 4:3-4==). That isn’t arrogance or carelessness about sin. It’s what freedom sounds like—a man so settled in the Lord’s verdict over his life that he’s stopped running his own courtroom.

## Application
Read the two descriptions again and be honest about which one sounds like your week. If it’s the first, the way out isn’t more self-examination—that’s the wilderness's favorite trap. It’s a redirected gaze: fixing your eyes on Jesus, the author and finisher of your faith (==Hebrews 12:2==). Close the courtroom. Let the Judge who already ruled in your favor lead you out of the wilderness.
