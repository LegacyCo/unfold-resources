## Commentary
In Judges 3, Israel is suffering under the oppression of Cushan-rishathaim because they have turned away from the Lord. But when the people cry out, God raises up Othniel as their deliverer: "...And his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim. So the land had rest forty years.”

The pattern is significant. Israel cries out, God raises up a judge, the enemy is defeated, and the land enters into rest. Rest does not come through ignoring what's wrong. It comes when God confronts the power that has brought His people into bondage.

In Scripture, judgment is not always opposed to rest. Sometimes judgment is the very thing that makes rest possible. God judges the enemy in order to deliver His people. He exposes what has enslaved them, brings it under His authority, and restores peace where oppression once ruled.

This is seen most fully in Christ. At the cross, God judged sin, defeated the powers of darkness, and broke the authority of the enemy. Jesus is the greater Judge and Deliverer who went out on behalf of His people and secured the rest we could never win for ourselves.

Even God’s discipline in our lives carries this restorative purpose. He does not confront sin to condemn us, but to remove what is disturbing His order and keeping us from freedom. His judgments are intended to bring everything back under the rule of His kingdom.

Othniel’s victory gave the land rest for forty years, but the rest Christ gives is deeper and lasting. We are no longer under the dominion of the enemy. We have been delivered into the kingdom of the Son, and we now live under the rule of the One whose judgment has made peace possible.

God’s judgment against what oppresses us is an expression of His mercy toward us. He confronts what steals our peace so He can bring us into rest.
