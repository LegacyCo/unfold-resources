## Commentary
“armed with bows, using both the right hand and the left in hurling stones and shooting arrows with the bow. They were of Benjamin, Saul's brethren.”

The first men named at Ziklag came from the last place you'd expect: Saul's own tribe. Benjamin had more to lose than any other tribe if David became king. The throne belonged to them. Saul was family, and staying with him meant staying close to power. Even years later, verse 29 says “the greatest part of them had remained loyal to the house of Saul.” So these men went against their family, their tribe, and their own self-interest. And they did it while Saul was still alive and still hunting David.

Why would they do that? Scripture doesn't record all the details, but the story tells us enough. Saul had already been told that God had torn the kingdom from him and given it to “a neighbor of yours, who is better than you” (==1 Samuel 15:28==). Even Jonathan, Saul's own son, said to David, “You shall be king over Israel, and I shall be next to you. Even my father Saul knows that” (==1 Samuel 23:17==). So the house of Saul knew. Most just wouldn't accept it. These Benjamites did. When loyalty to family ran contrary to loyalty to what God had said, they went with what God had said.

And they didn't come empty-handed. They could sling and shoot with either hand, which fits what we know about Benjamin. Back in the days of the judges, the tribe had seven hundred left-handed men, and “every one could sling a stone at a hair's breadth and not miss” (==Judges 20:16==). Every skill they'd sharpened in Saul's Israel now went to work for David. 

Jesus said His followers would face this same choice: “He who loves father or mother more than Me is not worthy of Me” (==Matthew 10:37==). Centuries later, another Benjamite named Saul had to choose between his own heritage and God’s King. We know him as the apostle Paul. He had every advantage his background could give him—“of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of the Hebrews”—and he counted all of it loss “for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord” (==Philippians 3:5-8==).
