## Commentary
“But now we have been delivered from the law, having died to what we were held by, so that we should serve in the newness of the Spirit and not in the oldness of the letter.”

Paul tells us we have been delivered from the law and then immediately tells us why. We have been delivered from the law—having died to what once held us—*so that we should serve*. Deliverance from the law is not deliverance from serving God. The believer who has died with Christ serves as much as he ever did; what has changed is the entire manner of the service. Paul sets the two modes side by side: the oldness of the letter, and the newness of the Spirit. The letter is the written code standing outside a man, commanding him, and condemning him when he fails—“the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life” (==2 Corinthians 3:6==). The Spirit is God Himself dwelling inside the man, producing from within what the letter could only demand from without. This is what God had promised through Ezekiel: “I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes” (==Ezekiel 36:27==).

This is why serving God in the new covenant is not the old religion with better forgiveness attached. It is a different kind of service altogether—the obedience of a living union rather than a legal arrangement (==Romans 7:4==), sons walking with their Father rather than employees keeping the handbook (==Galatians 4:6-7==).

## Word Study
**Newness** is the Greek *kainotēs* (καινότης), from *kainos*—new in quality and kind, not merely new in time. Greek has another word for recent, *neos*; *kainos* means something of a different order than what came before. Jesus put both words in one sentence when he taught the parable of new wine and new wineskins: men pour new (*neos*) wine into renewed (*kainos*) wineskins (==Matthew 9:17==)—the wine is newly made, but the skins must be new *in kind*, because the old kind cannot hold what is being poured. Paul uses *kainotēs* only twice, both in Romans: we walk in “newness of life” (==Romans 6:4==) and serve in “newness of the Spirit” here. The family of the word runs through everything the gospel gives us: the new covenant (==Luke 22:20==), the new creation (==2 Corinthians 5:17==), the new man (==Ephesians 4:24==), and the One who declares, “Behold, I make all things new” (==Revelation 21:5==). Summary: the service of Romans 7:6 is not the old service. It is service of a different kind entirely, the kind only the indwelling Spirit can produce.
