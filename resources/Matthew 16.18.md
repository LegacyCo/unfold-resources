## Commentary
“You are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.”

Peter receives the revelation of who Jesus is: “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” Jesus immediately responds by revealing what He intends to build.

His church.

That order is significnant. The revelation of Christ gives way to the revelation of His church. The Christ Peter has confessed is a King, and the King is building a people.

This is the first time the word _church_ appears in the Gospels, and Jesus speaks of it possessively: “My church.” It does not belong to a pastor, denomination, movement, or generation. Jesus purchased her with His blood, joins every member to Himself, and remains the Head over everything she becomes (==Acts 20:28==; ==Ephesians 1:22==).

He may find us alone, but He does not leave us alone. He calls us out of isolation and brings us into a family. Salvation is personal, but it is never private. Everyone joined to Christ is joined to everyone else who is in Christ.

This means following Jesus will eventually require following Him into the complicated, beautiful, sometimes painful life of His people. We may want Jesus without the misunderstandings, offenses, responsibilities, and inconvenience of the church. But the Jesus we want to follow is always found in the midst of the people He is building.

The church is imperfect because it is made of people still being transformed. But her imperfections have not changed His devotion to her. He has not abandoned the building project.

Neither should we.

## Application
Have you attempted to separate devotion to Jesus from devotion to His people?

Following Jesus means allowing Him to place you in His family—not just attending a gathering, but becoming known, accountable, useful, and committed. You cannot receive the Head while refusing the Body He has joined to Himself.
