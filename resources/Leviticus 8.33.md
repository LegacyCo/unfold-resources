## Commentary
Aaron and his sons had been washed, clothed, anointed, and consecrated, but they were still not permitted to begin serving. Before they could move toward the altar, they had to remain for seven days at the entrance of the tent of meeting. The waiting was an essential part of their ordination.

Those seven days stood between the call of God and the work of God. Aaron and his sons had to learn that being chosen did not give them the right to move whenever they desired. They could not use their consecration as permission to act independently. They were required to remain where God had placed them until the time appointed by Him was complete.

There are deep and subtle forms of ambition that can hide themselves inside a sincere desire to be used by God. We may speak of serving Him while secretly desiring recognition, significance, influence, or the satisfaction of feeling necessary. Waiting exposes these motives because it removes the activity through which they are usually fed. It asks whether we still want to serve God when He gives us nothing visible to do for Him.

The doorway became a place of death before it became a place of entrance. Aaron and his sons had to die to the need to prove their calling or hurry into the work for which they had been chosen. They could only enter the priesthood as men who had first learned to remain before God without demanding an opportunity from Him.

This is what Paul describes in Galatians 2:20: “I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me.” The waiting of consecration brings the self that wants to serve God for its own fulfillment to the cross. God is not just preparing our gifts for His use; He is bringing to death the person who wants to possess and direct those gifts. Only then can what we do for God come from the life of Christ within us rather than from a need to establish ourselves.

As Art Katz wrote, "The radiance of God is obtained in the place of waiting in the presence of God, by those who believe that there is such a place, and have the faith to enter it, and live and move and have their being from that place. Then the world will know the difference between the sacred and the profane."

## Application
Pay attention to what comes to the surface when God gives you no clear opportunities. Resentment, comparison, restlessness, and the urge to force a door open often reveal that the work has become more important to us than obedience...and the waiting is exactly what God uses to remove expose *and* remove those things.  Instead of asking God to accelerate the opportunity, allow the delay to search you. The person who can wait without becoming offended is becoming qualified for God to use.
