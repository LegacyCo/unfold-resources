## Commentary
“Then Jesus, being filled with the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was *led by the Spirit* into the wilderness.”

Notice who did the leading. Not the devil—the Spirit. The devil showed up in the wilderness, but he didn’t send Jesus there. And he never sent you there either. The devil never sent anyone to the wilderness, because he knows what most of us forget: the wilderness is the last place he wants God’s people to end up. Our enemy would rather keep us comfortable, distracted, and surrounded by noise. Unlike us, he sees the wilderness for what it really is—a place of deep communion between God and His people, where the voice of the Holy Spirit isn’t competing with a hundred other voices (==Hosea 2:14==).

Run the pattern through Scripture. It was God who brought Israel into the wilderness (==Deuteronomy 8:2==). It was God who raised up John the Baptist in the wilderness (==Luke 1:80==). It was the Spirit who led Jesus into the wilderness. And look at what followed each one: Israel came out of the wilderness into the Promised Land. John came out of the wilderness preaching the message that prepared the way for the Messiah. Jesus came out of the wilderness “in the power of the Spirit” and launched His ministry (==Luke 4:14==). All three wilderness seasons came right before a major breakthrough. That tells me the wilderness is not a punishment—it’s an invitation to promotion. There are things God wants to say to you and show you that are necessary for your next season, and they can only be found out there.

So resist the urge to rebuke your wilderness as an attack from the enemy. Jesus rebuked the devil in the wilderness—but He never rebuked the wilderness.

## Application
Change how you pray in the barren season. Instead of “God, get me out of here,” try “God, what did You bring me here to hear?” Rebuke the devil when he shows up—Jesus did, with the written word in His mouth (==Luke 4:4==)—but don’t rebuke the place. Then guard the gift of the quiet: the wilderness strips away noise so the Spirit’s voice comes through clearly, so don’t rush to refill the silence with distraction. If God has you in a stripped-down season, assume promotion is on the other side of it—and let Him finish saying what only the wilderness lets you hear.
