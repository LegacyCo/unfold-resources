## Commentary
This verse give us one of the few direct sayings of Jesus preserved outside the Gospels.

It's easy to think that a life that is blessed by God will look like having enough and no longer needing anything from anyone. In other words, we often mistake self-sufficiency for blessing.

But Jesus confronts that idea of what the blessed life will look like.

In the Kingdom of God, the mark of contentment and rest is no longer self-sufficiency. It is the freedom to give. The truly blessed person is not the one who has built a life so insulated that no one can make demands on them. The blessed person is the one who has learned that God is their supply, and because of that, their hands can stay open.

Paul quotes Jesus in the context of his own ministry. He worked hard. He did not covet silver or gold. He was not building a life around taking from the church, but pouring himself out for the church. His life had become evidence of the words of Jesus he quoted.

“It is more blessed to give than to receive” does not mean receiving is wrong. We are all receivers before we are givers. Everything we have comes from God. But once grace overwhelmed us, we are no longer trapped in the anxious preservation of self.

The gospel frees us from living exclusively for ourselves.
