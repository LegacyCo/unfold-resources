## Commentary
“Behold, the heavens were opened to Him, and He saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and coming upon Him.”

Follow the progression of the chapter. John preaches repentance in the wilderness. Jesus goes out to him. Jesus enters the waters of John’s baptism—and comes up out of the water under an open heaven, with the Spirit descending and the Father’s voice speaking over Him.

Here’s the staggering part: God never intended that scene to be the exclusive testimony of Jesus. It’s the pattern for everyone who is now *in Christ*. That’s the mystery of union. His death is now your death. His resurrection is now your resurrection (==Romans 6:4==). His life is now your life (==Galatians 2:20==). And His baptism—which John said would be a baptism of the Holy Spirit and fire (==Matthew 3:11==)—is now your baptism. The promise of the Spirit, Peter preached, “is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off” (==Acts 2:38-39==).

So the open heaven over the Jordan isn’t a scene you admire from the riverbank. In Christ, you’re in the water. What was declared over Him is declared over all who are His; the Spirit who rested on Him is the Spirit He gives.
