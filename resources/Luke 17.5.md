## Commentary
“And the apostles said to the Lord, ‘Increase our faith.’”

Look at what prompted this request. It wasn’t Jesus telling them to heal the sick. It wasn’t the assignment to raise the dead, cast out demons, or carry the gospel to the ends of the earth. None of those drove the apostles to cry out for more faith. What broke them was this: “If he sins against you seven times in a day, and seven times in a day returns to you, saying, ‘I repent,’ you shall forgive him” (==Luke 17:4==). Seven offenses. One day. Same person. Full forgiveness, every time. And the apostles—men who would later shake cities—heard that and said, *Lord, we can’t. Increase our faith.*

They were right to ask, because extravagant forgiveness is a miracle category. It runs against everything the flesh demands—justice, distance, at least a little coldness until the debt feels acknowledged. Forgiving like Jesus forgives is not a personality trait some people are born with. It’s supernatural, which is why it requires exactly what the apostles asked for: faith. Faith that God saw what happened. Faith that releasing the debt doesn’t mean the wrong didn’t matter. Faith that the One who said “Vengeance is Mine” (==Romans 12:19==) handles justice better than your resentment ever will.

So if forgiving someone feels impossible today, take the strange comfort this verse offers: the apostles thought so too. Jesus never pretended otherwise—He knows what forgiveness costs; He has the scars to prove it. The answer isn’t to grit your teeth and try harder. It’s the apostles’ prayer, borrowed word for word: *Lord, increase my faith.* He answered them, and He’ll answer you—because what is impossible with men is possible with God (==Luke 18:27==).
