## Commentary
"However, the Most High does not dwell in temples made with hands..."

This is the sentence that got Stephen killed. He had walked the council through their whole history—Abraham, Joseph, Moses, the tabernacle, David, Solomon—and then he arrived at the temple they were standing near and said the thing you were not allowed to say: God doesn't live here. He quotes Isaiah to prove it—"Heaven is My throne, and earth is My footstool. What house will you build for Me?" (==Isaiah 66:1-2==)—and the room turned. They stopped their ears, rushed at him, and picked up stones (==Acts 7:57-58==).

Notice the irony. Stephen was a *living* stone—full of faith and the Holy Spirit, his face like the face of an angel (==Acts 6:15==). And men who loved their building made of dead stones used dead stones to kill him. That's what happens when a structure becomes more sacred to us than the God it was built for. We will defend the house against the very people He wants to dwell in.

Where *does* the Most High dwell, then? Stephen already knew, and Paul would soon write it: "you are the temple of God and the Spirit of God dwells in you" (==1 Corinthians 3:16==)—"being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit" (==Ephesians 2:22==). God is not going to possess a building, a denomination, or a ministry brand. He is going to possess a *people*: broken, messy, hard-to-get-along-with living stones, conformed to the image of His Son and fitted together for His glory.

Here's the sobering part. The people who stoned Stephen weren't pagans. They were Bible-loving, God-fearing men guarding what God had given their fathers. The greatest enemy of what God is building is often what God *built*—a generation ago. Traditions and structures that once served His purpose can quietly become the thing that keeps Him out. Stephen wasn't against the temple. He was for the God who had outgrown it.
