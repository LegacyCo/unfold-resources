## Commentary
Regarding this passage, Art Katz wrote this in The Temptation of Christ, "Jesus's answer seems to acknowledge that there is value for bread, but not the whole value. Man shall not live by bread alone. There is a place for bread, but do not let it obtain a place more than its true worth. Do not give to material things a value greater than what God gives. Do not sell your soul for things that God does not value. Do not be blinded. There is a value to bread, but its value is within the place of God's intention. It is not alone that you live by bread; there is something else, namely, the Word of God, *every* Word of God. In what way, then, is the Word of God life-sustaining? How much of our life is centered in our bodily needs? Esau gave up his inheritance for a bowl of pottage (==Genesis 25:33==), for he was a man who lived eminently in his flesh and in the realm of his sensual being. And for him, self-gratification was his life."
