## Commentary
“Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision.”

Standing before a king with his life on the line, Paul summarizes decades of ministry in one sentence—and the summary is not a list of accomplishments. It is fidelity to what he was shown. The vision came from heaven; obedience to it was his part. That division of labor explains the strangest sentence in Paul's missionary career: “I labor, striving according to His working which works in me mightily” (==Colossians 1:29==). Paul labored—but according to *His* working. Because the vision is God's, the responsibility to bring it to pass is God's. He supplies not only the vision but the resources to fulfill it, the wisdom to walk it out, and the power to beat back every opposition to it. The briefest moment of His power working through a person will accomplish more than a lifetime of their best efforts ever could (==Zechariah 4:6==).

There is a lie the flesh tells the servant of God: you don't have time for the secret place when the world outside is dying. The truth is that He wants to save and heal the dying world more than we do—and He has a particular way of doing it. If we don't know His ways, we will be offended when He acts like Himself (==Isaiah 55:8-9==). The question is not, “If I seek Him, what will happen to the lost world outside?” The question is, “Will I sit with Him long enough to receive His vision for the lost world outside?” Paul could stand before Agrippa unashamed because he had done both: he had seen, and he had obeyed. A ministry that can give Paul's answer—not disobedient to the heavenly vision—has the only success heaven measures.
