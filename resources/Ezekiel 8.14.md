## Commentary
Ezekiel is brought to the north gate of the temple, where he sees women “weeping for Tammuz.”

Tammuz was a Mesopotamian fertility deity associated with the seasonal cycle of vegetation. His supposed death was mourned with ritual lament, and his return was connected with the renewal of fertility and life. The women Ezekiel sees are participating in that pagan mourning ritual within the temple complex itself.

They are performing this pagan mourning ritual at the very place where Yahweh had chosen to dwell with His people.

The tragedy is especially sharp because Israel is mourning for a false god who was believed to die and return with the seasons while turning away from the living God who had actually given them life, land, rain, harvest, and covenant blessing.

Throughout ==Ezekiel 8==, the prophet is taken deeper and deeper into the corruption of Jerusalem’s worship. The image of jealousy, the hidden idols of the elders, and now the women mourning Tammuz all reveal the same reality: the temple still bears the name of Yahweh, but the affections of His people have been given to other gods.

Idolatry is ultimately a disordering of love. Israel were grieving for, seeking from, and placing hope in what could never give them life.
