## Commentary
“As they ministered to the Lord and fasted, the Holy Spirit said, ‘Now separate to Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.’”

The mission did not begin with a strategy meeting, an assessment of regional need, or Paul’s personal desire to fulfill his calling. It began with a community ministering to the Lord. They were not gathered primarily to receive instructions. They were giving themselves to God.

Then the Holy Spirit spoke.

This reveals the order of spiritual sending. First there is priestly ministry to the Lord; then there is apostolic movement toward a lost world (==Acts 1:8==). Apostolic sending has its origin in worship. The work was not invented by human compassion or ambition, even though it would meet desperate human need. It originated in the heart of God and was released in the timing of God.

Paul and Barnabas could go because they had first been set apart to Him (==Exodus 28:1==). They could carry the fire of God into the nations because they had stood before the One who is a consuming fire. As Dr. Robert Gladstone once said, “If it’s not sent of God it will not move in the authority of God.”

We often reverse the order. We decide what must be done, begin moving, and then ask God to bless it. Acts 13 shows a people who remained before the Lord until His desire became clear (==Luke 24:49==). They did not use prayer to secure approval for their plans. Their plans were born from prayer.
