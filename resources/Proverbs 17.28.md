## Commentary
“Even a fool is counted wise when he holds his peace; when he shuts his lips, he is considered perceptive.”

Silence is so strongly associated with wisdom that it can make even a fool look wise. That’s not a joke at the fool’s expense—it’s a measurement of how much weight restraint actually carries. The verse right before it states the principle positively: “He who has knowledge spares his words, and a man of understanding is of a calm spirit” (==Proverbs 17:27==). The wise man *spares* his words—the way you spare money, because you know what they cost and what they’re worth.

Proverbs returns to this economy of words over and over. “In the multitude of words sin is not lacking, but he who restrains his lips is wise” (==Proverbs 10:19==)—more words, more exposure; every added sentence raises the odds that something sinful, careless, or destructive slips through. “A fool’s mouth is his destruction, and his lips are the snare of his soul” (==Proverbs 18:7==)—the fool is not usually trapped by his enemies; he’s trapped by his own words. “Do you see a man hasty in his words? There is more hope for a fool than for him” (==Proverbs 29:20==).

The restraint Solomon commends isn’t timidity, and it isn’t having nothing to say. It’s rule over the impulse to say it—the calm spirit of a person who doesn’t need to fill silence, win every exchange, or broadcast every reaction. James carries the standard into the new covenant: “let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak” (==James 1:19==). How much of what you said today actually needed saying? The wise have learned what the fool stumbles into by accident—that many of the words we spend purchase nothing, and some of them are expensive. Spare your words. They’ll be worth more when you do spend them.

## Word Study
- *spares / restrains* : **chasak** — to withhold, hold back, keep in reserve; the deliberate act of not spending something you possess. It’s the word in both ==Proverbs 17:27== (“he who has knowledge *spares* his words”) and ==Proverbs 10:19== (“he who *restrains* his lips is wise”).

Watch what else this word withholds:

- ==Genesis 22:12== — “you have not *withheld* (chasak) your son, your only son, from Me.” The costliest thing Abraham had, held ready either to give or to keep.
- ==Psalm 19:13== — “*Keep back* (chasak) Your servant also from presumptuous sins.” David asks God to do for his conduct what the wise do with their words.

Summary: chasak treats words like Abraham’s Isaac—something precious enough that withholding or releasing them is a weighty act. The fool spends words like pocket change. The wise hold them like an inheritance, and release them on purpose.
