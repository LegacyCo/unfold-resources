## Commentary
“And it shall come to pass afterward that I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions.”

Every phrase of this promise dismantles a boundary. Under the old covenant, the Spirit came upon particular people for particular tasks—prophets, priests, kings, craftsmen. Joel announces a day when the Spirit will be poured out on *all flesh*: sons and daughters—both sexes; old men and young men—every generation; and in the next verse, menservants and maidservants—every social station (==Joel 2:29==). Gender, age, and status, the three walls that organized the ancient world, all come down in one promise. What Moses could only wish for—“Oh, that all the LORD’s people were prophets and that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!” (==Numbers 11:29==)—Joel writes down as a scheduled event.

And Scripture tells us exactly when the schedule was kept. On the day of Pentecost, when the crowd demanded an explanation for a room full of Spirit-filled believers speaking the wonderful works of God, Peter stood up and reached for this text: “This is what was spoken by the prophet Joel” (==Acts 2:16-18==). Not “this resembles Joel” — *this is that*. The pouring out had happened. Then Peter closed his sermon by throwing the promise open to his hearers and beyond: “the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call” (==Acts 2:39==). All who are afar off includes us.

## Word Study
- *pour out* : **shaphak** — to spill, to pour freely and completely; the word used for emptying a vessel, not measuring from it. Under the old covenant the Spirit was given by measure, to some. Shaphak is the language of abundance without restraint—God tipping the vessel over.

Where else the word appears:

- ==Psalm 62:8== — “Pour out (shaphak) your heart before Him.” What God asks of us in prayer is what He promises to do with His Spirit—nothing held back.
- ==Lamentations 2:19== — “Pour out (shaphak) your heart like water before the face of the Lord.” The picture is total: water poured out cannot be gathered back.

Summary: God did not promise to sprinkle His Spirit on a qualified few. He promised to pour—freely, completely, on all flesh. That is the covenant you were born again into.
