## Commentary
Jesus had just said the hardest thing He ever said: unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you (==John 6:53==). Many of His disciples couldn’t take it—“This is a hard saying; who can understand it?”—and they walked away (==John 6:60==, ==John 6:66==). Jesus didn’t chase them down to explain Himself. He turned to the twelve and asked if they wanted to leave too.

Peter’s answer is one of the great confessions in Scripture precisely because it isn’t a resolution: “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.” Peter doesn’t claim to understand the saying. He acknowledges the difficulty—and stays. He plants himself in the tension of the mystery because of Who is speaking.

That’s the posture the table asks of us. Our instinct is to dilute Jesus’ words when they’re difficult—to sand “this is My body” down to “this *represents* My body” so it fits more comfortably in our categories. But we don’t get to soften His words just because they stretch us. And honestly, we’re inconsistent about it: we have no problem believing that Peter’s shadow or handkerchiefs from Paul’s hands could carry healing power (==Acts 5:15==; ==Acts 19:12==). Why should the celebrated body and blood of the Savior, received in faith, carry less?

## Application
Next time you take communion, don’t rush to make it manageable. Come to the table like Peter—honest about the difficulty, anchored in the Person. A simple prayer to bring with the bread and the cup: “Holy Spirit, call me up into the mystery.” The greatest things at that table can never be fully explained. Only received.
