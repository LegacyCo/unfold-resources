## Commentary
“Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock in Horeb; and you shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it, that the people may drink.”

God is merciful about where He starts with us. Israel was thirsty, faithless, and quarreling, and God met them right at their point of need: *strike the rock*, and water came. Sometimes we don’t yet have the maturity to step into a command with no explanation, so He graciously supplies reasons, details, and instructions we can hold onto.

But don’t mistake where God *meets* you for where God intends to *leave* you. The strike wasn’t a formula—Moses didn’t launch a strike-the-rock ministry, pulling the same move every time Israel got thirsty. It was a starting point. Almost forty years later, God called Moses higher: “*Speak* to the rock before their eyes, and it will yield its water” (==Numbers 20:8==). Same need, same God, new level—less method, more faith. By then Moses had seen the glory on Sinai and spoken with God face to face (==Exodus 33:11==); he had a history with God that should have made explanations unnecessary. The instruction changed because the man had grown—or should have.

That’s how walking with God works. Early on, the boundaries are wide and the explanations come freely. As intimacy deepens, He entrusts you with commands that come with less explanation—not because He’s withholding, but because your history with Him is now supposed to carry the weight the explanations used to carry. He meets you where you are so He can take you where He’s going. So wherever you are in your walk today, trust both halves of that sentence: He’s not ashamed of your starting point, and He has no intention of leaving you there.
