## Commentary
“As the deer pants for the water brooks, so pants my soul for You, O God. My soul thirsts for God, for the living God.” That’s the sound of a man alive with desperate passion for more of God. David talks the same way in Psalm 63: “My soul thirsts for You… my flesh faints for You… earnestly I will seek You” (==Psalm 63:1==). Hunger, thirst, longing, burning—this is the vocabulary of *normal* Christianity.

Which exposes the tragedy of much of modern western Christianity. It’s not that God is withholding Himself—His voice in the word, dreams, answered prayer, the still small voice, His manifest presence when we gather. It’s that we’ve learned how to function without these things. We still believe. We’re still saved. But we’ve become skilled at living the Christian life without needing God on a regular, ongoing basis.

Leonard Ravenhill diagnosed it in one sentence: “We will never have revival as long as we are content to live without revival.” Expand that and it starts to sting. We’ll never see healing if we’re content to live without seeing people healed. God will never speak to us in dreams if we’re content to sleep without them. We’ll never see the lost saved if we’re content to live without the lost being saved. And a church will never experience the power of God when it gathers if it’s content to gather without the power of God.

Here’s what makes this dangerous: distraction doesn’t kill the longing—it buries it. We get so consumed with the cares of this world that we can’t even *feel* our spirit’s longing for more of God, and then we mistake the numbness for normal. But numbness is not normal. The deer panting at the water brook is normal. If Psalm 42 sounds foreign to you, don’t adjust the psalm down to your experience. Ask God to raise your experience up to the psalm.
