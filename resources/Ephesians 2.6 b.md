## Commentary
God “raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus.”

The Christian life does not begin with walking; it begins with sitting. Before Paul speaks of the good works prepared for us in Ephesians 2:10, he declares that we are already seated with Christ in verse 6. Our work proceeds from a finished position, not toward one.

The world teaches us to labor until we have earned identity. The kingdom gives us identity in Christ, then teaches us to work from what we have already received. We do not labor in order to become accepted sons and daughters. We labor because the Father has already brought us into His house, and He has works prepared beforehand for us to walk in (see ==Ephesians 2:10==).

This order confronts the flesh because the flesh wants to establish itself through effort. It finds satisfaction in motion and visible progress—verifiable metrics. Rest requires us to acknowledge that the decisive work has been accomplished by Another (==Hebrews 4:10–11==).

To sit with Christ is not inactivity. It is the end of self-originated activity. From that place, we rise to walk in works prepared by God rather than works manufactured by ambition. We cease trying to earn a position and begin expressing the life of the One in whom we are seated (==Colossians 3:1–4==).

We sit, then we walk. We receive, then we give. We rest in His work, then participate in ours.
