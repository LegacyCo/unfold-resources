## Commentary
“Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution.”

There are no qualifiers in this sentence. Not “some who live godly,” not “those called to hard places,” not “believers in hostile nations.” *All.* Paul has just recounted his own persecutions—Antioch, Iconium, Lystra (==2 Timothy 3:11==)—and then he widens the statement to include every believer who desires to live godly in Christ Jesus. This verse is not just for the persecuted church in other parts of the world. It is a plain statement about the normal Christian life, anywhere it is actually lived.

Notice what draws the opposition: not obnoxiousness, not poor judgment—godliness. A life genuinely conformed to Jesus confronts the world around it simply by existing, the same way His life did. “If they persecuted Me, they will also persecute you” (==John 15:20==). A servant is not greater than his master, and the world has not changed its opinion of the Master. Persecution takes different forms in different places—in some nations it costs blood; in ours it more often costs reputation, relationships, opportunities, and standing. But the principle holds: godliness provokes a response.

This is why Scripture tells us not to be surprised when it comes (==1 Peter 4:12==)—and why the absence of any friction at all is worth examining. If a believer’s life never provokes anything, the question is not whether the world has grown tolerant, but whether the godliness is visible. Jesus pronounced a blessing over the ones this verse describes: “Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness’ sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven” (==Matthew 5:10==). The gospel never promised us the world’s approval. It promised us the kingdom.

## Cross References
The New Testament states this expectation everywhere: ==John 15:18-20== (“if they persecuted Me, they will also persecute you”), ==Acts 14:22== (“we must through many tribulations enter the kingdom of God”—preached, notably, in the very cities where Paul had just been persecuted), ==Philippians 1:29== (“to you it has been granted on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in Him, but also to suffer for His sake”), and ==1 Peter 4:12-14== (don’t think it strange; the Spirit of glory rests on the reproached).
