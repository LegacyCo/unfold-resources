## Commentary
Nicodemus asks the question any of us would ask: “How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb and be born?” (==John 3:4==). And Jesus answers him in his own terms: “Most assuredly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.”

“Born of water and the Spirit” isn’t describing two different births—it’s describing the one total born-again experience. Jesus is reaching back to a promise Nicodemus, as a teacher of Israel, should have recognized immediately: “I will sprinkle *clean water* on you, and you shall be clean… I will give you a *new heart* and put a *new spirit* within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh… I will put *My Spirit* within you” (==Ezekiel 36:25-27==). Washed with clean water, and indwelt by the Spirit—that’s the water-and-Spirit birth Jesus is talking about. And then He explains why nothing less will do: “That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit” (==John 3:6==). Flesh can only reproduce flesh. Natural life, no matter how moral or religious it becomes, can never turn into spiritual life. It has to be *born*, from a different parent.

Remember who Jesus is saying this to. Nicodemus was a Pharisee—an expert in the law, a man of high moral character and real devotion to God, a teacher of Israel. If anyone could have qualified for the kingdom by religious achievement, it was this man. And Jesus tells him, in effect: your first birth, with everything you’ve built on it, can never see what you’re looking for. Sure, you've been born of this earth—but have you been born from above? You've been born of the flesh—but have you been born of the Spirit?

That’s the question this verse leaves with every religious person, every moral person, every person raised in church: not “how much have you built on your first birth?” but “have you had the second one?” Flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God (==1 Corinthians 15:50==). But to all who received Him, He gave the right to become children of God—born “not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God” (==John 1:12-13==).
