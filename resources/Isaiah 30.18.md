## Commentary
The blessing of those who wait sounds beautiful until waiting is no longer theoretical. Waiting is easy to admire in someone else, but much harder when you're the one doing the waiting. Especially when your flesh is restless and the people around you are telling you to make something happen.

But Isaiah says the one who waits is blessed.

Why? Because waiting on God is not passivity. It's faith with skin on it. Waiting on God is refusing to lean on your own understanding when God has not yet spoken. It's standing still when every anxious part of you wants to move.

Sometimes God closes a door and does not immediately open the next one. And in that space, waiting can feel like failure. You are no longer doing what you used to do, but you are not *yet* doing what you will do. To everyone else, it may look like nothing is happening.

But heaven does not measure faithfulness by visible momentum.

There is a kind of suffering that comes from obeying God when obedience makes you look unsuccessful. Jesus said, “Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness’ sake” (==Matthew 5:10==). And sometimes that suffering is not persecution from enemies, but the pressure of misunderstanding.

Waiting exposes what activity can so easily hide. It reveals whether we trust God, or only trust Him when He keeps us busy. It reveals whether we can obey Him when obedience looks fruitful, and when obedience looks like standing still.

Those who wait are blessed because they are choosing God over self-preservation. And in the kingdom, standing still at the word of the Lord is not failure. It's obedience.
