## Commentary
The result of Noah finding grace in the eyes of Yahweh is that Yahweh shares His heart with Noah. God doesn't just tell Noah that judgment is coming; He tells him *how* salvation will be provided. He gives Noah instructions for building an ark, a vessel through which he and his household will be preserved.

This is a pattern of grace. Grace doesn't warn us about what's coming so we can find a way around it. Grace provides a way through it.

Noah finding grace for himself also means Noah finds grace for future generations. His obedience becomes the means by which a remnant of Adam’s line is preserved. Through Noah, humanity is carried through the waters of judgment and brought into a cleansed world on the other side.

We should be careful not to let familiarity with this story weaken its significance within the larger story Scripture is telling. Noah’s preservation not only impacts the generations after him, it also reaches back to those who came before him.

In Genesis 3:15, God promised that the seed of the woman would come and crush the head of the serpent. That promise depended on the preservation of Adam’s line. If humanity is wiped from the earth, the promise dies with them. But Noah finds grace. A remnant is preserved. The promise stands.

The ark, then, is not just a story about survival. It's a story about covenant faithfulness. God judges evil, but He does not abandon His promise. The flood may cover the earth, but it cannot bury the word God spoke in Eden.

Noah’s ark carried more than animals and eight human beings. It carried the promise of redemption. It carried the hope that one day a Son of Eve would come, pass through judgment Himself, and crush the serpent’s head forever.
