## Commentary
“As iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend.”

Sharpening requires contact, friction, and resistance. One of the ways God introduces structure and restriction into a believer's life is through people He has given authority to—people who help define safe boundaries for His family. This is the iron in the proverb. The enemy perverts authority, and restriction is sometimes misused to control and wound people; but the abuse of a thing God uses for His glory is not a reason to discard it. It is a reason to examine fruit.

Damon Thompson frames the test this way: “If there is an authority in your life that is bringing restriction, you have a question to ask: is this a perversion of authority that's controlling and manipulating me? If it is, pack your stuff and go somewhere else. But if that person loves you, then the restriction is actually helping you become who you're designed to be. And you're going to need that iron to help sharpen your iron.” He goes further: “The people who understand God's government and authority will ask for more restriction. Because they know it's increasing the necessary infrastructure around them. It's giving them a greater capacity for flow.” Loving authority that brings restriction is not opposition to your growth; it is the friction that produces the edge. Scripture assumes this kind of relationship is normal in God's house—“obey those who rule over you, and be submissive, for they watch out for your souls” (==Hebrews 13:17==)—and treats the person who refuses all sharpening as a fool (==Proverbs 12:1==).

## Application
Name the people God has placed in your life with the authority to restrict you—a pastor, a mentor, a spouse, a leader—and ask the honest question: do they love me? If the restriction comes from love, stop resisting it and start asking for it. If it comes from control and manipulation, that is a different matter, and leaving may be right. But the believer who has no iron in his life has no way to be sharpened.
