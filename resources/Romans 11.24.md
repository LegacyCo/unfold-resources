## Commentary
Paul introduces an interesting illustration from the natural world to explain the extraordinary nature of God's purposes for Israel. “For if you were cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature, and were grafted contrary to nature into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these, who are natural branches, be grafted into their own olive tree?”

Paul describes the Gentiles as wild branches that have been grafted *contrary to nature* into a cultivated olive tree. The Gentiles were not originally part of Israel's covenant history. They had been strangers to the covenants of promise, without the knowledge of Israel's God and without the hope that accompanied His promises (==Ephesians 2:11–12==). Yet God, in His extraordinary mercy, brought them into the life and blessings of a covenant they had not inherited by natural descent.

Paul then asks a stunning question: If God has accomplished something so unexpected in bringing the Gentiles into the cultivated tree, *how much more* can He restore the natural branches to the tree to which they originally belonged?

Paul is arguing from the greater improbability (Gentiles grafted in contrary to nature) to the lesser (Israel grafted into their own tree). If God has already accomplished the astonishing inclusion of the nations, why should anyone imagine that the restoration of Israel is beyond His ability or intention?

The miracle of Gentile inclusion becomes an argument for confidence in Israel's restoration.

The Jewish people do not need to become Gentiles in order to receive their Messiah. Jesus is the fulfillment of the promises made to their fathers. Their restoration is not an abandonment of Israel's calling, but a turning toward the One in whom that calling finds its fulfillment: Jesus Christ.

It is encouraging to see how Paul speaks of Israel's future with expectation rather than resignation. He doesn't look at their present unbelief and conclude that their story has ended. He sees a God whose covenantal love toward a rebellious people is sufficient to overcome the hardness of their hearts toward His Son.
