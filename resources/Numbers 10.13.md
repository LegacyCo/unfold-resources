## Commentary
This verse marks a significant turning point in Israel's journey. For nearly a year, the Israelites had been encamped at Mount Sinai. During that time, God had established His covenant with them, given them His Law, instructed them in the construction of the tabernacle, established the priesthood, and organized the tribes around His presence.

Everything God had been teaching them was now going to be put into practice.

When the cloud lifted from the tabernacle, Israel began its first journey under the newly established order God had given them. They were no longer simply a multitude of former slaves who had escaped Egypt. They had become a covenant people, organized around the presence of God, with instructions concerning how they were to worship, live, and move together.

Notice that Moses did not determine when it was time to leave. Verse 13 tells us that they departed "at the command of the LORD by the hand of Moses." Moses was the appointed leader, but the authority for their movement came from God. The same Lord who had brought them out of Egypt was now directing their steps toward the land He had promised them.

This passage shows us something important about the relationship between preparation and obedience. Israel had spent months receiving instructions from God, but those instructions were not intended to remain theoretical. Eventually, the cloud lifted, and the people had to move. All that God had established among them at Sinai was meant to shape the way they lived as they continued their journey.

The same is true for us. We are not called to just acquire knowledge about God. We are called to put what He has revealed into practice. James makes this distinction when he tells believers to be "doers of the word, and not hearers only" (==James 1:22==).

This passage reminds us that receiving revelation from God and responding faithfully to that revelation are not the same thing. We can know what God has said and still resist what He is asking of us.
