## Commentary
When Jesus says, “He who is not with Me is against Me, and he who does not gather with Me scatters abroad,” He is confronting the idea that someone can remain neutral toward Him and the kingdom He has come to establish. His words come immediately after the Pharisees accused Him of casting out demons by the power of Satan. Jesus has already explained that His authority over demons is evidence that the kingdom of God has come upon them (==Matthew 12:28==). Now He makes it clear that there are only two possible responses to the arrival of that kingdom: we either align ourselves with Him or stand in opposition to Him.

Throughout the Old Testament, God describes His people as sheep who have been scattered because of the failure of their shepherds. In ==Ezekiel 34:5–6==, the Lord rebukes Israel's leaders because His sheep have become scattered and no one is searching for them. He then promises that He Himself will gather His sheep, rescue them from the places where they have been scattered, and become their Shepherd (==Ezekiel 34:11–16==).

Jesus is identifying Himself with that very promise. He has come to gather the scattered people of God, restore them to the Father, and establish the kingdom promised through the prophets. This is why His accusation against the Pharisees is so serious. These men were supposed to be shepherds of Israel, leading God's people toward the fulfillment of His promises. Yet by rejecting Jesus and attempting to turn the people against Him, they were actually scattering those whom God had come to gather.

But the Pharisees believed they were serving God. Their opposition to Jesus was motivated, at least in part, by their conviction that they were protecting Israel from a dangerous religious influence. Yet their sincerity didn't change the reality of what they were doing. They were opposing the very One through whom God was accomplishing His purposes.

Jesus doesn't leave room for a type of Christianity that admires Him while remaining indifferent to His call for full surrender. To be with Him is to follow Him. And if His purpose is to gather people to Himself, then our lives should participate in that same work.
