## Commentary
Paul draws from ==Isaiah 28:16== to make a remarkable promise: “Whoever believes on Him will not be put to shame.” This is the second time Paul has quoted this passage (see ==Romans 9:33==). Having explained that righteousness comes through faith in Christ rather than through our attempts to establish it ourselves, Paul now describes the confidence and assurance that belong to those who have placed their trust in Him.

In its original context, Isaiah's prophecy concerned a cornerstone that God Himself would establish in Zion. While the leaders of Judah were placing their confidence in political alliances and human strategies for security, God promised to lay a foundation that could be trusted. Those who relied upon what God had established would not be disappointed. Paul identifies Jesus as the fulfillment of this promise. He is the cornerstone upon whom God has established His work of salvation, and those who place their confidence in Him will discover that their trust was not misplaced.

The Greek word translated "put to shame" is *kataischunō*, which carries the idea of being disgraced, dishonored, or disappointed because the object of one's confidence has proven unreliable. To be “put to shame” in the biblical world meant more than embarrassment. It was the public exposure of misplaced trust: leaning your whole weight on something and having it give way in front of everyone. Israel’s psalms return to this again and again: “They trusted in You, and were not ashamed” (==Psalm 22:5==). Idols shame those who trust them because they can’t hold anyone up (==Isaiah 44:9==). God holds up everyone who leans on Him.

Paul is not promising that believers will never experience embarrassment, humiliation, or rejection. In fact, many of those who first received this letter would suffer tremendous shame in the eyes of the world precisely because of their allegiance to Jesus.

Rather, Paul is describing the ultimate vindication of those who have placed their faith in Christ. There is a day coming when everything in which humanity has placed its confidence will be revealed for what it truly is. Wealth, status, religious accomplishments, personal righteousness, and every other foundation upon which people have attempted to establish their security will be revealed as unable to hold them up. But those who have trusted in Him will not stand disappointed. They will discover that everything He promised was true and that the righteousness they received through faith was sufficient.

This connects beautifully with Paul's earlier declaration in ==Romans 5:5== that "hope does not disappoint." Our hope is not founded upon our ability to remain faithful enough to earn God's acceptance, but upon the faithfulness of the One who has already reconciled us to Himself.

There is also something important about Paul's use of the word "whoever." He is preparing to explain in the following verses that there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, because the same Lord is rich toward all who call upon Him (==Romans 10:12–13==). The promise is not restricted by ethnicity, religious background, social standing, or a person's history of success or failure. Whoever believes in Christ is welcomed into the same righteousness and given the same assurance.

## Word Study
**Put to shame** is the Greek *kataischynō* (καταισχύνω): to disgrace, to dishonor, to disappoint by failing to come through. Paul uses the same word in ==Romans 5:5==, "Now hope does not disappoint," which could be rendered "hope does not put us to shame." Peter quotes the same Isaiah promise with it (==1 Peter 2:6==), and Paul uses it when he writes that God has chosen the foolish things of the world "to put to shame the wise" (==1 Corinthians 1:27==). Summary: the believer's trust will never be exposed as foolish, because the One we trust in will never let us down.

## Application
"Everyone" includes the person who assumes they're the exception: the one who thinks their past disqualifies them, who feels they came to faith too late, or who doesn't have the religious background others seem to have. Paul's added word was written for them. It also speaks to the believer who feels foolish for trusting God in a situation that hasn't resolved yet, like a diagnosis, a prodigal child, or a long season of waiting. The promise rests on God's faithfulness.
