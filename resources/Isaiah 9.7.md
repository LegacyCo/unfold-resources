## Commentary
“Of the increase of His government and peace there will be no end…”

Isaiah continues his description of the promised Messiah by revealing the nature of the kingdom He will establish. Unlike every earthly kingdom, which eventually declines, divides, or disappears, the kingdom of this Son will endure forever. But Isaiah says something even more remarkable: there will be no end to the _increase_ of His government and peace.

The Hebrew word translated “increase” is _marbeh_, which speaks of abundance, multiplication, or enlargement. Isaiah is describing a kingdom whose dominion and peace will know no limit. This is not simply a promise that the Messiah's kingdom will survive, but that its greatness and influence will be without end.

Throughout Scripture, the kingdom of God is presented as something that begins in ways the world often overlooks but ultimately fills the earth. Daniel describes a stone cut without human hands that strikes the kingdoms of this world and becomes a great mountain filling the whole earth (==Daniel 2:34–35==). Jesus compares the kingdom to a mustard seed that begins as something remarkably small but grows into something far greater than its beginnings would suggest (==Matthew 13:31–32==).

There is something important here about the nature of Christ's reign. His kingdom does not advance through the same means as the kingdoms of this world. Earthly rulers establish their authority through military strength, political influence, and the exercise of power. Jesus establishes His kingdom by dying for His people, coming back to life, the proclamation of the gospel, and the transforming work of the Holy Spirit. People from every tribe, tongue, and nation are being brought under His gracious rule by faith that He is who He says He is.

Isaiah also tells us that this King will sit “upon the throne of David.” This connects the prophecy to God's covenant with David in ==2 Samuel 7:12–16==, where God promised to establish the throne of David's descendant forever. Generations of kings would come and go, many of them failing miserably to represent the character of God. Yet God's promise remained. He would raise up a Son of David whose reign would never end.

When Gabriel announces the birth of Jesus to Mary, he deliberately uses the language of Isaiah's prophecy: “The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David. And He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and of His kingdom there will be no end” (==Luke 1:32–33==).

Following His resurrection and ascension, Jesus was exalted to the right hand of the Father, where He now reigns as the promised Messiah (==Acts 2:30–36==). His kingdom is already present and advancing, though we still await the day when His reign will be fully manifested and every enemy, including death, will be defeated (==1 Corinthians 15:24–26==).

But Isaiah is equally concerned with _how_ this King will govern. His kingdom will be established “with judgment and justice.”

The Hebrew words are _mishpat_ (judgment or justice) and _tsedaqah_ (righteousness). Together, they describe a government that operates according to what is right in the eyes of God. This was precisely what so many of Israel's kings had failed to do. They abused their authority, neglected the vulnerable, tolerated corruption, and often led the people away from the Lord. The Messiah, however, will govern with perfect justice and righteousness.

This is one of the great promises of the gospel. Jesus has not just come to forgive sinners and take them to heaven. He has come to restore God's rightful order to His creation. His kingdom is the fulfillment of God's original intention that humanity, made in His image, would exercise righteous dominion under His authority (==Genesis 1:26–28==). In Christ, the true Son of Man, that calling is finally fulfilled.

Isaiah concludes with an extraordinary statement: “The zeal of the LORD of hosts will perform this.”

The Hebrew word for zeal, _qin'ah_, describes intense passion, devotion, and determination. It can refer to jealousy, but in this context it communicates God's fierce commitment to accomplish what He has promised.

This is important because the fulfillment of this prophecy does not ultimately depend upon the faithfulness of Israel, the strength of earthly institutions, or the ability of human beings to establish God's kingdom. The Lord Himself has taken responsibility for bringing it to pass.

The same God who promised David an everlasting throne has provided the King, established His reign, and guaranteed its ultimate fulfillment.
