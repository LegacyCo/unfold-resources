## Commentary
God sets Ezekiel down in a valley full of dry bones and asks, “Son of man, can these bones live?” Ezekiel prophesies as commanded, and the miracle starts immediately: a noise, a rattling, bones coming together bone to bone, then sinews, then flesh, then skin covering them over. A valley of complete bodies. And then comes a vital statement: “*but there was no breath in them*.”

Everything was assembled. Nothing was alive. It took a second act—“Prophesy to the breath”—before breath came into them and they lived, and stood on their feet, an exceedingly great army (==Ezekiel 37:10==). Structure came first; life came separately. And that has always been God’s pattern. Adam was formed from the dust of the earth—a complete body, every part in place—but he didn’t become a living being until God bent down into the dirt and breathed into him the breath of life (==Genesis 2:7==). Formation is not life. Only breath makes the body live.

Now watch what the resurrected Jesus does when He comes to His disciples: “He breathed on them, and said to them, ‘Receive the Holy Spirit’” (==John 20:22==). Don’t miss the picture—the last Adam doing for His disciples what God did for the first Adam in the garden. These men had followed Jesus for three years; the structure of their faith was assembled. But Jesus knew a body without breath is a corpse with good bones. They had been born of water; now they were being born of the Spirit (==John 3:5==).

The application cuts close. A person can be fully assembled religiously—right doctrine, moral conduct, church attendance, bone to bone and skin over everything—and have no breath in them. So can a church: organized, staffed, programmed, and lifeless. The question of Ezekiel’s valley is the question of John 3: not “is the structure complete?” but “has the breath of God come into it?” If the answer is no, the remedy hasn’t changed—God still tells His people to prophesy to the breath. Ask for it. “How much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!” (==Luke 11:13==).

## Word Study
- *breath* : **ruach** (Hebrew) — breath, wind, and spirit: one word carrying all three meanings. It appears ten times in this one chapter—as breath entering the bodies, as the four winds, and as God’s Spirit: “I will put My *Spirit* (ruach) in you, and you shall live” (==Ezekiel 37:14==). The Greek equivalent, *pneuma*, has the same triple meaning—which is exactly the wordplay Jesus uses with Nicodemus: “The *wind* (pneuma) blows where it wishes… so is everyone who is born of the *Spirit* (pneuma)” (==John 3:8==).

Summary: in both testaments, God chose one word for breath, wind, and Spirit—because what breath is to a body, His Spirit is to a soul. Bones can be gathered and dressed. Only *ruach* makes an army stand up.
