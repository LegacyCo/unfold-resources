## Commentary
“So He humbled you, allowed you to hunger, and fed you with manna… that He might make you know that man shall not live by bread alone; but man lives by every word that proceeds from the mouth of the LORD.”

God *allowed* Israel to hunger. The hunger was not an oversight in His provision; it was part of the curriculum. He let His people feel the ache of an empty stomach so that the lesson could land where no full stomach lets it land—that bread is not what ultimately keeps a person alive. The word from His mouth is. God has always used hunger to teach His people, and this is the verse Jesus reached for in His own hunger, at the end of forty days of fasting in the wilderness (==Matthew 4:4==). The Son learned in His body what Israel was meant to learn in theirs.

This is the logic underneath the practice of fasting. When a believer fasts, he voluntarily steps into the classroom God once built into the wilderness—choosing the hunger so the lesson can be heard again. The stomach's ache becomes a confession: I am a dependent creature, sustained by Someone outside myself. Food is good, and food is a gift (==1 Timothy 4:4-5==)—but food is not God, and the fast is how the body relearns the difference. Israel had the lesson imposed for forty years; the person who fasts takes it up freely, and finds what Israel was meant to find: that the word proceeding from the mouth of the LORD is nourishment (==John 4:34==; ==Job 23:12==).
