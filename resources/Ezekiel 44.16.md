## Commentary
The sons of Zadok were distinguished from the other Levites because they remained faithful when Israel went astray. God said of them, “They shall come near Me to minister to Me.”

Others were permitted to serve around the house, but these priests were brought near to God Himself (==Exodus 28:1==). Their calling was not just to perform religious tasks but to stand before the Lord, offer to Him, and enter His sanctuary.

This is the inner-room ministry of the Spirit. There is service that takes place before men, and there is service that takes place before God alone. The latter is hidden, easily overlooked, and often regarded as unproductive. Yet everything that carries eternal life into the outer court must originate there.

The faithful priest is defined by proximity. He stands where God has called him to stand, nearest to the Lord, even when no one sees, applauds, or understands (==Deuteronomy 10:8==). His service is unto the Lord.

The distinction between the sons of Zadok and the other Levites warns us that it is possible to remain involved in sacred activity while losing the privilege of nearness. God desires a people who will come near, stand before Him, and minister to Him.

From that inward place, true service to others becomes possible (see ==Acts 13:2==).
