## Commentary
On the Mount of Transfiguration, Peter, James, and John are surrounded by the most remarkable sights any human had witnessed since Sinai: Jesus shining like the sun, and Moses and Elijah—the law and the prophets in person—talking with Him. Peter’s response is to start a building program: “let us make here three tabernacles: one for You, one for Moses, and one for Elijah” (==Matthew 17:4==). Peter is out of his mind. He’s just put Jesus on the same shelf as Moses and Elijah—one monument each for the law, the prophets, and the Son. Jesus doesn’t even respond to him. The Father does. A bright cloud overshadows them, and the voice thunders: “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. *Hear Him!*” The disciples fall on their faces in terror—until Jesus comes over, touches them, and tells them not to be afraid.

Then Matthew closes the scene with one of the most powerful sentences in the Gospels: “When they had lifted up their eyes, they saw *no one but Jesus only*.”

That’s the whole point of the mountain. Not the spectacle. Not the monument Peter wanted to build to the encounter. The point was to see Jesus *measured against everything else*—measured against the law, measured against the prophets, measured against the glory cloud, measured against their own terror—and watch everything else fade until only He remained. Moses was faithful in God’s house as a servant, but Christ is the Son over the house (==Hebrews 3:5-6==). The law and the prophets are precious, but they were the witnesses—He is the Word (==John 1:17-18==). When the cloud lifted, heaven had reduced the entire mountaintop to a single Person. No one but Jesus only.

And notice what happens next: they come *down* the mountain—with Jesus—straight into a valley where a desperate father waits with a demonized son (==Matthew 17:14-15==). That’s the test of every mountaintop: not whether you can build a monument to the encounter, but whether you carry the vision of Jesus-only into the day-to-day rhythm of real life. Years later, Peter finally understood. Recalling that day, he didn’t tell the church to seek mountaintop experiences. He pointed them to what the mountain had shown him: the prophetic word confirmed, “a light that shines in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts” (==2 Peter 1:18-19==). The encounter faded. The Radiance didn’t. Lift up your eyes—whatever else is on your mountain today, let it burn away until you see no one but Jesus only.
