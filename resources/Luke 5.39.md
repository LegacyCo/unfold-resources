## Commentary
"And no one, having drunk old wine, immediately desires new; for he says, 'The old is better.'"

Jesus has just said that new wine must go into new wineskins (==Luke 5:37-38==). Then He adds this—almost as an aside—and it may be the most sobering sentence in the passage. The problem isn't only that old wineskins can't *hold* the new wine. It's that people who have grown used to the old wine don't *want* the new. "The old is good enough."

I don't think Jesus means literally no one will ever receive what God is newly pouring out. I think He's saying that so few do that the ones who welcome it are the great exception. And that's the danger: everyone assumes *they* are the exception.

I'm a student of revival history, and when I picture myself in those first moments of outpouring in the past, I assume I would gladly receive what God was beginning to do. But if history is any indication, there's no guarantee we will recognize real wine in real-time. In fact, the majority of Jesus-loving, Bible-believing saints actually declined previous moves of God. For example, in the Hebrides Revival of 1949—one of the purest moves of God in recent memory—two of the three denominations on the islands did not participate. Not atheists. Not the wicked. Church people, who had tasted something of God from the past, compared it to the present, and said, "The old is good enough."

Which is why Jesus says new wine goes in *fresh* wineskins. Not because the old skins were bad—they served their generation well. But skins that have stretched around one vintage grow rigid; they can't flex around the next. The greatest enemy to tomorrow's move of God is often yesterday's move of God.

So don't just pray for new wine. Pray for a taste for it. Pray for the humility to receive from God something that doesn't look like what He did last time. And be honest about the words already forming in your mouth—because "the old is good enough" rarely sounds like skepticism. It sounds like wisdom, like discernment, like faithfulness. It's usually just comfort.
