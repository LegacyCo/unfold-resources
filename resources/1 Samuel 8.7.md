## Commentary
“And the LORD said to Samuel, ‘Heed the voice of the people in all that they say to you; for they have not rejected you, but they have rejected Me, that I should not reign over them.’”

Samuel took the elders' request personally. Verse 6 says it “displeased” him, and you can understand why. He'd served Israel faithfully his entire life, and now they were telling him he was old and his family wasn't fit to lead. So he prayed. And the first thing God does is lift that weight off him: “they have not rejected you.”

Then God tells him what's really going on: “they have rejected Me, that I should not reign over them.” Israel already had a King. At the Red Sea they'd sung, “The LORD shall reign forever and ever” (==Exodus 15:18==). When they tried to crown Gideon, he told them, “the LORD shall rule over you” (==Judges 8:23==). God had brought them out of Egypt, fed them, fought for them, and raised up a deliverer every time they cried out. Asking for a king “like all the nations” was telling Him they'd rather have someone else. And God says this is nothing new. They've been doing it “since the day that I brought them up out of Egypt” (==1 Samuel 8:8==).

It works this way all through Scripture. When Israel complained in the wilderness, Moses told them, “Your complaints are not against us but against the LORD” (==Exodus 16:8==). Jesus told His disciples, “he who rejects you rejects Me” (==Luke 10:16==). When people push back against what God has said, the person who delivered it often feels it as personal. God's word to Samuel is for them too.

This rejection reaches its lowest point centuries later. Jesus told a parable about citizens who sent a message after their king: “We will not have this man to reign over us” (==Luke 19:14==). And when Pilate brought Him out and said, “Behold your King!” the chief priests answered, “We have no king but Caesar!” (==John 19:15==). That's 1 Samuel 8 all over again, a king like the nations in place of the LORD.

But God never gave up being Israel's King. He took the very thing they'd demanded for the wrong reasons, the throne, and worked through it. From Saul's failure came David, and from David's line came the Son to whom God gives “the throne of His father David,” who “will reign over the house of Jacob forever” (==Luke 1:32-33==). 

## Word Study
**Rejected** is the Hebrew *ma'as* (מָאַס)—to refuse, to despise, to cast off. It's the word Samuel later uses to Saul: “Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, He also has rejected you from being king” (==1 Samuel 15:23==). It's also the word in a verse the New Testament applies to Jesus more than once: “The stone which the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone” (==Psalm 118:22==; ==Acts 4:11==). And God uses it in a promise about Israel in exile: “I will not cast them away… to utterly destroy them and break My covenant with them” (==Leviticus 26:44==). Summary: Israel rejected God, but God said He would not reject them, and the One they rejected is the One He made the cornerstone.
