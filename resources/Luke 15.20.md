## Commentary
“When he was still a great way off, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and fell on his neck and kissed him.”

The son had prepared a speech.

He would confess that he had sinned against heaven and against his father. He would admit that he was no longer worthy to be called a son. Then he would propose a solution: “Make me like one of your hired servants” (==Luke 15:18-19==).

He believed his father might let him return, but only if he came back under different terms. He no longer imagined himself entering the house as a son. Perhaps, if he expressed enough sorrow and promised enough labor, he could negotiate his way back as a servant.

That is religion: rehearsing the speech that will convince the Father to let you in.

But the father sees him while he is still far away. Before the son reaches the house, delivers the confession, or proposes his arrangement, the father is already running. Compassion moved faster than the speech.

The son begins what he has practiced: “Father, I have sinned against heaven and in your sight, and am no longer worthy to be called your son.” But he never reaches the part about becoming a servant. The father interrupts the negotiation and calls for the robe, the ring, the sandals, and the feast.

The confession was real, but the servant arrangement was based on a false understanding of the father. Repentance brought the son home; shame tried to determine the terms of his return. The father refused those terms.

The father didn't restore the son because the son delivered a flawless apology. He restored him because this is what the father is like.

The robe covered the evidence of the far country. The ring restored family authority. The sandals distinguished him from the barefoot servants. Every gift answered the lie the son had believed: “I am no longer worthy to be called your son.”

The father’s response was clear: your failure took you far from home, but it did not give you the authority to rewrite your identity.

## Application
Do not confuse repentance with negotiating a lesser place in the Father’s house.

Confess what you have done. Leave the far country. But do not insult grace by insisting that your failure is more powerful than the Father’s ability, or willingness, to restore you. You may return rehearsing the speech of a servant. He is already running toward you with the robe of a son.
