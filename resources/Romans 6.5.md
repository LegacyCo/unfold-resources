## Commentary
“For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection.”

The word Paul builds this verse on is *united*—joined, grown together, fused into one. Whatever happened to Christ happened to everyone who is in Him. His death was our death; His burial was our burial; His resurrection is our resurrection (==Romans 6:4==). This is not a metaphor for inspiration. It is the mechanics of the gospel: “I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me” (==Galatians 2:20==).

Consider what that means for everything that used to rule you. Before a person is born again, the throne of their life is occupied—by self, and by whatever powers self answered to: fear, greed, lust, ambition, shame, the approval of others. Each of these functioned as a ruling authority, and rulers do not abdicate voluntarily. What removes them is death. So when you were united to Christ in His death, the old rulers went into that death with you: “our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin” (==Romans 6:6==). Jesus didn’t die alone. Fear was nailed to that cross. Shame was nailed to that cross. Addiction, bitterness, and every anti-Christ way of living inherited from Adam went into the grave with the old man.

And here is the force of the verse’s second half: the resurrection did not bring the old rulers back. You were raised united to Christ—but the imposters were not raised with you. There is no throne in the resurrection life for fear to sit on, no seat for shame, no office for the old masters, because “death no longer has dominion over Him” (==Romans 6:9==)—or over those united to Him. When a believer still lives under the old rulers, it is not because they survived the cross. It is because a dead king is being obeyed as if he were alive. Paul’s instruction follows directly: “reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus” (==Romans 6:11==). Count as true what the union has already made true.
