## Commentary
“The Son of Man has come eating and drinking, and you say, ‘Look, a glutton and a winebibber, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!’”

The religious leaders meant it as slander—three charges meant to disqualify Jesus in one breath. The first two were false: He was no glutton and no drunkard. But look at the third accusation. *Friend of sinners.* That one was true, and Jesus never once denied it. He wore it. He ate in their homes, called them down from trees, let them wash His feet with tears, and recruited His team from their ranks. When the religious asked why, His answer was His mission statement: “I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance” (==Luke 5:32==).

Notice what Jesus was willing to risk to pursue the separated: His reputation among the respectable. You cannot befriend sinners from a safe distance, and you cannot call the lost without being seen with them. The Pharisees kept their reputations spotless and their tables closed—and the lost stayed lost. Jesus opened His table and absorbed the gossip—and tax collectors became apostles, prostitutes became worshipers, and a thief woke up in Paradise.

Kingdom people on mission inherit both the friendship and the risk. Are you willing to be a witness to the actual people in front of you—the neighbor, the family member, the barista, the addict, the skeptic, the coworker nobody else will sit with? Here’s a simple way to live this: if you want to know God’s will for you today, look at the person standing in front of you. That’s your assignment. God handles the where—your steps are ordered by Him (==Psalm 37:23==)—so the person He puts in your path is no accident. The only question is whether they’ll find in you what sinners found in Jesus: a friend.
