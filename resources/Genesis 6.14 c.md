## Word Study
The Lord tells Noah to completely seal the ark, both inside and outside, with a waterproofing material. The Hebrew word that the Bible uses for this waterproofing material is _zepheth_. Zepheth is like the asphalt material you see poured onto our roads. 

The material Noah used to cover the Ark may have been _zepheth_, but that’s not the Hebrew word used here in Genesis 6:14. The verse is translated, “…cover it inside and outside with *pitch*.” 

That word for “pitch” should be _zepheth_, but it’s not. Genesis 6:14 uses a different word all together that we translate as “pitch.” That word is _kaphar_. 

This word _kaphar_ is a very popular and important word used more than 70x in the Old Testament. But the only time this word _kaphar_ is translated as “pitch" is here in Genesis 6. Every other time this word is used in the Bible it is translated *atonement*.

The Lord told Noah that the ark of his salvation was to be covered in “atonement.”

What is atonement? At its most basic level, atonement is a “covering.” But spiritually speaking, atonement is massively important and profoundly significant. 

For example; when God made skins for Adam and Eve to cover their nakedness He was making “atonement” for them. Practically, they were just clothes to cover their nakedness. But there was a much deeper spiritual significance to the atonement that God provided for Adam and Eve. The skins didn’t just cover their bodies, the blood from that animal covered their sin. That’s the multi-layered significance of this word, _kaphar_.

“Make for yourself an ark…and cover it inside and outside with *atonement*.” 

There is a reason _Kaphar_ is in this verse instead of _Zepheth_. Scripture is telling us that salvation from death, storms, and judgment was found inside of a vessel that was covered in atonement. Noah was held inside of something that was completely "atoned for.” Every person and animal that entered the ark of salvation was brought through the storm. They were perfectly safe — and not a single one was missing — because the Ark foreshadows the One who would, centuries later, bring salvation to all of creation, completely atone for the sin of the world, and announce to all of creation that whoever would be found in Him, “…shall never perish, and shall never be plucked from His hand” (John 10:27–29).
