## Commentary
“After these things the Lord appointed seventy others also, and sent them two by two before His face into every city and place where He Himself was about to go.”

The seventy were not sent instead of Jesus; they were sent *ahead* of Him. Their assignment was preparation—announcing the kingdom, healing the sick (==Luke 10:9==), and readying each city for the arrival of the King who was already on the road behind them. A king does not just appear somewhere. A delegation goes first, so that when he comes, the place can receive him.

The ancient world had a picture for this. When a fleet of ships set out for new territory, the lead ship of the procession was called the apostle ship. It arrived at the conquered land first, ahead of the rest of the fleet and ahead of the king. And its role was not simply to land—it was to transform. The crew of the apostle ship set to work making the new land look like the land they had been sent from: its customs, its order, its culture. So that when the king finally arrived, he would step ashore and recognize the new territory as part of his own kingdom. The land was conquered when the fleet landed; it was *prepared* when the king could be at home in it.

This is the shape of everything apostolic. Sent ones receive the design of the King’s house and go ahead to prepare a place He will recognize when He arrives. Their mission is to make the culture they are sent to resemble the culture of the kingdom they are sent from—citizens of heaven establishing outposts of heaven, whatever their earthly address (==Philippians 3:20==; ==2 Corinthians 5:20==). John the Baptist worked the same office for the Lord’s first coming: a voice sent to prepare the way, to make the crooked places straight before the King appeared (==Luke 3:4-5==). The church works it now for His return.

## Word Study
- *sent* : **apostellō** — to send out with a commission, as an authorized representative; the verb behind *apostolos*, “sent one.” In Greek usage the word family carried the naval sense described above: an *apostolos* could be a fleet dispatched on a mission, with the lead vessel as the apostle ship.

The word in use:

- ==John 20:21== — “As the Father has *sent* (apostellō) Me, I also send you.” The commissioning transfers: the Sent One becomes the Sender.
- ==Romans 10:15== — “How shall they preach unless they are *sent* (apostellō)?” The office runs on commissioning, not ambition.

Summary: apostellō is not generic sending; it is a king dispatching representatives ahead of his own arrival. The seventy went before His face into cities He was about to visit—and the church still holds that assignment, preparing places the King intends to come.
