## Commentary
“…but exhort one another daily, while it is called ‘Today,’ lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

The writer has just warned believers—“brethren,” God’s people, not the lost (==Hebrews 3:12==)—that an evil heart of unbelief can develop in any of them. And the safeguard he prescribes is mutual, frequent, and spoken: exhort *one another*, *daily*. Not weekly from a platform. Daily, from the people who share your life. The assumption underneath the command is that left entirely to ourselves, none of us reliably notices our own heart hardening.

The reason is in the last phrase—sin *deceives*. It doesn’t announce itself as rebellion; it presents itself as reasonable. A delayed obedience feels like wisdom. A cooling devotion feels like maturity. A compromise feels like nuance. Hardening happens the way concrete sets: gradually, while everything still looks the same on the surface. That’s why the exhortation has to be daily and has to come from outside—someone close enough to see what you can’t, while the setting concrete can still be worked (==Proverbs 27:17==; ==Ecclesiastes 4:9-10==).

## Word Study
- *deceitfulness* : **apatē** — deception, delusion; that which gives a false impression, promising one thing and delivering another. Sin doesn’t harden hearts by force; it hardens them by fraud.

The word’s other appearances sharpen the picture:

- ==Matthew 13:22== — “the *deceitfulness* (apatē) of riches choke the word.” Wealth doesn’t announce that it’s choking anything; it just quietly crowds out the life.
- ==Ephesians 4:22== — the old man “grows corrupt according to the *deceitful* (apatē) lusts.” The desires that corrupt are the ones that misrepresent what they’ll cost.

Summary: apatē is why the verse commands community. A lie is hardest to spot from inside it—which is exactly where sin puts you. The daily voice of a brother or sister is God’s appointed way of hearing the truth about your own heart while it is still called Today.
