## Commentary
“And see to it that you make them according to the pattern which was shown you on the mountain.”

The tabernacle was, in one sense, just a tent—portable, simple, put up and taken down as Israel moved. And yet God had exact requirements for it, down to the loops and clasps. Why? Because it was *His* house. If you were building your own home, you’d have a say in the plans, right? You wouldn’t let the construction company pour whatever foundation they felt like because “we’ve seen houses before—we know what to do.” But that’s precisely how we tend to build God’s house. We look at what other churches have done, what we inherited from a previous generation, what’s worked somewhere else—and we assume that must be what God wants. He never said “build what you’ve seen.” He said, “build what I *show* you.”

We can build a church according to our own design—familiar, comfortable, even “successful” by our standards—and it won’t be His house. It will be our house that we invite Him into on the weekends. We keep the deed, He gets visitation rights. And it’s hard for Jesus to be Himself when He’s a guest in someone else’s house. This was Martha’s mistake: she assumed that Jesus’ presence in her house was an endorsement of her hard work—and Jesus gently told her that Mary had chosen the better part (==Luke 10:41-42==). Presence is not endorsement. We cannot design the house ourselves and then ask Him to bless it with Himself. “Unless the LORD builds the house, they labor in vain who build it” (==Psalm 127:1==).

So the first act of building is not construction—it’s ascent. Get alone with God on the mountain. Ask Him what He wants His house to look like in your generation, and be willing to hear an answer that doesn’t match anything you’ve seen.

## Word Study
- *pattern* : **tabnit** (Hebrew) — model, blueprint, design; the structure of a thing as conceived by its architect. The word insists that the design originates with God and is *received*, not invented.

Where else tabnit appears:

- ==1 Chronicles 28:11-12== — David gives Solomon “the plans (*tabnit*)” for the temple, “by the Spirit”—and adds, “the LORD made me understand in writing, by His hand upon me, all the works of these plans” (==1 Chronicles 28:19==). Two houses, two builders, one Architect.
- ==Hebrews 8:5== — Moses’ tabernacle was “the copy and shadow of the heavenly things,” which is exactly why the pattern couldn’t be improvised: it was tracing something real.

Summary: every dwelling place God has ever inhabited was built from plans He handed down—the tabernacle, the temple, and now a house of living stones. The pattern still isn’t ours to draw. It’s ours to receive.
