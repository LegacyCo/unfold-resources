## Commentary
“‘And you shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.’ These are the words which you shall speak to the children of Israel.”

This is God’s offer at Sinai before there was ever a tribe of priests. Not “you shall have a priesthood,” but “you shall *be* to Me a kingdom of priests”—the entire nation, every man and woman, standing before the Lord to minister to Him. A specialized priestly class was never the original design. It entered the story after the people retreated: when God descended on the mountain, Israel drew back and told Moses, “You speak with us, and we will hear; but let not God speak with us, lest we die” (==Exodus 20:18-19==). The nation chose a mediated relationship, and the Levitical order followed. But the offer of verse 6 was the plan.

The new covenant fulfills God's original desire. Peter writes to the church in exactly these terms: “you are a chosen generation, a *royal priesthood*, a holy nation, His own special people, that you may proclaim the praises of Him who called you out of darkness” (==1 Peter 2:9==). John says Jesus “has made us kings and priests to His God and Father” (==Revelation 1:6==). The priesthood is not the paid professionals, and it is not limited to those who preach or lead. If you have been called out of darkness, you are a member of the royal priesthood—commissioned to minister to the Lord.

That word *minister* deserves attention, because it runs opposite to the modern assumption. We assume the gifted and called exist to minister to people. But the first calling of a priest is to minister to the Lord—and only from that place of standing before Him does ministry to people carry any weight (==Ezekiel 44:15==; ==Acts 13:2==). Prayer is where this priesthood does its primary work: not first petition, but ministry to God—fellowship, worship, attention given to Him for His own sake. Whatever else God has called you to, He called you to this first. It has been the plan since Sinai.
