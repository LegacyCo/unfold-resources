## Commentary
“For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ.” The phrase “put on” means to be wrapped in a covering—clothed. This is covenant language. Union language. Oneness language.

The old covenant prophets knew a Savior would come, but none of them could have imagined a covenant cut between God and man that would permit God and man to become *one*—“do you not know that you are the temple of God and that the Spirit of God dwells in you?” (==1 Corinthians 3:16==). And unlike the covenant of law, which said what you *do* determines your standing, the new covenant says what you *believe* determines it. It doesn’t matter what family you were born into. It doesn’t matter what you’ve done. The only question is: do you believe that Jesus is who He says He is?

So what is baptism? Baptism is the covenant ceremony of our faith—the public declaration that testifies, “I no longer belong to myself. I belong to Jesus.” Think of a wedding. The covenant happens in the vows, but the ceremony declares it before witnesses and marks the moment two lives become one. And notice what else a wedding does: when I married my wife, I wasn’t only joined to her—I was joined to her *family*. Her family became my family. Baptism works the same way in both directions: it covenants you to Christ, and it covenants you to His church. You don’t get the Groom without the Groom’s household (==Ephesians 2:19==).

The New Testament even connects baptism to the old covenant’s mark of belonging. Circumcision physically declared that Israel was in covenant with YHWH—distinct from every nation on earth. Paul says that in Christ you were “circumcised with the circumcision made without hands”—the removal of the body of flesh—“buried with Him in baptism” (==Colossians 2:11-12==). Hold those together and the declaration of baptism becomes clear: everything that once joined me to death has been cut off and washed away. What I used to be in covenant with has been crucified and buried.

## Application
If you believe Jesus is who He says He is and you’ve never been baptized, the Ethiopian’s question is now your question: what hinders you (==Acts 8:36-37==)? Don’t wait until you feel ready—covenant isn’t a reward for readiness; it’s a declaration of belonging. And if you have been baptized, live like a person wearing the clothing of Christ. You left the water wrapped in Him. Walk into your week dressed like it—and treat His family as your family, because the ceremony joined you to both.
