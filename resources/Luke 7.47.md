## Commentary
“The one who is forgiven little loves little.” It’s tempting to hear Jesus saying some of us only needed a little forgiveness and think *He must be talking about me*. He isn’t. He’s saying some of us only *think* we were forgiven a little—either because we don’t understand the weight of our sin, or because we minimize the power of the cross to save us completely. The woman weeping at His feet wasn’t more forgiven than Simon the Pharisee. She just knew what she’d been forgiven of.

Here’s a way to think about it. I know how much my car is worth because I remember what I paid for it. But how much is a car worth if it was *given* to me? Now the value is measured differently—by what it cost the giver. If the giver owns a car dealership, that value may not be too high; he has a lot full of them. But what if it was the only car he ever owned? Go further: what if the car given to you was the only car that ever *existed*?

That’s the gospel. “For God so loved the world that He gave His *only* begotten Son” (==John 3:16==). The Father didn’t give us one of many. He gave us His One and Only. The worth of your salvation is measured by what it cost the Giver—and it cost Him everything.

And the evidence of whether we’ve grasped that isn’t in what we say. It shows up in how we live out our salvation: how we forgive those who injure us, serve the people around us, lay down our lives, and share a gift too priceless to keep to ourselves. Grace received lightly gets lived out lightly. Grace received as infinite declares the infinite worth of Jesus.

## Application
If your love has grown lukewarm, don’t start by trying to love harder—start by looking again by looking at Jesus. Remember how much you were forgiven and what it cost God. Every day with this gift, you get to decide: will I treasure it? Will I let it move me to worship? Will I share it? 

The scale of your love is set by your estimate of the gift. Re-estimate the gift.
