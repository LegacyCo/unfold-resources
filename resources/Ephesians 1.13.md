## Word Study
The word Paul uses here for sealed is *sphragis*. This word means a signet seal. In ancient times, every man had his own seal. The owner would put his mark on his possessions, his animals, his slaves. This would guard his property against theft. To that extent one can call it a "protecting sign" or a "guarantee." When used with documents (marriage license, wills, deeds of sale, etc.) the seal served as a signature to authorize what was written there. Things sealed were at the disposal of the possessor of the seal. Of course, the seal applied not only to private individuals, but also particularly to the authorities of a city and to kings. The seal symbolized their authority.

Seals were also significant in religious life. For instance, an animal could be designated by a seal as ritually pure and suitable for sacrifice. In secular culture, men would show themselves to be the possession of their deity by the imprint of their deities seal on their body. There are writings describing how worshippers of Dionysius would be branded by fire on their body with the ivy-leaf of their pagan god. This is similar to language God tells Isaiah about Israel in Isaiah 44:5 - "This one will say, ‘I am the LORD’S’; and that one will call on the name of Jacob; and another will write on his hand, ‘Belonging to the LORD,’ and will give himself Israel’s name with honor."

The use of the seal is mentioned 22x in Revelation. The imprinting of the seal which bears God’s name will distinguish believers from those who bear the “mark of the beast” on their hands and foreheads. God’s sign protects one from judgment; the mark of the beast brings it upon one. It's easy to discern here a word of consolation to a community which is living through the trials of persecution; it tells them of God’s protection.

-We aren't sealed with a ring, we're sealed with a Spirit.
-Israel was sealed by circumcision. We are sealed by the Spirit.
-"Seal" also implies something that is closed up and kept secret (Revelation 5:2). David prays for a seal to be placed on his lips to guard his words (Psalm 141:3).
