## Commentary
“I speak with him face to face, even plainly, and not in dark sayings; and he sees the form of the LORD. Why then were you not afraid to speak against My servant Moses?”

Here's what set Moses apart from every other prophet. God spoke to the others in visions and dreams (==Numbers 12:6==). With Moses, He spoke directly and plainly, the way “a man speaks to his friend” (==Exodus 33:11==). Moses even saw “the form of the LORD.” That doesn't mean he saw God's full glory. God told him, “You cannot see My face; for no man shall see Me, and live” (==Exodus 33:20==). But he was given a nearness no one else had. When Deuteronomy closes, this is what it says about him: “since then there has not arisen in Israel a prophet like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face” (==Deuteronomy 34:10==).

Twice in two verses God calls him “My servant Moses,” and He says Moses is “faithful in all My house” (==Numbers 12:7==). That's the ground of the rebuke. Miriam and Aaron thought they were talking about their little brother. But they were talking about God's servant. “Why then were you not afraid?”

The New Testament picks up this passage in two places. Hebrews quotes verse 7 and then goes further: “Moses indeed was faithful in all His house as a servant… but Christ as a Son over His own house” (==Hebrews 3:5-6==). Moses spoke with God face to face. Jesus *is* the Word, and John says, “No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, He has declared Him” (==John 1:18==). He is the Prophet like Moses that Moses himself said would come (==Deuteronomy 18:15==).

Paul borrows the language of this verse for every believer: “For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face” (==1 Corinthians 13:12==). The word “dimly” is literally “in a riddle” (_en ainigmati_, where we get our word _enigma_). It’s the same word the Greek Old Testament uses here for “dark sayings,” and this is the only place it appears in the New Testament. Paul has Numbers 12 in mind.

And he’s making the same comparison God made. In Numbers 12 there are two ways of knowing God. There’s the prophets’ way, through visions and dreams that come like riddles, and there’s Moses’ way, face to face. In 1 Corinthians 13 Paul is writing about prophecy and knowledge, and he says, “we know in part and we prophesy in part” (==1 Corinthians 13:9==). So everything we know of God right now, even through the real gifts of the Spirit, sits on the prophets’ side of that comparison. It’s true, and it’s partial. It’s like seeing someone in a mirror when you’d rather be looking straight at them.

Paul says we behold the glory of the Lord “with unveiled face” and are being changed by it (==2 Corinthians 3:18==), and that God has given us “the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ” (==2 Corinthians 4:6==). But even that is still looking from the perspective of the mirror. But the good news of the gospel is the face-to-face is our inheritance: “when that which is perfect has come, then that which is in part will be done away” (==1 Corinthians 13:10==). “Then I shall know just as I also am known” (==1 Corinthians 13:12==).

So what Moses alone had in the wilderness is what God has promised for all of His people. And it even goes *beyond* what Moses experienced. The same Moses who spoke with God face to face was told, “You cannot see My face” (==Exodus 33:20==). But the last chapter of the Bible says of those who are in Christ, “They shall see His face” (==Revelation 22:4==). John says, “we shall see Him as He is” (==1 John 3:2==). The access to God that set one man apart from every prophet in Israel is the future inheritance of everyone who belongs to Jesus.

## Word Study
**Face to face** is literally “mouth to mouth” in Hebrew—*peh el-peh* (פֶּה אֶל־פֶּה). This is the only time the phrase is used of God speaking with a person, and it pictures two people in direct conversation with no go-between. **Dark sayings** is *chidot* (חִידֹת), riddles. It's the word for the riddle Samson put to the Philistines (==Judges 14:12==) and for the “dark sayings of old” in ==Psalm 78:2==. Summary: other prophets received God's word in a form that had to be worked out. Moses received it in plain speech from God Himself.
