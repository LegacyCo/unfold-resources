## Commentary
“…for I am with you, and no one will attack you to hurt you; for I have many people in this city.”

What is vision? Simply put: knowing how you are to build because God has told you. This verse is a working example. Paul was in Corinth, blasphemed and resisted, and by every reasonable measure ready to move on to a more responsive city (==Acts 18:5-6==). Then the Lord spoke to him in the night: “Do not be afraid, but speak, and do not keep silent; for I am with you… for I have many people in this city” (==Acts 18:9-10==). The vision was to stay—to keep building God's house in Corinth, because Jesus had living stones there that had not yet been discovered. Notice that the vision contradicted the visible evidence: the people Jesus claimed were, at that moment, still unconverted Corinthians. He counts His stones before they are quarried.

The result of Paul receiving vision: he stayed a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them (==Acts 18:11==), the church at Corinth was established, and the Body of Christ received two books of the Bible out of it. The same pattern runs through Acts: Peter and Cornelius each receive vision, and the result is salvation and the Holy Spirit given to the Gentiles for the first time (==Acts 10==). Vision is not a mood, an ambition, or a strategic assessment of where ministry might succeed. It is God telling His builder what He is doing and where—and everything durable in the story of the church has been built by people who stayed, went, spoke, or waited because He said so.
