## Commentary
When the invited guests made their excuses, the master of the banquet didn’t cancel the feast. He widened the guest list: “Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in here the poor and the maimed and the lame and the blind.” The table stayed set. Only the seating changed.

That’s not just a parable about the kingdom’s reach. It’s a picture of what the church looks like when she gathers at the Lord’s table. William Willimon put it this way: “When people look at the church and ask, ‘Are you Christ’s body or shall we look for another?’ we have to point them to our table, to that gathering of sick, hurting people, with the nobodies at the head eating like somebodies, with the outcasts invited in and filled with good things. If this isn’t church, what is?”

Think about what the world actually sees at the Lord’s table. Not a stage. Not a performance. A gathering of people who could never have earned their seat, eating a meal they didn’t pay for, at the invitation of a Host who went out into the streets to find them (==Ephesians 2:12-13==). Every barrier man erects to keep out the different—race, blood, class—has no jurisdiction there, because the Lamb tore down the dividing wall of hostility (==Ephesians 2:14==).

The church has plenty of arguments for the gospel. But the table is not an argument—it’s evidence. Nobodies at the head, eating like somebodies. When the world asks whether Jesus actually changes anything, the truest answer we have may be, “Come see who’s at the table.”
