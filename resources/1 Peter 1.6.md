## Commentary
“In this you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while, if need be, you have been grieved by various trials.”

Peter attaches a purpose clause to the grief: the trials come “that the genuineness of your faith… though it is tested by fire, may be found to praise, honor, and glory” (==1 Peter 1:7==). A faith that cannot be tested cannot be trusted. The same is true of the chair you are sitting on, the building you are in, and the car you drove to get there—if it has never been tested, no one can trust it with weight. God is entrusting His people with His eternal plan, and they are no exception. “If need be” is Peter's acknowledgment that the testing is sometimes necessary, not incidental.

The purpose of the testing is not for God's information. He told Israel He led them forty years in the wilderness “to humble you and test you, to know what was in your heart” (==Deuteronomy 8:2==)—not because He needed to discover what was there, but because *they* did. They could not carry the land of promise without first dealing with what was in their hearts. In the same way, God tests His people so that they can rightly discern who they are and measure it against who He is making them. Glory is the inheritance, praise belongs on the lips, honor is how the family treats one another—but moving from glory to glory (==2 Corinthians 3:18==) does not simply happen. God tests in order to expose any place in the heart that cannot bear the next season's weight of glory.
