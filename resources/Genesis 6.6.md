## Commentary
In Genesis 3, Eve’s sin had caused her to experience pain in her sphere of labor. Adam’s sin had caused him to experience pain in his sphere of labor. And now in Genesis 6, the sin of Adam and Eve’s descendants have caused God to experience pain in His heart. 

God is not exempt from the pain and anguish that sin has brought into creation. When Israel rebelled and turned their backs on God to serve other gods, ==Isaiah 63:19== says, “(Israel) rebelled and grieved His Holy Spirit.” In ==Isaiah 43:24==, God tells Israel, “You have burdened Me with your sins, you have wearied Me with your wrongdoings.” In ==Ezekiel 6:9== God says that He has been hurt by Israel’s adulterous hearts when they turned away from Him. 

Our sin doesn’t just affect us, it affects God — and it affects Him deeply. What we feel when we see sin in the world, or in our own lives, is the sorrow that God Himself feels first. He is not distant and removed from our situation of sorrow. He enters into the grief with us. “I will not leave you nor forsake you” is not just the language of location, it’s the reality that God feels what we feel just as any empathizing father does. He really is intimately acquainted with *all* of our ways (==Psalm 139:3==). He is a passionate God who feels deeply.
