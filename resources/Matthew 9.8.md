## Commentary
“Now when the multitudes saw it, they marveled and glorified God, who had given such power to men.”

Matthew’s wording here is remarkable.

The word translated “power” is again _exousia_—authority, right, jurisdiction, the freedom and legitimate power to act. Just two verses earlier Jesus declared that “the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins” (==Matthew 9:6==). Now, after watching the paralytic rise and walk, the crowd glorifies God “who had given such authority to men.”

There is an echo here of humanity’s original calling.

When God created mankind, He created them in His image and immediately gave them authority within creation:

“Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness; let them have dominion…” (==Genesis 1:26==).

Human beings were created to represent God within His world. They were image-bearers—visible representatives of the invisible God—commissioned to rule beneath His rule. Their authority was never independent from God; it was delegated authority. Humanity was meant to exercise heaven’s government upon the earth as God’s representatives.

This is the language Psalm 8 later uses:

“You have crowned him with glory and honor. You have made him to have dominion over the works of Your hands” (==Psalm 8:5–6==).

But something catastrophic happened in Genesis 3.

Humanity reached for independence from God, and the authority entrusted to mankind became corrupted. The image remained, but mankind’s ability to faithfully represent the One whose image we carried was fractured. Instead of ruling creation under God, humanity became enslaved to sin, death, and the powers it had been created to overcome.

Then Jesus appears.

And significantly, He repeatedly calls Himself _the Son of Man._

Jesus is not just God visiting humanity. He is the true Man—the faithful human being Adam failed to become. He comes living completely under the authority of His Father, while simultaneously exercising extraordinary authority upon the earth.

He commands demons and they leave (==Matthew 8:28–32==).

He commands the winds and sea and they obey Him (==Matthew 8:26–27==).

He commands sickness and bodies are restored.

He speaks forgiveness over sinners.

Creation begins responding to a Man again.

What was distorted through Adam is being restored in Christ.

This is why the title “Son of Man” is so significant. ==Daniel 7:13–14== sees “One like the Son of Man” receiving dominion and authority from the Ancient of Days. The dominion originally entrusted to humanity is finally placed into the hands of a faithful Man.

Jesus is humanity as God intended humanity to be.

And this is also what makes Matthew’s statement so striking: the people glorified God “who had given such authority to _men_.”

Matthew could simply have said that they glorified God because Jesus possessed authority. Instead, he describes what they witnessed in terms of authority being given to humanity.

In Jesus, the human vocation is being recovered.

Paul develops this reality throughout the New Testament. Jesus is the “last Adam” (==1 Corinthians 15:45==), the beginning of a new humanity. What is true of Christ begins to become true of those who are united to Him. Through the life of Christ, God is restoring humanity’s original purpose.

This is why the New Testament can speak so boldly about believers reigning with Christ (==Romans 5:17==), being seated with Him in heavenly places (==Ephesians 2:6==), and ultimately reigning with Him over the renewed creation (==Revelation 22:5==).

The story of redemption is God bringing His design for creation to fulfillment through Jesus—the true Image, the true Son of Man, and the faithful Last Adam.

In Adam, humanity’s authority became corrupted by sin. In Christ, humanity is brought back beneath the rule of God so that we can once again faithfully represent Him.

The crowd saw a man stand up, pick up his bed, and walk home. But something much larger was being revealed. A Man was exercising heaven’s authority on earth again.
