## Commentary
Grace this strong always raises the question: “Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?” And Paul answers with the strongest negative in the Greek language: “Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?” (==Romans 6:2==). But it’s worth slowing down and asking *why*. Why does God despise sin? Because until you know why, “God hates sin” just sounds like an uptight rule-keeper guarding His regulations.

God despises sin because of what it does to His children.

Sin is a predator. And like all predators that prey on children, sin shatters purity and innocence. Think about how that wounding works: until there’s real healing of the mind and emotions, when another authority figure tries to get close—even a safe one, even a loving one—the wounded child reverts to the moment they were violated and responds in fear.

Sin does exactly that to the children of God. It lies to us, breaks us, separates us from God—and it shatters the image of who God really is. Then sin demands that we define God *through* our brokenness. So every time the Father draws close, we pull back. The lie whispers, “I wouldn’t get too close—remember what He did to Jesus.” And a child of God backs away from the only Father safe enough to heal them.

That’s why grace never makes sin safe. Grace makes sin *exposed*—revealed as the predator it is. God’s judgments are aimed at anything that interferes with love, and nothing interferes with love like sin does. He doesn’t hate sin because He’s protective of His rules. He hates sin because He’s protective of His kids.

So no, we don’t go on sinning that grace may abound. Not because we’re afraid of what God will do to us if we do—but because we’ve finally seen what sin does to us, and we’ve seen what our Father is actually like. We died to that predator. Why would we live with it any longer?
