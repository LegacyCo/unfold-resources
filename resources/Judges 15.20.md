## Commentary
“And he judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines.”

Samson judged Israel for twenty years—and his life was a mess. Impulsive, sensual, vengeful, chronically entangled with the very people he was raised up to deliver Israel from; and God used him anyway, empowering him by the Spirit again and again (==Judges 14:6==; ==Judges 15:14==). This is one of the terrifying mysteries of God's mercy: He will use broken things to fulfill His purposes. The healing evangelists of the 1950s carried serious error in some of their theology, yet God used them to restore the healing gifts to the church. Martin Luther helped the church break free from Rome, and the same man wrote sixty-five thousand words of vicious antisemitism that Hitler would draw on four centuries later. God spoke through Balaam's donkey (==Numbers 22:28==). He is so committed to reaching each generation that He will work through vessels that grieve Him.

The danger is in what we conclude from this. We live in a pragmatic culture that assumes if it works, it must be right—so we idolize results and assume God endorses whatever He blesses. Samson's twenty years prove otherwise. God's use of a man is not God's approval of a man; His mercy toward the people being reached is not His endorsement of the vessel doing the reaching. A church can be growing, and people can be getting genuinely saved in it, while the house itself is broken—because God is just that committed to reaching the lost. But every man's work will be tested by fire, and the fire will reveal what sort it is (==1 Corinthians 3:13==). Fruitfulness in the moment is mercy. Only what survives the fire is approval.
