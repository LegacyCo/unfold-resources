## Commentary
“Moreover, brethren, I declare to you the gospel which I preached to you, which also you received and in which you stand.”

Paul is writing to believers—people who have received and believe the gospel—and he still compelled to share the gospel with them: Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, was buried, and rose again the third day (==1 Corinthians 15:3-4==). The instinct that says “we're already saved; the world needs the gospel, not us” is an instinct Paul was not familiar with. The Corinthian church was carnal—divided, jealous, disputing, behaving like mere men despite their abundance of spiritual gifts (==1 Corinthians 3:1-3==)—and Paul's apostolic response to a church not being built God's way was not to teach them different techniques. He preached the gospel to them...again. He did the same for the struggling church in Rome: eight chapters of the most thorough gospel presentation in Scripture.

The cross is the only remedy that frees a person from the flesh, and the church never graduates from it. On this side of eternity there will always be flesh in the church trying to stay alive, and when carnality surfaces—division, offense, jealousy, unforgiveness—the remedy is always the same: back to the gospel. Peter shared the same conviction, telling saints who already knew the truth and were established in it, “I will not be negligent to remind you always of these things” (==2 Peter 1:12==). The death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus remain the working power for everything the church is called to be, including its life together (==1 Corinthians 2:2==). The gospel is not the entrance to the Christian life that mature believers move past. It is the ground they stand on, the power they are saved by, and the hope that anchors them as they mature.
