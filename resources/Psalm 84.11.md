## Commentary
“No good thing will He withhold from those who walk uprightly.”

This is a sweeping promise, but it has to be heard carefully. The psalmist is not saying that God will give us everything we want. He is saying that God will not withhold anything that is truly good for us.

We often define “good” by our own terms: what would make life easier, open the door, relieve the pressure, bring the opportunity, restore the relationship, or answer the prayer in the way *we* hoped. But God sees the whole journey. He knows what will form us and ultimately conform us to Christ. Sometimes His goodness is experienced in what He gives. And sometimes it's revealed in what He withholds.

The promise of this verse is not that nothing will ever be withheld. The promise is that no _good thing_ will be withheld.

That means when God says no, we can still interpret that through the lens of His goodness. We can trust that His withholding is as intentional as His giving. A Father who perfectly knows what is good does not hand His children something simply because they ask for it, nor does He keep back what would truly bless them.

Jesus makes the same point when He says that if earthly fathers know how to give good gifts to their children, “how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask Him!” (==Matthew 7:11==). Paul echoes this: “He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?” (==Romans 8:32==). The cross is the final proof that God is not withholding Himself from us.

The believer does not have to fear that God is keeping something truly good just out of reach. If it is good for us, He knows how to give it. If He does not give it, we can trust that He knows something we do not.
