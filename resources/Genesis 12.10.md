## Commentary
“Now there was a famine in the land, and Abram went down to Egypt to dwell there, for the famine was severe in the land.”

Why did God allow a famine in the very land He had sent Abraham to? One primary reason: the word God gave to Abraham needed to be tested. The worst thing that can happen in the earth is for unqualified people to receive a promise and never go through the testing that qualifies them to carry it—“the earth trembles… under a slave when he becomes king” (==Proverbs 30:21-22==). Abraham had been handed a promise that would touch generations, and to hold the weight of that word, Abram had to become Abraham; who he was had to become who the promise needed him to be. That only happens through pressure. Bob Gladstone puts it this way: “Destiny without maturity is the breeding ground for self-absorbed, superficial Christians.” Scripture is full of this pattern of testing: Abraham is promised descendants like the stars, then led with a barren wife into a barren land. David is promised an everlasting throne, then spends years in the desert while Saul chases him. Peter is told the church will be built on the rock of his confession—but not before Satan sifts him like wheat (==Luke 22:31-32==). A promise from God is not a lottery ticket; it is an invitation into a season of testing and refining, so the promise-holder develops the character that can bear God doing impossible things through them. Joseph knew the season well: “until the time that his word came to pass, the word of the LORD tested him” (==Psalm 105:19==).

Now look at what is missing from this verse: God telling Abraham to leave Canaan and go to Egypt. When the word of promise was starved and malnourished by the famine of Canaan, rather than enduring and trusting the God who brought him there, Abraham took it on himself to resuscitate the word with good decisions and logical planning—he packed up and crossed the boundary from God's land into Pharaoh's. Expectations are delayed resentment: when what Abraham expected of the land of promise didn't match reality, he began making his own decisions. Faith is moved by the current of peace and hope; unbelief moves in the direction of restlessness and fear (==John 16:33==). Abraham wasn't moving in faith. He was moving in fear.

## Application
When you face a famine, first ask what is standing in front of you—a Goliath to be fought, a devil to be resisted, or God's own process of maturing you through testing. Three different situations call for three different responses, and assuming every famine is the devil may mean resisting something God is using for your maturity. Then ask the better question—not “How can I get out of this?” but “What can I get out of this?” (==James 1:2-4==). And until God says move: stay. You are safer in the famine of His will than in the palace outside of it.
