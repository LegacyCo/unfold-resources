## Commentary
“You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall not commit adultery.’” Everyone listening knew the commandment, and many could check it off. The Pharisee hears it and thinks, “Check—haven’t done that.” Then comes “But I say to you…”—and suddenly Jesus is not singling out the adulterers. He is talking to everyone in the room.

Jesus is confronting a righteousness that goes only skin deep—a standard reduced to behavior management. True righteousness is never merely what we do or refrain from doing; it flows from what we believe about God, about ourselves, about His word, and about others. “Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness” (==Genesis 15:6==). Belief shapes identity, and in the kingdom, identity determines behavior.

Here is how the connection works: if I believe I can take advantage of another image-bearer in private and remain righteous simply because no one saw, then I am believing that God is someone other than who He actually is. The Pharisees measured righteousness by what their hands had or had not touched. But God sees to the heart (==1 Samuel 16:7==). His word pierces the interior realm of soul and spirit and judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart (==Hebrews 4:12==).

Jesus does not only ask, “What did you do?” He asks, “What are you allowing to rule you in private?” Hands can be spotless while the thought life is being discipled by fantasy. The kingdom’s standard reaches where religion’s checklist cannot—because the King is Lord of the invisible life too.

## Application
Stop measuring your righteousness by the sins you haven’t committed. That is the Pharisee’s scorecard, and Jesus has already dismissed it. Instead, ask the question He is actually asking: “What am I allowing to rule me in private?”

Then go a level deeper—to belief. Somewhere beneath every hidden compromise is a quiet lie about God: “He doesn’t see this. This doesn’t count. I’m still fine.” Repent at that level, not just at the level of behavior. Invite the Lord to do what His word does: “Search me, O God, and know my heart… see if there is any wicked way in me” (==Psalm 139:23-24==). Behavior modification produces a managed life. Believing rightly about God produces a transformed one.
