## Commentary
Psalm 5 contrasts the wicked with the one who comes before God in worship. David is clear that evil cannot dwell with the Lord, and the boastful cannot stand in His sight (Psalm 5:4–6).

Then he says, “But as for me…”

That could sound like self-confidence, but David immediately grounds his access to God in mercy. “I will come into Your house in the multitude of Your mercy.”

David doesn't enter because he is morally superior to everyone else. He enters because God is merciful. He knows that access to God is never earned by human righteousness. It is received through the covenant mercy of the Lord.
