## Commentary
One of the great tests of faith is moving from the season of believing while in the presence of Jesus (believing because you see) to believing even when you can't see. Thomas had no capacity to believe until he saw the nail prints in Jesus' hands and put his own hand into Jesus' side. Jesus is so kind to give Thomas what he needed in that moment. But He teaches us that there is a greater blessing for those who believe without seeing (v29). It is a higher level of maturity that He wants us to occupy. This is the faith of God, who sees what is not there and speaks as if it is.

Recall how Peter walked on water as he kept his eyes on Jesus (Matthew 14:31). In the next section Matthew records those in the land of Genesaret who were healed as they touched the hem of His garment (Matthew 14:36). The same goes for the woman with the issue of blood (Mark 5:25-34). These all received from Jesus because of "their faith." Faith rose up in them at the presence of Jesus. This is one of His benefits. But blessed are those who take their encounter with Him and let it grow into a level of maturity that can still access the blessings of His presence without His presence actually being present. 

Remarking on this passage in John 20, Warren Wiersbe writes this, "We call him 'doubting Thomas' but Jesus did not rebuke him for his doubts. He rebuked him for unbelief. Doubt is often an intellectual problem: we want to believe, but the faith is overwhelmed by problems and questions. Unbelief is a moral problem; we simply will not believe.

## Application
There’s a real difference between struggling to believe and refusing to believe. Doubt brings its questions to Jesus; unbelief hardens itself against Him. One is the ache of a heart that wants clarity. The other is the posture of a heart that won't yield. God is not intimidated by honest questions, but He does confront the kind of resistance that keeps us from surrender.
