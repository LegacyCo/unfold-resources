## Commentary
“I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance.”

A physician is not repelled by sickness—sickness is the reason he exists. In the same way, sin does not disqualify a person from the call of Christ; sin is precisely what the call addresses. The people at Levi’s table had nothing to commend them except their need, and their need was enough. Meanwhile the ones who considered themselves righteous—who felt no need—placed themselves outside the reach of a mission aimed at the needy (==Luke 18:9-14==). Jesus isn’t saying some people are righteous enough to skip repentance. He’s exposing the one thing that keeps a person from the Physician: the conviction that you’re well.

And notice where the calling happened: at a feast, over food, in the middle of Levi’s friendships. Jesus absorbed the reputation that came with those tables—“a friend of tax collectors and sinners” (==Luke 7:34==)—because you cannot call people you will not sit with. The call to repentance came wrapped in welcome, and it worked: Levi left everything, and his table became the doorway for his whole world to meet the Physician (==Luke 5:28==).

## Application
Take both seats at Levi’s table. First, the sinner’s seat: if you’ve been waiting to come to Jesus until you’re better—until the habit is broken, the mess is cleaned up, the guilt subsides—you have the order backwards. Sick people come to doctors sick. Come as you are, and let the Physician do what He came to do. Second, Levi’s seat: consider throwing your own version of his feast. Invite the people from your old world, or the ones the religious avoid, to your table.
