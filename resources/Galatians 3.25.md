## Commentary
The covenant God made with Abraham is the promise that Jesus steps into, not the law that was given through Moses. The law was "added" and served as a "tutor to bring us to Christ." V23 says that "we were kept under guard by the law, kept for faith which would afterward be revealed" by Christ. Now that faith in Christ has come, we are no longer under the guardianship of the law...it was a temporary addition.

This is why Jesus rebuked the Pharisees so strongly, they were serving the temporary guardian, not the covenant. This is why Jesus healed on the Sabbath and didn't mind His disciples eating the heads of grain on the Sabbath. Those rules were guardians to serve God's people until the Seed would come. And now that the Seed had come, the law was fulfilled. It had served its purpose.

This is from "The Coming Great Blood Communion Revival" by Dean Briggs: "The Law sprang from Israel's refusal to trust God, but here's an important fact: it neither replaced nor extended Abraham's covenant. The Law was a different thing altogether, stacked on top. Abrahams's covenant would never end, but because his descendants would not relate to Yahweh as Abraham had, God brought Israel to Sinai to negotiate new terms for the relationship. Instead of faith-based righteousness, Yahweh chose to impose performance-based righteousness."

## Application
For anyone to return to the tutor is foolishness (in Paul's language). Not only was it temporary, but it had no ability within itself to give life (v21). No one is justified by the law (v11). No one is saved by the tutor, so why return to it? We are adopted now as sons through faith in Christ (v26). Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us (v13).
