## Commentary
Many of us were taught that holy means “without sin.” But test that definition against the throne room. When the four living creatures cry “Holy, holy, holy” over God (==Revelation 4:8==), are they saying, “Lord, we worship You because You’ve never sinned”? That’s nonsense—Scripture says He cannot even be tempted with evil (==James 1:13==). No, they’re declaring something far bigger: “Lord, there is *none like You*.”

Now let’s be very clear: God *is* absolutely sinless. There is no darkness in Him at all (==1 John 1:5==), and sin has never touched His nature—it can’t. But His sinlessness isn’t what Scripture means when it calls Him holy. Sinlessness is one expression of His holiness, not the definition of it.

Holy means distinct. Separate. Altogether different from any other. So when God says, “Be holy, for I am holy,” He isn’t saying “never sin as I have never sinned.” He’s saying: *choose to be as distinct as I am.* That’s exactly how He used the word with Israel: “You are a holy people to the LORD your God… a special treasure above all the peoples on the face of the earth” (==Deuteronomy 7:6==). Was Israel sinless? Far from it. God was telling them there was no other nation like them. And Peter picks up that identical language for the church: “a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a *holy* nation, His own special people” (==1 Peter 2:9==). Holy doesn’t mean sinless. Holy means wonderfully distinct.

Now watch what that definition does to our motivation. It’s the distinction that compels us to refuse the sin that would contaminate it. I don’t avoid sin so I don’t go to hell. I refuse sin because I refuse to do anything that erases the uniqueness of who God says I am—I don’t dare contradict the identity He speaks over me.

Most of us have this backwards. We think what we do determines who we are: I drive trucks, so I’m a truck driver. I cook, so I’m a chef. I sin, so I’m a sinner. That’s not how the kingdom works. If you are in Christ, Jesus bought you with His blood—He owns you, so He gets to define you (==1 Corinthians 6:20==). And He says you are holy: distinct, chosen, a special treasure. When we believe what He says about us, our lives bear the fruit of holiness—and the world gets to see a people who look like Jesus.

## Word Study
- *holy* : **hagios** (Greek) / **qadosh** (Hebrew) — set apart, distinct, other; separated *to* someone as much as separated *from* something. The root idea is never mere moral spotlessness but otherness—being in a category of one.

Where the definition proves itself:

- ==Deuteronomy 7:6== — sinful, stubborn Israel called “holy”: not a verdict on their behavior, but on their distinct belonging.
- ==Exodus 3:5== — even *ground* can be holy. Dirt has no morality; it can only have distinction—set apart by the presence of God.

Summary: holiness is a category before it’s a conduct. God declares what you are—set apart, His—and the conduct grows out of the category.
