## Commentary
“Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken, let us have grace, by which we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear. For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hold the two halves of this sentence together. We serve God—near Him, before Him, in acceptable service. And our God is a consuming fire. The writer of Hebrews sees no contradiction; he sees a reason for reverence. It is no small thing that people can stand before this God and minister to Him. Under the old covenant, a priest underwent an entire process of consecration before entering the Lord’s house (==Exodus 29:1-9==)—and even then, tradition holds that the high priest entered the Most Holy Place with a rope tied around his waist, in case he fell dead in the presence and had to be drawn out. Nearness to this God was understood to be dangerous. Nadab and Abihu learned it in a moment (==Leviticus 10:1-2==).

Now consider what the new covenant claims: this same God—the consuming fire, unchanged in holiness—*lives inside His people* (==1 Corinthians 3:16==). The fact that God dwells in His people and they are not consumed is astounding. What makes it possible is not that the fire cooled; it is the blood of Jesus, which consecrates completely what the old order could only consecrate ceremonially: “having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus” (==Hebrews 10:19==). The rope has been retired, the consecration is real.

Boldness without reverence forgets what kind of God we approach—familiarity that takes the blood for granted. Reverence without boldness forgets what the blood accomplished—distance the veil-tearing was meant to end (==Matthew 27:51==). Hebrews commends both at once: serve Him acceptably, with reverence and godly fear, in a kingdom that cannot be shaken—remembering always what kind of God has made Himself approachable.

## Word Study
- *serve* : **latreuō** — to render sacred service; priestly ministry offered to God as worship. This is not the word for household service to a master, but for the service of the sanctuary—what priests do before the Lord.

The word’s reach:

- ==Romans 12:1== — presenting your body as a living sacrifice is “your reasonable *service* (latreia).” The living sacrifice is priestly work.
- ==Revelation 22:3== — in the city where His throne stands, “His servants shall *serve* (latreuō) Him.” The occupation of eternity is this word.

Summary: latreuō means the service this verse describes is worship rendered by priests—which is what the blood has made every believer (==1 Peter 2:9==). We serve the consuming fire from inside His own house, consecrated, unconsumed, and rightly awed by both.
