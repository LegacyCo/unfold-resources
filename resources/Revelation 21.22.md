## Commentary
“But I saw no temple in it, for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple.”

John has just heard the announcement that ends the story: “Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and He will dwell with them” (==Revelation 21:3==). So we would expect the description of the city to center on its temple—the crowning dwelling place, the final version of everything the tabernacle and temple pointed to. Instead John reports an absence: *I saw no temple in it.* And then the reason: the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb *are* the temple. The city doesn’t contain a dwelling place for God, because God Himself has become the dwelling place. There is no longer a structure that mediates His presence—His presence is the structure.

This tells us what every house of God in history actually was. The tabernacle, the temple, and every gathering place since were shadows of a Person (==Hebrews 8:5==; ==Colossians 2:17==). Whatever we build for Him in our generation is meant to testify of who He is—which is exactly why it matters that we build according to His design rather than ours (==Exodus 25:40==). A shadow is only useful if it has the shape of the thing casting it. Build the church according to our own preferences and the shadow testifies of us; build according to His pattern and people can read what He is like from what they see among His people.

The trajectory of this verse is already underway. God does not dwell in temples made with hands (==Acts 17:24==); His house now is one made of human beings—living stones being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit (==1 Peter 2:5==; ==Ephesians 2:22==). The end of the story is not a magnificent building with God in it. It is God and the Lamb, dwelling with a redeemed family, needing no building at all. Every faithful church between now and then is a preview of that—and the less our churches point to themselves, the more clearly they point to the day when the temple is the Lamb.
