## Commentary
The seventy years in Babylon were not arbitrary.

Israel’s exile was not random punishment. Second Chronicles says the land had to enjoy its Sabbaths. For years, Israel had possessed the land without honoring the Lord’s command to give the land its rest. They sowed, harvested, gathered, and consumed, but they refused the Sabbath rhythm God had commanded in Leviticus 25.

So God gave the land what it was owed. Because He keeps covenant even when His people do not.

God had commanded a Sabbath rest for the land every seventh year. Israel ignored that covenantal responsibility, but the Lord did not ignore it. If the people possessing the land would not honor the rest God had appointed for the land, then God would remove the people until the land received what had been stolen from it.

That is why the exile lasted seventy years. It was according to the Sabbaths Israel had robbed from the land.

The land had been treated as a possession without being honored as a trust. Israel had lived on covenant soil while disregarding covenant responsibility. They wanted the inheritance without the obedience. They wanted the produce without the divine rhythm. They wanted the blessing of the land without honoring the Lord of the land.

But the land remembered.

The same land that had witnessed the promises to the fathers also witnessed the disobedience of the sons. The covenant was made with a man, but the land was never incidental. It was promised, possessed, cultivated, and defiled under the eyes of God. And when Israel forgot the Sabbath of the land, God remembered.

Exile, then, became a forced Sabbath.

The people went into bondage, and the land finally rested. The silence of abandoned fields became the testimony that God’s word cannot be ignored forever. What man refuses to surrender willingly, God may eventually require through judgment.

Second Chronicles 36:21 reminds us that God’s covenant faithfulness is not sentimental. He is faithful to His promises, faithful to His warnings, faithful to His people, and faithful to the land He entrusted to them.

If we will not steward what God gives according to His ways, He loves His creation enough to intervene.
