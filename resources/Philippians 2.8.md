## Commentary
This is from *Jesus and the Undoing of Adam* by C. Baxter Kruger: There are at least two facts about the Christian God that are unparalleled: The first is the doctrine of the Trinity. The second is the humility of God. In no other religion do we have a god who stoops, a god who comes down to enter into human history in the most inconceivably personal way. But here in Christianity, we have a God who wants to be united with us and who is prepared to humble Himself and even to suffer to accomplish such a union. 

The gods of human imagination are indifferent towards the human race. Towering above us in their glory, they are distant and unapproachable, preoccupied with themselves and with things far more important than human existence. These gods exist in eternal separation from us, and whatever interest they take in human affairs serves their own ends.

## Application
If humility is the nature of God, then pride can never be the way of those who belong to Him. Jesus did not reveal His greatness protecting His status. He revealed His greatness by coming down.

To become like Christ is to learn how to stoop. It is to enter the pain of others, move toward the overlooked, serve without needing recognition, and give ourselves for the good of people who may never repay us. Humility is not thinking less of ourselves; it is becoming free enough from ourselves to love others well. As Andrew Murray wrote in his book, *Humility*, "Humility toward men will be the only sufficient proof that our humility before God is real. It will be the only proof that humility has taken up its abode in us and become our very nature that we actually, like Christ, have made ourselves 'of no reputation.'"

We should also stop assuming that God is reluctant to come near us in our weakness. The incarnation proves otherwise. He is not intimidated by our humanity. He has already crossed the distance between us.

Receive the God who comes down, and then follow Him downward. The path into His likeness will rarely lead toward self-importance. It will lead toward service, sacrifice, nearness, and love.
