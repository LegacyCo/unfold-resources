## Commentary
According to Markus Barth, "In approximately one fifth of the sentences in Luke's Gospel and Acts, meals play a conspicuous role." Koenig even describes eating and drinking as the "organizing principle" in Luke's story. There are over 50 references to food in Luke's gospel. It could almost be said that in Luke Jesus is either going to a meal, at a meal, or leaving a meal. I heard it said this way: Jesus walked people into the Kingdom of God one meal at a time.

Meals in Scripture are never just about food. A table is a place of fellowship, covenant, and revelation. It's where barriers come down. It's where strangers come friends and friends become family. In Luke's Gospel especially, Jesus doesn't only preach in synagogues or perform miracles in large crowds. He sits at tables. He eats with tax collectors. He allows sinful people to draw near. He reclines with Pharisees. While He feeds multitudes, He reveals Himself in the breaking of bread (Luke 24:31).

The table becomes a visible expression of the Kingdom of God because the Gospel is an invitation to communion. This is one of the beautiful aspects of Jesus's ministry: He shared space with the outcast. And nowhere was that more scandalous than at the table. In a culture obsessed with purity boundaries, religious rituals, and social rank, Jesus used the table as an opportunity to tear down walls. He showed us that the Kingdom of God comes close enough to touch and near enough to feast with.

And this theme runs straight into Acts and into the life of the early church. The people of God are formed around shared bread and shared life. The table was central to the early church. It was, and is, the gospel made visible.

Jesus walked people into the Kingdom one meal at a time because the Kingdom is the life of God shared with humanity.

## Application
We should not overlook the spiritual power of the table. Hospitality is not a small thing. Sharing a meal can be an act of ministry, a form of witness, and a doorway into deep fellowship. Some people may hear truth at a table before they ever hear it from a pulpit. The kingdom often comes in the normal rhythm of everyday life; through bread, conversation, hospitality, and love embodied in table fellowship.
