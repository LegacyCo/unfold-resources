## Commentary
“Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching.”

Notice that the writer of Hebrews says forsaking the assembly was already “the manner of some” in the first century. But our generation has turned it into a lifestyle with a theology attached. Covid made acceptable what many secretly hoped for all along: couch-potato church. “I can stay home, listen for a few minutes, send my tithe online, and never have to do life with people? Finally.” Let me be fair: YouTube sermons are great supplemental resources. But they are not going to clothe you in glory the way doing life in community will, and reposting your favorite social-media prophet will not guard you from the schemes of the enemy the way family will. Even attending services all over town doesn’t make you part of a church if you never open your life to anyone or submit yourself to a local body. Isolated Christianity has nothing in common with biblical Christianity—it’s a modern invention.

We understand this everywhere except church. Michael Jordan won six championships—and needed Scottie Pippen. Lennon needed McCartney. Steve Jobs was brilliant, and he needed Steve Wozniak to build Apple. The most gifted music students in the world still enroll in school, because they know their goals require things no one gets alone: training from someone further along, perspective on the weaknesses they can’t see, and experience someone else is willing to impart. If talent that extraordinary knows it can’t develop in isolation, why do we believe our souls can? And nature preaches the same sermon with teeth: watch what happens to the zebra that doesn’t stay integrated with its herd. The lion is not impressed by his independence. Your adversary walks about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour—and Peter’s very next word is about standing firm together (==1 Peter 5:8-9==).

The church isn’t perfect—but the family of God is still His designed environment for growing His people to fullness (==Ephesians 4:13==). So ask yourself the questions that expose whether you’re assembled or just adjacent: Who is discipling me, and who am I discipling? Who can I trust with what I’m actually struggling with? Who is continually calling me higher? If no names come to mind, you may be saved, you may be born again—but you’re grazing at the edge of the herd. And so much the more as you see the Day approaching: get into the middle of the family.
