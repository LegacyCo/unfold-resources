## Commentary
John the Baptist came as the forerunner of the Messiah, and Luke describes his assignment with road-building language borrowed from Isaiah: “Prepare the way of the LORD; make His paths straight. Every valley shall be filled and every mountain and hill brought low; the crooked places shall be made straight and the rough ways smooth” (==Luke 3:4-5==; ==Isaiah 40:3-4==).

In the ancient world, that’s what you did before a king traveled through your region—you graded the road. Filled the low places, cut down the high places, smoothed the rough stretches, so the king could come all the way to you. But the Jews knew the Messiah wasn’t just coming to visit; He was coming to *reign*. So hear the promise inside the road work: when this King takes His throne, the leveling isn’t just for the road—it’s for the people. The wild topography of life under lesser kings—the towering highs, the crushing lows, the crooked and rough stretches—starts to get smoothed out, because the King who is coming is the Prince of Peace (==Isaiah 9:6==).

That’s exactly what you see in people who live under His reign. Paul could take everything this world threw at him—abundance and hunger, open doors and shipwrecks—and say, “I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content” (==Philippians 4:11==). Not because his circumstances were level, but because his heart was. When the Prince of Peace is on the throne, His people carry a confidence: joy, because we have the best King; peace, because we know who He is. The dramatic swings—the high highs chasing an experience and the low lows when it fades—belong to a life still governed by valleys and mountains. Under the King, the ground levels.

So if your walk with Jesus feels like a rollercoaster, don’t start by fixing the terrain. Ask the forerunner’s question: has the way been prepared for the King to actually reign here?
