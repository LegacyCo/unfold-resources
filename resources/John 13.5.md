## Commentary
If we try to measure the progress of Jesus' ministry over three years, the numbers would be disappointing. Yet Jesus never took His eyes off of His primary responsibility. He spent three years building up the character of twelve young men. 

If you are wondering whether discipleship is easy to measure, look at the results of these three years. Even for the Son of God, the results were often messy and disappointing. Jesus was teaching them how to live in the kingdom of God on earth, and this is hard to measure with metrics. 

Discipleship is measured by its weight not statistics.

## Application
On the night before the cross, Jesus is on the floor washing the feet of twelve men, one of which would betray Him. That tells you something about how He measured ministry. He was shaping the inner life of men who would carry His message after He was gone. 

This is the challenge for us. We are often obsessed with what can be counted, while Jesus is concerned with what has weight. Numbers can rise without depth. But discipleship is the slow, hidden work of forming Christlike character in real people, and that kind of work is rarely impressive in the moment. So don't despise ministry that seems small, slow, or hard to measure. If it's producing humility, servanthood, faithfulness, and a deeper conformity to Jesus, then something weighty is being built—even if the metrics don't look impressive.
