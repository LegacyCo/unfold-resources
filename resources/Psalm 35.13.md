## Commentary
“…I humbled myself with fasting; and my prayer would return to my own heart.”

David defines what fasting actually is: a self-imposed posture of the body that humbles the soul. Self-imposed, because it is something we have the freedom to choose. A posture of the body, because it puts the body into submission to the will. And the humbling of the soul is the point—you bring your body low so that your soul can tell you the truth.

We are very good at deceiving ourselves. A person can be convinced that he depends on God while nothing in his soul is actually bowed low. But the stomach can't lie. By the afternoon of a fast, the body is preaching a sermon, and the sermon is this: you are a creature that survives only by consuming something outside yourself—something you cannot make for yourself. Every hunger pang is a confession of dependence, and fasting takes that confession and points it at God. The body says *I need*, and the spirit answers, *yes—and what I need most is God* (==Matthew 4:4==). It is astounding what surfaces when the body is denied what it needs to survive: the irritation, the short fuse, the impatience, the boredom normally pacified by snacking. The revelation of those things is God's mercy, showing you who you really are—bringing you face to face with the parts of your heart not yet fully submitted to the cross. Food is good; food is a gift; but food is not God. In fasting you give up a good thing so that a better, more honest thing can be heard.

## Word Study
**Humbled** is the Hebrew *anah* (עָנָה)—to bow down, afflict, humble. It is the technical term for fasting in the law: on the Day of Atonement Israel was to “afflict your souls” (==Leviticus 16:29==), and Isaiah uses it of the fast—“why have we afflicted our souls, and You take no notice?” (==Isaiah 58:3==). Ezra proclaimed a fast “that we might humble (*anah*) ourselves before our God” (==Ezra 8:21==). Summary: in Scripture's own vocabulary, fasting *is* the humbling of the soul—the body's bowing that invites the heart to follow.
