## Commentary
"But new wine must be put into new wineskins, and both are preserved."

We usually read this verse as a warning to the wineskin. It's also a word about the *timing* of the wine.

Leonard Ravenhill said, "We will never have revival as long as we are content to live without it." That's true. But there is another side to it, and it takes some maturity to see: sometimes the goodness of God is in *withholding* the very thing we're asking Him for—because we're not ready to hold it. If He poured out the measure of new wine we've been contending for into the skin we currently have, it wouldn't fill us. It would burst us. Jesus says so plainly: the wine is spilled and the skin is ruined (==Luke 5:37==). Nobody wins.

So consider that one reason we're not yet experiencing the dimension of glory we long for is not that God is reluctant, but that He is *kind*. He is giving us grace to prepare a wineskin that can actually contain what He wants to pour. He would rather delay the outpouring than waste it—because His goal isn't just that His people taste revival, burn bright, and burn out. It's that "both are preserved": the wine and the skin. The move of God and the people of God, together, lasting.

Think about how rare that is. No revival in history has ever been handed off to the next generation. Wales, Azusa, the Hebrides, Brownsville—glorious demonstrations of the kingdom that failed to *establish* the kingdom. The wine came, and the skins couldn't hold it. God is after something more than another season like that. He wants a people flexible enough, humble enough, knit-together enough (==Ephesians 4:16==) to hold His glory without spilling it—and to hand it to their children.

## Application
Turn your prayer for revival into a two-part prayer. Keep asking for the wine—Ravenhill is right that contentment without it is deadly. But add a second request: "Lord, make us a wineskin that can hold what You want to pour." Then get specific about what "new wineskin" means where you are: relationships that could bear the weight of a real move of God, a willingness to let structures change, a humility that would welcome Him even if He came in a way that embarrassed you. Preparing the skin *is* contending for the wine.
