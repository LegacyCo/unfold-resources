## Commentary
Naaman took with him two mule-loads of earth...soil from the land of Israel. He intended to build an earth-mound altar in Syria to the God of Israel. Why wouldn't he just use earth in Syria? "Now I know there is no God in all the earth except *in Israel*" (v15). He's not just the God of Israel, He is the God *in* Israel. He is the God of a specific place who has history and covenant with land and boundaries. As God tells His people in Exodus 20:24, "An altar of earth you shall make for Me, and you shall sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your sheep and your oxen. In every place where I cause My name to be remembered, I will come to you, and I will bless you."

Naaman must have somehow known that God's name was in those two mule-loads of soil. After encountering the one true living God, who covenants with people and places, he was going to do all he could to make sure God would come to Him again.

## Application
If we possess the earth that *remembers the Name*, then every sacrifice becomes an opportunity for encounter. He is the God who calls a land Beulah, married (Isaiah 62:4). His covenants are so strong the seep into the land.

Connect this to the sacrifice of praise/worship and you have God's recipe for re-digging a well of revival. This is the promise: every place where a previous generation built an altar in the name of Yahweh still holds within its soil the covenant promise that God will come there again and bless His people (Exodus 20:24).
