## Commentary
The word "new" is used three times here. Two of those times the word for new is *neon* and once the word for new is *kainos*.

Neon means that which has not yet been. It is quantitatively new. This is the word Jesus uses to describe the character of the wine.

Kainos means renewed, refreshed. It is qualitatively new not simply chronologically new. This is the word Jesus uses to describe the wineskin.

The point is this: periodically the wine skin was to undergo a baptism of oil so as not to harden. A wineskin was intended to continually experience renewal by way of submersion which would provide fresh tenderness. Jesus uses kainos to describe the state of the wineskin which is able to contain new wine.

Jesus is telling John's disciples that His disciples are being submerged in a baptism of renewal that comes from being with the Bridegroom. The Pharisee's and John's disciples are so accustomed to a routine rooted in ritual that they aren't able to discern the season of renewal that is available to them.

Paul uses kainos in ==2 Corinthians 5:17==, "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new (*kainos*) creation; old things have passed away, behold, all things have become new (*kainos*)." That's the state of every believer. We are in Christ and by way of that submersion into Him we have been made new.
