## Commentary
“The other disciples therefore said to him, ‘We have seen the Lord.’” There’s a subtle detail in the original language that’s easy to miss—and it changes how you read the whole week between the resurrection and Thomas’s encounter.

The verb John uses for “said” is a continuous action verb. It doesn’t mean they told him once. It means they *kept on telling him*. Day after day, for eight days: “Thomas, He’s alive. We saw Him. We’re not lying to you.” I imagine Jesus was all they talked about—and Thomas sat in the corner of his unbelief, arms crossed, conditions unmet: “I hear what you’re saying, but where’s the proof?”

When Jesus finally steps through the locked door, He walks straight to Thomas and says, “Reach your finger *here*… put your hand into My side” (==John 20:27==)—Thomas’s own demands, repeated back to him almost word for word. Jesus wasn’t in the room when Thomas set his terms. Or was He? “Wherever two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them” (==Matthew 18:20==). The risen Lord heard every condition the unbeliever set—and eight days later, He answered them personally.

This is a word for everyone who has a Thomas at their table. A spouse, a son, a father, a coworker who has heard you talk about Jesus for years and still says, “where’s the proof?” The disciples’ testimony didn’t convert Thomas—but it wasn’t wasted, either. It kept the room warm. It kept Thomas *with them* when Jesus came (==John 20:26==)—eight days of persistent witness is why he was in the house at all. Your words may never be the thing that finally opens their heart. But your words, and your refusal to stop saying them, may be the reason they’re still in the room when Jesus walks through the wall.

## Word Study
- *said* : **elegon** (Greek) — the imperfect tense of *legō*, describing continuous, repeated action in the past. Not “they told him” but “they kept on telling him, again and again.” John chose a tense that paints eight days of unrelenting testimony.

Summary: the imperfect tense is the hidden hero of this story. Keep on saying it.

## Application
Don’t stop talking about Jesus around your Thomas. Not arguing—witnessing. “We have seen the Lord” is testimony, not debate; the disciples didn’t construct proofs, they repeated what they’d experienced. So keep telling the stories of what He’s done for you, keep them at your table, keep them in the house. And take heart from the detail this passage hides in plain sight: Jesus hears every condition your Thomas has ever set. He knows exactly what they think they need. And He has a habit of showing up in person to meet it.
