## Commentary
“And after eight days His disciples were again inside, and Thomas with them. Jesus came, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst.”

The doors were shut—and Jesus walked right through them. Locked doors are never an obstacle for Jesus. Not locked doors on upper rooms, and not locked doors on hearts of unbelief.

Remember what’s behind those doors. Thomas had heard ten eyewitnesses—his closest friends, men he’d lived with and grieved with—tell him “We have seen the Lord,” and he’d looked them all in the eye and said, “Unless I see the print of the nails and put my hand into His side, I will not believe” (==John 20:25==). Those weren’t the words of a man wrestling with God; those were terms. A line in the sand. And when Jesus finally walked through the wall, He didn’t address the group. He didn’t make an announcement. He walked straight to the one man who had said *I will not believe*—and He rolled up His sleeves: “Reach your finger here… put your hand into My side.”

Sit with how backwards that is from the god most people imagine. A hard-hearted man refuses to believe, and instead of leaving him in his unbelief, God shows up—uninvited, undeserved—and humbles Himself to give the man exactly what he demanded. That's who God is. He doesn’t wait for you to resolve your questions before He comes to you. He doesn’t hold back until you’ve cleaned yourself up and sorted out your theology. He meets you *in* your unbelief, not after it. The father who cried, “Lord, I believe; help my unbelief!” got his miracle that same hour (==Mark 9:24==).

So maybe you’ve been sleeping in a bed of unbelief for years—unanswered prayers, disappointment, church hurt, conditions you set long ago that God never seemed to meet. Hear this: you are not too far. The Man who walked through a locked door to reach one unbeliever has no trouble reaching you. If you’re not afraid of His scars, He’s not afraid of your unbelief. And even faith that’s cracked and barely enough to limp through a door is faith He’ll receive—because when Thomas finally saw Him, all the terms and conditions collapsed into five words: “My Lord and my God!” (==John 20:28==).
