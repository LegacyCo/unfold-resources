## Commentary
Revelation 14 presents a striking contrast between those who worship the beast and those who belong to the Lord.

Of those who worship the beast, Scripture says, “They have no rest day and night.” Their judgment is more than something reserved for the future. They are already participating in the inheritance of the kingdom they have chosen. A life separated from God becomes marked by restlessness, agitation, chaos, unfulfillment, and the inability to find lasting peace.

Sin always promises freedom, but it produces slavery. The world offers countless ways to numb the soul, distract the mind, or satisfy desire, but none of them can give rest. A person can be surrounded by comfort and still have no peace. They can have success, pleasure, recognition, and wealth, yet remain inwardly tormented because rest is not found in circumstances. Rest is found in Christ.

Only two verses later, John writes, “Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on…so that they may rest from their labors” (==Revelation 14:13==).

The contrast is deliberate. Those who are outside of Christ have no rest, but those who are in the Lord enter into rest. Their labor ends, but their relationship with God does not. They have spent their lives in union with Christ, and death becomes the doorway into the full experience of the rest that was already theirs in Him.

This rest is not merely inactivity. It is the end of striving, resistance, and the burden of living in a fallen world. It is the soul finally experiencing without interruption what it has always possessed positionally in Christ.

The question of rest, then, is ultimately a question of worship. Whatever we give ourselves to will shape the condition of our souls. The beast produces endless agitation. The Lamb gives rest.

Those who are in Christ do not have to wait until death to begin experiencing this reality. We are already in the Lord. We are already joined to the Prince of Peace. We can live and work from rest now, knowing that one day every trace of restlessness will be gone.
