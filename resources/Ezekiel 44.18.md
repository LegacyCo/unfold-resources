## Commentary
“They shall have linen turbans on their heads and linen trousers on their bodies; they shall not clothe themselves with anything that causes sweat.”

The sons of Zadok, who ministered to the LORD in the inner court, were to wear linen—no wool, nothing that causes sweat—while the priests assigned to the outer court were given what one translation calls “the less important work” of the temple (==Ezekiel 44:14==). The two courts mark two kinds of ministry. The outer court is horizontal, facing the people, marked by activity, performance, and effort; there is no vision there, only repetitious tasks—an employee does not need vision, only instructions. The inner court is vertical, facing the Lord, and it is in that stillness that vision is given. Those who receive it are not marked by sweat. Under the new covenant the veil is torn and the inner court is open to every believer (==Hebrews 10:19-22==)—so if a minister today lives in the outer court, it is not God's assignment; it is his own choice of activity over stillness, ideas over revelation, performance before people over ministry to the Lord.

Why would anyone choose the outer court? Usually a combination of two things: a drive to “do something for God,” and an unbelief that God will actually give them vision. The outer court has recognizable residents. The *self-promoter*, content to demonstrate his gifting where people can see it, mistaking momentum and public approval for God's blessing—since he lacks God's vision, which comes with God's promotion. The *fundraiser*, anxious and desperate toward his supporters—since he lacks God's vision, which comes with God's provision, he is left funding ideas that were never His. The *CEO*, full of ideas, excited about everything and committed to nothing, leveraging the gifting of people drawn to his charisma to build what he never could alone—elevating them high enough to satisfy their sense of calling, never high enough to seek vision of their own. All three share a horizontal priority, and rest eludes them. God is not opposed to hard work, excellence, or tools like social media—but if God could speak through a donkey, He does not now need a verified account. A lack of vision smells like sweat.

## Application
Ask the uncomfortable questions before God and the people you are accountable to: which of the outer-court ministers am I prone to become—the self-promoter, the fundraiser, or the CEO? Which am I drawn to follow? Then choose the linen: prioritize ministering to the Lord over performing before people, stillness over activity, revelation over your best idea. The inner court is open. Anyone still in the outer court is there by choice.
