## Commentary
The Levites had already been chosen by God for service at the tabernacle, but being chosen did not eliminate the need for cleansing.

God tells Moses, “Take the Levites from among the children of Israel and cleanse them ceremonially” (==Numbers 8:6==). The process included sprinkling them with “water of purification,” shaving their bodies, washing their clothes, and offering sacrifices (==Numbers 8:7–8==).

This is important because the Levites were not being cleansed because they had committed some specific sin recorded in the text. They were being prepared to enter into service near the presence of God.

Throughout the Old Testament, holiness is fundamentally about being set apart to God. Something could be common and then become holy because God claimed it for Himself and devoted it to His purposes. But whatever was brought near to God had to be cleansed and consecrated.
