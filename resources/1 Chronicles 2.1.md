## Commentary
1 Chronicles 2 narrows the story of God's people.

Chapter 1 begins with Adam and moves through the nations, showing that the story of Israel belongs within the larger story of humanity. But here in chapter 2, the focus tightens around the sons of Israel, and especially around the tribe of Judah.

The Chronicler is not simply preserving family records. He is tracing the line of promise. After the exile, the people of Judah needed to remember that their story had not ended in Babylon. The covenant purposes of God were still alive.

So the genealogy moves from a general focus to a more specific focus — toward Judah.

Judah was not the firstborn. Reuben was. But Reuben forfeited his position through sin, and God’s redemptive plan moved through Judah instead (see Genesis 35:22, 49:3-4, 1 Chronicles 5:1). This is one of the consistent themes of Scripture: God’s purposes are not bound by human expectation, birth order, status, or visible strength. The Lord chooses according to His own wisdom.

And from Judah would eventually come David.

That is one of the main concerns of Chronicles. The writer is rebuilding the memory of a people who had been shaken by exile, and he does it by reminding them of David’s line. David’s house may look weak. The throne may seem empty. But God has not forgotten His promise.

This chapter also includes names that remind us the line of promise was not clean or impressive by human standards. Judah’s family history includes sin, failure, death, and grief. Yet God’s covenant continues moving forward. He does not wait for flawless families to accomplish His purposes. He works through real people, with real histories, and brings His promise through them anyway.

That does not excuse sin. But it does reveal mercy.

God is not embarrassed by the broken places in the story. He is sovereign over them. The genealogy of Judah teaches us that God can carry His purposes through complicated family lines, painful histories, and generations marked by both faithfulness and failure.
