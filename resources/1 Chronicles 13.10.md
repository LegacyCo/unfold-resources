## Commentary
Uzzah's death is one of the more troubling moments in the Old Testament, because at first glance he did what any of us would've done. David and all Israel are celebrating before God "with all their might," with singing, harps, tambourines, cymbals and trumpets (==1 Chronicles 13:8==). The ark is finally coming home. Then, at the threshing floor of Chidon, the oxen stumble, the cart lurches, and Uzzah reaches out to steady the ark. He wasn't trying to dishonor God. If anything, he was trying to protect something sacred.

So why would God strike him dead?

The answer has to do with the holiness of God and the instructions He'd already given about the ark. In ==Numbers 4:15==, the Lord told the Kohathites that although they were responsible for carrying the holy things, they weren't allowed to touch them, "lest they die." 

Uzzah's hand was the last link in a chain of bad decisions that started long before he reached out. The ark was never supposed to be on a cart in the first place (==1 Chronicles 13:7==). If it had been carried the way God said, there would have been no oxen to stumble and nothing to catch.

Most of us hear the word *holy* as a report card, a word for moral perfection. But when Scripture calls God holy, it's saying He's in a category of one. The Hebrew word *qadosh* means set apart, distinct. The ark was where that distinct presence dwelt among His people, and Israel had started handling it like cargo.

When Aaron's sons, Nadab and Abihu, offered unauthorized fire before the Lord, they were struck dead too. Moses reminded Aaron of what God had said: "By those who come near Me I must be regarded as holy; and before all the people I must be glorified" (==Leviticus 10:3==). Coming near to God is a privilege. It's never permission to handle Him on our own terms.

This account doesn't show a God who is harsh or indifferent to Uzzah's intentions. He'd already made His requirements known. Israel had lived with these instructions for centuries, and the God who struck Uzzah is the same God who revealed Himself to Moses as “merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth” (==Exodus 34:6==). In that same revelation He said He would “by no means” clear the guilty (==Exodus 34:7==). His patience and His holiness have never pulled against each other, and He doesn’t change (==Malachi 3:6==). The tragedy is that Israel didn’t take His instructions seriously. And the same presence that broke out against Uzzah went on to bless the house of Obed-Edom for the next three months (==1 Chronicles 13:14==).

Under the New Covenant, we've been given extraordinary access to the Father through Jesus. We're invited to come boldly, not because His holiness has diminished, but because Christ has opened the way by His own blood (==Hebrews 10:19–22==). Yet the same letter that invites us to the throne of grace also tells us to "serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear. For our God is a consuming fire" (==Hebrews 12:28–29==).

Grace has made it possible for us to draw near to the Holy One without being consumed. And the more clearly we see what that cost Jesus, the more seriously we'll treat the privilege of coming near.

## Application
You have more access to God than Uzzah ever had. There's no veil, no poles, and no Levites standing between you and His presence. Christ has opened the way, so come in freely.
