## Commentary
For Paul, contentment springs from complete readiness to accept whatever God gives. He makes no distinction between the necessary and the extravagant, but simply gives thanks for everything. He can accept both abundance and want as part of his life, and he gives thanks that he has received both as a gift. 

In the case of enduring his "thorn in the flesh," he is called upon to exercise the virtue of contentment and to request no more than God is graciously pleased to give (see 2 Corinthians 12:7ff). The thorn serves as a repeated reminder to him that he is utterly dependent, in all he does, upon the enabling power of the Lord.

## Application
Pay attention to the conditions you keep attaching to your peace: “I’ll be okay when…”

Paul is teaching us to bring those places before the Lord and learn contentment there, not after they disappear.

This doesn't mean you stop asking God for relief. Paul asked for the thorn to be removed. But while you ask, you also learn to receive the grace He is giving today.

Contentment begins when you stop treating your current circumstances as proof that God has withheld Himself from you. He may not have given you the answer you wanted yet. But He has given you Himself, and all the grace necessary to endure.

If Christ is enough in every season — both in seasons of abounding and being abased — then you, too, can "do all things through Him who strengthens me."
