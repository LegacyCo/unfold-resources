## Commentary
“Those that entered… went in as God had commanded him; *and the LORD shut him in*.” Read that last clause slowly. Noah built the ark, gathered the animals, and led his family aboard—but Noah didn’t close the door. God did. And there’s a world of comfort hiding in that detail.

When the fountains of the deep burst open and the rain began to fall, that sealed door protected Noah from more than water. Imagine what pressed against it from the outside—people who would have done anything to get through. And some of them, surely, were people Noah knew. I can hear the tension: “If I could just crack the door open for that family—they’re good people, Lord, why can’t they come?” This is why God closed the door and not Noah. We don’t know what we need. Left to ourselves, we’d drag half of the old season into the new one. Closed doors are the Lord’s divine protection for His people—and they protect us not only from what’s on the other side, but from ourselves.

It isn’t always people we’d smuggle aboard. Sometimes it’s stuff. But the door the Lord is asking you to walk through is often too small for what you got used to in a previous season. The maintenance of your lifestyle is too big to fit through the door. The way you allocate your time is too big. Your pride is too big. Abraham was told to leave his family behind and took Lot along anyway—and Lot cost him grief for years (==Genesis 13:7==; ==Genesis 19:29==). Everything from one season isn’t intended to travel with you into the next, and thankfully, God knows the difference even when we don’t.

And there’s a third layer of protection, the deepest one: closed doors guard our affection. Whatever competes with God for your heart will not be allowed through the door. If money and success have a hold on you, Jesus will nail your bank account to the cross—for your good and His glory. If what others think of you competes with what you think of Him, He’ll nail your reputation to the cross. If your twenty-year plan competes with His plan, He’ll nail your yellow brick road to the cross. It never feels like protection in real time. But the God who “changes the times and the seasons” (==Daniel 2:21==), who “opens and no one shuts, and shuts and no one opens” (==Revelation 3:7==), only closes doors on what would keep you from looking like Jesus. The shut door isn’t Him locking you out of something good. It’s Him sealing you into something better.
