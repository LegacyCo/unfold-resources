## Commentary
“And he moved from there to the mountain east of Bethel, and he pitched his tent… there he built an altar to the LORD and called on the name of the LORD.”

Abraham the journeyman has become Abraham the builder—but unlike his forefathers on the plain of Shinar, he is not building towers and cities to make his name great (==Genesis 11:4==). He is building altars of worship to the LORD. Warren Wiersbe observed that you can trace Abraham's steps through Canaan by the altars he left behind. Wherever he went, Abraham was marked by two things: his tent and his altar. The tent marked him as a stranger and pilgrim who did not belong to the world he was traveling through—“by faith he dwelt in the land of promise as in a foreign country, dwelling in tents… for he waited for the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God” (==Hebrews 11:9-10==). The altar marked him as a citizen of heaven who would not worship the gods of the land he passed through. Together they gave witness that he was separated from this world and devoted to the God of heaven.

What is an altar? A place of worship and a place of sacrifice. On this side of the cross, the ultimate sacrifice has been made by the Lamb of God once for all (==Hebrews 10:12==)—so the believer's altars are no longer places of sacrifice, but they are still places of worship, memorial stones that mark who God is and what He has done. Communion is an altar of worship (==1 Corinthians 11:26==). A date on the calendar can be an annual altar—the day you were born again, the day you saw your first miracle. A place can be an altar: a specific spot you go to unplug from the world and remember who God is. A journal can be an altar, documenting the miracles, dreams, and prophetic words that have shaped you. Altars are not an old covenant relic. Every believer's walk can be traceable the way Abraham's was—by the altars left behind.

## Application
Build both markers into your life. The tent: hold your residence in this world loosely, living as someone passing through rather than settling in (==1 Peter 2:11==). The altar: establish concrete places of remembrance and worship—take communion thoughtfully, keep the dates, keep the journal, keep the place you meet with God. Then ask the tracing question of yourself: if someone followed your steps through the last few years, would they find altars along the way?
