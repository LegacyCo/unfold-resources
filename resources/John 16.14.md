## Commentary
“He will glorify Me, for He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you.”

Jesus gives His disciples the surest way to recognize the Holy Spirit’s work: it glorifies Jesus. The Spirit of truth guides into all truth, speaks what He hears, and tells things to come (==John 16:13==)—but the aim of all of it is stated in this verse. He takes what belongs to Jesus and declares it to us. The Spirit’s ministry is not self-announcing; everything He does unveils the Son.

This gives the church a reliable test for anything claiming to be the Spirit’s work. A genuine move of the Spirit—in a meeting, a ministry, a prophetic word, or a private impression—will leave people more taken with Jesus. That is why the testimony of Jesus is called the spirit of prophecy (==Revelation 19:10==), and why teaching, worship, and spiritual gifts all bend toward the same end when the Spirit is actually behind them: Christ unveiled, Christ magnified, Christ loved. Where an experience produces fascination with the experience itself, or with the vessel who delivered it, something other than this verse is at work. The Spirit does not compete with the Son for attention; glorifying Him is His stated mission.

There is also a promise here for every believer who wants to know Jesus more deeply. “He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you”—the Spirit has access to everything that belongs to the Son, and His delight is to keep declaring it to us. The riches of Christ are unsearchable (==Ephesians 3:8==), but they are not inaccessible, because the One who searches the deep things of God lives in us (==1 Corinthians 2:10-12==). Growth in the knowledge of Christ is not a matter of intelligence or effort alone. It is the daily ministry of the Spirit, doing exactly what Jesus said He would do.

## Application
Use this verse in two directions. As a test: after a meeting, a prophetic word, a book, or a moment you believe was God—ask the simple question this verse supplies. Who was magnified? If the honest answer is Jesus, receive it with confidence. If the honest answer is the speaker, the experience, or you, hold it loosely. And as a practice: begin asking the Holy Spirit daily to do for you what Jesus said He would do—“take of what is Mine and declare it to you.” Ask it before you read the word, and expect Scripture to show you Christ. Ask it in prayer, and expect fresh sight of who He is. The Spirit’s favorite subject lives in you. Ask Him to talk about it.
