## Commentary
Jeroboam understood that worship would determine allegiance. If the people continued going to Jerusalem to sacrifice, their hearts might return to the house of David. So he built new altars, established new feasts, created new objects of worship, and appointed a new priesthood to support the kingdom he was trying to preserve.

Everything Jeroboam created resembled what God had established. He made a feast “like the feast that was in Judah.” He built places of worship. He consecrated priests. But something can look similar and originate from a completely different source. Jeroboam was not seeking a new expression of God’s will; he was constructing a religious system that would protect his own interests.

First Kings 13:33 says that “whoever wished” could be consecrated as a priest. At first glance, this can sound strangely close to the purpose of God. As we see in ==Isaiah 66:21==, the Lord intended to gather people from the nations and take priests and Levites from among them. The priesthood would not forever remain confined to the natural descendants of Aaron. And ultimately, In Christ, God would form a “royal priesthood” from every nation, tribe, people, and tongue (==1 Peter 2:9==).

But Jeroboam was not anticipating that promise. He was corrupting the priesthood God had already established because of his own motives. He attempted to extend the Levitical priesthood apart from the command of God, while God intended to establish another priesthood altogether—the priesthood of Melchizedek (see ==Hebrews 7:11-12==).

The difference is not just who was permitted to serve, but by whose authority they were brought near. Jeroboam consecrated whoever had ambition. The gospel does not declare that anyone may appoint himself a priest according to his own desire. God calls, cleanses, and brings near those who come to Him through Jesus Christ, the great High Priest “according to the order of Melchizedek” (==Psalm 110:4==; ==Hebrews 5:5–6==; ==Hebrews 6:20==).

Jeroboam created priests to sustain a false altar. Jesus creates priests through the sacrifice of Himself. Jeroboam’s priesthood was built around convenience, political security, and worship separated from the presence God had appointed. Christ’s priesthood is built upon an indestructible life and brings people from every nation into the true presence of God.

This is what makes counterfeit religion so dangerous. It often resembles something God intends to do, but it seeks to accomplish it through a means He has not authorized. Jeroboam could have argued that he was widening access, involving more people, and creating opportunities to serve. Yet God called it evil because the priesthood, the altar, the sacrifice, and the authority behind it had all originated in the heart of man.
