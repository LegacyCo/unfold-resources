## Commentary
Israel did not fail to get out of Egypt. They were completely delivered. Pharaoh was defeated and would never be a problem for Israel ever again. The Red Sea was opened. Their slavery was over. God had completely saved them from the power that once held them captive.

So their failure to enter rest had nothing to do with Pharaoh. It had nothing to do with Egypt. It had nothing to do with their past.

Their failure to enter rest had to do with what they believed *after* they were saved.

And I think we do the same thing.

Especially in much of our evangelical framework, we have a tendency to treat faith as something we need in order to be saved, while missing that there is also a faith *after* salvation. Not faith to believe we're forgiven and saved from our sin, but faith for now living the kingdom way of life.

There is a faith that saves, but there is also a faith that learns how to live from what salvation has accomplished.

And many people have been saved but have never really lived.

Their enemy has been defeated. Their old master has been overthrown. The power of sin has been broken. Their past in Adam has been dealt with in Christ. The Red Sea has closed behind them, and Egypt will never own them again.

But instead of letting salvation become the starting point for a life of rest and inheritance, many have made salvation the finish line.

Israel was meant to remember the Red Sea and say, “If God can do that, what can’t He do?”

That’s the logic of the gospel. Paul says it this way: “He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?” (Romans 8:32).

In other words, the cross is supposed to become the lens through which we see every other impossibility.

If God gave His Son, what is He withholding? If God conquered sin, death, and the devil, what power is there to intimidate us? If God brought us out of slavery by His own hand, why would we assume He brought us out just to leave us restless in the wilderness?

Salvation is not a waiting room for heaven.

Salvation is the beginning of inheritance.

Israel was fully delivered, but they failed to enter the promise. They were saved, but still not at rest. And that means their inheritance was not just deliverance from Pharaoh. Deliverance was the beginning of their entrance into inheritance.

Their inheritance was supposed to be a land of rest.

This is why it’s possible to be saved and still restless. It’s possible to have received salvation without yet understanding the measure of inheritance that salvation includes. You can be out of Egypt and still think like a slave. You can be forgiven and still live anxious. You can be free and still wander.

But the gospel was never meant to simply get you out of bondage. The gospel was meant to bring you into rest.

And Hebrews tells us that rest is still available.

“For we who have believed do enter that rest” (Hebrews 4:3).

Rest is not the reward for people who finally get their life together. Rest is the inheritance of those who believe. Not just believe that God saved them back then, but believe that what He accomplished in Christ is enough to teach them how to live now.
