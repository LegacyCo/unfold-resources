## Commentary
“Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for Him.” Two verses later comes the promise attached to that posture: “those who wait on the LORD, they shall inherit the earth” (==Psalm 37:9==).

There are two words on the license plate of our family car: *rest and wait*. People probably assume it’s a suggestion to drive slower. It’s not. It’s a reminder, bolted where we’ll see it every day, that the only way our family will step into what the Lord has promised is by resting and waiting *in* Him. Not by outworking the delay. Not by networking our way through the closed door. Psalm 37 is a declaration that the land doesn’t go to the best strategists or the sharpest decision-makers—it goes to the ones who rest in the Lord and wait patiently for Him.

Why does God delay the next door? Because He’s forming in you the character necessary to handle what’s behind it. I bought my boys pocket knives—a real gift, genuinely theirs—but the gift came with restrictions. Not because I wanted to spoil their fun, and not because I’m mean. The delay in full access came because they needed to learn how to handle the gift safely. Every gift comes with responsibility, and a good father matches access to maturity. We do this with our kids and call it good parenting—then God does it with us and we call it unanswered prayer. The delay between seasons is actually His goodness, keeping you from something you aren’t yet ready to carry.

So when the Lord hems you in between two closed doors and won’t give you what you want when you want it—worship. Seriously: worship right there in the hallway. Because His restraint is proof of relationship. “Whom the LORD loves He chastens… God deals with you as with sons” (==Hebrews 12:6-7==). He doesn’t train strangers. The delay that frustrates you is the discipline that confirms you’re His—and the ones who let patience finish its work come out of the hallway “perfect and complete, lacking nothing” (==James 1:4==).
