## Commentary
One of my favorite things about Psalm 7 is that David does not just ask God to judge his enemies. He *first* asks the Lord to judge him.

“O LORD my God, if I have done this…”

David is being accused, but he does not assume he's innocent. He invites God to search him and reveal truth. If there is iniquity in his hands, then he is willing for the consequences to fall on him (Psalm 7:3–5).

David wants vindication, but he does not want false vindication. He does not ask God to defend a lie. He brings the accusation before the Lord and essentially says, “If I am guilty, expose me. If I am innocent, defend me.”

It's a rare kind of integrity that can appeal for justice by beginning with self-examination.

## Application
The human heart often wants God to judge everyone else while leaving itself unexamined. But justice doesn't work that way. The person who truly wants justice must be willing for the light of God to shine everywhere, including within.

## Cross Reference
See also Psalm 139, where David prays, “Search me, O God, and know my heart… see if there is any wicked way in me” (Psalm 139:23–24). It also connects with Paul’s instruction to examine ourselves before the Lord (1 Corinthians 11:28; 2 Corinthians 13:5).
