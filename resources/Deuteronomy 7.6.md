## Commentary
When Yahweh calls Israel holy, He is not saying Israel is sinless. Far from it. They had grumbled in the wilderness. They built the golden calf. They rebelled against Moses. They doubted the promise and wanted to return to Egypt. Holiness here cannot mean moral perfection.

It means they were distinct. They were set apart. Chosen. Marked out by God as His own possession. Among all the nations of the earth, there was no other nation like Israel because there was no other nation God had joined Himself to in covenant like this.

And the Lord makes sure they understand the reason. “The LORD did not set His love on you nor choose you because you were more in number…for you were the least of all peoples; but because the LORD loves you” (Deuteronomy 7:7–8). Their holiness was not rooted in their greatness, strength, or superiority. It was rooted in God’s love and covenant faithfulness.

That means holiness begins with belonging before it ever becomes behavior. Israel was not holy because they had achieved something. They were holy because God had claimed them. Their obedience was meant to flow from that identity, not create it.

This is where many of us get holiness wrong. We think holiness means proving we are worthy to belong to God. But Scripture begins the other way around. God sets His love on a people, brings them out of bondage, calls them His own, and then teaches them how to live as the people they already are.

## Application
Don’t reduce holiness to behavior management. Holiness means you belong to God. You have been claimed, set apart, and made His own. Now you have the privilege of living like it.
