## Commentary
Ezekiel is being shown, in a prophetic vision, what is happening inside the temple in Jerusalem. The Spirit carries him “in visions of God” to the north entrance of the temple complex, where he sees “the image of jealousy, which provokes to jealousy.”

The exact identity of this image is not given. It was some kind of idolatrous statue or cult object that had been placed within the temple precincts, possibly an image associated with Asherah. Manasseh had earlier placed a carved image of Asherah inside the temple (==2 Kings 21:7==), which Josiah later removed (==2 Kings 23:6==), so some interpreters believe a similar image had been reintroduced.

It is called “the image of jealousy” not necessarily because that was the idol’s name, but because its presence provoked the jealousy of the Lord. Israel belonged to Yahweh by covenant, and the temple was the place of His dwelling. To place an idol there was therefore more than religious compromise; it was covenant unfaithfulness committed in God's own house.

This becomes the theme of the entire vision in ==Ezekiel 8==: the glory of God is still present, but His people have filled the place of His presence with rival worship. Ezekiel is being shown why judgment is coming—and ultimately why the glory of the Lord will depart from the temple.
