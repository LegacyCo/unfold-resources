## Commentary
James gives a beautiful and rich definition for the wisdom of God that is available to us. It is:
	- Pure
	- Peace-loving
	- Gentle
	- Willing to yield
	- Full of mercy
	- Full of good fruit
	- Impartial
	- Free of hypocrisy

Here's a brief word study on those descriptives for wisdom:
	- Pure: innocent, having no faults, clean
	- Peace-loving: characterized by promoting a state of wholeness, well-being, and happiness, a peacemaker
	- Gentle: kind, tolerant of slight deviations from moral or legal correctness, considerate
	- Willing to yield: open to adopting another position, belief, or course of action (with sufficient reason or evidence)
	- Full of mercy: leniency and compassion shown towards offenders by a person of authority (often penal/legal related)
	- Full of good fruit: the good consequence of some action or effort
	- Impartial: nonjudgmental, not tending to cause factions or divisions within a group
	- Free of hypocrisy: genuine, sincerely felt or expressed

It’s amazing to think the wisdom that God gives doesn’t come with all the right answers…it actually is marked by an openness to other ways of seeing and doing things without sacrificing truth. The “impartiality” of this higher wisdom prioritizes unity within the Body more than being right. I can be right in my argument and wrong in how it is delivered. If my "rightness" comes at the expense of unity with someone Jesus says I am to be one with (John 17:21), then I am not operating in God's wisdom.
