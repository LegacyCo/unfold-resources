## Commentary
“Then He said, ‘Hear now My words: If there is a prophet among you, I, the LORD, make Myself known to him in a vision; I speak to him in a dream.’”

Miriam and Aaron had asked, “Has He not spoken through us also?” God's answer starts by agreeing with them. Yes, He speaks to prophets. He makes Himself known in visions, and He speaks in dreams. That's how the word of the LORD came to Abram (==Genesis 15:1==), to Jacob at Bethel (==Genesis 28:12==), and later to Daniel (==Daniel 7:1==). God isn't taking anything away from what Miriam and Aaron had. Their gift was real.

But He's also setting up a comparison. Visions and dreams are real revelation, but they're indirect. They come intermittently, they come in pictures and parables, and they often need interpreting. Verse 8 will call them “dark sayings,” which means riddles. So when God says “Not so with My servant Moses” in the next verse, He's telling them there are different measures of revelation and different kinds of assignments, and that He's the One who decides who gets what.

That was the thing Aaron and Miriam missed. Having a real gift from God doesn't make every calling the same. Paul says the same about the church: “God has set the members, each one of them, in the body just as He pleased” (==1 Corinthians 12:18==), and we have “gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us” (==Romans 12:6==). Miriam's prophetic gift was never in question. Her mistake was treating it as grounds for a place of authority God had not given her.

## Application
Comparison is what turned a prophetess into a critic. If you find yourself measuring your gift or your role against someone else's, go back to what Jesus told Peter when he asked about John: “what is that to you? You follow Me” (==John 21:22==). Be faithful with the measure God has given you.
