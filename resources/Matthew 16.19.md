## Commentary
“I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven.”

Jesus speaks these words immediately after announcing, “I will build My church.” The keys of the kingdom are given within the context of the people He is building.

Keys represent entrusted authority. A key grants access, opens what has been closed, and carries the responsibility to steward what belongs to someone else. Peter was not being handed ownership of the kingdom. He was being entrusted with responsibility under the authority of its King.

And Peter would not exercise that responsibility alone. He would become part of the apostolic foundation upon which the household of God was built (==Ephesians 2:19-20==). In Acts, the kingdom advances through a praying, worshiping, discerning, Spirit-filled community—not through detached spiritual individuals protecting private platforms.

Western Christianity often treats spiritual authority as personal possession: my calling, my gift, my ministry, my revelation. But kingdom authority is safest and fullest within a submitted people. Gifts are distributed throughout the Body so that no member can say to another, “I have no need of you” (==1 Corinthians 12:21==).

You may receive a personal assignment, but you were never given a private kingdom.

The authority to bind and loose, proclaim the gospel, confront darkness, forgive, restore, and demonstrate the reign of Jesus belongs to a people living under the government of their King. The keys were not given to spiritual consumers. They were entrusted to servants who had taken their place within the house.
