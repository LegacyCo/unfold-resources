## Commentary
A few verses earlier, David was celebrating before God with all his might. Now he's standing at a threshing floor beside a dead man, first angry and then afraid. "David was afraid of God that day, saying, 'How can I bring the ark of God to me?'"

It's the right question. David just asked it too late. Before the procession, he had asked his captains and the assembly what seemed good to them (==1 Chronicles 13:1–4==). Only now, for the first time in the chapter, does he ask how anyone can bring the presence of a holy God near.

His first answer was to keep the ark at a distance. He left it at the house of Obed-Edom and went home without it (==1 Chronicles 13:13==). It's easy to understand why. But David's fear left him in the same place his carelessness had: without the presence of God. We often do the same thing. When something we did our own way goes badly, we feel the weight of it, and our instinct is to pull back like David did.

The word for the Lord's "outbreak" against Uzzah in verse 11 comes from the Hebrew *parats*, to break through. In the very next chapter, David defeats the Philistines and says, "God has broken through my enemies by my hand like a breakthrough of water," and he names the place Baal Perazim, which means "Master of Breakthroughs" (==1 Chronicles 14:11==). It's the same root. The God who broke out against Israel's carelessness was breaking through for David against his enemies.

But David didn't stay afraid. When he tried to move the ark again three months later, he started with what God had said, not what the people thought: "No one may carry the ark of God but the Levites, for the LORD has chosen them" (==1 Chronicles 15:2==). His fear had sent him back to God's word, and there it became the fear of the Lord, the kind that comes near the way God has asked to be approached. And when the ark finally came up to Jerusalem, it came with the same trumpets, cymbals and harps as the first time (==1 Chronicles 15:28==).

## Application
If you've been keeping God at a distance since something went wrong, don't stay there. Ask David's question, and let Scripture answer it. "Now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ" (==Ephesians 2:13==).
