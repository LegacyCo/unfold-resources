## Commentary
“God having provided something better for us, that they should not be made perfect apart from us.”

The heroes of Hebrews 11—Abel, Noah, Abraham, Moses, the ones who were mocked, sawn in two, and wandered in sheepskins—died in faith without receiving the promise (==Hebrews 11:39==). And this verse tells us why God arranged it that way: their story doesn’t reach completion without ours. They aren’t made perfect *apart from us*. God is not running thousands of individual projects across history; He’s building one thing, and every generation of the faithful is a section of the same wall. Their faith and yours are joined—one building, one completion, one day of unveiling.

In 1248, the city of Cologne laid the foundation stone for a cathedral—towers over five hundred feet, room for thousands. Every mason who worked on it knew he would never see it finished; neither would his children or grandchildren. Work eventually stopped, the money ran out, and for almost four hundred years the cathedral sat half-built. Then in the 1800s the original plans were rediscovered, and a new generation faced a choice: build with modern methods, or build according to the six-hundred-year-old design? They chose the original design—and in 1880, 632 years after the foundation stone, the cathedral was finished, matching the blueprints perfectly. Think of one mason somewhere in the middle of those centuries, spending his entire working life on one section of one wall. Was his life wasted because he never saw the towers? Ask it this way instead: could the towers exist without his wall? The building was not made perfect apart from him.

That’s your relationship to the saints of Hebrews 11—and to the generations that will follow you. You are standing on courses of stone that prophets and martyrs laid, and someone will one day build on yours (==1 Corinthians 3:10==). The canvas wasn’t blank when you arrived; the foundation stone went down long ago, and unless the Lord returns, the work continues after you. So be faithful to your portion of the wall. When the whole house is finally revealed, every generation of builders will be perfected together—them not apart from us, and us not apart from them.
