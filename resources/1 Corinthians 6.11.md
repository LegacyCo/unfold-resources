## Commentary
“And such were some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God.”

Every verb in that sentence is past tense. Paul has just finished listing the unrighteous—the sexually immoral, idolaters, thieves, drunkards, extortioners (==1 Corinthians 6:9-10==)—and then he turns to the church and says: that’s what some of you *were*. Not “that’s what you’re gradually ceasing to be.” Were. You were washed. You were sanctified. You were justified. Completed actions, spoken over people who—read the letter—were still making plenty of messes. Their behavior needed serious correction; but their *identity* was settled.

This is where we have to let Scripture reshape our theology of sanctification. We’ve been taught that sanctification is a lifelong process—that holiness is the finish line of a long climb. But watch the tenses. Maturity is absolutely a process. Healing from years of trauma or a former life of sin can be a process. Identity is not. When Jesus commissioned Paul, He described people “who are sanctified *by faith* in Me” (==Acts 26:18==)—not by decades of effort. “By that will we *have been* sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all” (==Hebrews 10:10==). Same instrument that justified you, same moment, same completeness. You don’t grow into holy any more than you grow into justified. You grow in your understanding of it, and your living catches up to it—but the thing itself was a gift, given whole.

I've had someone say to me, almost afraid to believe it: “I’ve prayed every day for most of my life for Jesus to make me righteous. Are you telling me that I am?” Yes. That’s exactly what this verse is telling you. Stop praying to become what God says you already are—and start living like the washed, sanctified, justified person He declares you to be.
