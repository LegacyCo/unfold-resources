## Commentary
The detail that the cart was *new* is interesting. David wasn't treating the ark carelessly. A new cart would have seemed like a fitting way to transport something so sacred. Yet the problem wasn't the condition of the cart. It was that God had never instructed Israel to transport the ark on a cart in the first place.

When God gave Moses instructions for constructing the ark, He required that it be fitted with poles so it could be carried (==Exodus 25:12–15==). The responsibility for transporting it belonged specifically to the Kohathites, a family within the tribe of Levi. Unlike the other Levites, who were given carts to transport their portions of the tabernacle, the Kohathites received no carts because the holy things entrusted to them were to be carried on their shoulders (==Numbers 7:6–9==).

So where did David get the idea of using a cart?

This was exactly how the Philistines had transported the ark when they returned it to Israel years earlier. After suffering under God's judgment, the Philistines placed the ark on a new cart and sent it back toward Israel (==1 Samuel 6:7–12==). They were acting according to their limited understanding of how to handle something sacred. But Israel had been given explicit instructions from God. They knew better.

Whether David was intentionally following the Philistines' example or simply using what seemed like a reasonable method of transportation, the result was the same. He was attempting to handle something holy according to human wisdom rather than divine instruction.

## Application
Perhaps the most revealing aspect of this account is that David wasn't doing something obviously irreverent. He was doing something that probably appeared appropriate, even honorable, to everyone watching. And that's what makes this kind of disobedience particularly dangerous. It can be difficult to recognize when our own understanding of what honors God has replaced what He has actually instructed us to do.
