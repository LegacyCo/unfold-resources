## Commentary
Jeremiah is not questioning God’s power in itself, but expressing the tension of a moment in which God’s saving action appears absent. The One known as the Mighty One of Israel is like a warrior who does not act.

This tension finds an unexpected parallel in the Gospels. In Mark 6:5-6, Jesus returns to Nazareth, and the text records that “He could do no mighty work there… and He marveled because of their unbelief.” Here again, the Mighty One stands present, yet His works are restrained. The limitation is not intrinsic to His power, but relational in its outworking. The unbelief of Nazareth creates a context in which the fullness of His works is not manifested.

The connection between these passages is not that God becomes powerless, but that His saving action is, at times, withheld or unexpressed in response to human condition. 

Thus, the imagery of Jeremiah—God appearing “like a man astonished”—finds a kind of embodiment in Nazareth. The astonishment, however, is not confusion or incapacity, but the response to a people whose disposition does not receive what God is willing to give.

In both cases, the text captures a tension: God remains the Mighty One who saves, yet His saving work is not always realized where it might otherwise be expected.
