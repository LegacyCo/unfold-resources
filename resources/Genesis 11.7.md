## Commentary
“Come, let Us go down and there confuse their language, that they may not understand one another's speech.”

The judgment on Babel was a judgment on language. One speech had made a rebellious unity possible—“the people are one and they all have one language” (==Genesis 11:6==)—so God confused the speech, and the confusion produced the scattering (==Genesis 11:8==). Division of language and dispersion of peoples enter the world together, as a consequence of a city built apart from God.

Scripture promises the reversal. Through Zephaniah God says, “I will restore to the peoples a pure language, that they all may call on the name of the LORD, to serve Him with one accord” (==Zephaniah 3:9==). That is what happens in Acts 2. God pours out His Spirit, and the first thing recorded is a unification of language—Parthians, Medes, Elamites, and visitors from every nation hearing the wonderful works of God in their own tongues (==Acts 2:6-11==). The secondary consequence of that unified language of the Spirit was a drawing together of the scattered: different tribes and tongues gathered into one church, “continuing daily with one accord” (==Acts 2:46==). The scattering of Genesis 11 begins to be undone at Pentecost, and it is still being undone as God draws people to Himself in Christ. Outside of Him there is only scattering, division, and confusion. In Him there is unity, peace, and family—until the gathering is complete and “a great multitude… of all nations, tribes, peoples, and tongues” stands before the throne with one voice (==Revelation 7:9-10==).

## Word Study
**Confuse** is the Hebrew *balal* (בָּלַל)—to mix, mingle, confound. The city's name is a play on the word: “its name is called Babel, because there the LORD confused (*balal*) the language of all the earth” (==Genesis 11:9==). Elsewhere the verb describes flour “mixed” with oil for the offerings (==Leviticus 2:4==). Summary: what man built to unify himself against God, God mixed into confusion; what God builds by His Spirit, He unifies.
