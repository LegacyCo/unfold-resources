## Commentary
Have you ever been in a season where the grace for it seems gone? The same relationships, rhythms, and responsibilities that used to be life-giving are suddenly draining. Nothing is obviously wrong—but the ease has lifted. I believe that discomfort at the tail end of a season is often God’s design. He allows a holy dissatisfaction to settle over the place He no longer intends you to stay, so He can draw you forward.

Numbers 10 gives us the picture. When it was time for Israel to break camp, the priests led the way carrying the ark—the very place of God’s presence: “The ark of the covenant of the LORD went before them for the three days’ journey, to search out a resting place for them.” Imagine the moment: the cloud of glory that had hovered over the camp—the visible assurance that God is with His people—suddenly lifts and starts moving toward the horizon, while two million people watch in silence. Before Israel could move, God went ahead. And in that moment, the grace, so to speak, lifted from where they were standing.

That’s what it feels like when God closes out a season. It can feel like He’s abandoned you. He hasn’t. He’s gone *ahead* of you—searching out your next resting place. But it’s here, in the tension of the three-day journey, that many believers lose their way. Confusion hardens into doubt; doubt sours into bitterness. The dreams of many well-intentioned saints lie buried in this stretch of wilderness, simply because they couldn’t survive the feeling of grace lifting and going on ahead.

Scripture holds one more three-day journey: the space between the cross and the empty tomb. Imagine the disciples in it—the Man they had staked everything on, gone. Talk about grace lifting. On the surface, everything had died. Underneath, everything was being made new. The grave wasn’t the end of the story; it was the passage to the resting place God was preparing (==John 14:2-3==).

So when the comfort drains out of a season, don’t assume you’ve done something wrong. You’re being weaned off what once fed you—because the place that’s grown comfortable is too small for who you’re becoming. The grace lifting isn’t His absence. It’s His goodness, pulling you forward.
