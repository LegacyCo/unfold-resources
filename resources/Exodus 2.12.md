## Commentary
“So he looked this way and that way, and when he saw no one, he killed the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.”

Was Israel oppressed and in need of a deliverer? Absolutely. Was Moses the man God intended to use? He was—Stephen says Moses “supposed that his brethren would have understood that God would deliver them by his hand” (==Acts 7:25==). The need was real. The calling was real. But what Moses did at forty originated in *Moses*. He had a burden; what he didn’t have was a commissioning. And a real burden acting without a divine sending produced a dead Egyptian in the sand and a forty-year exile in Midian.

Now put this verse next to Exodus 3. Same man, same people, same need—but listen to the difference in the grammar: “I have seen the oppression of My people… I have heard their cry… I know their sorrows… Come now, therefore, and *I will send you*” (==Exodus 3:7-10==). At forty, Moses *went*. At eighty, Moses was *sent*. One originated in human zeal; the other in divine commissioning. It took forty years of being emptied of his good ideas and ambition for Moses to learn the difference—and the difference was everything. 

Here’s the lesson for everyone who wants to build something for God: the spirit of this age will always present opportunities to build, and the brokenness of this age will always present needs to meet. But just because something *needs* to be done, or *can* be done, does not mean God is sending you to do it. The question that separates activity from assignment is this: does what I’m building for God originate in God? So apostolic people learn a strange discipline—they become suspicious of anything that originates in themselves. Not because initiative or creativity is bad; we’re made in the image of the Creator. But the world doesn’t need our originality nearly as much as it needs our obedience. If it’s not sent of God, it will not go in the power of God.
