## Commentary
“And it happened, as he sowed, that some seed fell by the wayside; and the birds of the air came and devoured it.”

Jesus tells us what this soil represents: “these are the ones by the wayside where the word is sown. When they hear, Satan comes immediately and takes away the word that was sown in their hearts” (==Mark 4:15==). The seed never had a chance to germinate—not because anything was wrong with the seed, but because the ground couldn’t receive it. The word bounced, sat exposed on the surface, and was gone.

This hard ground was a high-traffic area. It wasn’t made of different material than the good soil a few feet away; it had the same compositional makeup. The difference was footsteps. Years of coming and going, of constant traffic, had compacted ordinary soil into something close to concrete. That’s what makes this soil the most sobering of the four: it wasn’t born hard. It was *trafficked* hard.

The same thing happens to a heart. When the activity and interests of the world have free passage through your interior life—the busyness, the noise, the endless coming and going of things that each seem harmless—your heart gets walked into hardness. Not by rebellion. By traffic. The life of the word is not sustainable in an environment of hurry, distraction, and misplaced priority; it lands on Sunday, sits on the surface, and is snatched away before Monday ends (==Luke 10:41-42==). The good news is hard ground is not a permanent condition. Soil compacted by traffic can be broken up—“Break up your fallow ground, for it is time to seek the LORD” (==Hosea 10:12==). Close the thoroughfare. Take back the ground the world has been walking across. The soil underneath is the same soil that produces thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold (==Mark 4:20==)—it just needs the traffic stopped and the ground turned over.
