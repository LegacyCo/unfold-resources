## Commentary
“It is the glory of God to conceal a matter, but the glory of kings is to search out a matter.”

God conceals—and the concealing is His glory, not His stinginess. He does not hide the deep things of Himself because He enjoys games. He is not a God who hides anything in the dark—whatever is hidden by God is hidden in the light. He hides His mysteries to protect us from finding them casually. Revelation without intimacy would be dangerous, so He arranges things such that He “is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him” (==Hebrews 11:6==)—the diligence is the safeguard. The secret place is not called secret because it's hidden; it's called secret because so few find it. And so few find it because so few are willing to seek Him in the way He desires to be sought (==Psalm 91:1==; ==Matthew 6:6==).

He reveals Himself to those who are intimate with Him—and intimacy without covenant is prostitution. Only covenant lovers have permission to pray, “Show me Your glory” (==Exodus 33:18==). You will know you are in covenant with Him when His presence means more to you than His promise. Moses is the standard: offered the land of promise with an angel to lead the way, he answered, “If Your Presence does not go with us, do not bring us up from here” (==Exodus 33:15==)—a man who would rather have God without the promise than the promise without God. That's the kind of man who has permission to ask for glory. “The secret of the LORD is with those who fear Him, and He will show them His covenant” (==Psalm 25:14==). The concealing is real, but it is aimed at producing seekers—and for those in covenant with Him through the blood of His Son, the search has unlimited clearance. The glory of kings is to search it out, and He has made us kings and priests to our God (==Revelation 1:6==).
