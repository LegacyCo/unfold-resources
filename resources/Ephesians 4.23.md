## Commentary
Paul describes the believer’s transformation in three moves: “put off… the old man which grows corrupt according to the deceitful lusts, and *be renewed in the spirit of your mind*, and put on the new man which was created according to God, in true righteousness and holiness” (==Ephesians 4:22-24==).

Put off. Be renewed. Put on. Most of us try to skip the middle step. We take off the old behavior and immediately try to put on the new one—quit the sin on Monday, start the discipline on Tuesday—and we wonder why the old man keeps showing back up by Friday. Here’s why: you can’t put the new self on over an un-renewed mind. Notice what corrupts the old man in verse 22—“the *deceitful* lusts.” The old life runs on lies: lies about who God is, lies about who you are, lies about where life is found. If the lies stay, the old wardrobe comes back, because behavior always grows back from what we believe.

That middle step—“renewed in the spirit of your mind”—is repentance. Not a moment of feeling sorry, but the ongoing renovation of how you think, until your thoughts about God and yourself agree with what He says (==Romans 12:2==). This is why the gospel never settles for behavior modification. Changing what you do without changing what you believe just dresses the old man in better clothes.

And don’t miss what’s hanging on the rack. The new man is “created according to God, in true righteousness and holiness.” The new self isn’t something you achieve—it’s something already created, tailored in God’s own likeness, waiting to be worn. So work the sequence in the order Paul gave it: take off the old, let the Holy Spirit change your mind about God and about yourself, and then put on who you already are.

## Word Study
Two words carry the middle step:

- *be renewed* : **ananeoō** (Greek) — to be made new again, to be renewed in quality; this is the only place the word appears in the New Testament. The verb is passive and continuous: not “renew yourself, once,” but “keep on being renewed.” You don’t perform the renovation of your mind—you submit to it, and it never fully stops.
- *repentance* : **metanoia** (Greek) — from *meta* (change) and *nous* (mind): a change of mind. Open any lexicon and the first definition listed is “change of mind.” Repentance is first a changing of the way you think at such a core level that it then changes the direction you’re moving. That’s why Paul’s command here—“be renewed in the spirit of your mind”—is really a call to metanoia as a way of life, not a one-time event at an altar.

Where metanoia shows its meaning:

- ==Matthew 3:2== — John preaches metanoia to Pharisees who were already blameless by the law: their behavior was right, but their minds about God were wrong.
- ==Romans 2:4== — it’s the *kindness* of God that leads to metanoia. The mind changes when it finally sees Him as He is.
- ==Acts 3:19== — metanoia leads to sins wiped away and “times of refreshing.” A changed mind is a doorway, not a punishment.

Summary: the middle step of the wardrobe change is a mind under permanent renovation—continually re-thinking God, yourself, and the world until your thoughts agree with His.
