## Commentary
“Now the LORD said to Abram: ‘Get out of your country, from your family and from your father's house, to a land that I will show you.’”

Look at how the destination is worded: not “to the land of Canaan,” but “to a land that I will show you.” God names what Abram must leave in detail—country, relatives, father's house—and leaves the destination unnamed. The showing would come on the way. Hebrews tells the story the same way: Abraham “went out, not knowing where he was going” (==Hebrews 11:8==). It would be nice to have the directions before pulling out of the driveway; but that is not the arrangement. “We walk by faith, not by sight” (==2 Corinthians 5:7==). The call gives you the Person, not the map—and this is precisely why the journey of Abraham is the journey of everyone who responds to the call of the gospel.

The gospel gives us the same invitation. Come out of the land you are living in; leave the family you were born into—the lineage of Adam, which passed on to you a fallen nature and a mind that does not know God as He is—and go to a new land, where God gives you a new family of promise (==1 Corinthians 15:45-49==). Come out so you can enter in. Lay your life down at the cross, and let God determine what is resurrected and given back to you and what stays buried (==Matthew 16:25==). Leave Adam's house, and let Jesus, the last Adam, lead as you go. Where He takes His people and how He takes them there are His decisions, not theirs. What matters is not the route but who's with you: Abram did not know where he was going, but he knew he was not going alone—and that was all he needed to keep going.
