## Commentary
"Unless the LORD builds the house, they labor in vain who build it..."

Notice the psalm doesn't say the builders are lazy. It says they *labor*. Hard, sincere, sweat-of-the-brow labor—and all of it in vain, because the Lord isn't the one building. Effort is not the issue. Authorship is.

Here's the question every pastor, worship leader, and church planter in America would answer the same way: "Are you building God's house?" *Yes, brother, that's exactly what we're doing.* But here's a harder version of the question: Are we building *His* house according to *His* design—or are we building *our* house according to *our* design, and then asking Him to endorse it with His presence?

This was Martha's problem. She assumed that Jesus being in her house was an endorsement of all her hard work (==Luke 10:40-41==). It wasn't. He was there for a different reason than she thought, and He gently told her so. We can do the same thing with the church: build what we like, fill it with activity, and treat any sign of His presence as proof that He approves of the whole enterprise.

But God will only *dwell* in the house He builds Himself. He may visit ours. He may even bless it for a season. But dwelling—the settled, glory-filling, throne-establishing kind of presence—belongs to the house built according to the pattern shown on the mountain (==Exodus 25:40==). And His pattern is so contrary to our natural way of building that we can't guess at it. It has to be revealed. He builds with people; we build with programs. He builds family; we build organizations. He builds slowly, out of broken living stones; we build fast, out of whatever's efficient.

So the psalm is not telling you to stop working. It's telling you to make sure the labor is His—that the plans came down from the mountain and not up from the flesh.
