## Commentary
“Do not worry about how or what you should speak. For it will be given to you in that hour what you should speak; for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you.”

That little word “in” can also be translated “by”—as in the way a musician speaks *by* his instrument. The Spirit of your Father speaks by you. You’re not the musician under pressure to perform; you’re the instrument in the hands of One who has never missed a note.

This is a word for the quiet ones. If you’re a natural evangelist, you’ve probably never lost sleep over what to say. But many believers—especially the meek, the gentle, the ones leading quiet lives—stay silent about Jesus because they’re convinced that if the moment ever came, they wouldn’t have the words. Here’s what I’ve watched happen instead: when people who’ve been faithfully living the gospel finally believe that the witness of their life counts, words follow. The life comes first, and the life creates the moments. A coworker who has smelled the fragrance of Christ on you for two years will ask you a question they couldn't trust with a street preacher—“always be ready to give a defense to everyone who asks you a reason for the hope that is in you, with meekness and fear” (==1 Peter 3:15==). Notice: Peter assumes they’ll *ask*.

And when they do, you’re not on your own. Jesus promises that in that hour it will be *given* to you. The same Spirit who transformed you knows exactly how to talk about it (==Luke 12:11-12==). Your job isn’t to have a polished presentation ready. Your job is to be a yielded instrument—and to trust that the Father would never create the moment and then abandon you in it.

You do have a story to share. And if you’ll let Him, the Holy Spirit will speak through you—by you—right on time.
