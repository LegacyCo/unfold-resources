## Commentary
“How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever? How long will You hide Your face from me?”

David asks “How long?” four times in the first two verses. Two of the four are about God: will You forget me, and will You hide Your face. One is about himself: how long will he wrestle with his own thoughts and carry sorrow every day (==Psalm 13:2==). One is about his enemy: how long will that man have the upper hand. Extended seasons of difficulty does this to a person. It changes how God appears to them, how reality feels inside your own head, and how your situation looks, all at once.

The question “How long?” tells us David's problem isn't new. He has been in it for a while, and he can't see the end. Scripture doesn't say which period of his life this was. It would fit the years he spent running from Saul, when he had been anointed king and was living in caves. But the psalm leaves it open.

“Forget” and “hide Your face” describe how it felt to David. They don't describe what God had done. God says through Isaiah, “Can a woman forget her nursing child…? Surely they may forget, yet I will not forget you” (==Isaiah 49:15==). God's face shining on someone is the center of the priestly blessing: “The LORD make His face shine upon you” (==Numbers 6:25==). So when David says God is hiding His face, he's saying it feels as though the blessing has been taken away. He doesn't know why, and he admits that.

And David says all of these things *to* God. Every one of these questions is addressed to “O LORD.” A man who truly believed God had forgotten him wouldn't bother to pray. David's complaint is an act of faith, because he's bringing it to the only One who can do anything about it. This kind of prayer is called lament, and roughly a third of the Psalms are laments. God put these words in Scripture so His people would have something honest to pray when they can't find the strength for praise.

Others in Scripture pray similar laments. Habakkuk prayed, “O LORD, how long shall I cry, and You will not hear?” (==Habakkuk 1:2==). The martyrs under the altar in heaven cry, “How long, O Lord, holy and true?” (==Revelation 6:10==). And Jesus, on the cross, prayed the first line of another of David's laments: “My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?” (==Psalm 22:1==; ==Matthew 27:46==).

## Application
If you're in a long season of waiting, you don't have to clean up your prayers before you bring them to God. Tell Him how long it has been and what it feels like. Use David's words if you can't find your own. The real danger in a long trial is going quiet. You stop talking to God and only talk to yourself, which is what David describes in verse 2: “How long shall I take counsel in my soul?” Keep praying, even when the only prayer you have is “How long?”
