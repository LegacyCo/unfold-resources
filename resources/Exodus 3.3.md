## Commentary
Moses did not find God in a palace, a temple, or an impressive display of power. He found Him in an ordinary bush on the back side of the desert.

Moses could have dismissed the bush, kept walking, or treated it as another strange detail in an ordinary day. Instead, he said, “I will now turn aside and see.” It was when Moses turned aside that God called his name.

There are revelations of God that can only be received when we are willing to find Him in unlikely places. We often look for Him in the dramatic and the impressive, while He waits to meet us in the ordinary circumstances of life—in its failures, frustrations, delays, and vexations.

An apostolic person is not merely someone who has witnessed great things, but someone who has learned to recognize the greatness of God in ordinary places. Before Moses could be sent, he first had to see God in the wilderness. Before he could stand before Pharaoh, he had to stop before a bush.

God is often nearer than we realize, but we are moving too quickly, looking too far away, or expecting Him to appear in a form we consider more worthy of Him. Sometimes the most spiritual thing we can do is turn aside and look around.
