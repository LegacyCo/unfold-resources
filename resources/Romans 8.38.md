## Commentary
“For I am convinced that neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities nor powers, nor things present nor things to come, nor height nor depth, nor any other created thing, shall be able to separate us from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Most of us carry experiences that seem to argue against this verse—either things that were done to us (“How could a God who loves me allow that?”) or things we’ve done (“How could God love me after that?”). Here’s a distinction that can set you free: those barriers are *real*, but they are not *true*. If someone tells you a gas station is giving away free gas, the words are real, the trip you make is a real experience, and the anger you feel when you find out it was a lie is a real emotion—but none of that ever made the claim true. In the same way, what happened to you in the name of love is real, and the feelings that swirl around those memories are real. But your experiences do not have the power to redefine God’s love for you. Neither does sin—yours or anyone else’s.

Look at when God’s love for you started. Jesus says the Father loves you with the very same love He has for the Son—and that this love was in motion “before the foundation of the world” (==John 17:23-24==). That means He loved you before you had a chance to mess anything up. He loved you knowing everything you were going to do, before you had a chance to repent of any of it. He didn’t start loving you when you prayed the sinner’s prayer. Repentance doesn’t cause God to love you—repentance is you finally coming into agreement with what God has been saying about you all along: *I love you.*

Now run Paul’s logic: if God loved me before I sinned, knowing I would sin—if He “did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all” (==Romans 8:32==)—then what exactly is left that could separate me from that love? Paul looks through every candidate—tribulation, persecution, famine, danger, sword, death, demons, the future itself—and finds nothing with the authority to do it (==Romans 8:35==). This is what kingdom people get settled in their hearts, not as a doctrine they can recite but as a verdict they’ve stopped appealing: *I am loved, unconditionally and undeservedly, and nothing in creation can change that.* Everything else in the kingdom grows out of that soil.
