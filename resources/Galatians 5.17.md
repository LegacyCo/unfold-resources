## Commentary
“For the flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh; and these are contrary to one another, so that you do not do the things that you wish.”

If you’ve ever wondered why the Christian life feels like a fight, here’s the answer: because it is one. Two ages are present on the earth at the same time—the glorious age to come, which broke in when Jesus rose and the Spirit was poured out, and the present evil age, which hasn’t left yet (==Galatians 1:4==). They’re clashing all around us. And Paul says the same collision runs right through the middle of every believer: the flesh wants what is contrary to the Spirit, and the Spirit what is contrary to the flesh. You live in a war zone, and the front line is internal.

Understanding this changes how you interpret the struggle. Many believers feel the conflict and conclude something has gone wrong with them—“if I were really saved, would I still want the wrong things?” But Paul says the conflict is what living between the ages *feels like*. Notice: unbelievers don’t experience this war, because there’s only one side present in them. The tug you feel is evidence that the Spirit of the age to come actually lives in you and refuses to sign a peace treaty with your flesh. The struggle isn’t proof you’re failing. It’s proof you’re occupied territory—claimed by the new age while the old one contests the ground.

And the way to fight is not gritted teeth. Paul has already given the strategy one verse earlier: “Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh” (==Galatians 5:16==). Not “fight the flesh and you’ll find the Spirit”—walk in the Spirit and the flesh loses its supply lines. Feed the side of the war you want to win (==Romans 8:5-6==). The old age is passing away; the new one is here to stay (==1 John 2:17==). Live like a citizen of the age that wins.
