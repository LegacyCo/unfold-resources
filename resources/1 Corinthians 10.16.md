## Commentary
This was an opportunity for Paul to clearly state that the bread and the cup were symbolic representations of what happened on the cross. This was Paul's moment to soften the blow of Jesus' words in John 6 ("Most assuredly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you" - John 6:53). But Paul doesn't do that. Paul doubles down on what Jesus said.

Paul uses the Greek word koinonia here. Koinonia means fellowship with, participation in, a sharing in: "Is the cup of blessing which we bless not a sharing (koinonia) in the blood of Christ? Is the bread which we break not a sharing (koinonia) in the body of Christ?"

This cup, which is a blessing, is a blessing unto life when it is a participation in the blood of Christ. And this bread that sustains us, sustains us because it is the living bread of God that came down from heaven to give life to the world (John 6:33).

Matthew Henry, commenting on this passage, writes this, "In short, the Lord’s supper is a feast on the sacrificed body and blood of our Lord. And to eat of the feast is to partake of the sacrifice (1 Corinthians 10:18).

Joseph Exell, also commenting on this passage, writes this, "How often in Scripture is the natural consecrated to be the medium of the supernatural! The material rock gushing with streams in the desert was a vehicle of a spiritual rock, even Christ in effect."
