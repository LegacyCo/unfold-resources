## Commentary
“Then, having fasted and prayed, and laid hands on them, *they* sent them away.”

The Holy Spirit said, “Separate to *Me* Barnabas and Saul” (==Acts 13:2==)—and then the church laid hands on them and *they* sent them out. So who sent Paul? God did—through a church family that heard the same Spirit, agreed, and said with their hands and their blessing, “We send you.” Divine calling and community commissioning weren’t competing claims. They added up to one properly sent man.

Paul had an encounter that no conference speaker will ever top—a head-on collision with the risen Lord that left him blind on the Damascus road. Today, an encounter like that gets you a book deal and a speaking tour, because we assume the encounter itself qualifies a man for ministry. But watch what Scripture actually records. Paul still needed Ananias to lay hands on him and heal his eyes (==Acts 9:17==). He needed Barnabas to vouch for him when the Jerusalem church wouldn’t touch him (==Acts 9:26-27==). He needed years in the community at Antioch, teaching and being known (==Acts 11:25-26==), before the day the Spirit spoke and the family confirmed it. Yes, Paul received his gospel by revelation and not from man (==Galatians 1:11-12==)—but there is nothing in Scripture suggesting he attained maturity of *character* in one encounter. Jesus knocked Paul off his horse. It took the Jesus People to clothe him in the character of the Messiah.

Gifted, called, commissioned by God, *and* joined to a local church family—that’s the arithmetic that produced the apostle Paul. Remove the last term and the sum changes. 

## Cross References
The pattern of community confirming calling: ==Acts 9:17== (Ananias), ==Acts 9:26-27== (Barnabas vouches in Jerusalem), ==Acts 11:25-26== (a year of being formed at Antioch), ==1 Timothy 4:14== (the gift given “with the laying on of the hands of the eldership”), ==Proverbs 27:2== (“let another man praise you, and not your own mouth”).
