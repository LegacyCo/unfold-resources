## Commentary
From the kingdom’s perspective, wise counsel is rarely found in the voice of one person alone. God often brings clarity through a chorus of trusted voices. No single counselor sees everything. One may recognize the timing, another may see the danger, another may discern the motive, and another may identify what no one else has considered.

This is why Scripture speaks of a *multitude* of counselors. The goal is not to collect opinions until someone agrees with what we already want to do. It's to place our plans in the presence of wisdom and allow them to be examined, challenged, strengthened, and sometimes corrected.

Plans made in isolation are vulnerable because we all have blind spots. Desire can disguise itself as direction. Fear can sound like discernment. Ambition can convince us that urgency is the same thing as obedience. Wise counsel creates safety by exposing what we can't see on our own.

The multitude doesn't replace the voice of God, but it often helps us hear Him more clearly. Mature counselors do not control our decisions; they help establish them. Their agreement can confirm a direction, while their caution may protect us from stepping into something prematurely.

In the kingdom, independence is not maturity. Wisdom is willing to listen. God often establishes our plans by surrounding us with people who love us enough to affirm what is right, question what is unclear, and confront what is dangerous.

A plan that cannot survive wise counsel may not be ready to be walked out.
