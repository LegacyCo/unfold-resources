## Commentary
“Do not presume that I came to abolish the Law or the Prophets.” Jesus’ announcement was not, “Good news—all that difficult material in the Old Testament is now irrelevant.” The law is holy and good (==Romans 7:12==); the problem was never the law but what the law reveals in us. And that is precisely why Jesus does not remove it: the law still has a job to do. Its work of exposing our unrighteousness is what reveals our need for a Savior—the greatest gift the law can give anyone (==Galatians 3:24==)—and Jesus will not take that gift away. So He does not discard the law. He fulfills it.

The Greek word translated “fulfill” is *pleroo*—to fill full, to bring to a conclusion, to give the complete meaning of. Jesus is claiming that the entire story of the Old Testament finds its intended conclusion in Him. Every promise God gave to Israel, every sacrifice, every feast, every prophecy longing for the day when God would rescue His people—all of it points to the moment the Christ would come (==Luke 24:27==).

This means fulfillment is far more than a perfect record, as if Jesus merely never broke a commandment and so earned the authority to release us from it. He became the sacrifice every other sacrifice anticipated, the Priest no other priest could be, the King every throne pointed toward, and the faithful Son that Israel was called to be. He did not simply keep the law; He brought the story the law was telling to its destination. Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes (==Romans 10:4==).

Not the smallest letter or stroke passes away until all is accomplished—because the story is not being canceled. It is being completed.

## Application
Let this reshape how you read the Old Testament. It is not the obsolete half of your Bible; it is the first act of a story whose conclusion you know. When you read the law, the sacrifices, the feasts, and the prophets, ask of each one: where does this point to Christ? The Scriptures are not a code to master but a testimony to Him (==John 5:39==).

And let it reshape how you measure Jesus. He is not one more teacher offering commentary on the law. He is what the law was about. If every promise, priest, sacrifice, and king finds its completion in Him, then nothing in your life—no obedience, no discipline, no religious practice—is meant to stand on its own either. It all finds its meaning in Him, or it loses its meaning entirely.
