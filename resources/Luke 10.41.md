## Commentary
Corrie ten Boom once said that if the devil can’t make you sin, he’ll make you busy. There’s truth in that. Sin and busyness may look very different, but they can produce the same result: a distracted soul that has lost its awareness of God.

Martha wasn’t doing anything sinful. She was serving Jesus. But Luke tells us she had become “distracted with all her preparations” (Luke 10:40). Her activity *for* Jesus had begun to pull her attention away *from* Jesus. That’s the danger of busyness—it can fill your hands with good things while slowly emptying your soul of what is necessary.

Jesus didn’t rebuke Martha for serving. He addressed the worry, agitation, and inner fragmentation her busyness had produced: “You are worried and bothered about so many things.” She was physically near Jesus, yet inwardly disconnected from His presence. Meanwhile, Mary had chosen to sit at His feet and listen.

Busyness becomes spiritually dangerous when it keeps us from being present—with God, with other people, and even with what is happening inside our own hearts. We can move so quickly that we never notice what we’re becoming. We can become so consumed with responsibilities that prayer feels like an interruption, people feel like inconveniences, and silence becomes uncomfortable.

The answer isn’t inactivity. It’s learning to choose the “one thing necessary” before the many things demanding our attention. The healthiest activity flows from communion. Before we work for Jesus, we must learn to sit with Him.

## Application
Pay attention to what your busyness is costing you. Make space today to sit with Jesus. Don’t let a full schedule become an empty soul.
