## Commentary
“For You, Lord, are good, and ready to forgive, and abundant in mercy to all who call upon You.”

God does not become merciful when you call upon Him. He is already abundant in mercy. He does not become willing to forgive after you have demonstrated enough sorrow. He is ready before you call.

Prayer moves us toward the mercy already present in the heart of God.

We often approach forgiveness as though heaven’s door must be forced open. We search for the correct words, try to feel sufficiently terrible, and make promises about what we will never do again. Beneath all of it is the suspicion that God would prefer to keep us outside unless we can convince Him otherwise.

The psalmist destroys that picture with one word: _ready_.

This is the God Jesus revealed when a paralyzed man was lowered through the roof. Before the man confessed anything, Jesus saw faith moving toward Him and said, “Your sins are forgiven you” (==Luke 5:20==). The man did not catch Jesus on a good day. He encountered the God who is always ready to forgive.

Of course, this doesn't make repentance unnecessary. Calling upon Him is itself a turning—a recognition that life cannot continue apart from Him. But before that internal turning can be organized into flawless language, God sees the heart moving toward Him.

He is better at recognizing repentance than we are at explaining it.

## Application
Stop rehearsing the speech you think will persuade God to receive you.

Call upon Him. Bring the mistake, the confusion, the incomplete understanding, and the words you can't put together. His forgiveness is not waiting for your performance to make it available.

He was ready before you called.
