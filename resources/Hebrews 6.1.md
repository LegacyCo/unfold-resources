## Commentary
I once talked with a guy who loved hiking. Let’s call him Joe. Joe had all the gear—the newest boots, the pack, the premium trail-app subscription, the sticks. But the more he talked, the more something strange surfaced: every story Joe told was about the same place. The trailhead. He knew the parking lot. He knew the bathroom cleaning schedule. He never once mentioned a summit or the view from the top.

So I finally asked him how far he usually hikes. He kind of laughed and said, “Honestly… I don’t go very far. I just like being there at the trailhead.”

That’s not hiking. And the writer of Hebrews says a version of the same thing to believers: “Leaving the discussion of the elementary things of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works.” You can hear his frustration a few verses earlier: “By this time you ought to be teachers, but you still need milk and not solid food” (==Hebrews 5:12==).

Notice what repentance is called here: a *foundation*. A foundation is essential—nothing stands without it. But nobody builds by laying the same foundation over and over. A foundation is laid so that something can be built on it.

Yet plenty of us live at exactly that spot. Constant introspection, but no transformation. Always aware of sin, but never walking in victory over it. A life defined by what we’re turning *from* instead of Who we’re walking *with*. That’s not humility, and it’s not maturity. It’s camping at the trailhead.

Now, let’s be clear: the problem is never repentance itself. In many respects, Jesus people *are* repentant people—quick to admit wrong, quick to turn, tender toward the conviction of the Holy Spirit all their lives. You never outgrow repentance any more than a hiker outgrows the trailhead; every trail you’ll ever walk starts there, and you’ll pass it again every time you head out. The problem isn’t visiting the trailhead. It’s building a house on it.

Repentance is essential—it gets you out of your life in Adam and onto the path of Christ. But it prepares the way; it isn’t the way. The way is following Jesus—forgetting what lies behind, pressing on toward the goal (==Philippians 3:13-14==).

“Let us go on.”
