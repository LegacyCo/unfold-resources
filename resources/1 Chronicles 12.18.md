## Commentary
“Then the Spirit came upon Amasai, chief of the captains, and he said: ‘We are yours, O David; we are on your side, O son of Jesse! Peace, peace to you, and peace to your helpers! For your God helps you.’ So David received them, and made them captains of the troop.”

David has just asked these men a direct question: are you here to help me or to betray me (==1 Chronicles 12:17==)? The answer comes from Amasai, a military officer, and the text says the Spirit came upon him to give it. That's interesting, because Amasai isn't a prophet. What comes out of his mouth is a pledge of allegiance. The Spirit of God moved a soldier to declare whose side he was on.

“We are yours” is the language of belonging. And “son of Jesse” is significant. That's what Saul called David when he wanted to put him down (==1 Samuel 20:27==; ==1 Samuel 22:7-8==). Nabal did the same thing: “Who is David, and who is the son of Jesse?” (==1 Samuel 25:10==). Amasai takes the name people used as an insult and pledges his loyalty to it. Years later, when Sheba led a revolt against David, his rallying cry was Amasai's words turned inside out: “We have no share in David, nor do we have inheritance in the son of Jesse” (==2 Samuel 20:1==). Amasai also gives his reason: “for your God helps you.” He could see where God's favor was resting, and he lined himself up with it.

The Holy Spirit does the same thing in us when it comes to Jesus. Paul says, “No one can say that Jesus is Lord except by the Holy Spirit” (==1 Corinthians 12:3==). When Peter confessed, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,” Jesus told him, “flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but My Father who is in heaven” (==Matthew 16:16-17==). So a wholehearted “I am Yours” isn't something we work up on our own. The Spirit of God produces that declaration in us.

## Word Study
**Came upon** is the Hebrew *labash* (לָבַשׁ)—to put on a garment, to clothe. The sentence literally reads, “the Spirit clothed Amasai.” That verb is only used of the Spirit two other times in the Old Testament. The Spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon before he blew the trumpet and gathered Israel (==Judges 6:34==), and the Spirit of God clothed Zechariah the son of Jehoiada before he confronted the people over their sin (==2 Chronicles 24:20==). Jesus used the same picture after His resurrection: “tarry in the city of Jerusalem until you are endued”—clothed—“with power from on high” (==Luke 24:49==). Summary: when the Spirit clothes someone, He enables them to say and do what they wouldn't have the courage or insight to do on their own.
