## Commentary
“(Now the man Moses was very humble, more than all men who were on the face of the earth.)”

This sentence is in parentheses for a reason. It's explaining what Moses does next, which is nothing. He has just been attacked by his own brother and sister, and he doesn't say a word in his own defense. The LORD hears it (==Numbers 12:2==), the LORD calls the meeting (==Numbers 12:4==), and the LORD does all the talking (==Numbers 12:6-8==). Moses' humility is why he stays quiet.

He wasn't always like this. Forty years earlier, Moses saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, looked this way and that, and killed him (==Exodus 2:12==). Stephen says he “supposed that his brethren would have understood that God would deliver them by his hand” (==Acts 7:25==). That's a man who was sure of his calling and ready to enforce it himself. Then came forty years of keeping sheep on the backside of the desert. By the time we get to Numbers 12, the same man can be slandered by his own family and feel no need to set the record straight.

That isn't weakness. This is the same Moses who walked into Pharaoh's court with demands to let God's people go, and who broke the tablets when Israel worshiped the calf (==Exodus 32:19==). He would burn for God's honor. He just wouldn't fight for his own. A chapter earlier, when Joshua wanted to protect Moses' position, Moses said, “Are you zealous for my sake? Oh, that all the LORD's people were prophets” (==Numbers 11:29==).

So who wrote this verse? Some think a later hand, such as Joshua, added the comment. Others point out that Moses recorded his own failures just as plainly (==Numbers 20:12==), so he could have recorded this the same way under the Spirit's direction. Either way, it's God's honest assessment of him.

## Word Study
**Humble** is the Hebrew *'anav* (עָנָו)—lowly, meek, bowed down. It often describes people who've been brought low by affliction and have learned to depend on God. “The humble He guides in justice, and the humble He teaches His way” (==Psalm 25:9==). “The meek shall inherit the earth” (==Psalm 37:11==). The Greek Old Testament translates it with *praus*, the word Jesus uses in “Blessed are the meek” (==Matthew 5:5==) and the word He uses for Himself: “I am gentle and lowly in heart” (==Matthew 11:29==). Summary: *'anav* describes strength that has been brought under God's hand and no longer needs to defend itself.

## Application
When someone criticizes you unfairly, the first instinct is to defend yourself, correct the criticism, and make sure everyone hears your side. Try Moses' way. Say less than you want to. Take it to God before you take it to anyone else, and give Him room to speak for you. “Humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God, that He may exalt you in due time” (==1 Peter 5:6==).
