## Commentary
"And this stone which I have set as a pillar shall be God's house..."

It's a strange thing to say about a rock. Jacob had used this stone as a pillow, dreamed of a ladder with angels ascending and descending, and woke up saying, "Surely the LORD is in this place, and I did not know it... this is none other than the house of God" (==Genesis 28:16-17==). Then he stands the stone up, pours oil on it, and declares that *the stone* will be God's house. What does a stone have to do with the house of the Lord?

Hold that question and follow the stone through Scripture. Centuries later, when Peter confesses "You are the Christ," Jesus answers, "On this rock I will build My church" (==Matthew 16:16-18==). And Peter—that Peter—later picks up Jacob's language almost word for word: "You also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house" (==1 Peter 2:5==). Peter had understood the mystery Jacob only saw the shadow of: God's house is not built *out of* stones; it's built out of *people who have become* stones—living ones—fitted together on the Chief Cornerstone that the builders rejected (==1 Peter 2:4-7==).

Stephen said the same thing before he was killed, "The Most High does not dwell in temples made with hands" (==Acts 7:48==)—God dwells in houses made of human beings. And the men who loved their temple picked up cold, dead stones and killed a living one with them (==Acts 7:58==). We love our structures. Anyone who suggests that God is after a people rather than a building had better be ready to catch stones.

But that is what Jacob's pillar was pointing at all along. A stone, anointed with oil, standing where heaven touched earth, called the house of God. Jacob saw a ladder. Jesus said the ladder was Him (==John 1:51==). And the house at the bottom of that ladder—the place where God actually dwells—is not a shrine at Bethel. It's you, and the people God has set you among, being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit (==Ephesians 2:22==).

## Word Study
- *pillar* : **matstsebah** (Hebrew) — a standing stone, a memorial set upright to mark a place where something happened between God and man. Jacob raises another one at Bethel when he returns (==Genesis 35:14==), and Israel later stands twelve of them at Gilgal (==Joshua 4:20==). A matstsebah says: *God met us here.*

Summary: Jacob's stone was a witness to an encounter. Peter's living stones *are* the encounter—God no longer marking a place, but inhabiting a people.
