## Commentary
The disciples’ question in this verse is the point of the story: “What kind of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey Him?” (==Matthew 8:27==).

Matthew has spent the entire chapter answering that question from multiple angles.

Jesus cleanses the leper (==Matthew 8:2–3==). He heals with nothing more than His word (==Matthew 8:8, 13==). He bears sickness in fulfillment of Isaiah’s Servant prophecy (==Matthew 8:16–17==). He commands demons and they leave (==Matthew 8:16==). And now He speaks to the wind and the sea—and creation obeys Him.

For Matthew’s Jewish readers, this should sound familiar.

The Psalms repeatedly describe authority over the sea as something belonging uniquely to God: “You who still the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the peoples” (==Psalm 65:7==).

The connection is remarkable. What Psalm 65:7 says God does, Matthew shows Jesus doing.

Jesus does not pray for God to calm the storm. He does not appeal to a higher authority. Matthew says He “rebuked the winds and the sea, and there was a great calm” (==Matthew 8:26==).

The sea simply obeys Him.

This imagery reaches even further through Israel’s Scriptures. The sea often represents forces beyond human control, and God’s authority over it is repeatedly presented as evidence of His unrivaled sovereignty. God “rules the raging of the sea” and stills its waves (==Psalm 89:9==). When sailors cry to the Lord during a storm, “He calms the storm, so that its waves are still” (==Psalm 107:28–29==).

Now the disciples cry, “Lord, save us!” (==Matthew 8:25==), and Jesus stands and does exactly what the Psalms say the Lord does.

Matthew is continuing to reveal who Jesus is by showing us what Jesus does with the human condition. He restores.

This has been happening throughout the chapter. ==Isaiah 53:4== anticipated the Servant bearing sickness, and Jesus heals the sick (==Matthew 8:17==). ==Psalm 107:20== says God “sent His word and healed them,” and Jesus heals the centurion’s servant simply by speaking (==Matthew 8:8, 13==). ==Psalm 65:7== says God stills the roaring seas, and Jesus commands the storm into silence.

Matthew is showing us that _the hopes, promises, and descriptions of God’s saving activity throughout Israel’s Scriptures are finding their fulfillment in Jesus._

That is why the disciples’ question is so significant: “What kind of man is this?” Matthew lets the Scriptures answer that question.

_Who stills the sea?_

God does.

And Jesus just did.
