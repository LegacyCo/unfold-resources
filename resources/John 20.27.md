## Commentary
The risen Jesus still bears His wounds. “Reach your finger here, and look at My hands; and reach your hand here, and put it into My side.” Resurrection did not erase the marks of crucifixion. It transformed them—from evidence of what darkness did into testimony of what God has done. Even in the midst of the throne, He is the Lamb standing as though slain (==Revelation 5:6==).

In February 1945, Allied bombing devastated Dresden, and the Frauenkirche—one of the city’s great landmarks—collapsed into a heap of ruin that sat in the city center for nearly fifty years. When restoration finally began after German reunification, the builders did not throw the old stones away. They sorted the rubble carefully and set as many original stones as possible back into the restored church. The old stones were charred and disfigured, so when the work was complete, the marks of war were plainly visible in the walls. Dresden did not erase its scars. It incorporated them—and they became a testimony of healing.

That's the picture of kingdom restoration. God does not restore us by pretending the past never happened. He restores by taking what was broken and placing it within a testimony greater than the pain. Wounds become scars, and scars become testimonies. Like the scars on Jesus, they still tell a story—but no longer only a story of brokenness. They tell the story of what God does with broken things, as the restored are built like living stones into the house He is building (==1 Peter 2:5==).

## Application
Resist the urge to ask God to make your history go away; that's not how He restores. Offer Him the rubble instead—all of it, including the stones you are most ashamed of—and let Him decide where they fit in the restoration project of your life. 

Then let your scars serve someone else. A wound is private pain; a scar is a healed story that can be shown, the way Jesus showed His to Thomas. The comfort you have received is meant to become comfort you give (==2 Corinthians 1:4==). Somewhere near you is a person still sitting in their rubble, certain it can never be rebuilt—and your scars may be the proof they need that it can.
