## Commentary
“And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar, and they dwelt there.”

The location of this passage is important. The plain in the land of Shinar would become Babylon (==Daniel 1:2==)—the city that captured God's people in the sixth century BC and burned His temple (==2 Kings 25:9==), and the city that stands in Revelation for the wickedness of the whole world system, “Babylon the great, the mother of harlots” (==Revelation 17:5==). Genesis 11 is the first mention of a place that will run through the story of Israel and into the final clash between light and darkness at the end of the age. What is built on this plain is not just a tower; it is the foundation of everything that will later carry Babylon's name.

The verse also records a movement and a decision: they journeyed, they found a plain, and “they dwelt there.” God had told Noah's family to be fruitful, multiply, and fill the earth (==Genesis 9:1==). These people found a good place and stopped. Settling in Shinar was the first step away from the covenant directive, and the city of the next verses was the second. Scripture will use this geography again and again: Babylon is where God's people are carried when they abandon Him (==Jeremiah 25:11==), and it is what His people are called out of before the end (==Revelation 18:4==). The story of that city begins here, with a group of people who chose a plain over a promise.
