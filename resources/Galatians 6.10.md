## Commentary
In the early days of the church there were no hospitals and there were no hotels. We actually get both of those words from the word hospitality. Medicine was rare and travel was very dangerous. The early church picked up on the need and came in to fill a void in society. This was their primary form of evangelism. What we now know of as hospital and hotel started around the tables in the homes of followers of Jesus practicing hospitality.

Hospitality reaches across the social boundary lines that divide us. Biblical hospitality testifies of the new social order of the Kingdom. This was the primary way the gospel spread at such a rapid pace: from a few hundred people eating together in an upper room in Jerusalem to over half the population of the Roman empire in just three centuries, toppling paganism itself. It was all done with no political power, no internet, no sound systems, no printing press, no church buildings, no celebrity pastors, no legal protection, under waves of persecution...the gospel spread from one table to the next.

Jesus and the early church picked up on a high-value practice of the day, hospitality, and instead of using it as a way to parlay favor with the rich and move up the ladder, Jesus and His followers used hospitality to move down the ladder. Hospitality was downward spirituality. It was a way to serve the poor. His hospitality was aimed down, not up, and it changed the world.
