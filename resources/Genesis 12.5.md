## Commentary
This is from "Apostolic Foundations" by Art Katz: Abraham obeyed the call of God to come out of Ur of the Chaldees only after his father died. There was a delay in the city of Haran (modern-day southeastern Turkey), where he did not obey fully to the call of God, which was to get out of nation, kindred and father's house, and to follow the Lord into the land that was to be shown him. He collected much substance and many souls in Haran, but it was not the place of blessedness. 

How many of us are in the equivalent of Haran today? How many have misconstrued increase of substance and goods as if it were a sign of blessedness? More likely, we are in a place of delay, still attached to flesh, still attached to kindred and father's house, and we have not come out to begin the priestly walk where true blessedness begins. 

## Application
Haran can look successful. It can look stable. It can even look fruitful. Outwardly, it may have seemed that life was moving forward for Abraham. But God had not called him to Haran. Haran was supposed to be just another city to pass through on the way. Haran was a place of partial obedience, and partial obedience always delays the purpose of God.

We can mistake comfort, growth, provision, and visible results for the blessing of God, when in fact they may only be signs that we have learned how to settle in a place God never intended us to be. Haran lets us keep what God told us to leave behind. Haran postpones the costly break with old identities and habits.

Don't measure your life only by what is increasing. Measure it by whether you are obedient to the call of God. A season may be productive and still be a delay. You may be gaining substance while losing time. You may be building something impressive somewhere God never intended you to be.

True blessing is in the soil of obedience. It begins when we stop clinging to what is familiar and yield to what God has spoken.
