## Commentary
Righteousness is the state of one who is as He ought to be. To be righteous means to be in alignment with how you are originally designed by God. It means you are living as God intended when He said, "Let us make man in our image, according to our likeness" (Genesis 1:26). Being the righteousness of God is God looking at you and saying "you remind of Me." 

We can't grow in righteousness. Righteous is something you either are or are not. And if you are in Christ, then His righteousness has become your righteousness. You can grow in your understanding of righteousness...and you should. But you can't become more of something you already are; because righteousness is an imputation, a transfer, not a process. Righteousness is transferred to us on the basis of faith. For example, Paul writes in Romans 4:3, "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness." Faith was the gate that opened up the transfer of righteousness from God to Abraham.

------

Regarding sanctification: long ago I heard the term "unfolding revelation" to describe sanctification. I can't remember where I first heard it, but I like that language. Rather than sanctification being a process, which lends itself to works and effort, revelation is the language of understanding who we are in Christ and what He has accomplished.

We have to know that we *are* sanctified before we can actually *live* sanctified. If not, no amount of yielding to a process will ever bring us to the "destination" of sanctified. Sanctification (and holiness) is a pre-ethical term, meaning the identity comes before the actual evidence is even seen; and it's that identity which then produces Christ-like behavior. Most of us have that backwards ("I have to act like it to earn the title"). If we believe that sanctified is the starting line of our walk with Jesus, not the finish line, then we actually live out the identity that God speaks over us...sanctified by the blood of the Lamb. Christ is our sanctification.
