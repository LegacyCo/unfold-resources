## Commentary
"And walk in love, as Christ also has loved us and given Himself for us..."

Ephesians opens with vertical language: Paul prays that "the Father of glory may give to you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of Him" (==Ephesians 1:17==). It closes with horizontal language: *walk in love*. Everything in the letter moves from the one to the other—from revelation of who God is to a way of living with each other that shows what He's like.

Here's a pattern I've seen over and over in revival history. Revivals *begin* when God's people get a revelation of the majesty of Ephesians 1, 2, and 3—chosen before the foundation of the world, made alive, raised and seated, built together into a dwelling place of God, filled with all His fullness. Hearts catch fire on that. And revivals *end* when God's people ignore the necessity of Ephesians 4, 5, and 6—bearing with one another, putting away lying and anger, speaking words that build up, being kind and tenderhearted, forgiving as we've been forgiven, husbands and wives and parents and children and workers loving each other well (==Ephesians 4:2==, ==4:25-32==, ==5:22-6:9==). The ongoing, daily, unglamorous rhythms of life-on-life fellowship. That is what *establishes* the kingdom in a community: the people of God demonstrating what their King is like by how they love one another (==John 13:35==).

So the two halves need each other. We can't do Ephesians 4-6 without Ephesians 1-3—we can't build family, not the kind God intends, without a mighty outpouring of the Spirit. That's why we need revival. But revival that never becomes Ephesians 4-6 is a fire with nothing to burn in; it flares and goes out. Revival gives us the *grace* to build God's house His way. Walking in love is what building it actually looks like.

Notice the standard Paul sets for the walk: "as Christ also has loved us and given Himself for us." Not love as sentiment—love as self-giving. That's the only kind that can hold a family together long enough for God to move in and stay.

## Application
Read Ephesians 4:25-32 slowly and treat it as a diagnostic, not a devotional. Where in your closest relationships is there a lie you've let stand, an anger you've slept on, a word that tore down instead of built up, a grudge you've called "boundaries"? Pick the one that costs you most and deal with it this week—confess, forgive, repair. That's not a distraction from revival. That's the wineskin.
