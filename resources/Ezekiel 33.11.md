## Commentary
“Say to them: ‘As I live,’ says the Lord GOD, ‘I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live. Turn, turn from your evil ways! For why should you die, O house of Israel?’”

God swears an oath about His own heart. “As I live” is the strongest oath God can take—He swears by Himself (==Hebrews 6:13==)—and what He stakes His own life on here is this: He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked. None. What He delights in is the turning. Judgment is real in Ezekiel; this verse doesn’t soften that. But it settles forever the question of what God *wants*. When the wicked die in their sin, heaven does not celebrate. Something God desired did not happen.

Listen to the pleading that follows the oath: “Turn, turn from your evil ways! For why should you die?” This is not the voice of a judge eager to sentence. It is the voice of a Father calling to children who are walking toward a cliff He never built for them. The question “why should you die?” has no good answer—that’s the point. There is no reason. The way of life is open, the invitation is doubled (“turn, turn”), and every death that follows a refusal is a death God pleaded against. Peter carries this same heart into the new covenant: the Lord is “not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance” (==2 Peter 3:9==), and Paul writes that God “desires all men to be saved” (==1 Timothy 2:4==).

## Word Study
- *pleasure* : **chaphets** — to delight in, to take joy in, to bend toward something with desire. The word describes what a person’s heart leans toward, not merely what they permit.

Where else the word reveals God’s heart:

- ==Isaiah 62:4== — “you shall be called Hephzibah… for the LORD *delights* (chaphets) in you.” The name literally means “My delight is in her.” What God does delight in is His people, restored.
- ==Micah 7:18== — “He does not retain His anger forever, because He *delights* (chaphets) in mercy.” Mercy is what His heart bends toward.

Summary: God has chaphets—but not in death. His delight is in mercy, in turning, in a people He can name Hephzibah. When you picture God’s posture toward the lost—including the lost person you’ve nearly given up on—this verse is the sworn testimony of His heart.
