## Commentary
In 1885, seven young men from Cambridge University left England to join Hudson Taylor’s China Inland Mission. These were not desperate men with no options. Several came from wealthy or aristocratic families. C.T. Studd was a famous cricket player. Stanley Smith was captain of the Cambridge rowing team. These were men with careers, reputation, comfort, and national influence ahead of them.

But then Jesus called.

And they responded.

Their departure became a national event in Britain. They toured universities before leaving, calling other students to surrender their lives to Christ and the mission of God. Their obedience stirred missionary zeal across England. C.T. Studd gave the rest of his life to taking the gospel around the world, pioneering gospel work in China, India, and the Congo. He and the others knew that the kingdom demanded everything. 

So they gave everything.

In this passage, Jesus begins to call people into His kingdom. But He is not just calling them _away_ from their current life — fishing. He is calling them _into_ a new way of being human.

Most of us in the West may not understand the cost of saying "yes" to the invitation to follow Jesus because we grew up in a Judeo-Christian culture. But make no mistake, just because we may not have grown up Muslim or Buddhist does not mean we have an advantage in understanding the kingdom of God. 

In some ways, we are at a _disadvantage_. 

We deceive ourselves into thinking that following Jesus is not all that different from how we’ve always lived. “Just make a few adjustments here and there, add church every so often, put some money in the plate...” 

We've been conditioned in the West to think being a follower of Jesus is something good to add to our life. That’s a luxury mindset that is completely foreign in other parts of the world. 

They don’t think that way in China. 

They don’t think that way in Iran. 

They don’t think that way in Nigeria. 

Responding to the invitation to follow Jesus in North Korea is a death sentence. 

The blessing of living in a free nation has another side to it: we often don’t understand that following Jesus means a complete end to the only life we’ve ever known. Before the gospel is ever "come to Jesus and live," it first is "come to Jesus and die."

For example: “If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow Me” (Luke 9:23-24).

“I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me…” (Galatians 2:20)

“He died for all, that those who live should live no longer for themselves, but for Him who died for them and rose again” (2 Corinthians 5:15).

“You are not your own; for you were bought at a price” (1 Corinthians 6:19).

## Application
The kingdom of God demands everything. Not because God is mean or greedy, but because He knows how He designed us. Until we surrender the inferior things we think are ultimate, we’ll be weighed down trying to live a life we were never designed for.

Being born again implies a death to everything you brought with you. And then the King determines what walks out of the tomb re-born with resurrection life and what stays dead and buried. Relationships, habits, desires, plans, where you live, what you do, how you spend your money — it all belongs to the King. And because He’s wonderful, _He’s_ the One you really want in charge of those things. When God controls the details of your life, your life is in order.
