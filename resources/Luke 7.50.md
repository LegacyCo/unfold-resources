## Commentary
“Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.”

The woman entered the house carrying an alabaster flask and a reputation. Everyone in the room knew what kind of woman she had been. Simon the Pharisee saw her tears, her worship, and her extravagant affection for Jesus—but all he could see was her category: “She is a sinner” (==Luke 7:39==).

Jesus saw something else. He saw faith.

The woman does not present a theological explanation of salvation. She does not recite a formula, defend her presence, or convince the room that she has changed. She simply moves toward Jesus with the full weight of her life exposed. Her tears, kisses, and costly perfume reveal that something has already happened within her.

Repentance begins internally before it ever takes on words. It begins when a person sees God differently and, because they see Him differently, they can no longer remain where they were. The woman entered the room believing this Rabbi was nothing like the religious men who had already decided what she deserved.

Her love did not purchase forgiveness. Her love revealed that she had encountered it. Jesus says that the one who is forgiven much loves much (==Luke 7:47==). The fragrance filling the room was the evidence of a heart that had already begun to understand mercy.

Simon had the correct furniture, the respectable reputation, and Jesus sitting at his table. But the woman was the one who truly received Him. Religion had proximity without surrender. She had faith without a formula.

## Application
It is possible to know the language of salvation while remaining suspicious of anyone who receives it differently than you did.

Do not turn your testimony into a formula that everyone else must follow. The same Jesus who met you through your particular path knows how to recognize faith in someone whose story looks nothing like yours.
