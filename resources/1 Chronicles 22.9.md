## Commentary
The Lord tells David that his son will build His house. David had desired to build a dwelling place for God, but the Lord told him that the work would be entrusted to another: “He shall be a man of rest” (1 Chronicles 22:9).

This is the defining characteristic of the son who is given permission to build God’s house. Solomon would be a man of rest, and even his name testified to the peace God would give His people. Solomon means “peaceful.” His reign would be marked by rest from surrounding enemies, and from that place of peace he would build the temple.

David was a man of war. He fought the battles, subdued the enemies, and prepared the materials. Solomon built from what had already been accomplished. He inherited the victory of another and constructed the house of God from a place of rest.

This points us forward to Christ. Jesus is the true Son of David and the greater Solomon. He is the Prince of Peace, and through His death and resurrection He defeated the enemies we could never overcome. He established peace with God and brought us into His finished work.

But in the new covenant, the house of God is no longer a temple made with human hands. We are now the house of God. The church is “being fitted together” and growing “into a holy temple in the Lord,” a dwelling place for God in the Spirit (==Ephesians 2:21-22==). Peter says that we are “living stones” being built into a spiritual house (==1 Peter 2:5==). God no longer dwells primarily in a building. He dwells in His people.

That makes the pattern even more significant. The house of God is still built from rest. Christ, the true Man of rest, is building His church from the victory He has already secured. And those who participate in building His house must learn to live from that same rest.

This doesn’t mean passivity or inactivity. Solomon still had to plan, gather workers, oversee construction, and finish the work. But his activity flowed from a settled place. He wasn’t fighting to obtain the kingdom. He was building within a kingdom that had already been secured.

The same is true for us. We don’t labor to become the house of God. In Christ, we already are His dwelling place. We don’t serve to earn His presence. We serve because His presence already lives within us. Ministry that flows from unrest will eventually become striving, but ministry that flows from union with Christ carries the peace of the One who is building His house through us.
