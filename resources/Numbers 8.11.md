## Commentary
This is a strange image because the Levites themselves are described as a “wave offering.”

A wave offering was normally a portion of a sacrifice presented before the Lord and then designated for priestly use (see ==Leviticus 7:30–34==). Here there is no animal or grain being presented. _The Levites are the offering._

Obviously Aaron did not physically wave thousands of Levites back and forth. The language describes a ceremonial presentation before God. Israel first lays hands on the Levites (==Numbers 8:10==), and then Aaron presents them to the Lord as Israel’s offering so that “they may perform the work of the LORD” (==Numbers 8:11==).

Before the Levites can be given to the work, they must first be given to God.

This is why God says a few verses later, “They are wholly given to Me from among the children of Israel” (==Numbers 8:16==). Their primary calling was not to a task but to the Lord. The work they performed flowed out of the fact that they already belonged to Him.

Scripture never treats ministry primarily as work we accomplish _for_ God. It begins with people who have first presented themselves _to_ God.

Paul picks up remarkably similar language when he urges believers to “present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God” (==Romans 12:1==). Under the old covenant, the Levites were presented before the Lord as an offering. Under the new covenant, every believer is invited to live this way—not offering God portions of our lives, but placing our whole selves at His disposal. Our time belongs to Him. Our gifts belong to Him. Our ambitions belong to Him. Our work belongs to Him because _we belong to Him._
