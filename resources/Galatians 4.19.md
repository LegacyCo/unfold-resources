## Word Study
The word Paul uses here for "formed" is morphoō (the verb of morphē). Morphoō means to form or shape. It speaks to the outward appearance that has been conformed to another, but also the interior character.

Morphoō is not merely focused on the external appearance that has changed. Rather, the change is a transformation of the essential character. There is a place in the born again reality where there is neither man nor woman nor even forms, but a perpetual, ineffable light. The change of appearance is the expression (the evidence) of the internal change that has taken place.

In Philippians 3:21, Paul uses a similar word again, "who will transform our lowly body that it may be conformed (symmorphos) to His glorious body, according to the working by which He is able even to subdue all things to Himself." The image Paul is painting for us is not that of clothing, in the sense of covering the external form, but that of the deeper, interior transformation of the lowly body into a completely different one which is a glorified body. Symmorphos doesn't mean becoming like, similar or equal. It signifies an existence in Christ whereby His life permeates ours without dissolving our own distinction. 

And in Romans 8:29, "For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed (symmorphos) to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brethren." Again symmorphos means that we shall not only be similar to or like Christ, but that we will come into the same realm of power as He. We will be identified with the same substance as He, and enter into the same essential nature as Christ (see 2 Peter 1:4 - "partakers of the divine nature). Within this relationship Christ, retains separate identity (“the firstborn among many brethren”)(see also 2 Corinthians 3:18).
