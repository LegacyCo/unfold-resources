## Commentary
“For the law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.” We love that verse—but notice it says grace *and* truth. Jesus didn’t come demonstrating one without the other, and you can’t receive Him without receiving both.

Grace is God loving the world so much that He sent His Son (==John 3:16==). Truth is the Son saying that anyone who doesn’t take up his cross and follow Him cannot be His disciple (==Luke 14:27==). Grace is the Holy Spirit opening your eyes to see the beauty of the gospel in the face of Jesus (==2 Corinthians 4:6==). Truth is Jesus telling you to repent and follow Him (==Mark 1:15==). Grace is Jesus feeding the five thousand. Truth is Jesus telling the same crowd that unless they eat His flesh and drink His blood, they have no life in them (==John 6:53==).

Grace is never the barrier to someone following Jesus. Truth is. Grace attracts the masses; truth sifts them. Truth is the filter every professing believer eventually passes through—and it’s where the numbers start falling off. Follow the trajectory of Jesus’ ministry and watch it move from mass acceptance and curiosity to rejection and betrayal. Why? Because it was easy to follow the Man multiplying bread and healing every sickness—that was grace poured out. But when that same Man began speaking truth that penetrated hearts, truth exposed what was actually inside the crowds (==John 6:66==). Truth forced a decision: am I willing to follow this Man, or not? He offended their minds to reveal their hearts.

The majority in Jesus’ day saw the miracles, heard the stories, even professed to believe—and ultimately decided this Man could not be trusted with their lives. Anyone who wants His works but not His word can never receive His life. So when the word of Jesus cuts across your preferences, your politics, your grudges, your comforts—“love your enemies,” “forgive the one who wounded you”—don’t treat the offense as a reason to leave. Treat it as the filter doing its work, and pass through it. On the other side of the sifting is the Jesus the crowds never got to know.
