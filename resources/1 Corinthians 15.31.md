## Commentary
“I affirm, by the boasting in you which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord, I die daily.”

There are two kinds of death in Scripture: a death that leads to death, and a death that leads to life. Jesus died the death that leads to death and defeated it, so that everyone who believes in Him could die the other death—the death of the flesh that brings the spirit alive, the death that ends in resurrection. Most believers know the devil wants to kill them. Fewer have understood that Jesus wants them to die too—“if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live” (==Romans 8:13==). The devil is not the only one seeking a death in your life; he is simply seeking it for the wrong reason.

Paul knew there was an old man in him that had to be put to death so that Christ could rule through the new man. The believer has both: an old man that bears your image, inherited from Adam, and a new man re-created in the image of Christ. As long as the old man is alive and has a voice, the new man cannot reign. So Scripture commands, “put off… the old man… and be renewed in the spirit of your mind, and… put on the new man which was created according to God, in true righteousness and holiness” (==Ephesians 4:22-24==). The old man is removed by death—“I have been crucified with Christ” (==Galatians 2:20==); “you died with Christ” (==Colossians 2:20==); “we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that… we also should walk in newness of life” (==Romans 6:4==). What is true positionally is worked out daily. For Paul it was a daily execution of the old self; it is no less for us.

## Application
Treat the death of the old man as a daily practice rather than a one-time event. Each morning, identify the specific place the old self is asking for a voice today—the resentment you want to nurse, the appetite you want to indulge, the reputation you want to defend—and by the Spirit put that thing to death before it acts (==Romans 8:13==). Then deliberately put on the new man in its place: respond as Christ would in that exact situation. The believer who dies daily is the one in whom Christ lives daily.
