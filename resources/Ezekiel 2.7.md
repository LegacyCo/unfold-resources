## Commentary
Ezekiel is commissioned to speak to a people God already described as rebellious. Their response to what Ezekiel tells them is uncertain, but his responsibility is not.

“Speak My words to them.”

God doesn't tell Ezekiel to speak because the message will be received. He doesn't tell him to continue speaking if there is visible fruit. Ezekiel's obedience is not dependent on the people. Ezekiel is responsible to speak what God gives him to speak. What happens next belongs to God.

One of the temptations in any form of ministry is to let the people's response become the measure of success. If people listen, we assume we are succeeding. If they resist, we assume something has failed. But that's not how God measures success. For Ezekiel, success was determined by whether he faithfully spoke the word of the Lord or not.

Ezekiel could not let the hardness of his audience become an excuse for disobedience. God tells him plainly: “Whether they hear or whether they refuse.” Ezekiel was not called to produce repentance. He was called to bear witness. 

## Application
You and I may not be prophets in the same office as Ezekiel, but we are still called to bear witness to the truth of God. And we face the same temptation to adjust our message depending on how people respond. But the worthiness of our message is not determined by the response of those around us. God's word remains true whether it is received or refused.

This is why our gospel witness must be anchored in obedience, not outcome.

Paul says something similar in ==2 Corinthians 2:15–16== when he describes believers as the aroma of Christ. To some, that aroma is life. To others, it is death. The same witness produces different responses.

That is both the burden and the freedom of faithful witness. The burden is that we must speak even when rejection is possible. The freedom is that we don't have to bear the responsibility for how people respond.
