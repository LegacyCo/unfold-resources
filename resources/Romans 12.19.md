## Commentary
“Beloved, do not avenge yourselves, but rather give place to wrath; for it is written, ‘Vengeance is Mine, I will repay,’ says the Lord.”

Paul calls his readers “beloved” here, and it's the only time he does in this chapter. He knows what he's about to ask is hard. He's writing to Christians in Rome who would be wronged, slandered, and before long, killed. So he speaks tenderly, and then he tells them not to take revenge.

His reason isn't that the wrong doesn't matter or that justice is unimportant. His reason is that justice belongs to someone else. “Give place to wrath” means make room for God's wrath, as the quotation from ==Deuteronomy 32:35== shows: “Vengeance is Mine, I will repay.” When I take revenge, I'm sitting down in a seat that's already occupied. Paul's instruction is to get out of it and leave the case with the Judge.

God is far better at this than we are. We don't know the whole story, we can't see motives, and our anger nearly always goes too far. Lamech boasted that he would repay an injury seventy-seven times over (==Genesis 4:24==). God's repayment is exact. And He does repay. Every wrong done to you will be answered in one of two places. If the person who wronged you turns to Christ, that sin was judged at the cross, where God showed Himself “just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus” (==Romans 3:26==). If not, it will be judged at the last day (==2 Thessalonians 1:6==). In neither case is the injury overlooked.

Paul lived by this verse. Near the end of his life he wrote, “Alexander the coppersmith did me much harm. May the Lord repay him according to his works” (==2 Timothy 4:14==). He named the wrong and left the repayment with the Lord. Jesus is the pattern for all of it: “when He suffered, He did not threaten, but committed Himself to Him who judges righteously” (==1 Peter 2:23==).

It's important to note that giving place to God's wrath doesn't mean staying silent about a crime. Six verses later, Paul says the governing authority in a community is “God's minister, an avenger to execute wrath on him who practices evil” (==Romans 13:4==). He uses the same two words, vengeance and wrath. One of the ways God repays in this age is through courts and law. Reporting abuse or a crime is not personal revenge. It's handing the matter to the authority God appointed for it.

## Word Study
**Give place** is *dote topon* (δότε τόπον)—give room, make space, yield ground. Paul uses the same expression in ==Ephesians 4:27==, where he warns against letting anger linger: “nor give place to the devil.” The two verses belong together. Anger that I hold on to and act on gives room to the devil. Anger that I hand over gives room to God. **Vengeance** is *ekdikēsis* (ἐκδίκησις), the carrying out of justice, setting right what was wrong. Jesus uses it when He promises that God will “avenge His own elect who cry out day and night to Him” (==Luke 18:7==). Summary: vengeance in Scripture is justice being done, and it is good. This verse settles who carries it out.
