## Commentary
“And you He made alive, who were dead in trespasses and sins.”

Before Jesus got hold of us, we were not merely lost in our sin—we were dead in it. That distinction settles an important question about seeking God. Scripture promises that God will be found by those who seek Him with all their heart (==Jeremiah 29:13==), but dead things do not have the ability to seek. No one seeks God his way into salvation, because a corpse pursues nothing—“there is none who seeks after God” (==Romans 3:11==). We did not seek God and receive salvation. Salvation sought us and chased us down: “the Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost” (==Luke 19:10==).

This puts seeking in its right place. Salvation does not come to those who seek Him; salvation is what makes seeking Him possible. It is the gate by which we now have permission to chase after God. When Scripture says He is “a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him” (==Hebrews 11:6==), it is speaking to those who have crossed over from death to life, reborn by the life-giving power of His Spirit (==John 5:24==)—and the reward He gives the saints who pursue Him is more of Himself. First He makes the dead alive, entirely by grace and entirely His doing (==Ephesians 2:8-9==); then the ones He raised get to spend the rest of their lives seeking the One who sought them first (==1 John 4:19==).
