## Commentary
“For though you might have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet you do not have many fathers; for in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel.”

Paul draws a line the church still needs: instructors are plentiful; fathers are rare. An instructor gives you information. A father gives you himself. The Corinthians had no shortage of teaching—ten thousand voices, then as now, and today the number is higher with every feed and podcast. What they lacked was what only Paul had been to them: the one who was there at their birth, who labored over them, who carried them as his own. “My little children, for whom I labor in birth again until Christ is formed in you” (==Galatians 4:19==)—that’s not the language of a content provider. That’s a parent.

This is how kingdom people understand their place in God’s family. For Paul, the empty tomb didn’t just mean he was saved—it meant he belonged to a family, and he took up family roles in it. He was a brother to the churches, a father to the ones he begot in the gospel, a dad to Timothy and Titus—“a true son in the faith” (==1 Timothy 1:2==; ==Titus 1:4==). He didn’t connect with the church casually when it was convenient. He knew that being in Christ made him part of something larger than himself, with people who had a claim on him.

So take the inventory this verse invites. You almost certainly have teachers—more than any generation in history. But do you have fathers and mothers in the faith: people who know you, labor over you, and would travel through anything to see Christ formed in you? If not, ask God to place you in those relationships. And the harder question—is anyone able to call *you* that? Ten thousand instructors will keep the church informed. Only fathers and mothers will keep her a family.
