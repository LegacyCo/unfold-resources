## Commentary
Jeremiah was likely a young man when the word of the Lord came to him. God called him, put His words in his mouth, and told him what was coming. Judgment would come from the north. Jerusalem would be confronted for her unfaithfulness. The city that seemed secure would eventually be broken.

But that word did not happen overnight.

Jeremiah had to carry a prophecy that would take decades to unfold. It began to take shape years later, but the full weight of it did not come until Jerusalem and the temple were destroyed by Nebuchadnezzar in 586 BC. Which means Jeremiah spent much of his life waiting on a word that looked delayed, mocked, resisted, and misunderstood.

I wonder what those years felt like for him.

Did he second guess himself? Did he lie awake wondering if he had heard God correctly? Did the people point to the passing years and say, “See, nothing happened”? The longer the word waited, the easier it would have been for the people to dismiss the prophet.

But delay did not make the word false.

It revealed the mercy of God.

Jeremiah’s waiting was space for repentance. God was not slow because He was uncertain. He was patient because He is merciful. As ==Ezekiel 33:11== says, God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but desires that they turn and live.

Jeremiah had to learn how to carry a word without controlling its timeline. He had to keep speaking when fulfillment was not immediate. He had to remain faithful when the evidence seemed delayed.

And that may be one of the hardest forms of obedience: to keep trusting what God said while time keeps passing.
