## Commentary
“I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore with lovingkindness I have drawn you.”

There is a drawing toward God that is initiated by God, not man—and it has to be, because of what sin left us as. Scripture doesn’t say we were weakened in our trespasses; it says we were *dead* in them (==Ephesians 2:1==). Dead things don’t move. Dead things don’t wake up one morning and determine to seek God. So if anyone has ever moved toward God, something outside of them supplied the movement. Jesus said it without qualification: “No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him” (==John 6:44==).

But look at what God uses to do the drawing: lovingkindness. Not dread. Not the cattle prod of guilt. The everlasting love that existed before you did reaches out and pulls—and the pull feels like kindness, because it is kindness (==Romans 2:4==). If you’ve ever felt an unexplainable tug toward God—in a church service, in a crisis, in a quiet moment that ambushed you—that wasn’t your spiritual initiative. That was a rope around you, held by Someone who has loved you from everlasting.

And Jesus told us exactly where the rope is anchored: “And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all peoples to Myself” (==John 12:32==). The cross is the drawing power of God. Every person ever tugged toward heaven is being pulled toward a Man lifted up with His arms spread open.

## Word Study
Two words, one in each Testament, tell the same story:

- *drawn* : **mashak** (Hebrew) — to seize and pull; to cause something to move in a particular direction by exerting force upon it. This is not a suggestion whispered from a distance. It’s a grip that moves what could not move itself.
- *draw* : **helkō** (Greek, in ==John 6:44== and ==John 12:32==) — to drag, to haul. John uses this exact word for the disciples *dragging* a net full of 153 fish to shore (==John 21:6==; ==John 21:11==). Fish don’t swim into the boat. They get hauled in.

Summary: mashak and helkō agree—nobody drifts to God, and nobody is argued there. We get pulled, and the rope is His everlasting kindness.
