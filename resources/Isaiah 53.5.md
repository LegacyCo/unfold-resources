## Commentary
Art Katz wrote in *True Fellowship*: “We need to know Jesus exactly as He is, and He is nowhere presented more accurately as the reflection and image of God than in His suffering unto death.”

That sentence should reorder how we celebrate the incarnation. Every year we remember the scandalous moment when the uncreated, eternal, holy God wrapped Himself in flesh and came to us as one of us. But to grasp the full weight of God becoming man, we have to see the nativity scene in the shadow of the cross. Bethlehem without Calvary is a bedtime story—a cute Hallmark movie. God came to us to *die* for us. He took on flesh so that He could bleed for the sin of the world. He didn’t wrap Himself in power and bring us salvation; He wrapped Himself in Adam and brought us salvation (==Philippians 2:5-8==).

There’s something in us that wants to look away from suffering—it’s natural to avoid it. But Isaiah won’t let us look away, because this is where God is most accurately seen: “He was pierced for our transgressions, He was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was upon Him… and the LORD has laid on Him the iniquity of us all” (==Isaiah 53:5-6==). Every phrase is substitution. Our offenses—His piercing. Our wrongdoing—His crushing. Our peace—His punishment. He poured out His life unto death, bearing our sin and carrying it away forever (==Isaiah 53:12==).

Our real enemy could never be defeated by strength. Internal enemies are only defeated by sacrifice. Someone had to die—because everything bad that has ever happened since the beginning of time traces back to sin, and both the result of sin *and* the remedy for it is death (==Romans 6:23==). But the patient can’t heal his own disease, and a sinner can’t make the sacrifice for his own sin. So the Servant suffered in our place.

To know God as He truly is, we must know Him here: the suffering Servant, sacrificed for us. The manger is where He arrived. The cross is why He came.
