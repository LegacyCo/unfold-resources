## Commentary
“For He healed many, so that as many as had afflictions pressed about Him to touch Him.”

Think about who was in that crushing crowd. Jesus knew exactly what was in every heart pressing toward Him (==John 2:24-25==). He knew that many of them wanted His works but not His word. He knew some would follow the bread and vanish at the truth (==John 6:26==), and that the same region shouting for miracles could one day shout for His crucifixion. He healed them anyway.

Jesus still meets the needs of the multitudes who come to Him. He doesn’t withhold the miracle as punishment for an immature heart. The healing was never a wage paid out to the deserving—it was grace, flowing out of who He is. He is the exact image of the Father who “makes His sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust” (==Matthew 5:45==). Ten lepers were cleansed; one came back (==Luke 17:15-17==). Jesus didn’t revoke the other nine healings.

This matters for how you see God, and for how you minister like Him. If you’ve believed that God rations His kindness according to spiritual performance—yours or anyone else’s—you’ve misread the crowd scenes of the Gospels. His kindness isn’t a contract; it’s His nature, and it’s the very thing that leads people to repentance (==Romans 2:4==). So pray for the coworker who may never come to church. Believe for healing for the neighbor who mocks your faith. The miracle isn’t a reward for arriving. It’s often the kindness that starts someone on the way.
