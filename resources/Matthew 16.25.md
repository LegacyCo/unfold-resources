## Commentary
“For whoever desires to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for My sake will find it.”

Remember the story of Noah's ark? Let's tie it to this verse. What did it cost to get on Noah’s boat. The ticket was free—grace held that door open for years. But when Noah walked through it, he turned his back on everything he had ever known: his home, his land, his possessions, his routines, his neighbors, his extended family. Getting on the boat was free, and it cost him everything.

That’s the gospel. The gospel is not “come to Jesus and let Him improve the life you’re already living”—that’s the gospel of the American Dream, where Jesus exists to answer the question, “What can You do for me?” Nobody on the road to Damascus said, “I met a Man who increased my bank account, upgraded my house, and removed every uncomfortable thing I didn’t like.” Paul said, “I met a Man who knocked me off my horse with His blinding light—and I was never the same again” (==Acts 9:3-5==). “I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live” (==Galatians 2:20==). Deny yourself, take up your cross, and follow Me (==Matthew 16:24==). A gospel that costs you nothing is not the gospel of the kingdom.

Ever wonder why nobody else got on the ark? The door stood open for years. The answer is the oldest irony in the world: they loved the life they were living too much—the very life the flood was about to take anyway. And here’s the testimony of everyone who has ever paid the full price of the free ticket: what you leave behind turns out to look like garbage compared to what you gain. That’s Paul, doing the accounting at the end of his life: “I count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord… I count them as rubbish, that I may gain Christ” (==Philippians 3:8==). Lose your life for His sake, and you don’t end up bankrupt. You end up finding the only life that was ever worth saving.
