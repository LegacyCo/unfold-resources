## Commentary
“Now King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all she desired, whatever she asked, besides what Solomon had given her according to the royal generosity.”

The queen came with the mind of the world: answer my questions and I’ll give you my gold. She arrived with camels loaded with spices, precious stones, and a staggering quantity of gold (==1 Kings 10:2==). But watch how the visit ends. Solomon sends her away with *all that she desired*—whatever she asked—plus more from his own royal generosity. She wasn’t purchasing access to the king. Solomon couldn’t be bought; he was lavish. His wisdom and his wealth were made available to her simply because she came to see if what she’d heard was true. She left with more than she brought.

So it is with anyone who comes to the true King of Israel. You can’t buy an audience with Him—He gives everyone the same invitation: come, sit, eat, drink, stay, ask, learn, become like Me. He doesn’t need the world’s gold; He owns the cattle on a thousand hills (==Psalm 50:10==). But notice what Solomon did with the queen’s gifts: the almug wood and treasures she brought went into the house of the LORD and became instruments for the singers (==1 Kings 10:11-12==). That’s the King’s way with what you offer Him. He doesn’t need it—but He will receive what you bring and let it become part of His house, turned into music for His courts. Nothing given to this King is wasted, and no one leaves His presence poorer than they came.

Jesus Himself pointed back to this story—and put Himself in it: “The queen of the South will rise up in the judgment with this generation and condemn it, for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and indeed a greater than Solomon is here” (==Matthew 12:42==). A pagan queen crossed a continent on rumors of a king. The King the rumors were really about is now the One inviting you to come with all the questions of your heart—and He still answers every one.
