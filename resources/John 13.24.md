## Commentary
When Peter wanted to know who Jesus was talking about, he asked John—the one who was leaning against Jesus. The question passed through the disciple who was closest to Jesus, and the answer was given.

John’s posture is a picture of priestly nearness that all followers of Jesus have access to. He was not striving for information or proving his usefulness. He was simply leaning into Jesus. And from that place of rest came revelation.

We often seek answers from a distance. We want God to explain His plans but His first invitation is usually not to help us understand more—it’s to come closer (==James 4:8==). Revelation is safest in the hands of those who desire the Lord more than the answer.

The kingdom often entrusts its secrets to those who have learned to rest on the heart of the King (==Psalm 25:14==).

Sometimes ministering to the Lord simply looks like a disciple reclining against Him, content to remain close. And the one resting nearest to His heart often hears what others cannot (see ==John 15:15==).
