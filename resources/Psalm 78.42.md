## Commentary
One of the repeated sins in Psalm 78 is forgetfulness.

Israel had seen the works of God. They had seen His judgments in Egypt, walked through the sea, eaten manna in the wilderness, and drunk water from the rock (==Exodus 14:21–31==; ==Exodus 16:1–15==; ==Exodus 17:1–7==). They were not lacking evidence of God’s power. But when pressure came, they forgot.

Biblical forgetfulness is not simply the loss of information. It is the failure to let what God has done shape how we trust Him now. Israel remembered enough to tell the stories, but they did not remember in a way that produced obedience, gratitude, and faith.

That's why forgetfulness is so dangerous. It makes yesterday’s deliverance feel irrelevant to today’s trouble. It disconnects the people of God from the memory of God’s faithfulness and leaves them vulnerable to fear, complaint, and unbelief.

Moses warned Israel about this before they entered the land. When they lived in houses they did not build and enjoyed vineyards they did not plant, they were to beware lest they forget the Lord who brought them out of Egypt (==Deuteronomy 6:10–12==). The disciples faced a similar danger when they worried about bread after Jesus had already fed the multitudes. Jesus asked them, “Do you not remember?” (==Mark 8:14–21==).

Forgetfulness is not a memory problem. It is a trust problem.

## Application
Remembering is an act of war against unbelief. We remember what God has done so that fear can't redefine who God is to us.
