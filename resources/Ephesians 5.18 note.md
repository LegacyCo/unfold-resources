## Commentary
“Do not be drunk with wine… but be filled with the Spirit.” In the original Greek, “be filled” is a continuous, ongoing action—not a one-time event. A better translation would be, “*Keep on* being filled with the Holy Spirit.”

Some will ask, “I’m saved—I have the Holy Spirit. Why would I need to fill something that’s already full?” Because what was once full may not be as full as it once was. Many of us genuinely believed the mystery of God dwelling in us, but that moment of faith was never reinforced with fresh encounters, and we plateaued and grew cold. When Spurgeon was asked why he needed to be continually filled with the Spirit, he answered in three words: “Because I leak.”

We live in the age between “the kingdom has come” and “the kingdom is yet to come”—and in this age, filling is not automatic. We must strive to enter His rest (==Hebrews 4:11==), pursue Him with our whole hearts, diligently seek Him (==Hebrews 11:6==)—or the space that was once filled with passion for God will quietly be filled by something else. This is why Paul’s contrast with wine is so instructive. No one gets drunk once and stays drunk for life—the influence wears off, and staying under it requires returning to the source. The filling of the Spirit works the same way—with one crucial difference. The Spirit isn’t fickle the way wine’s influence is; He is faithful. But He is also a Person, not a force: He can be grieved (==Ephesians 4:30==) and His fire can be quenched (==1 Thessalonians 5:19==), and where sin is tolerated and His voice ignored, His manifest presence pulls away. Most often, though, the distance is our drifting—attention that wanders, affections that turn to lesser influences. Either way, the returning is ours to do: we come back, again and again, to the faithful Source, and yield our thoughts, desires, and affections to Him afresh. Everyone lives under some influence. The only question is which source you keep returning to.

God designed us to constantly breathe in the breath of His Spirit—to live in the oxygen of His atmosphere. And you can’t store up yesterday’s breath. You need a fresh one, moment by moment: a continuous awareness of your union with Him that keeps you *in* Him while everything in this age is trying to pull you away from Him.

## Application
Treat the Spirit’s filling like breathing, not like a certificate. Somewhere today, come back to the source on purpose—in worship, in prayer, in the word, in asking plainly: “Father, fill me again” (==Luke 11:13==). Not because you lost the Spirit, but because you leak. Yesterday’s breath carried yesterday. Today needs its own.
