## Commentary
“Wisdom calls aloud outside; she raises her voice in the open squares.”

Mark Twain joked, “When I was a boy of fourteen, my father was so ignorant I could hardly stand to have the old man around. But when I got to be twenty-one, I was astonished at how much he had learned in seven years.” The father hadn’t moved. The boy had. And that’s remarkably close to what Proverbs says about wisdom herself.

Solomon locates wisdom not in a cave requiring an expedition, not behind a riddle for the elite few—she’s *outside*, in the streets, at the head of the noisy intersections, at the city gates, calling aloud (==Proverbs 1:21==). If wisdom is hiding, she’s hiding in plain sight. Getting wisdom is not primarily a matter of *finding* something elusive; it’s a matter of *responding* to Someone who wants to be found. Hear her promise to anyone who turns: “Surely I will pour out my spirit on you; I will make my words known to you” (==Proverbs 1:23==). And again: “I love those who love me, and those who seek me diligently will find me” (==Proverbs 8:17==). James carries the same open-door policy into the new covenant: “If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and without reproach” (==James 1:5==). No rationing. No reproach. The shortage has never been on the supply side.

## Application
The test of whether you’ve heard wisdom’s call is in the crisis. When the pressure hits, what do you reach for first? The world's wisdom is the factory setting: fear, anger, manipulation, complaining, tension, playing the victim. Wisdom's voice calls over all of it. So build the habit before the crisis: when a situation turns serious this week, refuse the factory settings, and instead pray in the Spirit and search the Scriptures until God's wisdom for that specific situation becomes clear. It may tell you to stay silent under criticism. It may tell you to bless the person who hurt you. It will often look foolish from the outside—cross-shaped wisdom usually does (==1 Corinthians 1:18==). But she's standing in your street, calling. The only question is whether you'll turn at her rebuke and let her pour out her spirit on you.
