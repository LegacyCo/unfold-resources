## Commentary
Contention is easiest to stop before it begins. Once strife is released, it quickly moves beyond the original issue and becomes difficult to contain. Proverbs compares it to rushing water breaking through a dam. What begins as a small opening can become a flood.

The wise learn to recognize the first signs of conflict and refuse to feed them. They don't insist on having the final word, rehearse every offense, or allow pride to turn disagreement into division. They understand that winning an argument can still cost them a relationship.

An offended brother can become harder to win back than a fortified city. Walls go up, trust breaks down, and words spoken in anger continue working long after the moment has passed. This is why wisdom acts early. It softens the answer, overlooks what can be overlooked, and seeks peace before the offense takes root.

A person given to strife eventually makes every environment unbearable. It becomes better to live alone in discomfort than to share a home with constant contention (see ==Proverbs 21:9==). The wise therefore guard peace while it can still be guarded, because some floods are easier to prevent than to clean up.
