## Commentary
When Luke tells us that Apollos "knew only the baptism of John," he's pointing to an important distinction: Apollos understood repentance, but he hadn't yet grasped union.
John's baptism was a baptism of preparation - turning away from sin, making yourself ready for the Kingdom that was coming. It was powerful, but it was incomplete. John himself said, "One is coming after me who will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire" (==Matthew 3:11==). John's baptism pointed forward. New Covenant baptism declares what has already happened in Christ.

When we're baptized into Jesus, were not just saying we've made a decision. Were being immersed into His death and resurrection (==Romans 6:3-4==). We're declaring covenant - that His death is our death, His life is our life. Paul says it clearly: "All of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ" (==Galatians 3:27==).

This is union language. This is being joined into a single, living entity with Christ. Apollos knew the Scriptures. He was fervent in spirit. But he was still living in the "not yet" - passionate about what was coming, but not yet standing in the finished work of the cross and the indwelling presence of the Spirit. When Priscilla and Aquila pulled him aside, they weren't correcting his theology as much as they were inviting him into the fullness of what had already been accomplished. The Kingdom hadn't just drawn near. It had come. The Spirit wasn't a future promise anymore. He was here, dwelling in the people of God (==1 Corinthians 6:19==).

That's the difference. John prepared the way.
Jesus is the way. And baptism into Christ isn't a ritual - it's the declaration that we've crossed over from the old Adam into the Last Adam, from death into resurrection life.

## Application
Where do you see yourself in this? Are you still waiting for something Jesus has already finished, or are you living from the reality that you are already in Him?
