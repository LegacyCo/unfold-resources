## Commentary
“You have loved them just as You have loved Me.” Sit with what Jesus is praying here: the exact same love that the Father has for His Son, the Father has for you.

Many of us hear that and say amen while still believing something altogether different. We believe that when we confessed our sin and invited Jesus into our hearts, *then* God began to love us because of what Jesus did on the cross. And we don’t realize what we’ve just done: we’ve made God the judge before we believed, who then changes into the loving Father as a result of our confession. The spirit of religion loves that version, because if His love has a beginning, then it can also have an end. The enemy wants you convinced that God’s love depends on you keeping your end of the bargain.

So here’s the question: at what point did the Father begin loving the Son? Listen to what Jesus prays in the very next verse: “Father… You loved Me before the foundation of the world” (==John 17:24==). The Father’s love for the Son has no beginning—and Jesus says His love for you is *just as* He loved Him. Paul says the same thing: He chose us in Him “before the foundation of the world” (==Ephesians 1:4==). Not when we stopped doing bad stuff. Not when we got serious about holiness. Before there was a world to sin in.

That means your confession of faith didn’t produce the Father’s love—it brought you into agreement with it. Your repentance didn’t earn His love, because His love predates the sin that made your repentance necessary. While we were still enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son (==Romans 5:10==). Love came first. Love always came first.

Don’t hear what I’m not saying. Do we do good works? Absolutely—we walk in the good works prepared for us beforehand (==Ephesians 2:10==). We pray, we fast, we repent, we pursue holiness. But we do those things *because* we are loved, not to become loved. The whole kingdom life is a response to a love that was there before you were.
