## Commentary
“And because lawlessness will abound, the love of many will grow cold.”

When the disciples asked Jesus for the signs of His coming and the end of the age (==Matthew 24:3==), we expect the answer to be wars and earthquakes—and those are in the list. But look at the sign we skip past: “then many will be offended, will betray one another, and will hate one another” (==Matthew 24:10==). Jesus could have said that as the kingdom advances, offense among His people would fade out. He said the opposite: at the end of the age, offense *increases*.

The word for “love” in this verse is *agapē*—God’s own love, the selfless love that expects nothing in return. The world doesn’t have agapē to lose; that love belongs to God and the people of God. So Jesus isn’t describing the world getting colder. He’s describing *believers*. Believers offended. Believers betraying one another. Believers whose God-given love has iced over into hatred. Follow the progression: offense taken → offense held → betrayal → cold love. And notice what rises in that environment: “many false prophets will rise up and deceive many” (==Matthew 24:11==). Deception flourishes among the offended, because unforgiveness blinds—an offended heart can no longer rightly divide truth, which makes it an easy audience for a lie.

In the last days, forgiveness will become survival equipment. The believer who keeps short accounts—who refuses to let any offense take root—keeps their love hot and their eyes clear, and false voices find no foothold in them. Jesus finishes the thought with a promise attached to endurance: “But he who endures to the end shall be saved” (==Matthew 24:13==). Enduring to the end isn’t only about surviving persecution from outside the church. It’s about refusing to let offense from *inside* it freeze the agapē out of you.
