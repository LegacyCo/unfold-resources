## Commentary
Isaiah opens with a challenging word. God is rebuking Israel, but not for neglecting religious activity. He is rebuking them because they are *full* of it. In many ways, they were still doing the "right" things. They were bringing offerings (==Isaiah 1:11==). They were observing appointed feasts (==Isaiah 1:14==). They were showing up in the courts of the Lord (==Isaiah 1:12==). From the outside, the machinery of worship was still functioning.

But God calls their sacrifices *futile*. Israel was bringing sacrifice without surrender. They were offering God the rituals while keeping their hearts far from Him.

So God says, “Bring no more.”

God is not impressed by religious activity that never touches the heart of the worshiper. He will not be satisfied with offerings that function as a substitute for repentance, love, justice, and communion with Him.

Under the New Covenant, we may not bring bulls or grain offerings to the temple, but we can still bring futile sacrifices while withholding what God actually longs for.

We can bring self-discipline and effort that give the appearance of righteousness, but if our hearts are not yielded to God, are we any different from the Pharisees ("whitewashed tombs" - see ==Matthew 23:27==)?

Could it be that God is speaking to our generation through Isaiah 1:13, "Bring no more futile sacrifices"?

In Christ, we have been fully accepted in the Beloved (==Ephesians 1:6==). While we were still sinners, Christ died for us (==Romans 5:8==). We do not obey or serve to purchase access. We have been brought near by the blood of Christ (==Ephesians 2:13==), and His love has already been poured into our hearts through the precious Holy Spirit (==Romans 5:5==).

This does not mean obedience no longer matters. Far from it. It means obedience now flows from a different source. Under the gospel of God's grace, obedience is no longer the anxious labor of someone trying to earn God’s approval. It is the fruit of a life that has received undeserved mercy. 

The gospel frees us from futile sacrifices. Jesus has already offered the only sacrifice that can satisfy God. And what satisfies His heart is a heart fully devoted to Him (see ==2 Chronicles 16:9== and ==1 Kings 8:61==).
