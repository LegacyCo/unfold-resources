## Commentary
What we often interpret as slowness in God is actually the patience of His love. God isn’t delayed, distracted, or struggling to keep pace with our lives. He moves according to a wisdom and purpose that can’t be measured by our urgency.

In his book Three Mile an Hour God, Japanese theologian Kosuke Koyama writes: “God walks ‘slowly’ because he is love. If he is not love he would have gone much faster. Love has its speed. It is an inner speed. It is a spiritual speed. It is a different kind of speed from the technological speed to which we are accustomed. It is ‘slow’ yet it is lord over all other speeds since it is the speed of love.”

Love refuses to be hurried when hurrying would harm the person it loves. God takes time to form what we would rather rush, expose what we would rather avoid, and prepare us for what we think we are already ready to receive. His pace may frustrate us because we are usually focused on arrival, while He is also concerned with who we are becoming along the way.

Jesus repeatedly demonstrated this pace. He stopped for people others overlooked. He allowed Himself to be interrupted. He remained where He was for two more days after hearing that Lazarus was sick. None of this came from indifference. He moved at the pace of communion with the Father and the pace required by love.

The speed of God may feel slow in a world shaped by immediacy, but He is never late. His patience is purposeful. His apparent delay is often the space where mercy is working, maturity is forming, and love is accomplishing something speed never could.

Faith means learning to trust not only where God is taking us, but also the pace at which He chooses to lead us.

## Application
Resist the urge to rush what God is doing. Trust His pace, stay present to the work He is doing now, and refuse to measure His faithfulness by how quickly your circumstances change. Love has its own speed, and God is never late.
