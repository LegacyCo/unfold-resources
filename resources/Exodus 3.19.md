## Commentary
When God appeared to Moses in the burning bush, He made an incredible promise: Israel would be delivered from Egypt. But then He immediately added something unexpected: “I am sure that the king of Egypt will not let you go, no, not even by a mighty hand” (Exodus 3:19).

In other words, God told Moses from the beginning that obedience would not produce immediate success.

Moses was being sent with the word of the Lord, yet his first encounter with Pharaoh would end in failure. Then another failure. And another. Ten times Pharaoh hardened his heart before the promise finally came to pass.

That means the apparent failure was never evidence that Moses had missed God. It was part of the process God had already revealed. The word was certain, but the fulfillment would come through testing.

We often assume that if we are truly walking in God's will, doors should open immediately and resistance should disappear. But sometimes the clearest evidence that you have heard God is not immediate success, but the grace to remain faithful when obedience seems to accomplish nothing.

God was never wondering whether Pharaoh would let Israel go. He already knew the outcome. The delay was not for God's benefit. It was part of the formation of Moses, the exposure of Pharaoh's heart, and the display of God's glory.

Don't measure the truth of God's word by your first results. Sometimes the promise has to pass through resistance before it reaches fulfillment.
