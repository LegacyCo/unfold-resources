## Commentary
“and said to him, ‘Look, you are old, and your sons do not walk in your ways. Now make us a king to judge us like all the nations.’”

The elders begin their argument with two things that are true. Samuel is old, and his sons are corrupt (==1 Samuel 8:3==). Israel had a real leadership problem, and nobody could fault them for seeing it.

Asking for a king wasn't the sin either. God had told Abraham, “kings shall come from you” (==Genesis 17:6==). Jacob had prophesied a scepter in Judah (==Genesis 49:10==). Moses had even written instructions for the day Israel would have a king, and he said that king must be one “whom the LORD your God chooses” (==Deuteronomy 17:14-15==). A king was always part of God's plan.

The trouble is in the last four words: “like all the nations.” Israel's whole calling was to be *unlike* the nations. God had called them “a special treasure to Me above all people… a kingdom of priests and a holy nation” (==Exodus 19:5-6==). Balaam looked at them and said, “a people dwelling alone, not reckoning itself among the nations” (==Numbers 23:9==). Now the elders look around at what everyone else has, and they want that. When Samuel warns them what it will cost, they make their argument even more emphatically: “that we also may be like all the nations, and that our king may judge us and go out before us and fight our battles” (==1 Samuel 8:19-20==). They wanted someone they could see to do what God had been doing for them all along. Samuel later tells them that fear was part of their motivation. They'd seen the king of Ammon coming against them and said, “a king shall reign over us,” when the LORD their God was their king (==1 Samuel 12:12==).

They also don't ask Samuel to pray, and they don't ask what God wants. They show up with the decision already made: “Now make us a king.” It's Samuel who takes it to the LORD (==1 Samuel 8:6==).

So God gave them what they asked for, and He gave it to them in the form they asked for it. Saul looked exactly like a king of the nations, head and shoulders taller than anyone else (==1 Samuel 9:2==). Hosea sums up the situation: “I gave you a king in My anger, and took him away in My wrath” (==Hosea 13:11==). Meanwhile, God had His own king in mind the whole time, “a man after His own heart” (==1 Samuel 13:14==). The need for leadership in Israel was real. But God's people wanted a king like the nations had, and they wanted him immediately. God had a different king in mind, and *His* king would come in *His* time.

## Application
You can be right about the problem and wrong about the solution. When something in your life, your family, or your church clearly isn't working, pay attention to where your proposed solution is coming from. Is it what God has said, or is it what everyone around you is doing? Bring Him the problem before you bring Him your plan. “Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God” (==Romans 12:2==).
