## Commentary
“Then Jesus said to His disciples, ‘If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me.’”

Every follower of Jesus gets handed a cross—and the cross always leaves a mark. Walking in His ways requires enduring things that scar us: not necessarily the literal wounds some of our persecuted brothers and sisters bear on their bodies, but the marks every true disciple bears on the soul. The death of pride is going to leave a mark. Crucifying selfishness will leave a mark. The uprooting of greed, choosing to forgive, believing when there’s nothing visible to believe with—each one wounds the flesh’s way of living, and each wound heals into something glorious: a scar that testifies you chose the way of the Spirit when your flesh wanted the opposite.

Why must it cost this much? Because His character is so opposed to our inherited nature. Our salvation and adoption were given to us *freely*—but sonship is not developed *cheaply*. We were purchased with blood; we are matured by fire. Betrayals, disappointments, frustrations, struggle—God wastes none of it. He uses all of it to shape us into the image of His Son (==Romans 8:28-29==). Jesus paid the price so we could receive His life without cost, and now He calls us to work that life into our relationships, our behavior, and our thoughts—and that working-in is where the marks come from.

And really, what else should we expect? “A servant is not greater than his master” (==John 13:16==). Right now there is a Man seated on heaven’s throne with scars on His body—resurrection didn’t erase them; it glorified them (==John 20:27==). If the Master has scars, so will His people. His tell the story of a love that gave itself for the world. Ours tell the story of every time we denied ourselves and followed Him. To the world, scars are something to hide. To the people of the cross, they’re beautiful.
