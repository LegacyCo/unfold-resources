## Commentary
Communion is God's kiss. As Andre Dubus so succinctly puts it, "Without the Eucharist, God becomes a monologue." He's right. We need more than words, we need to be physically touched. Without tangible participation, our faith can be reduced to ideas, sermons, and language about God without a lived encounter with Him. Those things matter, but God has not fashioned the Christian life to remain at the level of abstraction. He made us to receive Him with our whole being (see John 6:53-58). 

This is what happens at the Table of the Lord and why communion, and every other Christian sacrament, always has some tangible, physical element to it - a laying on of hands, a consuming of bread and wine, an immersion in water, an anointing with oil. An embrace needs to be physical, not only something imagined. The body remembers what the heart believes. The gospel is not only announced to the ears, it's brought into the hands, the mouth, the senses. We are made for encounter, and the invisible grace of God comes to us through visible means. 

The word truly has taken on flesh and dwells among us. The incarnation tells us that God is not reluctant to touch the material world. He sanctifies it by entering it. And His table is no different. It is a holy act of receiving Christ. It rescues us from a thin, shallow, verbal Christianity. It brings us back to participation. It reminds us that ours is not a faith to analyze, but one to enter into. At the table, God is not a subject to discuss. He is a gift to receive.
