## Commentary
Ezekiel now sees “seventy men of the elders of the house of Israel” standing before the idolatrous images, each holding a censer while “a thick cloud of incense went up.”

The seventy elders likely represent the leadership of Israel as a whole, echoing the seventy elders appointed alongside Moses (==Numbers 11:16–17==). These are not outsiders corrupting Israel from the margins; they are respected leaders participating in hidden idolatry at the very heart of the temple.

The incense makes the scene especially disturbing. Incense belonged to Israel’s worship of Yahweh and was meant to rise before Him in the sanctuary (==Exodus 30:7–8==). Here, however, the elders are offering incense before false images. They have taken something associated with true worship and redirected it toward idols.

Jaazaniah is singled out by name, possibly because of his prominence among the elders. His father, Shaphan, had served under King Josiah and was involved in the rediscovery of the Book of the Law (==2 Kings 22:8–10==). If this is the same family, the contrast is striking: one generation helped recover the Word of God, while another now stands inside the temple offering incense to idols.

The vision exposes the hidden spiritual condition of Jerusalem. Outwardly the temple still stands and worship continues, but behind closed doors the leaders have filled God's house with rival loyalties. Ezekiel is being shown that Judah's coming judgment is not arbitrary. The corruption has reached even those responsible for leading God's people.
