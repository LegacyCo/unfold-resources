## Commentary
John knew his message had a limit: “There’s One coming after me who is mightier than I. I’m not even worthy to carry His sandals. I baptize with water unto repentance, but He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire” (Matthew 3:11).

Repentance turns you. But only the Spirit transforms you.

Repentance brings you to the water to wash you so that Jesus can fill you with the Spirit — who then becomes in you a river of living water (John 7:37-39).

This is the promise of Ezekiel 36:25-27, “I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean; I will cleanse you from all your filthiness and from all your idols (_That’s step one_). (_Step two_:) I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes."

Step one is the cleansing of repentance. Step two is life in the Spirit. 

And just because you take step one does not mean you are living a life of the Spirit. 

Consider that the reason we have the New Testament letters is because unbelievers had received the gospel and genuinely repented and turned to Christ (*step one*)…and yet somewhere on their journey of faith they either stalled out or turned back to the law. They repented but never grew up. For example, Hebrews 5:12 says, “By this time you ought to be teachers, but you need someone to teach you the elementary principles of the actual words of God, and you still need milk and not solid food.”

And Hebrews 6:1, “Leaving the discussion of the elementary things of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works…of the doctrine of baptisms.”

It is possible to never leave the elementary things of the faith, to camp out at the starting line and never mature into the life of the Spirit.

You can’t live the Jesus way of life without repentance, but you can be repentant and not live the Jesus way of life. 

Look at the progression of this passage again: John preaches repentance in the wilderness. Jesus goes out to him in the wilderness. Jesus enters into the waters of John’s baptism, and comes up from the water clothed in the Spirit.

## Application
God does not intend for this to be the exclusive testimony of Jesus. This is the way for anyone who is now in Christ. That’s the mystery of union: His death is now your death. His resurrection is now your resurrection. His life is now your life. And His baptism, which is a baptism of the Spirit and fire, is now your baptism.

So what does it practically look like to live in this freedom? Remember, people of the wilderness are: Stuck in the cycle of guilt and shame. Always trying to fix themselves. Bitter. It’s hard for them to forgive others because they can’t forgive themselves. They relate to God through distance and effort.

A people of the Spirit are: Aware of sin, but not defined by it. Free, not just forgiven. Led by the Spirit, not their mistakes. They know they are in process and receive God’s grace when they fall short. They are walking with God, not just trying to get back to Him.

One is always looking at themselves. The other is too fascinated with God to be worried about themselves. 

That’s why Paul can say this in ==1 Corinthians 4:3-4==, “I do not even judge myself. For I know of nothing against myself…He who judges me is the Lord.”

That’s what freedom sounds like.

The starting line is always an important part of the journey. It opens up the possibility of exploring the trail. It focuses your attention on the journey ahead. 

God doesn’t intend for your journey with Him to top out at repentance.

Repentance is, of course, essential. It gets you out of your life in Adam and onto the path of Christ. It prepares the way, but it is not the way. 

The way is following that Man who drew the Holy Spirit down like a heavenly dove.

Repentance joins you to Him so He can take you higher.
