## Commentary
Matthew ends this story abruptly. Jesus delivers the demonized men, the demons enter the herd of pigs, and the entire city comes out to meet Him. But instead of welcoming Jesus, “they begged Him to depart from their region” (==Matthew 8:34==).

Matthew leaves the story there. But Mark and Luke tell us what happens next, and it is remarkable. In their accounts, one of the delivered men begs Jesus to let him come along. After everything he has experienced, it makes sense. He wants to get into the boat and follow the Man who gave him his life back.

But Jesus tells him no. Instead, He gives him an assignment: “Go home to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you” (==Mark 5:19==).

Mark then tells us: “And he departed and began to proclaim in Decapolis all that Jesus had done for him; and all marveled” (==Mark 5:20==).

The word translated “proclaim” is _kēryssō_ (κηρύσσω), meaning to announce, herald, or proclaim publicly. It is the same family of language the New Testament regularly uses for the proclamation of the gospel.

This makes the man one of the earliest evangelistic witnesses Jesus sends out in the Gospel narratives—and perhaps the first person explicitly commissioned in Mark to go and proclaim what Jesus had done.

And consider who Jesus chooses. Not a scribe. Not a priest. Not one of the Twelve.

_A formerly demonized man._

Only moments earlier, this man had been living among tombs, possessed by demons and isolated from ordinary human life (==Mark 5:2–5==). Now Jesus entrusts him with the responsibility of telling an entire region about what God has done.

Luke makes the story even more striking. Jesus tells the man, “Return to your own house, and tell what great things God has done for you.” Luke then immediately says, “And he went his way and proclaimed throughout the whole city what great things Jesus had done for him” (==Luke 8:39==).

Jesus says, _Tell them what God has done._ The man goes and tells them _what Jesus has done._ His message was simply his encounter with Jesus.

There is also something beautiful happening in the larger story. The people of the region ask Jesus to leave (==Matthew 8:34==), and He does. But He does not leave them without a witness.

_He leaves behind a testimony._

The man who had once terrified the region would now travel through it telling people about the One who had set him free.

And notice what Jesus does not tell him to do. He does not tell him to explain everything. He does not require him to have every theological question answered. He gives him something much simpler:

_Tell them what the Lord has done for you._

His testimony becomes his message.

Jesus takes a man who had been one of the clearest demonstrations of the kingdom of darkness and turns him into a living announcement that another Kingdom has arrived.

The place that asked Jesus to leave would continue hearing about Jesus from the man He had set free.
