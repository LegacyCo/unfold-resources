## Commentary
“But if you had known what this means, ‘I desire mercy and not sacrifice,’ you would not have condemned the guiltless.”

Jesus is quoting ==Hosea 6:6==, where God confronts Israel for maintaining the outward practices of worship while neglecting the very character those practices were intended to produce. The people continued offering sacrifices, but their covenant faithfulness had deteriorated. They were keeping the forms of religion while failing to reflect the heart of the God they claimed to worship.

The Hebrew word translated “mercy” in Hosea is *chesed*, a word that speaks of steadfast love, covenant loyalty, and faithfulness. God was not saying that sacrifices were unnecessary. He was confronting the idea that religious observance could substitute for a life characterized by His own covenant love.

This is actually the second time in Matthew's Gospel that Jesus directs the Pharisees to this passage. When they criticized Him for eating with tax collectors and sinners, He told them, “Go and learn what this means: ‘I desire mercy and not sacrifice’” (==Matthew 9:13==). Now, three chapters later, He tells them they still haven't learned the lesson.

The Pharisees were so confident in their interpretation of the Sabbath that they were willing to condemn people whom God had not condemned (“You would not have condemned the guiltless”). Their commitment to defending the Law had become disconnected from the character of the Lawgiver. They believed they were protecting God's holiness, yet their judgments were misrepresenting His heart.

## Application
It is possible to know the commands of God and still misunderstand the God who gave them. We can become so concerned with identifying what is wrong in others that we fail to recognize where God is extending mercy.

Jesus is not asking us to choose compassion over obedience. He is showing us that obedience which fails to express the heart of God has misunderstood the very purpose of His commands. As Paul would later explain, “Love is the fulfillment of the law” (==Romans 13:10==).

Ask yourself whether your understanding of Scripture is making you more like Jesus or more like the Pharisees. Are you studying to show yourself approved before God (==2 Timothy 2:15==), or simply to win arguments and prove others wrong? Is your knowledge of Scripture producing greater humility, compassion, and a love for the things God loves? Think about how you respond when someone disagrees with your interpretation or fails to meet your expectations. Are you quick to correct and condemn, or willing to listen, understand, and extend mercy? God forbid we become so concerned with being right that we end up condemning people God has declared innocent.
