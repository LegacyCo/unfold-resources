## Commentary
“Even them I will bring to My holy mountain, and make them joyful in My house of prayer. Their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be accepted on My altar; for My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations.”

When Jesus overturned the tables in the temple, this is the verse He was quoting: “Is it not written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations’?” (==Mark 11:17==). Seven centuries before that day, God had already declared what His house was for—and who it was for. Read who “even them” refers to: the sons of the foreigner who join themselves to the LORD, and the eunuchs who keep His covenant (==Isaiah 56:3-6==)—the two groups most certain they had no place in God’s house. The foreigner said, “The LORD has utterly separated me from His people.” The eunuch said, “I am a dry tree.” God answers both: I will bring you to My holy mountain, and make you *joyful* in My house of prayer.

Three words in this verse carry Scripture’s definition of God’s house. *House* speaks to the family of God in communion with one another. *Prayer* speaks to the family of God in communion with Him. *Nations* speaks to His desire for those not yet in His family to be brought in. Hold all three together and you have what the church is for—and notice that most expressions of church emphasize one and neglect another: strong community with little outreach, or strong outreach with little depth of communion. God’s stated design holds all three.

The house is never finished filling: “The Lord GOD, who gathers the outcasts of Israel, says, ‘Yet I will gather to him others besides those who are gathered to him’” (==Isaiah 56:8==). God’s house has always been aimed at the people who assumed the door was closed to them—which is exactly why Jesus was angry enough to flip tables when He found the court of the nations turned into a marketplace. He wasn’t defending a building. He was defending this promise.
