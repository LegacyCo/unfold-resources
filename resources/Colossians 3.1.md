## Commentary
“If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God.”

Paul grounds the command in a location. Seek things above—not because heavenly things are generically better than earthly ones, but because of who is *there*: “where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God.” The believer’s orientation follows the believer’s King. Since you were raised with Him, and He is enthroned, your attention, your affections, and your frame of reference belong where your life now actually is: “for you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God” (==Colossians 3:3==).

This is the orientation of a people of the ascension. It is possible to believe in the risen Savior and still live entirely referenced to things below—circumstances, threats, appetites, the next crisis. Paul’s logic runs the other way: the enthronement of Christ reorders what a person seeks. Contentment worked this way for him. Whether he was abased or abounding, in a fruitful city or shipwrecked on the way to one, he had learned to be content (==Philippians 4:11-12==)—because Jesus was Lord over every city, every ship, and every synagogue, whatever they did with him. A person whose King is seated above their circumstances is not governed by their circumstances.

The verse also supplies a diagnostic. Persistent fear, anxiety, and the dramatic swings of an inconsistent walk are worth reading as signals—indications that something other than the seated King is holding the attention of the heart, and probably its throne. The instruction for that condition is not condemnation; it is this verse's reorientation. He meets us where we are, and He does not intend to leave us there (==2 Corinthians 3:18==).

## Application
Let what you seek reveal what you serve. Take an honest inventory of where your attention goes under pressure: when trouble comes, do your thoughts run first to the circumstance, or to the throne above it? Identify the one area of life where fear or anxiety most reliably takes over—that area is where the seated Christ is least acknowledged—and deliberately bring it under His lordship in prayer: naming Him King over it, and returning your attention there each time it drifts. Seeking things above is a practice before it is a disposition. It is built by redirected attention, one pressure at a time, until the mind is set where the life is hidden (==Colossians 3:2==).
