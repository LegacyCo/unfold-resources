## Commentary
The disciples wake Jesus with a desperate plea: “Lord, save us! We are perishing!” (==Matthew 8:25==).

Mark preserves an additional detail in this story that Matthew leaves out. According to Mark, they wake Jesus and say, “Teacher, *do You not care* that we are perishing?” (==Mark 4:38==).

The disciples are not only afraid of the storm. _They are beginning to question Jesus because of the storm._

“Don't You care?”

It is one of the most painfully human questions in Scripture.

Jesus is in the boat with them. They have already watched Him cleanse a leper (==Matthew 8:2–3==), heal the centurion’s servant with a word (==Matthew 8:5–13==), heal Peter’s mother-in-law (==Matthew 8:14–15==), and deliver the demonized and heal the sick (==Matthew 8:16==).

They have plenty of evidence that Jesus is powerful. What they are suddenly questioning is whether He _cares._

Because Jesus is asleep in the middle of their crisis.

His apparent inactivity is interpreted as indifference.

That is often what fear does. When God does not respond in the way or timing we expect, we are tempted to interpret His silence as His absence and His apparent inactivity as His lack of concern. The circumstances become our evidence for what we believe about God.

But Jesus sleeping in the boat does not mean He doesn't care about the disciples. It means He is not afraid of the things they are afraid of.

The storm has not altered His identity, His authority, or His intentions toward them. In fact, the safest place those disciples could possibly be was exactly where they were: _in the boat with Jesus._

This is particularly important because Jesus is the reason they are on the water in the first place. He had given the command to “depart to the other side” (==Matthew 8:18==), and the disciples “followed Him” into the boat (==Matthew 8:23==).

Their obedience led them directly into a storm. The storm was not evidence that they had missed God. And Jesus sleeping was not evidence that He had stopped caring. They were exactly where following Jesus had taken them.

## Application
Storms have a way of turning questions about our circumstances into questions about God’s character.

_Why hasn’t He done something? Why is He silent? Does He even care?_

The disciples made the mistake we often make: they interpreted Jesus through the storm instead of interpreting the storm through what they already knew about Jesus. 

Be careful not to let a difficult season rewrite what you know about the heart of God. You may not understand why He has not changed the circumstances. But the character of Jesus is not determined by the condition of the sea.

And remember: _Jesus is still in the boat._

His presence does not always mean there will be no storm. But it does means the storm does not get the final word.
