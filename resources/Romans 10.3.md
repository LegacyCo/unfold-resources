## Commentary
Paul identifies one of the central problems with Israel's pursuit of righteousness. They were genuinely zealous for God, but their zeal was misdirected because they misunderstood how a person becomes righteous before Him. Rather than receiving the righteousness God had provided through Jesus, they were attempting to establish their own.

The Greek word translated "establish" is *histēmi*, which means to stand, set up, or cause something to stand. Paul is describing people who were attempting to establish a righteous standing before God on the basis of their own obedience to the Law — their own ability to stand before Him. They were not necessarily indifferent toward God or unconcerned with holiness. Quite the opposite. They were deeply committed to doing what they believed God required of them. 

Paul understood this struggle personally. Before encountering Christ, he had devoted his life to the meticulous observance of the Law. He could describe himself as blameless concerning the righteousness found in the Law (==Philippians 3:6==). Yet after encountering Jesus, his entire understanding of righteousness changed. He writes that he desires to "be found in Him, not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness which is from God by faith" (==Philippians 3:9==).

Paul didn't exchange one set of religious requirements for another. He exchanges an entire way of understanding how he stands before God. His confidence is no longer in what he has accomplished through obedience, but in what Christ has accomplished on his behalf. Righteousness is something he has received through his union with Christ.

This reveals something important about the nature of faith. Faith requires us to abandon the idea that we can stand before God on our own merits. It requires us to acknowledge that even our best efforts cannot accomplish what Christ has already accomplished for us. This reorients our relationship with obedience. Rather than obeying God in order to become righteous (which is what Paul says the unbelieving Jews were still doing), we obey because we *have* been made righteous in Christ. Our obedience is the fruit of our new identity, not the means by which we obtain it...the overflow of love for the God who gave everything to draw us near.
