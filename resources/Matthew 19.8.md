## Commentary
“He said to them, ‘Moses, because of the hardness of your hearts, permitted you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so.’”

The Pharisees came asking about the legality of divorce, and Jesus answered with a distinction that reaches far beyond marriage: the difference between what God permits and what God intends. Moses *permitted* divorce—there was a lawful provision for it—but the provision existed because of hardness of heart, not because it reflected God's design. For the design, Jesus goes back past Moses to the beginning: one man, one woman, one flesh, joined by God (==Matthew 19:4-6==). Divine permission is not divine intention. God, in mercy, makes accommodation for hard hearts, and the accommodation can even carry His blessing—but it remains an accommodation, and He never revises the original design to match it.

That distinction has a wide application. There may be a great deal in the life of God's people that He permits, tolerates, and even graciously uses, about which He would say the same sentence: from the beginning it was not so. The presence of God's mercy on an arrangement is not proof of God's design in it. The way back, in marriage or in anything else, is always the same direction Jesus pointed the Pharisees: back to the beginning, back to what He designed before hard hearts required permission (==Genesis 2:24==).
