## Commentary
"...until I find a place for the LORD, a dwelling place for the Mighty One of Jacob."

This is David's vow: "I will not give sleep to my eyes or slumber to my eyelids, until I find a place for the LORD" (==Psalm 132:4-5==). He's the anointed king. He's got his own house of cedar. And he can't rest—because God doesn't have one (==2 Samuel 7:2==).

David isn't asking God for a blessing. He's refusing his own rest until *God* has rest. A few verses later he prays it out loud: "Arise, O LORD, to Your resting place" (==Psalm 132:8==). The whole psalm is a man consumed with one thing—not his throne, not his legacy, but a dwelling place for the Presence.

That's a different posture than most of our praying. We tend to ask God for a visitation. David was after a *habitation*. We ask Him to come and move among us; David wanted Him to come and *stay*. And notice the title he uses: "the Mighty One of Jacob"—the God who met Jacob at Bethel, where a stone became the house of God (==Genesis 28:22==). David wanted to build what Jacob had only glimpsed.

God answered by flipping the vow. David said, "I'll build You a house." God said, "No—I will build *you* a house" (==2 Samuel 7:11==), a house that turned out to be Christ Himself, and through Him, a people—living stones built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit (==Ephesians 2:22==). God still wants a resting place. And it's still not a building. It's us—joined together as family, holding His glory, His way.

So make David's vow ours. Lord, we give You no rest until You establish Your place of rest. Not a house we build and ask You to bless, but a house You build and choose to fill.
