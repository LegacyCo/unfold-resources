## Commentary
“Your fathers, including Terah, the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor, dwelt on the other side of the River in old times; and they served other gods.”

Joshua's summary of Israel's history begins with an uncomfortable fact: the father of the faithful came from a household of idolaters. Abram grew up in Ur of the Chaldeans—in the region of the Tigris and Euphrates, in the same country as Babel, due east of the place that would become Jerusalem. The god of his fathers was not the LORD. His worship was the worship of many gods, and pagan worship of that kind lived in uncertainty: a worshiper never knew whether his gods were pleased or angry, so there was always more to be done to stay on their good side—up to and including the sacrifice of children, a practice that makes the later test on Mount Moriah all the more striking (==Genesis 22:2==).

Then a God Abram had never heard of came to him in that land. The LORD was not one of the gods of Ur. He revealed Himself to a pagan, spoke to him, and called him out (==Genesis 12:1==). Abram did not find God; God found Abram. Stephen says the same before the Sanhedrin: “The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia” (==Acts 7:2==). This is the beginning of every relationship with the true God—not a man's search arriving at heaven, but heaven's initiative arriving at a man. Joshua tells Israel where they came from so that they will know that everything they have was given, not earned: “I took your father Abraham from the other side of the River” (==Joshua 24:3==).
