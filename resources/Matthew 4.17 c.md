## Commentary
“Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.” Repentance is not feeling bad about what you’ve done and promising to do better next time. It is the realization that the whole way you learned to live must be retrained by the King—that everything you thought you knew about life has to be re-learned inside His kingdom.

Many people assume being a Christian means learning to do the old life better. But Jesus did not come to make us more skilled at the old life. He brought an entirely new way of being human. He brought a whole new world, and now He teaches us how to live inside it.

This is why the old life cannot inform the new one. The survival instincts of the old kingdom are counterproductive in following this King: self-preservation will not teach you how to deny yourself. Retaliation will not teach you how to forgive. Ambition will not teach you how to become a servant. Fear will not teach you how to walk by faith. New wine cannot be poured into old wineskins (==Luke 5:37-38==).

So we cannot simply add Jesus to the life we already had and call it Christianity. His kingdom has come, and it confronts the old life, exposes it, and invites it to the cross—so that we can become born-again citizens of a new world (==Romans 12:2==).
