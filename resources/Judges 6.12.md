## Commentary
“And the Angel of the LORD appeared to him, and said to him, ‘The LORD is with you, you mighty man of valor!’”

Israel had been overrun by Midian for seven years. Every harvest, the Midianites came up like locusts, stripped the land, and left. And the result was the people of God hiding in caves (==Judges 6:1-2==). That's where the story finds Gideon: threshing wheat in a winepress. You don't thresh wheat in a winepress—a winepress is a pit dug into rock. Wheat is threshed on a hilltop, out in the open, where the wind can carry the chaff away. Gideon is down in a hole, hoping nobody sees him. And the Angel of the LORD greets this man with, “you mighty man of valor.” There is nothing valiant about him.

Gideon's answers tell us exactly how Gideon sees himself. First the bitterness: “if the LORD is with us, why then has all this happened to us?” (==Judges 6:13==). Then the disqualified résumé: “my clan is the *weakest* in Manasseh, and I am the *least* in my father's house” (==Judges 6:15==). Weakest. Least. Hiding. And God does not argue with any of it. He simply answers, “Surely I will be with you, and you shall defeat the Midianites as one man” (==Judges 6:16==). Before Gideon had done anything to earn the identity of valiant warrior, God spoke that identity over him—and the rest of the story is God teaching Gideon how to live up to the name he had already been given. That's the order of things in the kingdom: identity first, evidence second. God names Abram “father of a multitude” while he is still childless (==Genesis 17:5==), calls Peter a rock while he is still unstable (==Matthew 16:18==), and calls a man in a hole a warrior. He speaks over His people who they are in His sight, and then grows them into the name.
