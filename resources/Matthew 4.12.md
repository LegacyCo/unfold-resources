## Commentary
I heard someone put it this way: the kingdom of God doesn’t arrive like the 82nd Airborne Division. It comes like leaven. Its arrival is like a seed going into the ground. The Kingdom is here with persuasion not brute force. 

This kingdom, which is the kingdom to rule all other kingdoms, is not like any other kingdom.

Matthew makes that clear for us in a very subtle way, “When Jesus heard that John had been put in prison, He departed for Galilee.”

The kingdom is at hand, but good people are still experiencing bad things. 

The lesson for us is if we’re not careful, we end up imposing our ideas of what God's kingdom should be like. This happened to John when he was thrown in prison. He expected the king to overthrow Rome. Instead, he winds up in a jail cell sending his disciples to ask Jesus if He really is the Messiah (see ==Matthew 11:2-6== and ==Luke 7:18-23==).

The mystery of _this_ kingdom is that it overthrows all other kingdoms by offering up its King as a sacrifice. Other kings offer their people as a sacrifice. Not this kingdom. And in doing this, the King shows us what it’s like to live in His kingdom.

Because the Kingdom of God comes differently than we expect, we have to let Jesus redefine what it means to be Christian.

## Application
Many people think being a Christian means learning how to live our life *better*. Jesus is seen as a kind of teacher who helps us improve the instrument we already kind of know how to play. As if we’ve been handed a piano and Jesus is teaching us how to play Mozart and Beethoven.

But that’s not what Jesus came to do. Jesus did not come to help us become more skilled at the old life. He brought an entirely new instrument. A new sound. A new rhythm. A new way of being human. He brought a whole new world. And now He’s teaching us how to live inside *His* world.

That means the way we’ve always lived can’t inform us on how to live in His Kingdom. The survival instincts of the old kingdom are actually counterproductive in helping us follow this King. Self-preservation will not teach you how to deny yourself. Retaliation will not teach you how to forgive. Ambition will not teach you how to become a servant. Fear will not teach you how to walk by faith.

This is why Jesus says, “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”

Repentance is not you feeling bad about what you’ve done and promising to do better next time. Repentance is realizing the whole way you learned to live has to be retrained by the King. Everything you’ve thought about life has to be re-learned.

You can’t just add Jesus to the life you already had and call it “Christianity.” That's like trying to play Chopin with a bag of Cheetos.

His Kingdom doesn’t come to improve your old life. It confronts that life, exposes that life, and invites that life to the cross where it can be surrendered.

So when we say the Kingdom has come, but not like we expected, we mean this: Jesus did not come to endorse the world you and I are trying our best to live within. He came to bring us into an entirely different world that only God can show us how to live in.
