## Commentary
Paul now brings us to the cross. God put Christ forward publicly as the place where sin would be judged and mercy would be given. 

Paul uses the Greek word *hilastērion* in this word, which we translate "propitiation." Hilastērion carries the idea of a place or means by which wrath is dealt with, sin is covered, and mercy is made available. It is a loaded word. It draws from the world of sacrifice, atonement, and the mercy seat in the tabernacle.

In the Greek translation of the Old Testament, the hilastērion was the word used for the mercy seat — the gold cover of the ark of the covenant where blood was sprinkled on the Day of Atonement.

In Leviticus 16, once a year, the high priest entered the Holy of Holies and sprinkled blood on and before the mercy seat. Beneath that mercy seat were the tablets of the Law — the very commandments Israel had broken. Above it was the place where God’s presence was manifested. So the mercy seat stood between the broken law and the holy presence of God.

Blood was brought there because sin had created a real problem. God’s justice had to be satisfied. Sin had to be judged. Guilt had to be covered. 

So when Paul says God put Jesus forward as the hilastērion, he is saying something staggering:

Jesus is now the true mercy seat.

He is the place where sin is judged and mercy is released. 

This is why “propitiation” is such an important word. It's not just forgiveness. It means the righteous wrath of God that was aimed at you and I has found it's target in Christ. And anyone now coming to Him for forgiveness finds in Him the propitiation they could never be for themselves.

That is why Paul immediately says this was done “to show God’s righteousness.” The cross shows that God has upheld His holiness while making a way to justify sinners.
