## Commentary
Faith does not mean a list of things we agree with. Bill Hull writes in "The Complete Book of Discipleship," "The church is up to its eyeballs in agreement, but that moves nothing and poses a threat to no one." 

The blind men in Matthew 9 didn't just agree that Jesus possessed the power to heal. They followed Him, cried out to Him, entered the house with Him, and placed their need entirely into His hands. Their faith moved them toward Jesus and positioned them to receive from Him. When Jesus asked them, “Do you believe that I am able to do this?” He was not testing their theological vocabulary but shining a light on the confidence of their hearts.

“According to your faith” doesn't mean that faith is a force by which we control God or guarantee whatever outcome we desire. Faith is not confidence in our own believing; it is confidence in the character and ability of Jesus. It takes what Christ has said and acts as though He can be trusted. Agreement can remain safely within the mind, leaving our habits, decisions, and loyalties untouched. Faith crosses the distance between what we confess and how we live.

The world is not threatened by a church that merely agrees with Jesus. It is confronted by a people who trust Him enough to obey Him, follow Him, risk for Him, and depend upon Him. Faith becomes visible when our lives begin to take the shape of the One we claim to believe.

## Application
We must restore the powerful, history-making original meaning of faith; that mysterious attribute which creates action. Restoring faith back to what Jesus means when He says, "Let it be done to you according to your faith" puts the power back in the gospel.
