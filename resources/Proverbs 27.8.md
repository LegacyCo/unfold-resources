## Commentary
“Like a bird that wanders from its nest is a man who wanders from his place.”

A wandering bird isn’t necessarily lost. It can still fly, still sing, still look impressive gliding from branch to branch. But it has no nest—no place where it’s known, fed, protected, and building something. It’s covering ground without belonging anywhere. And the proverb says: that’s what a person looks like who wanders from their place.

Apply that to the way many believers relate to church. There’s a whole style of Christian life that never lands: sampling this church for its worship and that one for its preaching, drifting on when something disappoints, always visiting and never joining. Lots of branches, no nest. But kingdom people are *planted* people. “Those who are planted in the house of the LORD shall flourish in the courts of our God” (==Psalm 92:13==)—and notice, the flourishing is promised to the planted, not the perched. A tree only bears fruit where its roots have had time to go deep (==Psalm 1:3==).

The early church had a word for this rootedness: *devoted*. “They devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and prayer” (==Acts 2:42==). Devoted people don’t just listen to sermons—they sit under teaching in a family where they’re known. 

So find your place—and then do the harder thing: stay in it. The nest is where the next generation gets raised, where you’re fed when you’re too weak to hunt, where you’re missed when you don’t come home.
