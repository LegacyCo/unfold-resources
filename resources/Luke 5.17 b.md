## Commentary
“There were Pharisees and teachers of the law sitting by, who had come out of every village of Galilee, Judea, and Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was present to heal.”

They had traveled from everywhere to hear Jesus. They occupied the room, listened to His teaching, and witnessed the power of God—but Luke describes them as “sitting by.”

They sat by the Word without placing themselves under the Word.

The Pharisees had not come hungry. They had come to observe, analyze, and determine whether Jesus agreed with what they had already decided was true. Prideful religion will enter an atmosphere where truth is being preached while never allowing the truth to preach to it.

It is possible to become so familiar with Scripture that we use it to evaluate everyone else. We hear a sermon and immediately think of the person who needed to hear it. We study the Bible to strengthen conclusions we have already reached. We sit close enough to Jesus to hear every word while remaining protected from anything He might expose in us.

Meanwhile, outside the house, a paralyzed man and his friends were tearing through the roof because they believed one encounter with Jesus could change everything.

The religious leaders had proximity without hunger. The men on the roof had hunger without access. And hunger found a way into the room.

The power of the Lord was present, but presence alone did not guarantee transformation. The same atmosphere contained critics who remained unchanged and a helpless man who left carrying the bed that once carried him.

The difference was not how close they sat to Jesus. It was how they approached Him.

## Application
How do you approach the Word of God?

Do you come to confirm what you already believe, evaluate the preacher, and identify what everyone else needs to change? Or do you come willing to be searched, confronted, healed, and remade?

Do not settle for sitting by the Word. Place yourself under it.
