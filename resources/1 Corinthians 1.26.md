## Commentary
“For you see your calling, brethren, that not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called.”

Abraham is the original case study. When God called him, Abram was a seventy-five-year-old man, married to a barren woman, living in a godless, pagan city (==Genesis 12:4==; ==Joshua 24:2==). Why would God choose such an unlikely person to be the father of nations? Paul gives the reason: “God has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise… and the things which are not, to bring to nothing the things that are, that no flesh should glory in His presence” (==1 Corinthians 1:27-29==). The age, the barrenness, the deception of idol worship—Abraham was exactly the man God could use to show the world what He can do with an empty vessel of yielded obedience and faith.

God is not looking for the most talented, the smartest, the most powerful, or the wealthiest. He is looking for the yielded, the trusting, the faithful, the obedient, the empty. He desires all to be saved (==1 Timothy 2:4==), but something about this gospel is routinely rejected by the comfortable. Share the gospel door to door in the wealthiest neighborhood you can find, then do the same in the poorest neighborhood in your city, and note the difference in reception. Jesus said plainly how hard it is for the wealthy to enter the kingdom (==Matthew 19:23==)—not because God withholds it from them, but because they are already full, and God fills empty things. He uses the weak things of this world to demonstrate that He is all-sufficient, doing the impossible through anyone with faith to believe He is who He says He is. Are you empty and surrendered to God? Then you have the qualifications of a world-changer.
