## Commentary
“…God our Savior, who desires all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.”

Sit with the logic of that verse for a moment. God desires *all* to be saved. All are not yet saved. Which means God is currently desiring something in our generation that He does not yet have. There is an unsatisfied longing in the heart of God—and that longing is the engine of the church’s mission.

This is the same heartbeat God revealed through Ezekiel: “I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live. Turn, turn from your evil ways! For why should you die?” (==Ezekiel 33:11==). The word is the language of desire and delight—what God takes pleasure in. What He takes pleasure in is not judgment falling; it’s the wicked turning and living. And right in the middle of God’s desire and man’s free will stands the church, entrusted with making the way of life known.

Here’s why this matters for how we witness. Is the world lost and headed for a Christless eternity? Yes—“all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God” (==Romans 3:23==), and Jesus was clear about the stakes (==John 8:24==). But your neighbor being on their way to hell is not what gives the gospel its power. The longing in God’s heart does. We don’t share our faith primarily because the world is desperate; we share it because *God* is desirous—because the Father wants His children home, and every salvation satisfies something in Him. John Piper put it this way: “Missions exists because worship doesn’t.” Until every person in your community worships Jesus as Savior and Lord, there is still a longing in God’s heart—and still a mission for His people.

So let this reframe your next gospel conversation. You’re not a recruiter working a quota, and you’re not motivated only by rescue. You are a son or daughter helping the Father obtain the desire of His heart. That’s a mission you can carry with joy instead of dread—because it starts and ends not with the badness of the world, but with the goodness of the God who wants all of it back.
