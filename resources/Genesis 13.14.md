## Commentary
“And the LORD said to Abram, after Lot had separated from him: ‘Lift your eyes now and look from the place where you are—northward, southward, eastward, and westward.’”

The wording is deliberate. Lot “lifted his eyes” on his own initiative and took what he saw (==Genesis 13:10==). Abram is *told* to lift his eyes—by the LORD, after Lot had separated from him. Same action, opposite order. Lot’s lifted eyes led to self-chosen gain; Abram’s lifted eyes came as God’s response to self-chosen loss. Abram had just surrendered his right to the best land, and now God shows him all of it—north, south, east, and west, including the plain Lot had taken—and says, “all the land which you see I give to you and your descendants forever” (==Genesis 13:15==).

This is the kingdom principle Abram had tapped into: rather than exalting themselves and pushing their way into the best this world offers, the meek wait on the Lord and allow Him to do the exalting. “Wait on the LORD, and keep His way, and He shall exalt you to inherit the land” (==Psalm 37:34==). By sacrificing his claim on the altar of Lot’s choice, Abram’s meekness guaranteed that the promised land would be his. He may have failed the test of famine, and he may have failed the test of Pharaoh, but he passed the test with Lot—and the timing of God’s word here, “after Lot had separated from him,” marks the connection. Satan wants to use circumstances, people, and possessions to bring out the worst in us; God uses the same things to refine us and form the best in us. What Lot grasped, he lost. What Abram released, God gave him forever.
