## Commentary
Adam is given two responsibilities: to serve God in the garden and to rule over the earth (Gen 1:26). As Peter Leithart wrote in *A House For My Name*, God divided creation into two realms—garden and *not* garden. In the garden, Adam meets with God and worships Him as a priest. In the world outside the garden, Adam is to rule and subdue as king.

The Hebrew verb "abad" describes Adam's assignment in the garden. This word has interesting parallels to the priestly duties. The word is built on two Hebrew verbs meaning to work, to serve, to keep, to observe, or to guard. When these words are arranged the way they are arranged here in God's instructions to Adam, the words directly relate to the duties of the priests in later books. They parallel one another, as Michael Miller points out in *His House, His Presence*, which again leads us to conclude that Adam was a priest in the sanctuary of Eden. Man wasn't just tasked with gardening a plot of land, he was first appointed to protect and guard the sacred space of Eden.
