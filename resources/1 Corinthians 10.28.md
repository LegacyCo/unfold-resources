## Commentary
“But if anyone says to you, ‘This was offered to idols,’ do not eat it for the sake of the one who told you, and for conscience' sake.”

Paul has just given the Corinthians complete freedom. Eat whatever is sold in the meat market without asking questions, “for the earth is the LORD's, and all its fullness”; if an unbeliever invites you to dinner, eat whatever is set before you (==1 Corinthians 10:25-27==). Nothing is unclean in itself (==Romans 14:14==). The believer's freedom in Christ removes any fear that meat once offered to demons could harm him.

Then comes the restriction. If someone tells you the meat was offered to idols, do not eat it, *for the sake of the one who told you*. The restriction comes when a believer's freedom would cause someone else to stumble. “All things are lawful for me, but not all things are helpful” (==1 Corinthians 10:23==). The law of love restricts liberty so that liberty does not harm someone less mature in the faith: “if your brother is grieved because of your food, you are no longer walking in love. Do not destroy with your food the one for whom Christ died” (==Romans 14:15==). The restriction does something the freedom alone could not: it creates an opportunity for the believer to demonstrate love. The covenant family of God enjoys the freedom it has been given and understands the beauty of a restriction accepted for someone else's sake.

## Application
Identify a freedom you hold that a weaker brother or sister struggles with—what you drink, watch, spend, or say—and practice setting it aside in their presence without resentment. Your liberty is not diminished by restraint; it is completed by it. Ask, before exercising a freedom, who is at the table with you and what your freedom will do to their conscience.
