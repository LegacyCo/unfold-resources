## Commentary
This is the first of three times in the remainder of Romans 1 where Paul uses the language of God “giving them up” or “giving them over” (Romans 1:24, 26, 28). Paul is showing us that the judgment of God is not always seen in God actively striking someone down. Sometimes His judgment is seen in Him allowing people to have what they have insisted on having.

This reveals something significant about the nature of God. God does not force obedience. He does not create people for communion with Himself and then coerce them into love. He reveals Himself and invites us to walk with Him, but He does not violate the will in order to produce worship. Love cannot be forced. Obedience that has to be coerced is not the obedience of faith.

In Romans 1, humanity has exchanged the glory of God for idols. They have suppressed the truth, refused gratitude, and chosen created things over the Creator. So Paul says, “God gave them up.” In other words, God allowed their desires to become their master. He handed them over to the very cravings they had chosen in place of Him.

That is a terrifying form of judgment.

It means one of the most dangerous things God can do is let a person have the life they keep demanding apart from Him. Sin always presents itself as freedom, but when God gives someone over to it, it becomes bondage. The desire that promised satisfaction becomes the appetite that governs them.

This pattern is seen all over Scripture. Psalm 81:11–12 says, “But my people did not listen to my voice; Israel would not submit to me. So I *gave them over* to their stubborn hearts, to follow their own counsels.” God’s people refused His voice, so He gave them over to the path they had chosen. The judgment was not that God withheld every desire. The judgment was that He allowed their stubborn desires to lead them.

But, praise God, the opposite is also true. God gives more of Himself to those who seek Him. Jeremiah 29:13 says, “You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart.” James 4:8 says, “Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.” Jesus says in Matthew 7:7–8, “Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you.” God is not reluctant to be found. He gives Himself to those who want Him.

This is seen in people like Moses, who asked to see God’s glory and was met by God’s self-revelation (==Exodus 33:18–19==). It is seen in David, who sought the Lord as his one desire, “to gaze upon the beauty of the Lord” (==Psalm 27:4==). It is seen in Mary of Bethany, who sat at the feet of Jesus and was commended because she had chosen “the good portion” (==Luke 10:39–42==).

God gives people over to what they truly desire.

To those who desire sin, He will eventually give them over to the rule of sin. To those who desire Him, He gives more grace, more fellowship, more light. God responds to the heart that wants Him, because He Himself is the one who awakens that desire and invites it to come.

## Application
Romans 1:24 should sober us. Desire that is out of alignment with our design to be in fellowship with God is not harmless. If we continue to choose inferior things, there may come a point where God allows that choice to run its course. But the same truth should also awaken hope. God is not only the God who gives the wicked over to their cravings. He is the God who gives Himself to those who seek Him.

The question underneath this verse is, “What do I really want?” Because one of the clearest signs of judgment is being handed over to the carnal desires that replace God. And one of the clearest signs of mercy is a heart that wants only Him.
