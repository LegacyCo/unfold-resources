## Commentary
“Then they said to one another, ‘Come, let us make bricks and bake them thoroughly.’ They had brick for stone, and they had asphalt for mortar.”

There were no stones on the plain of Shinar, so the people made their own. They were intelligent and resourceful: they took the clay of the earth, shaped it, baked it, and produced a substitute for stone. Then they used the elements of this world to build something intended to touch God's world—a tower “whose top is in the heavens” (==Genesis 11:4==). This is what religion does. Man builds a system that makes sense to him, out of the resources he has at his disposal, and expects that system to do the impossible: bridge the chasm between his world and God's. Every man-made approach to God is brick—manufactured on earth, out of earth, and aimed at heaven.

Man makes bricks; God makes stones. Man builds his house out of what he can manufacture; God builds His house out of living stones (==1 Peter 2:5==). When God gave instructions for His altar, He forbade cut stone altogether—“if you lift your tool on it, you have profaned it” (==Exodus 20:25==)—because He does not want His house made of man's shaping. And the bridge between heaven and earth that Babel tried to build was never going to be constructed from below. It was given from above: a ladder set up on the earth with its top reaching to heaven (==Genesis 28:12==), fulfilled in the Son of Man on whom the angels ascend and descend (==John 1:51==). God's house is built His way, with His materials, or it is not His house.

## Application
Examine what you are building with. Programs, strategies, and methods borrowed from the world's way of doing things are bricks—uniform, efficient, and manufactured—and they can produce something impressive that God did not build. Ask of any effort in ministry, family, or personal devotion: is this a living stone God has formed, or a brick I have baked because it was faster? Refuse to do things the world's way and call it holy. Build His house with what He supplies.
