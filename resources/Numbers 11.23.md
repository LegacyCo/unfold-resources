## Commentary
God had just promised to provide enough meat to feed the entire nation of Israel for a full month, and Moses responded by questioning how such a thing could possibly happen. He even reminded God that there were six hundred thousand men, in case God had somehow forgotten the details of the situation.

Think about who is asking this question. This is Moses, the man who had witnessed the ten plagues of Egypt, watched God divide the Red Sea, seen water come from a rock, and experienced the miraculous provision of manna every morning. He had witnessed some of the most extraordinary demonstrations of God's power in human history. Yet when God promised to do something beyond what Moses could calculate or comprehend, he actually questioned whether God could accomplish it.

God's response is remarkable: “Has the LORD's arm been shortened? Now you shall see whether what I say will happen to you or not.”

In other words, had God's power somehow diminished? Had the God who delivered Israel from Egypt suddenly become incapable of providing meat in the wilderness? Was there now some limitation upon His ability that had not existed before?

The Hebrew imagery of a shortened hand or arm speaks of diminished power or an inability to act. God uses similar language in ==Isaiah 50:2==, asking, “Is My hand shortened at all that it cannot redeem? Or have I no power to deliver?” Later, in ==Isaiah 59:1==, the prophet declares, “Behold, the LORD's hand is not shortened, that it cannot save.”

What makes Moses' response so revealing is that he was evaluating God's promise according to the resources he could see rather than the power of the One who had spoken. Moses could count the people, calculate the available livestock, and recognize the impossibility of feeding such a multitude. What he struggled to account for was the unlimited ability of God to accomplish what He had promised.

Moses was not questioning whether God existed or whether He had demonstrated His power in the past. He was struggling to believe that the God who had done extraordinary things before could do something equally extraordinary in his present circumstances.

And isn't this something we can all relate to? We can look back over years of God's faithfulness, recount answered prayers, remember extraordinary provisions, and testify to His goodness. Yet when we encounter a new challenge that exceeds our resources or understanding, we can find ourselves asking the same question Moses asked: How could God possibly do this?

We sometimes allow the size of our present circumstances to overshadow the magnitude of God's past faithfulness. We begin calculating what is possible according to what we possess rather than considering who God is.

There is an interesting parallel in ==Psalm 78:19–20==, where Israel's unbelief is expressed in almost identical terms: “Can God prepare a table in the wilderness? ... Can He give bread also? Can He provide meat for His people?” The very question that characterized Israel's unbelief was now, in effect, being raised by Moses himself.

Yet God did exactly what He had promised. He sent a wind that brought quail from the sea, providing an extraordinary abundance of meat (==Numbers 11:31==). The provision was also accompanied by judgment upon the people's sinful cravings, reminding us that God's ability to accomplish something does not mean every human desire reflects His will.

## Application
When God has genuinely spoken, we must resist the temptation to measure His promises by our present limitations. Faith does not require us to pretend that our circumstances are easy or that our resources are sufficient. It requires us to recognize that neither of those things determines God's ability to fulfill His word. He is, and always will be, the God who does exceedingly, abundantly beyond anything we could ask or imagine (==Ephesians 3:20==).
