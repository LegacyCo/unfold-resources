## Commentary
Throughout Romans 8, Paul appears to draw upon one of Scripture’s great foundational stories: the Exodus. “For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.” The language of being _led_ by the divine presence recalls Israel being led out of slavery, through the wilderness, and toward their inheritance. God went before Israel in the pillar of cloud and fire (==Exodus 13:21–22==), and later Scripture describes that same wilderness journey in explicitly Spirit-centered language: “You also gave Your good Spirit to instruct them” (==Nehemiah 9:20==). Isaiah similarly remembers the Exodus by asking, “Where is He who put His Holy Spirit within them?” and then says that “the Spirit of the LORD caused him to rest” (==Isaiah 63:11–14==).

Paul is telling the story of God’s people as a kind of new Exodus. They have been delivered from a greater slavery—not just from Pharaoh, but from sin, death, and “the law of sin and death” (==Romans 8:2==). They have passed through the waters of baptism into Christ (==Romans 6:3–4==), and they are now being led by the Spirit toward the inheritance God has prepared for His children.

This makes Paul’s movement from the Spirit’s leading in verse 14 to sonship and inheritance in verses 15–17 especially significant. Israel had already been called God’s “son” during the first Exodus: “Israel is My son, My firstborn” (==Exodus 4:22==). Now those who belong to the Messiah receive “the Spirit of adoption,” by whom they cry, “Abba, Father” (==Romans 8:15==). The Spirit who leads them is also the Spirit who testifies that they are God’s children, and “if children, then heirs—heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ” (==Romans 8:16–17==).

But the inheritance Paul has in view is larger than a single strip of land. The promised inheritance has expanded until it fills the world.

So when Paul says that the sons of God are “led by the Spirit,” he is saying far more than that Christians should receive occasional inward guidance about individual decisions. The Spirit is leading an Exodus people. He is bringing those whom God has liberated from slavery through the wilderness of the present age and toward their inheritance. He is forming them as sons and daughters, conforming them to the image of the Son (==Romans 8:29==), and preparing them to share in the renewal and rule of God’s creation.

The destination of the Spirit’s leading is nothing less than _glory_.

N. T. Wright describes Romans 8 as “the richest, deepest, and most powerfully sustained climax anywhere in the literature of the early church, and perhaps anywhere else.” Paul is gathering together Exodus, adoption, inheritance, creation, suffering, resurrection, and humanity’s original vocation into one sweeping vision: the God who brought Israel out of Egypt is once again leading His children out of slavery—and this time the inheritance awaiting them is the renewed creation itself.
