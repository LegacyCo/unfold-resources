## Commentary
Peter does the same thing here that Paul does in Ephesians 4:1: because of *this* our response is now *that*...

Peter tells us in v4 that we are "partakers of the divine nature." He then says because of this grace, we are then to respond a certain way. But the response is rooted in the identity of who we are. Because you are recipients of the divine nature, you now have permission to live like Jesus. And if you're going to live like Jesus, these are the types of traits that need to be yours in abundance: excellence of character, knowledge (gnosis - knowledge that comes from perceiving with the senses, experiential knowledge), self-control, perseverance, piety, brotherly kindness, and love. 

Peter begins his train of thought by saying "add to your faith excellence of character." Faith is the one thing every believer has in common. Without faith there is no salvation, there is no born again reality that connects us to the divine nature. But faith doesn't equal excellence of character. Some believers never progress past the starting line of faith. They have divine *nature* but they still have the *character* of Adam. How can this be? Being a follower of Jesus doesn't mean we are now holy robots who never fall into temptation. It's the purity of the divine nature in us that sounds the alarm when anything carnal is taken in or participated in. 

For example; have you ever been grieved over your sin? That's the presence of the divine nature in you mourning the loss of purity in that moment. The presence of the Holy Spirit inside of you, the divine nature, recognizes that something is trying to contaminate your holiness, your distinction...and the Spirit in you is making the announcement, through the response of sorrow, "We are no longer of this kind!"

Peter then says if these things are yours, and abound in you, you will neither be barren (useless, idle, lazy) or unfruitful (unproductive). Translation: when you are who God now says you are, you will not only love Him and yourself, you will be *productive*. You will have an effect. You will make an impact. You will walk in the works that have been prepared for you (Ephesians 2:10).

## Application
The order of all of this, like in Ephesians, is important: identity first, *then* purpose. If purpose comes first, it looks like religious striving and legalism. 

But we can't respond to *that* error with another error; the error of sloppy grace that has no self-discipline, lazy love that only receives, starting-line-living that never presses on and pursues God for more (Philippians 3:12-14, 1 Timothy 6.:12). Because you are now partakers of the divine nature, a nature that is limitless in its glory, a nature that has no bound to its holiness and righteousness, because this is now who you are, *abound* in the things that are boundless. *Increase* in the things that are limitless. Press on and pursue what is yours because of who you are.
