## Commentary
Have you ever wondered what God’s will is for your life? You’re not alone—it may be the most common question pastors are asked. And here’s the surprise: ==Ephesians 1:9-10== answers it outright. Paul says God “made known to us the mystery of His will”—that in the fullness of the times He would “gather together in one all things in Christ.”

That’s the answer. God’s will for your life is that you would be *gathered*. Gathered out of the world. Out of your sin and separation from Him. Out of your wrong ideas about who you are and who God is. Out of your trauma, your shame, your addiction—out of everything you inherited from Adam—and placed, together with everyone else He’s gathering, into union with Jesus forever. To put it simply: you were born to be with God. And not just *with* God—*in* God, your very finite life animated by His divine life.

This is the center of gravity in Ephesians. In this one twelve-verse sentence, Paul says “in Him” or “in Christ” nine times; before the letter ends, he’ll say it about twenty-five. God has purposed that all things be found in Christ—and nothing of eternal value is found outside of Him (==Ephesians 1:10==).

Which reframes all the smaller questions we usually mean when we ask about God’s will. Where should I live? What should I do for a living? How do I raise my kids, spend my money, use my time? God’s will is that every one of those things would flow from—and come into order because of—the superior reality that you are in Christ. He rarely hands out life maps. He hands us Himself, and the map gets sorted inside the wonder of union.
