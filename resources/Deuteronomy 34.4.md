## Commentary
This is from "And They Crucified Him" by Art Katz: After forty years of guiding Israel through the wilderness, Moses was not permitted entry into the Land of Promise. All he could do was glimpse it from Mount Nebo, but he could not enter. To be denied that entry was a remarkable judgment. The sin that evoked that judgment was the vexation in his spirit whereby he hit the rock when he was told to speak to it (Numbers 20:12). God was faithful to see that the water flowed out, but God's indictment of Moses was as follows: "You did not treat Me as holy in the midst of the sons of Israel." In other words "By disobeying My instructions, you misrepresented My character to My people. You are identified as one with Me; therefore your conduct is a statement of Me, My nature, who I am.' In that act of disobedience, Moses neither honored God, sanctified Him. He failed to hallow God's name. 

## Application
It is possible to be used by God powerfully and still misrepresent Him.

That's what makes the judgment against Moses so sobering. Moses wasn't judged because he stopped serving God. He wasn't judged because he couldn't get water to flow from rock (see Numbers 20:1-12). The people still received what they needed. Outwardly, his ministry “worked.” But God was looking at something deeper than the outward result. He was looking at what Moses’ conduct declared about God to the people around him.

We are not free to represent God however we please, even when we are frustrated, provoked, exhausted, or under pressure. Moses struck the rock in anger when God had told him to speak to it. And in that act, he misrepresented God in his own frustration ("hear now, you rebels!") as harsh, irritated, and severe in a moment where God intended to reveal Himself differently. 

The same can so easily said for us. We think the issue is whether something was produced or not. Whether the prayer worked. Whether the sermon landed. Whether the need was met. But God is not *only* concerned with outcome. He's concerned with representation. He is jealous over how He is made known through those who bear His name.

If we do not sanctify Him in the sight of others by accurately demonstrating who He is, then even our fruitfulness can become a misrepresentation.

When you speak for God, handle Him carefully. When you act in His name, don't let irritation, woundedness, ambition, or immaturity put words in His mouth. To hallow His name is to represent Him rightly.
