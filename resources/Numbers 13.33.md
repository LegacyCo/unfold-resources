## Commentary
The spies have already called the people of Canaan "men of great stature" (==Numbers 13:32==). Now they go a step further: "There we saw the giants (the descendants of Anak came from the giants); and we were like grasshoppers in our own sight, and so we were in their sight."

The word translated "giants" is *Nephilim*, the same word used for the mighty men before the flood in ==Genesis 6:4==. It appears nowhere else in the Old Testament. Whatever the actual connection between the Anakim and the Nephilim, the spies reached for the most frightening word they had.

Then they described themselves. "We were like grasshoppers in our own sight." That part was an honest assessment. They felt small. But they kept exaggerating: "and so we were in their sight." Nobody in Canaan had told them that. They took their own fear and assumed their enemies saw them the same way.

They were wrong. Almost forty years later, Rahab told Joshua's spies, "the terror of you has fallen on us, and... all the inhabitants of the land are fainthearted because of you" (==Joshua 2:9==). The people of the land had been afraid of Israel ever since they heard what the LORD did at the Red Sea (==Joshua 2:10==).

Scripture uses the same Hebrew verb, *masas* ("to melt"), to describe Israel's fear of the Canaanites and the Canaanites' fear of Israel. When Moses retold this story, he said the spies "have discouraged our hearts" (==Deuteronomy 1:28==). Literally, they made our hearts *melt*. Rahab used that word for Jericho: "as soon as we heard these things, our hearts melted" (==Joshua 2:11==). Israel's hearts melted over the Canaanites while the Canaanites' hearts were melting over Israel. The difference was that Israel had God's promise, yet they chose to trust their feelings more than the word of God.

The grasshopper image shows up again in ==Isaiah 40:22==: "It is He who sits above the circle of the earth, and its inhabitants are like grasshoppers." Next to God, the Anakim were grasshoppers too. The spies measured themselves against their enemies, and by that measure they were small. But they never measured their enemies against God.

## Application
"We were like grasshoppers in our own sight." Before the Canaanites ever saw them, the spies had already decided who they were. They self-identified based on how they felt, and became someone God had never called them. The generation that called itself grasshoppers lived like grasshoppers, and they never entered the land (==Numbers 14:29–30==).

We can do the same thing. "I'm just not a disciplined person." "I'll always be the one who struggles with this." Say it long enough and it becomes the identity you live out of, even though God never gave it to you.

Israel was never what the spies said they were. God had already called them His son (==Exodus 4:22==) and "a special treasure to Me above all people" (==Exodus 19:5==). If you're in Christ, your identity comes from what God has said about you, not from the size of what's in front of you: "as He is, so are we in this world" (==1 John 4:17==).
