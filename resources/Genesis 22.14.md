## Commentary
“And Abraham called the name of the place, The-LORD-Will-Provide; as it is said to this day, ‘In the Mount of the LORD it shall be provided.’”

The mountain already had a name—Moriah (==Genesis 22:2==). But after Abraham passed his test, he renamed it. He re-identified the place of his testing, and the new name stuck: *The LORD Will Provide.* That’s what happens when God brings you through a test. You come down the mountain with something you didn’t carry up: the authority to re-identify situations. The place that was named for your trial gets renamed for His provision.

And this is the family trait of Abraham’s children, because the God Abraham believed is the God “who gives life to the dead and calls those things which do not exist as though they did” (==Romans 4:17==)—and His sons and daughters learn the family speech. The doctor says “stage 4,” but I say healed. The past says “addicted,” but I say freedom. The bank account says “empty,” but I say blessed. The child says “prodigal,” but I say as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord. That’s not positive thinking or denial of the facts. It’s the vocabulary of people who have watched God provide on the mountain and now speak from what they know of His character.

Jesus shows us the full-grown version of this faith. On the night He was betrayed—before the cross—He prayed, “Father, I have finished the work You have given Me to do” (==John 17:4==). Finished? He hadn’t gone to Calvary yet. But faith allows a man to reach forward and lay hold of what shouldn’t be available to him yet—to look through the cross and speak of what will be as though it already is.

## Application
What mountain does God have you on right now that He wants you to rename? What situation in front of you needs to be re-identified? See the test for what it is—an opportunity for increase. Those who lean into His refining come down changed. They may walk with a limp, and they will certainly have scars. But they also have the authority to rename mountains.
