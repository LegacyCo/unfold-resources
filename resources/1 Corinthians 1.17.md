## Commentary
In this verse Paul begins a sixteen verse discussion on wisdom. He begins here by saying the "wisdom of words" can render the cross of Christ "of no effect." The word translated "of no effect" is *kenoo*. This word means empty, neutralized, truth-less, powerless, without result or profit. Consider that leaning on the wrong wisdom (the wisdom of this world - v20) can render the sacrifice of Christ powerless in that situation. Choosing to trust in the wisdom of this age will cause the power of the cross to be neutralized in your moment. 

For example, the wisdom of this world says do whatever is necessary to get ahead. Lying is virtuous if it gets you where you desire to go (so says the world). But if I lie on my resume' to get a job, the blessings that Jesus died to give me are not going to be able to flow to me. By trusting in the wisdom of the world, I've cut off the power of the cross to bless with, and God will allow me to continue taking care of myself. Or if I refuse to forgive when I am wounded by another, the freedom from bitterness that Jesus died to give me will not profit me. 

It is almost inconceivable that anything could cause the cross of Christ to be of no effect; yet something that is so readily available us all — the wisdom of this world — something we are trained in from a young age, will do just that.

------

There is wisdom from above (as James says in James 1:17) and there is wisdom of this age. Interestingly, both James and Paul still call the inferior form "wisdom," but it is a wisdom of words, a "wisdom of the wise" (v19), a "wisdom of this world" (v20), human wisdom (2:4), wisdom of men (2:5), carnal, and demonic (James 3:15). 

True wisdom, as Paul teaches in this letter, belongs to God. And His wisdom is contrary to every other wisdom. And not only contrary, the world receives it as "foolish."

In God's economy, the cross of Christ was the pinnacle wisdom. What looked like shame and defeat became, in fact, the vehicle of eternal salvation. When God raised Jesus from the dead, and enthroned Him over all creation, He showed us that through defeat comes victory, through weakness comes strength, through humiliation comes glory, through death comes life. The cross is the ultimate expression of God's wisdom.  

As Dr. Robert Gladstone writes in his book *Marks of the Cross*, the cross now cuts a line through every generation, shouting a question to everyone who stares up at that Man hanging on the tree: Will we embrace God's cross-shaped wisdom, or will we see it as foolishness? Will we humble ourselves, admitting how foolish, weak, and utterly useless our philosophies are? Will we follow the narrow path of this strange wisdom embodied by the crucified One, a wisdom rejected by the world but accepted by God? When we face serious crisis, or endure the challenges of daily life, do fall back on the world's wisdom; resorting to fear, anger, manipulation, complaining, tension, or playing the victim. Or do we instead pray in the Spirit and search the Scriptures till God's unique, cross-centered wisdom becomes clear for that situation. God's wisdom may require us to remain silent under criticism, forgive wrongs, bless someone who hurt us, or pray in the Spirit for an hour. Any of these things may look foolish to the world or to our natural souls. But they reflect the wisdom of the cross.

"If we are not willing to embrace the foolishness of the cross, neither can we encounter its wisdom. If we will not embrace its weakness, we cannot expect its power. O, how often we have tried to benefit from the cross' wisdom without welcoming its foolishness!" - Dr. Robert Gladstone

## Cross Reference
This passage reminds of two other passages: Mark 7:13, where Jesus tells the Pharisees and scribes that their traditions have made the word of God "of no effect" (the Greek word here is *akyroo* - to invalidate, make void, render ineffective, to take away the legal force). The second passage is Mark 4:19, where Jesus tells the parable of the sower. Jesus says the thorny soil chokes the word of God, causing the life-giving power of the word of God to become "unfruitful" — unproductive.
