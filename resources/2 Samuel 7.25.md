## Commentary
God had just made David a staggering promise—a house, a throne, a kingdom established forever. And look at how David prays in response: “Now, O LORD God, the word which You have spoken concerning Your servant and concerning his house, establish it forever and *do as You have said*.”

Do as You have said. David takes the promise God spoke and prays it straight back to Him. And those may be the three most powerful words a person can ever pray: *God, You said.* When things fall apart, when the money runs out, when your enemies rise against you—turning toward heaven and saying “God, You said” is like having a superpower.

Here’s why it carries that kind of weight: when God sends you into a situation, the battle doesn’t belong to you. It belongs to the One who sent you. Was Pharaoh Moses’ problem, or God’s? Was Goliath David’s problem? Sarah’s barrenness, the walls of Jericho, the lions in the den—none of those burdens belonged to the saints standing in front of them. And each of them, in one way or another, stood before the impossibility and prayed some version of the same prayer. God, delivering Your people was *Your* idea. God, this giant is lying about who *You* are. God, *You* said Sarah would bear a son. God, if these lions eat me, what does that say about *You*? When we were in Iraq, it didn’t matter what situation I found myself in—I knew God had sent me there. If it was danger: “God, You said to tell this people about You, and You said no weapon formed against me would prosper. So for the glory of Your name, save me.”

But understand what this prayer costs. I don’t get to say “God, You said” if He didn’t. That’s why becoming men and women of His word is essential—so we know what His voice sounds like. That’s why we walk in holiness and integrity—so we know the trial we’re facing isn’t the fruit of disobedience. That’s why we seek Him in prayer *before* we move—so we know we’ve been sent with divine authority instead of moving in our own.

And here’s the mark of a person who has learned this prayer: the opposite of “God, You said” is *what if*—and the same tongue can’t speak both. A man of prayer forgets how to say “what if.” Those words fall out of his vocabulary, because all he knows to do when he faces a challenge is lean on the Lord’s faithfulness and cry out, one more time: “God, You said.”
