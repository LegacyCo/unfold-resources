## Commentary
At first, those in Jesus's hometown were astonished by His wisdom and miracles (v2). But then they began to reason and question; "Is this not the carpenter?" "Is this not Mary's Son?" They knew Jesus as the Son of Mary...*THAT* Mary...the one who had her first baby not enough months after she married Joseph. "And they were [[128|offended]] at Him."

This word offended is rich. The Greek word is *skandalon*. The root word means to jump up and snap shut. It was originally the piece of wood that kept open a trap for animals. The Scriptures take this illustration and use it to mean to cause to sin (cf the nations of Canaan that Israel did not destroy caused them to sin - Judges 8:27). Skandalon is a trap set through questions.

Is this not how Satan trapped Eve? By asking a question he caused her to question God. "Did God really say?" (Genesis 3:1)

The word is often used to imply a stone or obstacle in the way, over which one can trip and fall. In this way the noun means that which causes offense, scandal, division, or hinders faith. 

The New Testament stresses to us that Jesus constantly becomes an offense. 
	- The disciples took offense at His sufferings (Matthew 26:31).
	- John the Baptist was offended as he sat in prison (Matthew 11:6).
	- Peter took offense at Jesus' mention of the cross and Jesus identified the source of that way of thinking as Satanic (Matthew 16:22).
To be clear, Jesus is not the one setting the trap by way of a question...He is the stone in Zion that many will stumble over (Romans 9:33). It's the darkness that causes them to stumble, not the Stone. 

All of these were offended because Jesus was not fitting into their preconceptions of who the Messiah would be.

The basis of all of these offenses is the cross. The cross nullifies all human wisdom. The cross does not match man's expectations of God. It is an offense to the Jews and foolishness to the Gentiles (1 Corinthians 1:23). It is seen as evidence of a man cursed by God (see Galatians 3:13 - "Cursed is every man who hangs on a tree").

Yet the cross is the gift of God to all of creation, he who believes will not be put to shame (Romans 9:33). But to him who is offended, the gift becomes a stumbling stone and it is the man's blindness that makes Jesus an occasion for destruction. 

But Jesus gives us help in the Holy Spirit, "I have said all this to you to keep you from skandalon" (John 16:1).

The consequence of offense in the town of Nazareth: "He could do no mighty works there except heal a few of their sick" (Mark 6:5). Jesus was astonished at their unbelief. And their unbelief kept Him from being Himself. To those who knew Him the longest, He was the woodworker from down the street who, fittingly, died nailed to a piece of wood.

## Application
Could it be, if we are not experiencing the mighty works that the cross has purchased for us, that we also are seeing Jesus simply as the Son of Mary — and not the Son of God?
