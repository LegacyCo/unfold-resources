## Commentary
When we talk about righteousness, we often begin in the wrong place. We start with what a person does instead of what a person believes. But from the very beginning, righteousness was never presented as something man could manufacture through effort. It was something received and witnessed to by faith.

Abel’s offering was accepted because Abel came by faith. Cain brought an offering too, but Hebrews makes it clear that the difference wasn't just the substance of the sacrifice. The difference was the posture of the worshipper's heart. Abel trusted God, and through that faith, he “obtained witness that he was righteous.”

That means righteousness by faith did not begin with Paul. It didn't even begin with Abraham. Abraham chose the better path, but Abel shows us the path was always there. God has always been looking for faith before performance, trust before sacrifice, communion before religious activity.

So righteousness must begin where Scripture begins: not with striving to become what God will finally accept, but believing what God has revealed about Himself and approaching Him on that basis. The holy life is not produced by trying harder to prove ourselves righteous. It grows from the confidence that righteousness comes by faith.
