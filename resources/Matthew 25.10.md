## Commentary
Ten virgins wait for the bridegroom. His arrival is delayed—so long that midnight comes. Five brought enough oil to outlast the delay; five didn’t, and while they’re off buying more, the bridegroom arrives: “and those who were ready went in with him to the wedding; *and the door was shut*.” When the five return and knock—“Lord, Lord, open to us!”—the answer comes back through a closed door: “I do not know you” (==Matthew 25:11-12==).

Notice what the shut door does: it separates. It’s the same thing the ark’s door did in Noah’s day. Until the very last moment, that door stood wide open—no guard, no bouncer; anyone could have walked on board. But when God closed it, everyone inside was separated unto life and everyone outside was separated unto death—and which one a person received came down entirely to where they were standing when the door closed (==Genesis 7:16==).

And here’s the sobering detail both stories share: the ones outside *knew* all about the door. Everyone in Noah’s region knew about the crazy man building the boat; they could have given you directions to it. The five virgins knew the bridegroom—they were in the wedding party! They knew where to find him, they carried lamps for him. But when the door shut, what you knew didn’t matter. All that mattered was where you stood: inside or outside. It doesn’t count to know the stories. It doesn’t count to join a friend group that cleaned up its lifestyle. Those things are good—but if you aren’t *in* Christ, born again inside the ark of His salvation, nearness to the door is not the same as being through it.

The door is still open. That’s the mercy of this hour—the delay of the Bridegroom is itself an invitation (==2 Peter 3:9==). But Scripture is unembarrassed to say the delay has an end. So the question of this parable is not “how much do you know?” It’s the question of address: where are you standing—today, “while it is called ‘Today’” (==Hebrews 3:13==)—in relation to the door?
