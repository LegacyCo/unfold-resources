## Commentary
“…If you will not believe, surely you shall not be established.”

The word comes to King Ahaz at a moment of national panic. Syria and Israel have allied against Judah, and when the news reached Jerusalem, the heart of the king and his people “was moved as the trees of the woods are moved with the wind” (==Isaiah 7:2==). God sends Isaiah to meet the trembling king with a promise: the enemies' plan “shall not stand, nor shall it come to pass” (==Isaiah 7:7==). Then the promise closes with this condition, and in Hebrew it is a play on a single word. *Believe* and *be established* are the same root, *aman*—the root behind our word “amen.” The sentence sounds something like: *if you will not stand firm, you will not be made firm*. God's word to Judah was solid ground; whether the kingdom stood on it was up to the king. Unfortunately, he did not. Behind a mask of piety he refused even the sign God offered him (==Isaiah 7:12==), hired Assyria to save him instead (==2 Kings 16:7-8==), and the empire he trusted became the razor that shaved his kingdom bare (==Isaiah 7:20==).

Interestingly, another story with another king contains this sentence's mirror image. King Jehoshaphat was facing a similar enemy invasion. As Jehoshaphat's army marched out toward three enemy nations, he stood and told the people: “Believe in the LORD your God, and you shall be established; believe His prophets, and you shall prosper” (==2 Chronicles 20:20==)—the same wordplay, *believe* and *be established* from the same root. But unlike King Ahaz, Jehoshaphat did not say this as a warning, he said it as a promise. Two kings, two crises, and the same fork in the road. Jehoshaphat believed, and Judah stood still and watched the salvation of the LORD (==2 Chronicles 20:22==). Ahaz would not believe, and nothing he built stood. The condition God set before both kings is the one He sets before everyone who receives His word: the promise is firm on His side—*aman*, sure, worthy of amen—but it becomes firm ground under a person's feet only by faith (==Hebrews 11:6==; ==Romans 4:20-21==). What you will not stand on cannot hold you up.
