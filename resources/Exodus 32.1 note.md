## Commentary
Watch what triggers the golden calf. Not persecution. Not false teaching. A *delay*: “When the people saw that Moses delayed coming down from the mountain, the people gathered together to Aaron and said… ‘Make us gods that shall go before us.’” When things didn’t fit Israel’s timeline, fear began to write its own story.

And notice what Israel actually did—because it’s subtler than we think. They didn’t abandon God. They remade Him into something more manageable. Aaron built the calf and declared, “This is your god, O Israel, that brought you out of the land of Egypt” (==Exodus 32:4==)—the language of reverence, borrowed so they could keep singing and dancing. The golden calf was rebellion, but not just against God. It was rebellion against the *delay* He had them in. A refusal to live in the tension of uncertainty. Impatience dressed up as worship. And because they refused God’s process, their worship was redirected to a god they could carry instead of a God they had to follow.

We read this story and think we’d never do such a thing. But just because we’ve never melted down jewelry doesn’t mean we haven’t tried to remake God into an image more agreeable to our expectations—a god who moves at our pace, a god who validates our urgency, a god who blesses our impatience. Any attempt to force our way out of the in-between in our own strength is us following a god of our own making. When we force the door, we’re no longer following the God who led us to it.

But here’s the redemptive side: the delay between seasons is deliberate. God knows exactly how many seconds need to tick before who we really are shows up—not to shame us, but to heal us. He already knows what’s in us; He wants *us* to see it (==Deuteronomy 8:2==), so that the old self—the one not invited into the next season—can be buried in the soil of the in-between. There are things from your last season that aren’t allowed to come with you: habits, wounds, sin patterns, unbelief, even some relationships. It’s God’s goodness to hold you at the threshold until they’re dealt with. The space between seasons can become the burial ground of who you used to be—if you can endure the delay.
