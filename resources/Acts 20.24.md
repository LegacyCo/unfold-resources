## Commentary
“But none of these things move me; nor do I count my life dear to myself, so that I may finish my race with joy, and the ministry which I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the gospel of the grace of God.”

Look at what “these things” are. The Holy Spirit had just testified to Paul that chains and tribulations were waiting for him in every city (==Acts 20:22-23==). Paul isn’t being brave about vague hypotheticals—he’s been told, plainly, that suffering is ahead. And his response is this verse: none of it moves me. Why not? Because Paul had already settled the question of what his life was for. A man who doesn’t count his life dear to himself has nothing left that chains can threaten. The unmovable life is simply the surrendered one.

Notice what Paul lives to finish: not his life, but his *race*—and “the ministry which I *received* from the Lord Jesus.” Received. Paul didn’t invent his ministry or build it from a burden he felt one day; it was handed to him, and a received ministry never belongs to the one carrying it. That’s why he moved through his own life’s work with such open hands—planting churches and letting Apollos water them (==1 Corinthians 3:6==), weeping with the Ephesian elders on this very beach and then sailing away (==Acts 20:36-38==). You can hold work loosely when the work was never yours. What you own, you protect; what you’ve received, you finish.
