## Commentary
“All these men of war, who could keep ranks, came to Hebron with a loyal heart, to make David king over all Israel; and all the rest of Israel were of one mind to make David king.”

Israel hadn't been united in a long time. After Saul died, the nation split. Judah followed David. Everyone else followed Saul's son Ish-bosheth. “There was a long war between the house of Saul and the house of David” (==2 Samuel 3:1==), and for seven and a half years David was king over Judah alone (==2 Samuel 5:5==). This verse is where that division ends. 

Two phrases describe them. They came with “a loyal heart”—in Hebrew, a *whole* or *complete* heart (*lebab shalem*). It's the same expression David used years later when he charged Solomon to serve God “with a loyal heart and with a willing mind” (==1 Chronicles 28:9==). And the rest of Israel was “of one mind”—literally, *one heart* (*leb echad*). So each person's heart was whole, and together their hearts were one. The second depends on the first. People whose own hearts are divided won't stay united with each other for long.

And what brought them together was a person. The tribes didn't sit down and negotiate a settlement or work out a compromise between their competing interests. These were people with different histories, different territories, and fresh bloodshed between them. They became one because every tribe wanted the same king. A. W. Tozer had a great picture for this: “one hundred pianos all tuned to the same fork are automatically tuned to each other.”

The New Testament describes the church the same way. The believers in Jerusalem “were of one heart and one soul” (==Acts 4:32==), and the very next verse tells us what they had in common: the apostles were giving witness “to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus” (==Acts 4:33==). Paul says the church grows into “the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God” (==Ephesians 4:13==). God even promised it through the prophets: “I will give them one heart and one way, that they may fear Me forever” (==Jeremiah 32:39==). Unity among God's people comes from shared allegiance to God's King. So where unity is missing, the place to start is our own heart's loyalty to Him.
