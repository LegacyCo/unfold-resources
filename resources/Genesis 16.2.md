## Commentary
“So Sarai said to Abram, ‘See now, the LORD has restrained me from bearing children. Please, go in to my maid; perhaps I shall obtain children by her.’ And Abram heeded the voice of Sarai.”

Here’s the real-life tension of walking with a God who opens and closes doors as He sees fit: His doors don’t usually close and open at the same time. There’s a window between seasons when He hems you in between two closed doors—a hallway. And the hallway is where the serpent starts asking his oldest question: “Did God really say?” (==Genesis 3:1==). Did God really say to move your family to that city? Did God really say to start that ministry, marry that person, leave the comfortable job, live off less so you can give more? When those questions are firing and you need answers, the fruit of the tree of knowledge starts looking tempting: “Maybe I made a mistake. Maybe a bite will give me some clarity.”

That’s exactly where Sarah was. God had spoken a promise, the years had piled up, and the door to its fulfillment stayed shut. So she reached for a reasonable, culturally acceptable solution: maybe the promise comes through Hagar. Abraham heeded her, Ishmael was born—and the household inherited a conflict that outlived them all (==Genesis 16:4-6==; ==Genesis 21:9-10==). Mark this down: Ishmael is always born in the hallway between the season of God’s promise and the fulfillment of that promise. He’s conceived the moment we decide God needs our help birthing what He swore to deliver.

Sometimes we even spiritualize the grab: “I see what’s happening—God hasn’t opened the door because He wants me to show initiative and make a strong leadership decision.” That’s the trap Saul fell into when Samuel’s arrival was delayed and he offered the sacrifice himself—and it cost him the kingdom (==1 Samuel 13:11-14==). The Bible never teaches that the sharpest decision-makers inherit the promises. “Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for Him… those who wait on the LORD, they shall inherit the earth” (==Psalm 37:7==; ==Psalm 37:9==).

## Application
Learn to recognize hallway temptation by its vocabulary. It says *perhaps*—“perhaps I shall obtain children by her”—because self-effort always deals in maybes, while God’s promise deals in certainties. It comes dressed as the reasonable option, often endorsed by people who love you, like Sarai. And it always offers a shortcut that bypasses waiting. So when you’re hemmed in between two closed doors this season, name the Hagar within reach—the plan B you could execute tomorrow—and deliberately set it down. Then answer the serpent’s question out loud: yes, God really did say. Rehearse the last thing He told you and stay under it. The hallway isn’t where promises die. It’s where Ishmaels are conceived—or where faith grows into patience (==Hebrews 6:12==).
