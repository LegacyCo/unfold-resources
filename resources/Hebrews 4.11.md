## Commentary
At first glance, this verse almost sounds contradictory. The writer has just told us that God rested from His works, and that the one who enters God’s rest also ceases from his own works. Then he turns around and says, “Be diligent,” or in some translations, “labor to enter that rest.”

So are we supposed to stop working or work harder?

The point is not that we effort our way into rest. The point is that we stop delaying our entrance into what God has already made available.

The word used there carries the idea of eagerness, haste, and promptness. In other words, the warning is not, “Try harder until you finally earn rest.” The warning is, “Don’t put this off. Don’t stand outside of what God has opened. Don’t keep living restless when rest has already been provided.”

Hebrews 4 keeps pointing us back to “Today.” “Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts.” Rest is not just a future promise for someday. It is an invitation for today. God has appointed a day called *today*, and the danger is that we keep postponing what He is presently offering.

Which means restlessness is not always something happening *to* us. Sometimes it is something we continue choosing. That may sound blunt, but Hebrews is blunt. Israel had the promise. They had the invitation. They had the provision. But they did not enter because of unbelief. The issue was not that rest was unavailable. The issue was that they refused to respond to what God had made available.

And if Israel was rebuked for not entering rest before the finished work of Christ, what excuse do we have on this side of the cross?

This is where we have to be careful, because religion will often make restlessness sound spiritual. It will convince us that anxiety is responsibility, striving is diligence, and exhaustion is proof that we're giving God all we have.

For many of us, restlessness has even become part of our identity. We call it being driven. But sometimes what we’re really doing is baptizing our inability to trust God. We use urgency as permission to abandon Christlike character because “somebody has to get something done.”

But the fruit of the Spirit is not hurry. The way of Jesus is not frantic. And the inheritance of God is not received by those who are constantly striving to prove they deserve it.

Hebrews 4:11 is not calling us to work harder for rest. It is calling us to be quick to enter what God has already provided.
