## Commentary
A Gentile woman hears that Jesus is passing through her region and rushes out to meet Him: “Have mercy on me, O Lord, Son of David! My daughter is severely demon-possessed” (==Matthew 15:22==). And Jesus—stands there. Silent. Not a word. Not even an acknowledgment that she exists.

She keeps begging. The disciples get irritated and ask Him to send her away. When Jesus finally speaks, it’s no comfort: “I was not sent except to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.” But that only tells the woman He knows she’s there. She seizes the moment, comes closer, falls at His feet, and worships. Then comes the hardest sentence in the story: “It is not good to take the children’s bread and throw it to the little dogs.”

And she is unfazed. She takes her stand on the little faith she has—if He does this for Israel’s children, surely there are crumbs for her daughter: “Yes, Lord, yet even the little dogs eat the crumbs which fall from their masters’ table.” Silos full of nuclear warheads pale in comparison to the power of a mother’s faith. And Jesus turns: “O woman, *great* is your faith! Let it be to you as you desire.” Her daughter was healed that very hour.

Jesus was not being cruel to that woman. He was being kind. He was placing soil around the seed of her faith—applying the necessary pressure that would promote growth: first through silence, then through a challenging word. He wasn’t trying to destroy her faith; He was maturing it. Healing the daughter was the easy part—He never even went to the house. Meeting the mother at her level of faith and *refusing to leave her there* mattered just as much to Him. God is always after increase.

Which means it’s the goodness of God when we experience the silence of God. It’s His goodness when He allows the challenging word, the situation that doesn’t resolve on the first cry. He knows what you can handle, and He won’t let the pressure crush you (==1 Corinthians 10:13==)—but He will give your faith room to be tested, because tested faith is how “little” becomes “great.”
