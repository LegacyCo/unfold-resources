## Commentary
“Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.” If you are in Christ, the Spirit of God lives in you—which means freedom isn’t waiting for you somewhere. It’s *resident* in you now.

A lot of western Christianity disagrees. We inherited a leaven that taught us to see ourselves as barely-changed sinners doing time until heaven—Luther describing the believer as snow-covered dung, William Carey demanding his tombstone read, “Here lies a wretched, poor, and helpless worm.” That posture sounds humble, but watch what it produces: a majority of believers fighting the same impulses they wrestled with before they were born again, waiting for death to finally set them free from sin so they can be with God.

Here's a question for you: if death is what ultimately sets me free from sin and joins me to God, then what saves me—death, or Jesus? My Bible is clear that Jesus is the Savior. So I need to stop waiting for death to send me to heaven and start living like Jesus already gathered heaven and earth together in Himself (==Ephesians 1:10==).

The freedom of this verse is total. Freedom from everything you ever did as a member of Adam’s family—and freedom from every temptation that still tries to re-identify you by your former name. You’re not part of that family anymore. Salvation is a great place to start and a terrible place to stop: you weren’t born merely to be saved. You were created to be with God, to re-image Him, to show the world what He looks like—the language of a people who can say with their Lord, “if you’ve seen me, you’ve seen something of Him” (==John 14:9==; ==John 17:22-23==).

## Application
So sit with the questions until they get underneath you. What would happen if you actually believed you were one with God? What would your life look like if you knew the Spirit of Jesus indwells your spirit? What kind of peace would you carry if you trusted that the Prince of Peace has gathered you into His rest? And what could you possibly be afraid of, if you believed you were one with the One who has overcome the world?
