## Commentary
For Paul, contentment springs from complete readiness to accept whatever God gives. He makes no distinction between the necessary and the extravagant, but simply gives thanks for everything. He can accept both abundance and want as part of his life, and he gives thanks that he has received both as a gift. 

Numbers 11 gives us a picture of what happens when contentment gives way to craving. The “mixed multitude” who came out of Egypt with Israel began longing for what they had left behind: “We remember the fish which we ate freely in Egypt...but now there is nothing at all to look at except this manna...Who will give us meat to eat?” (==Numbers 11:4-6==)

They had been delivered from slavery, sustained in the wilderness, and fed every morning by the hand of God. But because the provision had become familiar, they began treating manna like it was nothing. Their cravings convinced them that what God had given wasn’t enough.

That discontent eventually became a demand. And their demand brought judgment (==Numbers 11:33-34==). It also placed such a heavy burden on Moses that he asked God to take his life rather than make him continue carrying the people alone (==Numbers 11:11-15==).

When we refuse to receive where God has us, we begin romanticizing what He delivered us from, despising what He has provided, and demanding that He give us something different. Contentment receives both abundance and want as coming through the hands of a faithful God.

## Application
Receive the season you’re in without demanding that it become something else before you can trust God. Whether you have much or little, practice gratitude for what He has given and refuse to let changing circumstances determine your peace. Contentment grows when you stop measuring God’s goodness by your comfort.
