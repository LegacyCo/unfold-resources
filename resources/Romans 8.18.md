## Commentary
“For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.”

Revealed *in* us—not just to us. Paul isn’t saying heaven will compensate us for what we endured, the way an insurance payout compensates for a wreck. He’s saying the suffering is *producing* something in us that will one day be unveiled. The sufferings and the glory aren’t two unrelated ledgers; one is forging the other. That’s why he can weigh them against each other and find the comparison laughable—“our light affliction, which is but for a moment, is working for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory” (==2 Corinthians 4:17==).

The pattern is the Man on the throne. Jesus rose from the dead, and resurrection didn’t remove His scars—it confirmed them, glorified them (==John 20:27==). His scars announce His character forever: covenant love, sacrifice, sin and death defeated. And a servant is not greater than his master. Whether or not we're ever called to bear literal marks like our persecuted brothers and sisters, every true disciple bears the marks of the cross on the soul—the healed-over places where we suffered the loss of a right, denied the flesh, forgave, believed in the dark. Bob Gladstone writes in *Marks of the Cross*, “In the kingdom people with scars radiate… Does not every scar that marks a loss become an aperture through which resurrection life beams? … Don’t despise these scars. They testify of the character we will take into the age to come.”

The character formed through present suffering is the one thing we carry into the resurrection. Gladstone again: “Developing Christ’s image during this age of spiritual warfare is when it counts. We will not have this opportunity after the resurrection.” This age—with its resistance, its wounds, its daily deaths—is the only forge there will ever be. So don’t despise what the pressure is producing. Creation itself is on tiptoe waiting for the unveiling of what God is making in you (==Romans 8:19==)—and when it’s revealed, the sufferings won’t even be worth mentioning in the same sentence.
