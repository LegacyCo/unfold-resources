## Commentary
“Then God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the animals that were with him in the ark. And God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters subsided.”

The waters had prevailed for a hundred and fifty days (==Genesis 7:24==). For five months Noah sat inside a sealed ark with no window on the horizon, no word from heaven recorded, and nothing but water in every direction. Then the text turns on one clause: “God remembered Noah.” This does not mean God had forgotten him and suddenly recalled him. In Scripture, when God remembers, He acts—the remembering is the beginning of the deliverance. The same verb marks the turning point when God remembered Abraham and brought Lot out of Sodom (==Genesis 19:29==), when He remembered His covenant and moved to bring Israel out of Egypt (==Exodus 2:24==), and when He remembered Hannah and she conceived (==1 Samuel 1:19-20==).

The silence of those months did not mean absence. There is an old account of how certain Native American tribes brought a boy into manhood: on the night of his thirteenth birthday he was blindfolded, carried miles from home, and left alone in a dark forest until morning. The sounds and the cold and the uncertainty of that night brought the boy to the end of himself. But when dawn broke, he saw the outline of a man standing a few feet away with a bow and arrow—his father, who had been there the entire night. That is the picture of God in the months before Genesis 8:1. He had never left the ark unattended. “I will never leave you nor forsake you” (==Hebrews 13:5==) was true before the wind ever began to blow.

## Word Study
**Remembered** is the Hebrew *zakar* (זָכַר)—to remember, to call to mind, and, when God is the subject, to act on behalf of the one remembered. It is covenant language: “God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob” (==Exodus 2:24==) leads directly to the Exodus, and the thief's prayer, “Lord, remember me when You come into Your kingdom,” is answered the same day (==Luke 23:42-43==). Summary: when Scripture says God remembered, it is announcing that His faithfulness has moved into action.

## Application
In the season when heaven seems silent and the water has not begun to go down, the question is not whether God has forgotten you but whether you will trust that He is standing in the dark a few feet away. Rehearse His covenant faithfulness rather than your circumstances—the way Israel was told to remember the wilderness (==Deuteronomy 8:2==)—and wait for the wind. God's remembering is never late; it arrives when the work of waiting is done.
