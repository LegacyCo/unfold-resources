## Commentary
This section of Genesis covers more than 1,500 years of human history. Genesis 5 is one of the major genealogical sections in Genesis. Here in chapter 5, we see the lineage from Adam to Noah. Then, in Genesis 10–11, we see the lineage from Noah to Abram.

“The book of the genealogy of Adam” is an important phrase. It appears here in Genesis 5:1, and then a strikingly similar phrase appears again in the opening line of the New Testament: “The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ” (==Matthew 1:1==). 

There is nothing accidental about that repetition.

The Old Testament is the written account of a God who, instead of turning His back on humanity, continues to move the story forward toward restoration and redemption. The Old Testament points ahead. It is the first movement of a larger redemptive story that reaches its fulfillment in Christ.

That's helpful when we come across Old Testament stories that feel unresolved or difficult to reconcile with the fullness of God revealed in Jesus. If a story in the Old Testament feels unfinished, that's because it is. The Old Testament is not meant to be read in isolation from Christ. To read it in a vacuum is to stop before the story reaches the One it was always pointing toward.

So when we arrive at the opening line of the New Testament, Matthew begins with words Israel should recognize: “This is the book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ.”

Those words would have landed like a thunderclap for Matthew’s Jewish audience. They are intentional. They announce that the story has reached its decisive moment. *This* is the genealogy of the Man who will redeem humanity. *This* is the Son of David. *This* is the Son of Abraham. *This* is the promised Offspring of the woman who would crush the head of the serpent (==Genesis 3:15==; ==Matthew 1:1==; ==Galatians 4:4–5==).

Genesis 5 gives us the genealogy of Adam — the line of the man through whom sin and death entered the world.

Matthew 1 gives us the genealogy of Christ — the Man through whom grace, redemption, and new creation come.

Adam’s genealogy leads to death. Christ’s genealogy leads to life.

This is why the Bible has to be read as one unified story. The first Adam is not the end of the story. The last Adam has come. And in Him, God begins the restoration of all things (==Romans 5:12–21==; ==1 Corinthians 15:45–49==).
