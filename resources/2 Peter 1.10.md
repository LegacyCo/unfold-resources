## Commentary
Peter says if we "do these things" (the things mentioned in v5-7) we will not stumble. If *we* do these things. Jude says in Jude 24 that *Jesus* keeps us from stumbling. This is a great example of the kingdom tension of God's *ability* partnering with our *responsibility* to accomplish His will in our lives. He is able to do exceedingly, abundantly beyond what we could imagine (Ephesians 3:20)...and we are responsible to be diligent (zealous) to make our calling and election sure, as Peter says here in this verse.

But is this not "works-based righteousness?" No. Works-based righteousness is built on the foundation of what I *do*. What Peter is saying here is built on the foundation of identity; who God says I now am. Because of who we are, we are now called to live like who Jesus says we are; righteous, holy, children of God, co-heirs with Christ, image-bearers of God, partakers of the divine nature, recipients of all things that pertain to life and godliness and every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places. This is an issue of maturity, not righteousness; and for sure not salvation.

## Word Study
What does it mean to stumble? The meaning of the Greek word Peter uses here (ptaíō) is to suffer a reverse, to err, to sin. It's used 5x in the NT: James 2:10, James 3:2 (2x), Romans 11:11, and here in 2 Peter 1:10. Jude doesn't explicitly use the word in v24, but it is implied ("He is able to protect you and make you stand in the presence of His glory, blameless with great joy"). 

Plainly stated, to stumble in this sense means to sin (see instances in 1 John where he writes something similar; 1 John 3:6 for example).

## Application
Peter is saying if we add to the faith that saved us:
		- excellence of character
		- knowledge
		- self-control
		- perserverance
		- piety (godliness, reverence)
		- brotherly kindness
		- love
then we will not sin/stumble/err/suffer a reverse. By living this way—and practicing these things—we make "His calling and choice of us certain" (as the NASB translates it).
