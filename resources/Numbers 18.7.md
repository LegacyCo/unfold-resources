## Commentary
The priesthood was given to Aaron and his sons as a gift, but it was a gift that could only be possessed through service. God did not give them a title to enjoy. He brought them near to His presence so that they could serve Him and His people.

This reveals something essential about every gift and calling that comes from God: it is never given merely for the benefit of the one who receives it. Whatever God places upon a life is intended to become grace for someone else. The gift may be entrusted to us, but it is not ultimately about us.

Scripture presents the gift and calling as something that increases our responsibility. To be chosen by God is to be made available to Him. To be brought near is to be brought low. The closer the priest stood to the presence of God, the more completely his life belonged to the service of God.

This is the pattern Jesus later established for His disciples: “He who is greatest among you shall be your servant” (==Matthew 23:11==). Greatness in the kingdom is not measured by how many people recognize our gift, but by how many people are strengthened through it. Authority is not permission to stand above others; it is grace to stoop beneath them.

The priesthood was therefore both privilege and obligation. Aaron and his sons were given access others did not have, but that access placed demands upon their entire lives. They could not separate the honor of the calling from the service required by it. The gift and the service were one.

Everything God gives us carries the same purpose. Our knowledge, resources, influence, time, strength, experience, and spiritual gifts are blessings placed into our hands for the sake of others. A gift becomes kingdom-shaped only when it becomes service.
