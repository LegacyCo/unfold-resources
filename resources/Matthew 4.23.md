## Commentary
I love how Matthew orders this passage in chapter 4: First, the Kingdom is found in Jesus. Then, the Kingdom is established in His people. Then, the Kingdom extends into the world.

That’s the process of heaven coming to earth: Jesus. People. More people.

But here’s the rub: there’s no further expansion of the Kingdom without a people who have surrendered everything to the King.

This is why the call of Jesus is not supplemental. It’s total. When Jesus says, “Follow Me,” He’s not asking if you want to add Him to your life. He wants to live His life through you. “It’s no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me" (Galatians 2:20).

If the Church refuses to live the Jesus way of life, the Church cannot carry His Kingdom. We may carry His name. We may sing His songs. We may use His language. But the Kingdom is only established where the King reigns. 

And if He’s not reigning in us, then how can His kingdom expand to those around us?

The church occupies the delicate place in the world between the King and those who are far from Him. Before the freedom of His kingdom can touch the world, it has to be established in His people. Just as there can be no church without Jesus, there can be no further extension of God's kingdom without the church.

This is why Peter and Andrew leave their nets. This is why James and John leave their boat. Their leaving is not just a career change. It’s full surrender. It’s the old life being brought under the authority of the King.
