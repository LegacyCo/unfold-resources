## Commentary
“Now all things are of God, who has reconciled us to Himself through Jesus Christ, and has given us the ministry of reconciliation.” In the span of three verses, Paul uses the word *reconcile* five times. When a writer under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit hammers one word five times in three sentences, he’s telling you where the center of gravity is—reconciliation isn’t a side benefit of the gospel. It *is* the gospel.

So we’d better know what the word means. Reconciliation is the restoration of the original after a time of hostility. Which raises the question: what’s “the original”? Go back to the beginning—man made in God’s image and likeness (==Genesis 1:26==), no separation, no fear, no hiding; union and intimacy with our Creator. That’s the original. Then sin severed the relationship, and like a branch broken off the tree, humanity was cut off from its source of life—still recognizable, but decaying, because nothing broken from its source keeps living (==John 15:5-6==). Reconciliation means God didn’t settle for pardoning the broken branch. He grafted it back into the tree. He restored the original.

And notice the direction of every verb. God reconciled *us* to Himself. God was in Christ reconciling the *world* to Himself, not counting their trespasses against them (==2 Corinthians 5:19==). Nowhere does Scripture say God had to be reconciled to man—as if the Father were the one who had turned away, and heaven’s heart had to be softened toward us. The cross was not man persuading God to turn back toward us. The cross was God’s ultimate turning toward man: while we were *enemies*, we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son (==Romans 5:10==). He saw there was no man who could make the sacrifice, so He wrapped Himself in zeal and came to make it Himself (==Isaiah 59:16==). Even the things we do—repentance, confession, faith—are responses to a reconciliation He initiated, not contributions to it. Anything we bring to God is an answer to a God who moved first.

That’s why Paul’s appeal lands the way it does: “we implore you on Christ’s behalf, *be reconciled* to God” (==2 Corinthians 5:20==). He doesn’t say “reconcile yourselves”—you can’t. He says *be* reconciled: receive what has already been accomplished. God has done His part in full; the restoration is complete and offered. What remains is for you to take your place in it—and then, astonishingly, to carry the word of it to others as His ambassador, because the message of reconciliation has been committed to the reconciled.

## Word Study
- *reconciled* : **katallassō** (Greek) — to change thoroughly, to restore a relationship to its original state after hostility; in secular Greek, used for exchanging currency and for restoring estranged parties to friendship. The word assumes there was an original worth returning to.

Where Paul builds his gospel on it:

- ==Romans 5:10== — “while we were *enemies* we were reconciled (katallassō) to God through the death of His Son.” Reconciliation happened while the hostility was still ours.
- ==Colossians 1:20-22== — through the blood of His cross, God reconciled “*all things* to Himself”—including you, who were “alienated and enemies in your mind”—“to present you holy, and blameless, and above reproach.”

Summary: katallassō means the gospel doesn’t just move you from guilty to acquitted. It moves you from hostile stranger back to Genesis 1:26—the original design of union restored.
