## Commentary
Before Paul says a word to wives or husbands, he lays down the header over the whole household: “submitting to one another in the fear of God.” Mutual submission isn’t a concession Paul makes to soften the verses that follow. It’s the atmosphere they all breathe—and its pattern is older than marriage, older than creation. It lives inside the Godhead.

God is three in one, joined in covenant union with Himself. And watch how the Persons of the Trinity relate. The Son says, “I only do what I see the Father doing” (==John 5:19==)—the Son lovingly giving Himself to the role the Father serves. The Spirit “will not speak on His own authority, but whatever He hears He will speak,” taking what belongs to the Son and declaring it (==John 16:13-14==). And the Father, in perfect harmony, loves so much that He gives the Son, so the Son can redeem and receive a bride. Each Person considers Another before acting. The mutual self-giving, and the distinct role each plays in the story of redemption, is precisely what holds the identity of God together. The moment the Father tried to be the Son, or the Spirit tried to speak from another source, the glory would distort.

That’s what submission is in the kingdom: not the erasure of a person, but the glory of a person freely honoring the role of another. Which is why we can’t take the beauty of biblical order and shrink it down to rule-book ethics—who submits to whom, who holds the power. Filter God’s design through our own biases and power structures, and we lose the witness. Order in the home matters, but only as far as it demonstrates who Jesus is.

So when a family lives this way—each considering the other, each releasing the other to flourish in their God-designed role—that home isn’t just running smoothly. It’s bearing the family resemblance. It looks like the God who has forever existed as Persons who defer, honor, and give themselves to one another in love.
