## Commentary
“And He is the head of the body, the church… that in all things He may have the preeminence.”

In *all* things. Not most things. Not everything except the Sunday service order. If Jesus is the head of the body, then the moment any individual inserts himself into the head position, Jesus is cut off from doing what He wants to do through His body. A body takes direction from its head—and a body can only have one.

Jesus won’t fight you for the position. He doesn’t impose Himself. The Son of Man is still looking for a place to lay His head (==Luke 9:58==)—and the way we gather either opens the door to His leadership or shuts it. To the degree that we look to Him as Lord when we come together, He will lead. And how does He lead? By His Spirit in each member of the body (==1 Corinthians 12:7==; ==1 Corinthians 12:11==). Not exclusively through one man. The full expression of the Head never comes through a single member—that’s the whole point of having a body.

This is the tension every leader has to carry, and I’ll be honest about it. There are seasons when a leader has permission to say, “Imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ” (==1 Corinthians 11:1==). But a leader also has to know when to sit down—when Jesus wants to speak through someone else, when His Spirit has given a revelation to another member of the body, when the part of Himself He wants to express tonight isn’t the part He put in me (==1 Corinthians 14:30==). A leader who can’t sit down has changed seats—out of the body and into the head.

Admittedly, gathering this way takes practice, especially when we’ve been conditioned for so long to do church another way. The goal is not to get it right every time. The goal is to yield to the leadership of Jesus in our midst. He knows our frame—He knows we’re sheep, and sheep are not known for their brilliance. But blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God (==Matthew 5:8==). If our motives are pure when we gather, we will see Him lead us well.

## Application
For anyone who leads anything—a church, a small group, a family devotion, a worship set: build the sit-down into your leadership. Before you gather, pray a dangerous prayer: “Jesus, You have the preeminence tonight—lead through whoever You want.” Then leave actual room for it: a pause after the teaching to ask what the Spirit showed others, an unhurried moment at the table, a willingness to shorten your part when Jesus is clearly moving through someone else’s. And watch your reactions—if it stings when God uses somebody else’s gift instead of yours, thank Him for showing you, and sit down anyway. The Head doesn’t need a substitute. He needs a yielded body.
