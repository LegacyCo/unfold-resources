## Commentary
“And all Mine are Yours, and Yours are Mine, and I am glorified in them.”

Jesus says He is glorified *in* His people. His glory shines through weak, frail jars of clay (==2 Corinthians 4:7==)—but a jar has to be emptied before it can be filled with treasure. This is the reason for the surrendered life: not duty, but the glory of Jesus made visible through people who have yielded themselves to Him. Jesus received His own glory the same way. Though He existed in the form of God, He did not consider equality with God something to use for His advantage, but emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant—and for this reason God highly exalted Him (==Philippians 2:6-9==). Having emptied and surrendered Himself completely, He could pray, “glorify Me together with Yourself, with the glory which I had with You before the world was” (==John 17:5==). God only fills empty things. He can only use surrendered things.

Corrie ten Boom made the point with an empty glove: the glove can do nothing on its own—it moves only when a hand fills it. That is the believer's life in Christ. “I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me” (==Galatians 2:20==). When Paul is empty, Jesus lives His life through Paul. And this is why surrender is not an act of discipline, works, or effort. Surrender is what makes an exchange possible. There is a yielding on the believer's part that says: I am insufficient in myself to live this Christian life, so the things I used to trust in and depend on must be emptied out—and in the exchange, God fills the life with Himself, the Spirit empowers, and God animates the life and lives His life through it. The glove does not strain to act like a hand. It surrenders to one.

## Application
Locate what you are still gripping—the comfort, reputation, or self-sufficiency you reach for when life presses on you. Those are the things keeping the glove full of emptiness. Practice the exchange this note describes: confess your insufficiency for the specific situation in front of you, yield it, and ask Christ to live His life through you in it. The goal of surrender is not loss. It is that He would be glorified in you.
