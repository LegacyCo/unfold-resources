## Commentary
Psalm 7 begins with David taking refuge in God while under accusation and threat.

The historical heading tells us this psalm was written “concerning the words of Cush, a Benjamite.” We don't know exactly who Cush was. Because he is called a Benjamite, some connect the background generally to the hostility David experienced from Saul’s house, since Saul was also from the tribe of Benjamin. 

What is clear is that David is bringing his injustice before the Lord. Something has been spoken against him. He is facing slander, accusation, or some form of verbal attack that has become dangerous.

David doesn't defend himself before people. He doesn't take matters into his own hands. He handles the situation by entrusting himself to God.

“O LORD my God, in You I put my trust.”

Psalm 7 is not the picture of a man pretending injustice doesn't matter. It's a man refusing to become his own judge, jury, and executioner. He appeals to the righteous God who sees what is true.

This part of the larger character trait that made David a "man after God's own heart." When Saul pursued him, David had opportunities to kill Saul, but he refused to stretch out his hand against the Lord’s anointed. He entrusted judgment to God instead of forcing justice by his own hand (1 Samuel 24:1–12; 26:7–11). Paul gives the same instruction in Romans: “Beloved, do not avenge yourselves, but rather give place to wrath” because vengeance belongs to the Lord (Romans 12:19).

## Application
There are few things harder than being misunderstood, misrepresented, or falsely accused.

Everything in us wants to defend ourselves. But Psalm 7 teaches us to bring accusation into the presence of God before we bring it anywhere else.

That doesn't mean we never speak up. David speaks plainly in this psalm. But he speaks *first* to God.
