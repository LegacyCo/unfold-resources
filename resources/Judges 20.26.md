## Commentary
“Then all the children of Israel… went up and came to the house of God and wept. They sat there before the LORD and fasted that day until evening.”

Note the length: *that day, until evening*. When we hear “fasting” we tend to think forty days, twenty-one days, seven days. Those are extended fasts, and they occur in Scripture—but they were exceptional rather than the basic pattern. The ordinary practice of biblical fasting was one day, until sundown. Israel fasted “that day until evening” here; David and his men “mourned and wept and fasted until evening” for Saul and Jonathan (==2 Samuel 1:12==); Israel “fasted that day” at Mizpah (==1 Samuel 7:6==); David refused food “till the sun goes down” (==2 Samuel 3:35==).

God's people kept that modest rhythm for generations. The Pharisees fasted Mondays and Thursdays (==Luke 18:12==); the early church wanted to continue the weekly rhythm but refused to be seen “fasting with the hypocrites,” so they fasted Wednesdays and Fridays, typically until mid-afternoon—keeping the rhythm while declining to share the Pharisees' calendar. John Wesley kept the same Wednesday-and-Friday pattern centuries later. The point is not the days, and it is not even the length. It is that fasting has been a regular, ordinary practice of God's people in every generation. A generation raised on stories of extended fasts tends to conclude that if it can't do a forty-day water fast, it can't fast at all. Scripture says otherwise. The gift is far more accessible than that—most of the fasts in the Bible ended at sundown.

## Application
Start where Scripture's ordinary pattern starts: one day, until evening. Pick the day, set it apart to seek God, and end it at sundown. A sustainable weekly or monthly rhythm of single-day fasting will form more in you over a year than one heroic extended fast attempted and abandoned. Let the extraordinary fasts come when God calls for them; build the ordinary one into your life now.
