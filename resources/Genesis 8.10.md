## Commentary
“And he waited yet another seven days, and again he sent the dove out from the ark.”

The judgment was finished. The wickedness had been wiped from the earth, and God could now rebuild with His man Noah. Yet God did not speed up the process. He let the waters recede naturally, over roughly seven months, and He kept Noah waiting in the ark—seven days between the raven and the dove, seven days more, and seven days again (==Genesis 8:12==). The story of Noah is built on sevens: seven pairs of every clean animal (==Genesis 7:2==), seven days of waiting before the rain (==Genesis 7:10==), and now seven days at a time while eight people wait to be released into their next season.

The number is not incidental. Six is the number of man—man was created on the sixth day, and the mark of the beast is man's number in excess (==Revelation 13:18==). Seven is the number of completion and rest—of man coming to the end of himself and relying wholly on God. On the seventh day no work was done; man rested with God (==Genesis 2:2-3==). A priest could not begin his service until he had remained seven days at the door of the tabernacle (==Leviticus 8:33-35==). Elijah prayed seven times on Carmel before the rain came (==1 Kings 18:43-44==). The seventh day is the Sabbath, the seventh month holds the Day of Atonement, the seventh year is the year the land rests, and the seventh seven-year cycle brings the Jubilee when debts are forgiven (==Leviticus 25:4==; ==Leviticus 25:8-10==). At Jericho seven priests carried seven trumpets, and on the seventh day they circled the city seven times—and Israel watched what God does to impenetrable walls when man has come to the end of himself (==Joshua 6:4==).

This is why God made Noah wait. Before He brought Noah into the next season, He brought him to the end of himself. The delay was not neglect; it was the last stage of preparation. God brings His people to the end of themselves so that the old self can die, so that His power can fill an emptied vessel (==2 Corinthians 12:9==), so that faith can be tested and found genuine (==1 Peter 1:7==), so that endurance can be formed (==James 1:3-4==), and so that He alone receives the glory for what comes next. The seven days Noah waited were doing that work.
