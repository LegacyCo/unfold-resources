## Commentary
“God is Spirit.” We usually quote those three words in discussions about worship. But they also unlock something in the very first chapter of the Bible.

When God said, “Let us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness” (==Genesis 1:26==), whatever else that means, it first and foremost means we are spiritual beings—made in the image of the God who is Spirit. And spirit is unlike anything else in creation. Spirit has no boundary to it. It isn’t confined by skin. Spirits don’t sit side by side like bricks; spirit permeates, the way breath fills a room. Which means that in God’s design, spirit can indwell another without mixing into a diluted mass. Perfect Love can fully possess you without erasing your personality or your uniqueness.

That is the mystery at the heart of the Trinity: the Godhead is a mutual indwelling without any loss of self (==John 14:10-11==). Marriage carries an echo of it, too: two distinct individuals, with their own identities, giftings, and dreams, joined so completely that heaven recognizes them as one—without either losing who they are (==Genesis 2:24==).

So the image of God in humanity was never mere resemblance. We were *designed* for union—created as spiritual beings to be one with God while retaining our own unique created purpose. Sin broke that union; Adam became a man of the flesh when God had designed him to be a man of the spirit. But in Christ, the design is restored: “he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him” (==1 Corinthians 6:17==). The God who is Spirit made you to be one with Him—His fullness filling your particular, unrepeatable life without overwriting a single line of it.
