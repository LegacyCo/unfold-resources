## Commentary
“O LORD, I know the way of man is not in himself; it is not in man who walks to direct his own steps.”

Jeremiah states it as something he *knows*—settled, learned, no longer up for debate: the capacity to direct his own steps is simply not in man. This is not a comment about intelligence or sincerity. Men's intentions can be good intentions, even godly intentions, attached to genuinely good ideas—and the stories of Scripture teach that man's intentions and ideas, left to themselves, lead away from God's intentions every time. “There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death” (==Proverbs 14:12==). The problem is not that our ways seem wrong and we choose them anyway; it is that they seem *right*.

This is why God's counsel to His people is not “try harder to choose well” but “stop leaning”: “Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths” (==Proverbs 3:5-6==). The alternative to self-direction is not paralysis; it is instruction—“I will instruct you and teach you in the way you should go; I will guide you with My eye” (==Psalm 32:8==)—and the same psalm warns against being like the horse or mule that must be forced. Jeremiah's confession is the doorway to being led. The person who still believes the way of man is in himself will keep directing his own steps; the person who knows it is not will finally ask for directions.
