## Commentary
“He has delivered us from the power of darkness and *transferred* us into the kingdom of the Son of His love.”

That word “transferred” deserves a long look. In the ancient world it described the deportation of a population from one country to another after a military defeat—a conquering king relocating an entire people into his own domain. But notice the glorious reversal: earthly rulers transfer the *defeated*. Jesus transfers the *victorious*. The population He relocates isn’t marched out in chains; it’s carried out in triumph, from the domain of darkness into the kingdom of the Son.

It’s the Exodus pattern. God rescued Israel *out* of Egypt so He could bring them *in* to the land of promise (==Deuteronomy 6:23==). He brings us out—of sin, of shame, out from under the authority of the devil—so He can bring us in. And where is “in”? Seated with Christ in heavenly places (==Ephesians 2:6==), citizens of a kingdom whose King has already overcome every enemy. “As He is, so are we in this world” (==1 John 4:17==).

This should transform the way we pray. If the battle is already won, we are not praying *for* victory—we are praying *from* it. Those are two entirely different postures. One begs from the outside of an outcome still in doubt; the other administrates an outcome already secured, with unwavering faith and unstoppable hope.

Before her coronation, the queen of England was taken into the treasury to see the crown. She said to the attendant, almost sheepishly, “It’s so beautiful—do you think I could try it on?” The attendant replied, “Ma’am, it’s *your* crown. You can do whatever you want with it.” Many of us pray timidly because we don’t know what seat we’re actually sitting in. But Jesus has made us a kingdom of priests (==Revelation 1:6==). Come boldly to the throne of grace (==Hebrews 4:16==)—not to try on borrowed authority, but to wear what the King already placed on your head. It’s your crown. Pray like it.

## Word Study
- *transferred* : **methistēmi** — to remove from one place and settle in another; used in the ancient world for the forced relocation of conquered peoples into the victor’s realm. Here, the Victor relocates us—not as spoils of war, but as sons brought home.
