## Commentary
Jesus’ response sounds almost unnecessarily harsh until we understand what the man is likely asking.

The man says, “Lord, let me first go and bury my father” (==Matthew 8:21==). We naturally picture a grieving son whose father has just died and who is asking Jesus for a few hours to attend the funeral. But that is probably not the situation.

In the Jewish world, burial normally happened very quickly after death. If the man’s father had just died, we would expect him to be occupied with the burial rather than standing among the crowd discussing whether to follow Jesus. For this reason, many interpreters understand “let me bury my father” as an idiomatic way of saying, _Let me remain with my father until he dies and fulfill my obligations to him._

There was also an economic dimension to that obligation. Family property and inheritance were deeply connected to the death of the father and the son’s place within the household. One scholarly interpretation of this passage understands the man’s request as involving continued responsibility within the family until his father’s death, after which his inheritance would be secured.

If so, the important word in the man’s request is _first._

“Lord, let me _first_ go…”

The problem is not that he loves his father too much. Scripture repeatedly commands children to honor their parents (==Exodus 20:12==), and Jesus Himself affirms that command (==Matthew 15:4==). Nor is Jesus establishing a general rule that disciples should abandon family responsibilities.

The issue is that the man wants to follow Jesus _after his other securities have been settled._

His request amounts to something like: _I will follow You, but first let me remain where I am until my responsibilities are finished, my father has died, and my future is secure._

That changes the nature of Jesus’ answer: “Follow Me, and let the dead bury their own dead” (==Matthew 8:22==).

Jesus is contrasting physical death with spiritual life. Let those who remain occupied entirely with the concerns of the present age attend to its affairs. _You have been called to follow Me._

And notice how perfectly this follows ==Matthew 8:19–20==.

The first man says, “I will follow You wherever You go,” and Jesus warns him that following the Son of Man may mean having nowhere to lay his head.

The second man effectively says, _I will follow You, but first let me make sure I have somewhere to come back to._

One is confronted with the loss of security. The other wants to secure his future before surrendering it. That is the deeper challenge of this passage. The man seems willing to follow Jesus, but he wants a backup plan. He wants to know that if following Jesus does not turn out as expected, the old life—and potentially the family inheritance—will still be waiting for him.

Jesus does not permit that kind of discipleship.

“Follow Me” means placing your future in His hands before you know where following Him will lead.

## Application
Following Jesus often requires us to surrender the need for a backup plan.

We can say, _“Lord, I will follow You,”_ while still adding, _“but first…”_ First let me make sure the finances are secure. First let me know how this will turn out. First let me leave myself a way back if obedience becomes more costly than expected.

But faith rarely gets that kind of guarantee.

When Jesus says, “Follow Me” (==Matthew 8:22==), He is asking us to entrust not only our present to Him, but our future. There are moments when obedience means stepping forward without preserving an escape route back to the life He called us from.

Ask yourself: _What am I keeping as a backup plan in case following Jesus does not turn out the way I hoped?_

Whatever comes to mind may be the very thing Jesus is asking you to place into His hands. But Jesus does not ask you to step into an uncertain future alone. He asks you to follow _Him._ You may not know where the road leads, but you know Who is leading you. And when Jesus is in front of you, whatever you leave behind can never be more secure than what you will find in Him.
