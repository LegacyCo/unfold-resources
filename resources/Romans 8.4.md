## Commentary
“…that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.”

Catch the preposition, because the gospel is hiding in it. The righteous requirement of the law is fulfilled *in* us—not *by* us. We couldn’t fulfill it; the law was weak through our flesh, so God did what the law could not do by sending His own Son (==Romans 8:3==). Jesus suffered the condemnation that belonged to us, and now the very righteousness the law demanded but could never produce gets worked out in the lives of people who walk by the Spirit. The law made demands from the outside; the Spirit produces the goods from the inside. That’s the difference between the old covenant and the new.

And the gospel really does transform at that depth. It takes a person whose life is moving away from God—dominated by self-seeking, steered by the appetites of the flesh—and makes him a new creature dominated by God’s will and the desires of the Spirit who now lives in him. In Christ, you no longer live *in* the flesh, and you’re no longer obligated to live *according to* it. The law of sin and death has lost jurisdiction over you (==Romans 8:2==).

But notice the word Paul chooses for how this new life gets lived: *walk*.

## Word Study
- *walk* : **peripateō** — literally to walk around; used figuratively for the whole conduct of one’s life, the ordinary, step-by-step way a person lives. Paul uses it repeatedly to contrast the old life and the new: we once “walked according to the course of this world” (==Ephesians 2:2==), but now we “walk in newness of life” (==Romans 6:4==), “walk by faith” (==2 Corinthians 5:7==), and “walk in the Spirit” (==Galatians 5:16==).

Summary: Paul didn’t say fly according to the Spirit, or leap, or soar. He said *walk*—the least dramatic verb of transport in the language. Life in the Spirit isn’t a series of mountaintop moments; it’s a direction traveled one ordinary step at a time: the next decision, the next conversation, the next yielding. The promise is real, but it isn’t automatic—the reborn person must keep choosing, step after step, to live in the reality that is already his. That’s why the exhortation never stops: you live in the Spirit; now walk in Him.
