## Commentary
Fear and shame caused Adam to hide from God. Let's look at shame first and then fear.

If you go back in the story to chapter 2, which is before Adam and Eve sinned, v25 says that they were naked and *not* ashamed. But now, as a result of being guilty of disobeying God, they feel shame for the first time. 

Guilt is a condition based on something you have done. 
Shame is a feeling. 
Guilt says, "I have done something bad." 
Shame says, "I am bad." 
Being able to acknowledge our guilt and experience conviction from the Holy Spirit when we have done something wrong is good...it acknowledges guilt and leads to repentance. 

But shame tries to lie to you and tell you that there is something fundamentally broken with who you are.

Shame is not from God. Shame is a result of man’s sin in Genesis 3.

## Application
Studies have been done on the brain to see what happens neurologically to a person when they experience shame. The results of these studies show that there are two parts of our brains that activate when we feel shame. These parts of the brain cause us to form moral judgements about ourselves *and* respond to what those judgements are telling us. 

When we are experiencing shame, our brains actually react as if we were facing physical danger. So the result of feeling shame is that our brain will generate a fight/flight/freeze response. The flight response to shame triggers the feeling of needing to disappear. Studies show that children who have this response to shame will try to become invisible. They will literally look smaller and their expressions become blank so as not to draw attention to themselves. One of the doctors conducting these studies, Dr. Bessel van der Kolk, concluded, "People who feel this quality of shame go through the world hiding.” 

And in Adam’s response to his shame, he too tried to make himself invisible; ”I heard the sound of You in the garden...so I hid myself.” Don’t let shame keep you from the loving God who is looking for you.
