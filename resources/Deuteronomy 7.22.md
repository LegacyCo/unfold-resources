## Commentary
The Israelites would take the land "little by little," or else the beasts of the field would become too numerous for them. These "beasts of the field" are the same creatures God gave Adam dominion over and commissioned him to subdue (see Genesis 1:24, 26, & 28). 

The assumption is that the seven wicked nations, by way of their occupation of the land, are subduing the wild creatures. The Israelites are not numerous enough to fill the void that would be left by all seven nations being removed at once. God's plan is a patient, multi-generational plan—which would be for Israel to multiply to a population size that is sufficient to fill the void, thus subduing the wild animals by sheer numbers.

A couple of questions to consider:

Why would Israel not have authority over the beasts of the field? God intended man to subdue creation by way of the authority He gave them. This shows that Israel may have been chosen and blessed and given promises, but God did not/could not give them the Genesis 1 Garden authority...not in the current state they were.

Why would God not give them divine protection from the beasts? He was giving them protection from the seven nations that occupied the land, so why not the wild animals of that land? Israel had just been in the wilderness for 40 years, where there were many "beasts of the field." Why are the beasts all the sudden an issue when Israel is coming into the inhabited land? 

God was demonstrating Himself *to* Israel so that one day He could demonstrate Himself *through* Israel...namely, the King of Israel and His people, the church. It would be that demonstration which gained back the lost authority of the Garden.

If you look back at Genesis 1:26, you see God giving man dominion over every living creature, but He does not include the land and the fruit of the land in that authority. God's intention was that man would subdue the earth by way of filling it with himself, by being fruitful and multiplying the image of God in man across the earth (see Genesis 1:28).

The living creatures, in which there was life and a living soul (see Genesis 1:30) required dominion and authority to be subdued. Israel did not have this authority, so they would need population in order to subdue...exactly the same method that was effective for the Caananites, Jebusites, etc. Such a vivid picture of the old covenant.

God is not anxious or in a hurry for His people to take the land. What is ultimate to Him is that His people survive, multiply, and know Him.
