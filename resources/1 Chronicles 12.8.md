## Commentary
“Some Gadites joined David at the stronghold in the wilderness, mighty men of valor, men trained for battle, who could handle shield and spear, whose faces were like the faces of lions, and were as swift as gazelles on the mountains.”

Gad's territory was on the east side of the Jordan. So getting to David meant leaving home, crossing the river, and heading into the Judean wilderness. And verse 15 tells us when they crossed: “in the first month, when it had overflowed all its banks.” That's the Jordan at flood stage, the same obstacle that stopped all of Israel in Joshua's day until God cut off the waters (==Joshua 3:15-16==). The Gadites went through it, and then put to flight everyone who stood in their way on both sides of the valley.

Nobody else in the chapter gets a description like theirs. They had faces like lions, which echoes Moses' blessing over their tribe: Gad “dwells as a lion” (==Deuteronomy 33:20==). They were fast as gazelles and skilled with shield and spear. They were captains, the least of them over a hundred men and the greatest over a thousand (==1 Chronicles 12:14==). Men like that had options. Saul's army would've taken them in a heartbeat. But they brought all that strength to a hideout in the wilderness, where God's anointed king was living like an outlaw.

And there was nothing comfortable about David's stronghold. There were no guarantees either. These men shared David's hardship before they ever shared his kingdom. It works the same way for those who follow Jesus: “if indeed we suffer with Him, that we may also be glorified together” (==Romans 8:17==).

## Word Study
**Joined** is the Hebrew *badal* (בָּדַל)—to divide, to separate, to set apart. Older translations say they “separated themselves unto David.” It's the word used when God “divided the light from the darkness” (==Genesis 1:4==), when He told Israel, “I… have separated you from the peoples, that you should be Mine” (==Leviticus 20:26==), and when the Levites were separated from the congregation to be brought near to Him (==Numbers 16:9==). In Scripture, you're separated *from* one thing because you're separated *to* another. Summary: the Gadites' left what they had so they could belong to what they saw in David.
