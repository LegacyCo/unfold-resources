## Commentary
“When He saw their faith, He said to him, ‘Man, your sins are forgiven you.’”

The paralytic never asks Jesus to forgive him. Luke records no confession, apology, or promise that the man will change. Jesus sees the faith that carried him through the roof and responds by declaring his sins forgiven.

This does not mean forgiveness is arbitrary, as though the man happened to catch God on a generous day. God is righteous. His mercy never asks Him to be unjust. The cross would reveal the righteous foundation beneath every act of forgiveness Jesus extended (==Romans 3:25-26==).

But this moment does reveal how ready God is to forgive.

Faith was the movement that brought the man toward Jesus. It did not earn forgiveness; it placed him before the One from whom forgiveness freely comes. Like a door designed to open when someone approaches it, something in the heart of God responds to faith in the person who turns toward Him.

Religion wants a formula it can measure:

Did he say the right words?  
Did he confess every failure?  
Did he demonstrate enough sorrow?  
Did he follow the correct process?

Jesus looked beneath everything people could measure and saw faith.

God still sees what man cannot. Man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart (==1 Samuel 16:7==). Before repentance becomes a polished sentence, God sees the internal turning that caused a sinner to move toward Him. Before the man lowered through the roof could explain why he had come, Jesus already knew.

Faith does not force open a reluctant heaven. Faith discovers that heaven was more ready to forgive than we were prepared to believe.
