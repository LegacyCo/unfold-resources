## Commentary
Where was the moment of repentance? (cf [[17e]]) Where was the sacrifice? How was it legal for Jesus to extend forgiveness to a man who had not even asked for it? On what basis did Jesus have the authority to forgive sins? He is God. And on what basis did the man have the right to receive forgiveness? What sacrifice was made that pulled forgiveness down? 

Nothing had changed in this man's life except he had faith to believe this Rabbi had the power and the authority to change his life. He saw Jesus as kind and good. When Jesus saw faith, He gave forgiveness. He is the God who desires mercy and obedience, not sacrifice. 1 Samuel 15:22 says God takes no delight in sacrifices, but rather on those who obey the voice of the Lord. "Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice." Faith tugged on the heart of the Lord in a way that a thousand slaughtered lambs never could.

Was this a one-time exception to the typical way that Jesus dealt with people, or is this instance revealing something about the nature of God that Jesus came to reveal? 

No, this was not an exception:
-In Luke 7, a woman of questionable character finds her way to Jesus while He was eating in the home of a Pharisee. where Jesus had been invited to have a meal with a Pharisee in the Pharisee's home. As she sat at His feet washing them with her tears, He says, "Your faith has saved you. Go in peace" (Luke 7:50). No request for forgiveness. No confession of sin. Just undeserved mercy.

-In John 8, we find the story of the woman caught in adultery. The Pharisees bring her to Jesus, insisting that she be stoned for her sin. But Jesus — His response was to stoop down in the dust and write something with His finger. 

Was this a stalling tactic so He could figure out what to do? Was He writing out sins that each of them were guilty of? Or was He pausing and remembering the moment at the beginning of time when He had stooped in the dust of the garden and formed man? 

Who can say. 

But when He does finally speak, He looks at the Pharisees, "Fine, you want to condemn? Then condemn. Kill the woman for her sin. Whoever of you is without sin, you get to throw the first stone." 

The NKJV translates v9, saying that they were "convicted by their conscience." Jesus is left alone with the woman. "Woman, where are your accusers? Has no one condemned you?" "No one, Lord." "Neither do I condemn you; go and sin no more."

Where was the woman's apology? Where was the plea for forgiveness? 

It's not there, at least not in the way that we would prefer to see it. 

Was there repentance? Absolutely. If there was forgiveness, then there was first repentance. But remember, repentance is not so much a verbal declaration of sorrow as it is a changing of the mind about who God is. And God is revealing Himself through Jesus to be a God so eager to extend forgiveness that He can't wait for the time it takes for what happened inside of the woman to become words that come out of the woman. 

Paul writes in Romans 10:10, "With the heart one believes unto righteousness." Then he quotes Isaiah, "Whoever believes in Him will not be put to shame." This woman believed in the Man stooping down in the dust in the midst of accusation and guilt. The woman wiping His feet with her tears believed in the Man sitting at the table she would never be invited to. The paralytic believed in the Man even when the way to Him was blocked by religion and law. 

And Jesus was not going to let any of those things keep them in shame.

## Application
Our God is not a God of condemnation. He is not a God of ritual sacrifice and legalistic order. He is not demanding you get it right according to the letter of the law before He grudgingly forgives. He is not waiting for you to say the right formula of words before He extends grace. That has more in line with witchcraft then it does His kingdom, yet we bring those things into His house and attach our ideas about who He is to Him: some say you have to do *this* to be saved, *that* to be saved. Some say you have to be baptized to be saved. Others say you have to take communion to be saved. And yet others say you have to speak in tongues to be saved. Meanwhile, the world is stuck outside in their sin and can't find their way to Jesus because the way is blocked by legalists and hypocrites...Pharisees and scribes who think they have God all figured out.

Jesus Christ, our Redeemer and Savior, has the power and authority to forgive sins. A broken and contrite heart He will not turn away. We cannot be a people who become the stumbling block and the barrier to the broken hearts that Jesus is longing to forgive.
