## Commentary
“For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them.”

We quote this verse for comfort—and it is comforting. But read it for what it actually claims: when the church gathers, Jesus gathers. Physically absent, yes, but present by His Spirit. But here’s an important follow-up question: in what *capacity* is He present? Because Jesus doesn’t show up to our gatherings as a spectator to what we’re doing. He is there as Lord, as King, and as Chief Shepherd of His sheep (==1 Peter 5:4==). He doesn’t attend the meeting. He presides over it.

When we worship, we aren’t performing songs about a distant God—we’re singing to a King who is standing in the room receiving praise. When we study the Scriptures, we aren’t reviewing a historical record—we’re learning about the Person in our midst, the way He opened the Scriptures on the Emmaus road and explained “in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself” (==Luke 24:27==). When we fellowship, we aren’t just hanging out—we’re functioning as members of His body, encouraging and building one another up under His eye. And when we come to the table, it isn’t just a meal. It’s the *Lord’s* supper (==1 Corinthians 11:20==)—He’s the host, not the honored memory.

This is why we don’t have the right to gather however we want to gather. We are His body, and He is in the midst of it—walking among His lampstands, seeing everything, leading everything He’s allowed to lead (==Revelation 2:1==). The church that takes Matthew 18:20 seriously stops asking, “What do we want to do when we meet?” and starts asking a much better question: “Lord, You’re here with us—what do You want to do?”
