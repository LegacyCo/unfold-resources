## Commentary
“…that their hearts may be encouraged, being knit together in love, and attaining to all riches of the full assurance of understanding, to the knowledge of the mystery of God.”

Paul’s desire for believers he had never met face to face (==Colossians 2:1==) was not merely that they would gather—it was that their hearts would be *knit together* in love. Knitting is interior work. Threads don’t just sit beside each other; they are looped through one another until they hold as one fabric. And notice what Paul says the knitting leads to: encouraged hearts, full assurance, and the knowledge of the mystery of God. The riches come to a people who are joined, not just assembled.

Think of it this way: would you live in a house that had walls but no electricity or running water? The structure can be standing and still have no life in it, because the life of a house runs through what is inside the walls—the hidden connectedness of wires and pipes. Stones gathered into a room is the beginning of a house, not the end of one. A church, according to God’s design, is not defined by the number of people who attend its meetings. It is the interconnected body of people who have been transformed by Jesus and are joined to one another in genuine relationship—“knit together by joints and ligaments,” growing with a growth that is from God (==Colossians 2:19==; ==Ephesians 4:16==). Casual proximity once or twice a week cannot produce that. Love looping lives through one another does.

## Application
Do the knitting on purpose, because it doesn’t happen by accident. Exchange phone numbers and invite someone into your home. Take a meal to a family when they’re sick—or for no reason at all. Watch someone’s kids so their parents can have a night out. Help someone move; be the first call when a car breaks down. Ask someone to disciple you, and ask the Lord for someone to disciple. Be accountable to someone who is also accountable to you. Take the initiative—be the first to invite, the first to share your heart, the first to let someone in when you’re hurting. Every one of these is a thread passing through another life. Enough of them, over enough time, and a gathering of strangers becomes what Paul prayed for: hearts knit together in love.
