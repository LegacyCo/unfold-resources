## Commentary
After months inside the ark, Noah finally steps onto dry ground. There are homes to build, crops to plant, animals to tend, and an entire human civilization to begin again. Yet before Noah does any of it, “Noah built an altar to the LORD” (==Genesis 8:20==).

This is the first explicit mention of an altar in Scripture.

Noah takes from the clean animals and birds and offers burnt offerings to God. What is particularly significant is that these animals are scarce. Noah does not have enormous herds from which he can comfortably give something away. These animals have just survived the flood with him.

His offering therefore costs him something.

But Noah understands that the new world beneath his feet is not something he preserved for himself. _It is something God gave back to him._

So his first response to deliverance is worship.

There is a principle here that will echo throughout Scripture: salvation produces worship. Israel will eventually pass through the waters of the Red Sea and respond with a song (==Exodus 14:29–31==; ==Exodus 15:1==). The redeemed surrounding the throne will sing because the Lamb has redeemed them (==Revelation 5:9==).

Noah establishes that pattern early in the biblical story.

He survives the waters, steps onto dry ground, and worships.

Before Noah begins building his new life, _he acknowledges the One who gave it to him._
