## Commentary
Jesus dismantles the oath system piece by piece, and His reasoning matters as much as His command. Do not swear by heaven—it is God’s throne. Not by the earth—it is His footstool (==Isaiah 66:1==). Not by Jerusalem—it is the city of the great King (==Psalm 48:2==). Then verse 36 removes the last refuge: not even by your own head, “because you cannot make one hair white or black.” The one thing a man might assume belongs entirely to himself—his own person—turns out to be God’s as well.

The whole hierarchy of oaths rested on a hidden assumption: that some things are connected to God and other things are not. That God attends to the sacred zones of life—the vow, the prayer, the sanctuary—while the ordinary zones remain ours to manage. Jesus’ logic closes every gap. If heaven belongs to God, and the earth belongs to God, and even the head on your shoulders belongs to God, there is nowhere left to stand and say something you do not mean.

This transforms how we understand ordinary speech. We instinctively sort our words into weight classes—sacred speech that counts and throwaway speech that doesn’t. God does not recognize those categories. Before a word is on the tongue, He knows it altogether (==Psalm 139:4==). The casual promise, the negotiation, the way we describe someone who is not in the room—all of it is spoken in His presence.

In the kingdom there is no God-free zone, and therefore no word-free-of-God zone. What sounds at first like a heavier burden is actually an invitation into wholeness: a life in which nothing has to be hidden, because nothing was ever outside His hearing to begin with.
