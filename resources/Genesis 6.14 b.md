## Commentary
The Hebrew word translated “ark” in Genesis 6:14 is _tēḇá_. This word is rare. It appears only here in the story of Noah and later in Exodus 2, when Moses’ mother places him in a small ark among the reeds of the Nile. In both stories, a chosen deliverer is preserved from death by being placed inside a vessel of salvation. Noah is carried through the waters of judgment. Moses is carried through the waters of death. Both are preserved because God has a redemptive purpose that cannot be drowned. 

This means the ark is not just a detail in the story. It is a picture of salvation. Noah did not survive the flood because he was strong enough to outlast the storm. He survived because he was hidden inside what God had provided. The ark was God’s idea, God’s design, and God’s appointed means of rescue. Noah’s part was obedience, but the plan itself came from the heart of Yahweh.

The ark points us forward to Christ. Just as Noah was saved by being placed inside the ark, we are saved by being brought into Christ. Salvation is found in union with the One God has provided. Jesus brings us into Himself. We are hidden in Him, covered by His blood, and carried through judgment into new creation.

Here are six parallels between the ark in Genesis 6 and the salvation that is found in Jesus.

1. Salvation is God’s idea

Salvation for Noah and his family originated in the heart of God. Other than obedience to God’s word, Noah had no part in the plan. And because none of this was Noah’s idea, it wasn’t up to Noah to make sure the design was right and the boat would bring him to safety. That was God’s business. Because salvation was God’s idea, God was the one who guaranteed the result of His plan for salvation.

In the same way, Jesus is the author *and* the finisher of our faith. ==Philippians 1:6== says, “He who began a good work in you will complete it.” Because He is the One who authored salvation, He will do what only He can do: bring us through the storm. Our responsibility is the same as Noah’s: obedience to what the Lord has said. So let’s lay aside every weight, and the sin that so easily ensnares us, and run with endurance the race that’s set before us. Let’s keep our eyes on Jesus. He is the author *and* finisher of our faith, who for the joy set before Him endured the cross (==Hebrews 12:1-2==). He guaranteed safe passage through the storms of this life and the day of judgement to come.

2. There was only one ark

God did not provide a fleet of ships and say, “Take your pick.” There was only one place of refuge available. In the world today there may be multiple religions all saying that they will bring you to the other side safely. But there is only one vessel of salvation provided by God. Jesus said, “… I am the way, the truth, and the life: no one comes to the Father, except thru Me (==John 14:6==).

3. There was only one door on the ark

There was only one way onto the boat that was going to save Noah and his family. Jesus said, “I am the door. If anyone enters by Me, he will be saved…” (==John 10:9==). 

There is only one door that leads to salvation…and that door is Jesus.

There is no mention in Genesis 6 of the dimensions of the door onto the ark. That tells me that nothing was too big to fit through the door. In the same way, nothing you have done, or will do, is "too big" to fit through the door of salvation. There is always room for you in Christ.

4. The ark had one window

Not only was there one ark, and only one door on that ark, but there was only one window on the ark. If I’m designing a boat that I’m going to be on for the better part of a year, I’m putting lots of windows on my boat. And it seems like common sense to put those windows on the *sides* of the boat so you can see where you’re going and what’s going on around you. Yet God tells Noah to just put one window on his boat — and to put that window on the roof. It makes no sense. And that’s the point of Noah’s window: Noah and his family were not to be looking *out* at the storm around them, they were to keep their eyes looking *up* toward Yahweh. When storms surge around us, we have to remember where to fix our eyes — on Jesus.

5. There was no way to steer the ark

The Ark had no sails, no oars, no engine (obviously), not even a rudder to help guide the boat. But don’t think that Noah and his family just drifted aimlessly on the waters. They were carefully steered by the hand of God Himself. It’s the same for us when we trust Jesus, and enter into the Ark of His salvation. We aren’t left to drift aimlessly on the swirling tide of this age. We must remember that every moment of our journey brings us closer to the destination that He has for us.

6. The Ark was a partnership between God and man

This plan of salvation involved a partnership between God and man. This was a type and shadow of the incarnation of God, of God taking on the form of flesh (==Romans 8:3==) to bring us salvation. There was a plan in the heart of God to provide salvation for humanity, but just as Noah built the ark, humanity played a role in the execution of God’s plan for our salvation. Think of it this way: in order for Jesus to save humanity, Jesus Himself had to become a man. A man was required to take back the authority that man lost in the garden. 

God said it was the Seed of the woman who would crush the head of the serpent. But what son of Eve could ever produce the perfect blood required to destroy sin and death? He couldn’t just be a man, the Savior had to be both God *and* Man. So when the fullness of time had come, “God sent forth His Son (_divine_), born of a woman (_human_)” (==Galatians 4:4==).

Salvation was a scandalous partnership between God and man.

As man, Jesus could shed the necessary blood that’s required to atone for (cover) man’s sin; and as God, His blood would be pure. 

Because He was a man, He could stand in man’s place. 

Because He was God, He was sufficient to carry away the sin of the world.

Salvation, just like the ark, was birthed in the heart of God, and then the baton was passed to a son of Adam to walk that plan of salvation out and establish it in the earth.
