## Commentary
The phrase “your desire shall be for your husband” is difficult to translate. The ESV translates that statement, "Your desire will be contrary to your husband”, The NASB translates it, "for your husband”, The NKJV has in its footnote, "toward your husband.” However we translate the desire of the woman for her husband, we can clearly say that the relationship between the man and the woman is now in conflict. The two who once reigned as one must now learn how to live together through conflict. God's design is now broken and fallen man will now attempt to rule over fallen woman. 

It should go without saying, but this conflict and striving between man and woman are not God's design, they are the result of the Fall. When we see men and women trying to rule over the other, we see the result of sin. God created man and woman as image-bearers who would, together, show creation who God is. And as the only part of His creation that is created in His likeness, we were designed to demonstrate how two can exist as one while still being mutually distinct. That’s what God is like; existing in perfect harmony as one while always being distinct as three. The Father is the Father, the Son is the Son, and the Spirit is the Spirit. They exist in perfect unity, yet are each distinct in their roles and functions. That was God's design in man and woman.

## Application
The cross of Christ redeems this relationship. A husband and wife can now live in the harmonious design that God intended for us. 

As most of us know, this doesn't happen automatically just because we profess faith in Jesus. We must grow in maturity and understand what it means to love one another like Jesus...and serve one another like Jesus. But now, in Christ, what was broken by the Fall can now be put together again.
