## Commentary
“I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, as God Almighty, but by My name LORD I was not known to them.”

This word comes at a low moment. Moses has obeyed God, gone to Pharaoh, and everything has gotten worse—and God answers his discouragement with a revelation of His name: “I am the LORD” (==Exodus 6:2==). The patriarchs knew Him as El Shaddai, the name under which the promises were *made* (==Genesis 17:1==). Yahweh is the name under which the promises are *kept*—the verses that follow stack up what this name will do: I will bring you out, I will redeem you, I will take you as My people, I will bring you into the land (==Exodus 6:6-8==). Abraham received promises from the Almighty and died in faith without seeing them; Moses' generation would watch God keep covenant, and in the watching know Him by a name their fathers never knew (==Exodus 6:7==). Did God change between Abraham and Moses? Of course not. He revealed more of Himself. The revelation of God to His people is progressive, and each name unveils more of His nature and character.

For the roughly thirteen hundred years between Moses and Jesus, God's people knew Him as Yahweh. But when the fullness of time had come (==Galatians 4:4==), the Son revealed the name every earlier name was leading to: Father. “I have manifested Your name to the men whom You have given Me” (==John 17:6==). The Spirit now cries that name from inside every believer—“Abba, Father” (==Galatians 4:6==). He is El Shaddai. He is Yahweh. And to the family He has redeemed, He is Abba—the Father revealed in Christ (==John 14:9==).
