## Commentary
Wise counsel is not the same as giving someone the answer. Often, wise counsel is helping them recognize the wisdom God is already forming in them.

Proverbs says that the counsel, or purpose, within a person is like deep water. It is there, but it may not be immediately accessible. Fear, confusion, pain, immaturity, or competing desires can make it difficult for someone to understand what's happening in their own heart. A person of understanding who is invited into the situation doesn't just reach in and take control. They patiently draw out what's beneath the surface in the individual.

This is one of the differences between control and counsel. Control makes the decision for someone. Counsel helps them learn how to discern. Control creates dependence on the counselor. Wise counsel equips the person to walk in wisdom when the counselor is no longer there.

This is consistent with the work of ministry described in ==Ephesians 4:12==. The purpose is to equip the saints, not to keep them permanently dependent on someone else’s maturity. Good counsel teaches people how to think with God, how to recognize truth, how to examine motives, and how to apply spiritual wisdom to their own circumstances.

Making every decision for a person may feel helpful in the moment, but it can stunt their growth. It treats them like a child who can't be trusted to discern. Drawing them into the perspective of wisdom treats them like an adult who is capable of growing.

A wise counselor therefore asks good questions. What do you believe God is saying? What principle of Scripture applies here? What is motivating this decision? What fruit is this producing? What would obedience look like?
