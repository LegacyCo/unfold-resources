## Commentary
“He has not dealt with us according to our sins, nor rewarded us according to our iniquities.”

That sentence contains the whole scandal of mercy. God has not given us what our sin deserved.

This does not mean He ignored sin, excused it, or decided it didn't matter. God is too righteous to pretend evil is harmless. At the cross, He dealt completely with everything that stood between humanity and Himself. Sin was judged, its debt was paid, and its authority was canceled.

Because God dealt with our sin at the cross, He does not deal with us according to our sin.

The psalmist asks, “If You, Lord, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could stand?” (==Psalm 130:3==). The answer is no one. If God’s relationship with us were governed by a running record of everything we had done wrong, none of us could remain in His presence.

But mercy does not keep the record open. God removes our transgressions “as far as the east is from the west” (==Psalm 103:12==). He does not forgive us and then continue relating to us as the person we were before forgiveness. He separates the sinner from the sin and restores the person to Himself.

Religion tells you that God’s posture toward you rises and falls with your performance. The gospel tells you that His posture toward you was revealed at the cross.

You deserved distance, and He came near. You deserved judgment, and He carried it. You deserved death, and He gave you His life.

Mercy is God refusing to give you what your sin earned because Jesus received it in your place.

## Application
Where are you still expecting God to deal with you according to your failure?

Confess what you've done, but don't continue carrying what He has removed. Repentance agrees with God about sin. And faith agrees with God about forgiveness.

If He has removed your transgression, stop dragging it back into the relationship.
