## Commentary
In 1988, an Iranian refugee named Mehran Nasseri flew from Paris to London. Unable to produce the papers proving his identity, he was sent back—but without the right documents, France would not admit him either. Terminal 1 of Charles de Gaulle Airport became his home for eighteen years. He stood on French soil, ate French food, and spoke with French people every day. He could not have been any closer to France. But proximity is not citizenship. The immigration officer was never asking, “How hard have you worked to become French?” Entry was a matter of identity, not effort.

Jesus’ words land with the same force: “Unless your righteousness far surpasses that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven.” His listeners must have thought, “Well, there goes my chance.” The scribes and Pharisees were the religious professionals, their entire lives ordered around obedience to the law. Telling fishermen and farmers to exceed them was like telling someone who jogs twice a month to outrun the Olympic team.

But Jesus is not setting an impossible bar and waiting to watch us miss it. He is revealing the bankruptcy of one kind of righteousness so He can give us another. Many people live close to the kingdom—they know the songs, the language, and the rules, and they have adopted the culture. Yet closeness earns nothing. The kingdom is not entered by effort or proximity but by identity: not “How hard have you tried?” but “Whose child are you?” You must be born again (==John 3:3==), clothed in a righteousness you did not produce (==2 Corinthians 5:21==).

## Application
Ask yourself honestly: am I close to the kingdom, or in it? Familiarity with Christian things—the songs, the language, the gatherings, the moral code—can feel like citizenship while being only proximity. Eighteen years in the terminal never made Nasseri French.

If you find yourself pleading your effort—“I’m trying, I’m improving, I’m better than most”—notice that you are answering a question the King never asked. The question is one of identity: have you been born into His family? Stop negotiating with the immigration officer and receive what can only be given. And if you have received it, stop living like a resident of the terminal—enter the country that is now yours.
