## Commentary
One of the clearest marks of wisdom is the ability to receive correction. A person of understanding does not need to be repeatedly confronted, pressured, or disciplined. A single rebuke can reach them because their heart remains teachable. They may not enjoy the correction, but they are willing to consider it, repent, and change.

The fool is different. Even severe consequences may fail to produce understanding. The problem is not that the correction was unclear or that the consequences were too small. The problem is the posture of the heart. The fool does not delight in understanding; he only wants to express what is already within him (==Proverbs 18:2==).

This is why wisdom also teaches us to discern whom we correct. Scripture warns that rebuking a scoffer can bring hatred, insult, and harm upon the one offering correction (==Proverbs 9:7-8==). The fool does not receive correction as an invitation to grow. He experiences it as a threat to his pride and often turns against the person trying to help him.

Not every argument deserves an answer, and not every person is ready to hear the truth. There are times when continuing to correct someone is no longer wisdom. It's casting words into a heart that has already decided not to listen. Proverbs therefore tells us not to speak in the hearing of a fool when we know he will despise the wisdom of our words (==Proverbs 23:9==).

Discernment asks more than, “Is what I am saying true?” It also asks, “Is this person willing to receive it?” Correction is fruitful where humility is present. Without humility, even a hundred blows may accomplish nothing.

The wise receive one rebuke and grow. The fool can endure repeated consequences and remain unchanged.
