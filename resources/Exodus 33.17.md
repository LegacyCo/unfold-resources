## Commentary
That verse is staggering because Moses had already been given a word from the Lord. God had told Israel, “Go up to a land flowing with milk and honey; for I will not go up in your midst” (Exodus 33:3). In other words, they could have the promise, the land, the victory, and the inheritance—but not the Presence in the same way.

But Moses refused to accept a promise from God without the Presence of God. So he prayed. He appealed to the relationship he had with God and to the identity of Israel as God’s people. “If Your Presence does not go with us, do not bring us up from here” (Exodus 33:15). Moses understood the gift is meaningless if God is absent from it.

And then the Lord answered, “I will also do this thing that you have spoken.” 

This is one of the great mysteries of prayer. God is sovereign, but He has chosen to make room for His friends. He does not change His nature, His character, or His eternal purposes, but He does allow the working out of His purposes to be shaped through a praying people.

Moses wasn’t manipulating God. He was partnering with God. He was praying from within the covenant, appealing to God’s own heart, God’s own promises, and God’s own name. And the Lord received that prayer.

This means prayer is not pretending we know better than God. Prayer is coming close enough to God to carry His burden back to Him. It's boldly standing before the Lord because we have found grace in His sight.
