## Commentary
Mt. Sinai is in the region of Arabia. It is a important place in Israel's historical interaction with God. It was where God had come down in fire and gave Moses the Torah; it was the place of revelation, the place of law, the place where the covenant between God and Israel, established earlier with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, was solemnly ratified. As N.T. Wright says, "Sinai, the great mountain in Arabia, was, in that sense, the place of beginnings. It was the place to which subsequent generations looked back as the starting point of the long and checkered relationship, the often shaky marriage, between this strange, rescuing, demanding God and his willful, stiff-necked people."

Sinai was where Elijah went seeking refuge (1 Kings 19:8). 

And Sinai was where Saul of Tarsus went for the same reason.

The parallel with Elijah—the verbal echoes are so close, and the reflection on "zeal" (1 Kings 19:14) so exact, that Paul must have intended them. This implies that he, like Elijah, made a pilgrimage to Mt. Sinai in order to go back to the place where the covenant was ratified. He wanted to go and present himself before the One God, to explain that he had been "exceedingly zealous (for the law)," but that his vision, his entire worldview, had been turned on its head by an encounter with Jesus. And like Elijah, Paul received his instructions: "Go back and announce the new king."
