## Commentary
The psalm concludes with a beautiful image: “Righteousness will go before Him, and shall make His footsteps our pathway.”

Throughout this psalm, the writer has been describing the restoration of God's people and the land they inhabit. Salvation draws near, mercy and truth meet together, righteousness and peace embrace, and the land yields its increase. Now, in the closing verse, he describes righteousness going before the Lord, preparing the way for His presence.

In the ancient world, a king's arrival was often preceded by messengers who prepared the way before him. The psalmist uses similar imagery, personifying righteousness as something that goes before God. Wherever He comes, His righteousness accompanies Him. His presence establishes what is right, restores what has been broken, and brings His creation into alignment with His character.

But the second half of this verse introduces another beautiful thought: “And shall make His footsteps *our* pathway.”

The Hebrew wording allows for some variation in translation, which is why the NASB translates this "And will make His footsteps into a way." Some translations emphasize righteousness preparing a way for God's footsteps, while others emphasize His footsteps becoming a pathway. The latter offers a meaningful picture of what it means to follow God. He does not simply tell His people which way to go; He goes before them, establishing a path for them to walk.

There is a wonderful connection here to ==Psalm 23:3==, where David declares, “He leads me in the paths of righteousness for His name's sake.” The responsibility of the sheep is not to create the path, but to remain near the Shepherd who leads them.

This is an important distinction in our relationship with God. We are not attempting to establish our own righteousness in hopes that God will follow us and bless what we have built. We are following the One whose righteousness goes before us.

The invitation is to recognize where the Lord is leading and to order our lives accordingly. His footsteps become our pathway, and His righteousness becomes the manner in which we walk.
