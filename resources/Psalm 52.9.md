## Commentary
“I will praise You forever, because You have done it; and in the presence of Your saints I will wait on Your name, for it is good.”

Most of Scripture’s waiting language is personal—one soul before the Lord. This verse places it somewhere else: *in the presence of Your saints.* David commits to waiting on God’s name in the company of God’s people. Waiting is not only a private discipline; it is something the gathered family of God does together—and David adds his assessment of the practice: “for it is good.”

Corporate waiting is nearly extinct in modern gatherings, and its absence shapes them. Most services are built to eliminate silence: every minute programmed, every transition covered, stillness treated as a technical failure. But a church that never waits together never gives God room to lead the gathering anywhere the plan didn’t anticipate. The early church knew the other way—it was “as they ministered to the Lord and fasted” that the Holy Spirit spoke and redirected the course of missions history (==Acts 13:2==). The instruction of ==Psalm 46:10==, “Be still, and know that I am God,” is written to a people, not only a person.

Waiting together can be practiced anywhere the family gathers: unhurried space in worship where no one rushes to fill the quiet; a pause at the Lord’s table long enough to actually attend to Him; room in fellowship for the Spirit to bring a word, a Scripture, or a prayer no one scheduled (==1 Corinthians 14:26==). A people comfortable with the stillness and silence of heaven will hear things a programmed hour cannot deliver. David’s resolve makes a fitting one for any church family: in the presence of Your saints I will wait on Your name—because it is good.
