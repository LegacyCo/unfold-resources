## Commentary
Jesus Christ stands at the center of God’s eternal plan, but He does not stand there alone. From the beginning, hidden in the heart of God, there was a bride prepared for His Son. The Church was not a reaction to sin, or a backup plan God introduced after Israel failed. She was always part of the Father's eternal plan.

Paul says that through the Church, the manifold wisdom of God is now made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places, according to the eternal purpose accomplished in Christ Jesus. That means before Eden had a garden, before Adam drew breath, before sin fractured the world, God already had this in His heart: Christ would have a people joined to Him.

This is why Paul reaches all the way back to Genesis when he speaks about marriage and says, “This mystery is great, but I am speaking about Christ and the Church” (Ephesians 5:31-32). The covenant between husband and wife was always pointing beyond itself. It was a signpost. A shadow. A whisper of something older and greater in the heart of God.

God’s eternal purpose was never simply to forgive isolated sinners and send them to heaven. It was to unite a people to His Son. To give Christ a body, a bride, a kingdom, a family. A people who would share His life, bear His image, carry His Spirit, and display His fullness in the earth.

So when we speak of God’s eternal plan, we are not only speaking of Christ crucified, risen, and enthroned. We are speaking of Christ joined to His bride. The Head with His body. The Bridegroom with His beloved. The Son bringing many sons to glory.

Jesus is the center. But the Church is not an afterthought. She stands beside Him in the mystery of God’s purpose, chosen in Him, filled by Him, and destined to display His glory before all creation.
