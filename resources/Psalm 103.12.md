## Commentary
“As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from us.”

Notice David didn’t say north from south—that distance is measurable; travel far enough north and you eventually start heading south. But east and west never meet. Start traveling east and you will be traveling east forever. That’s the distance God chose to describe what He did with your sin.

Keep the flood in mind here. The door of the ark tells us two things about God’s heart that never change: grace and mercy. Grace gives us what we don’t deserve—life with Him. Mercy holds back what we do deserve—judgment for our sin. Noah didn’t deserve to be spared; grace washed over him so the flood couldn’t touch him. And the blood of Jesus has done something greater than the flood ever did: it has flooded the whole earth with God’s mercy, mercies that are new every morning (==Lamentations 3:22-23==).

This is why I have no patience for legalistic religion. Religion’s full-time job is reminding you of the things you’ve done. But if the blood of Jesus has shut the door on who you were, if your sin has been removed as far as the east is from the west, if love “keeps no record of wrongs” (==1 Corinthians 13:5==) and God says “their sins and their lawless deeds I will remember no more” (==Hebrews 8:12==)—then why would you keep reminding Him of things He’s already chosen to forget? There is an accuser who brings up your past for a living (==Revelation 12:10==), and he’s always hiring. Don’t take the job. When the record was demanded, heaven answered with a distance: east from west, and still traveling.
