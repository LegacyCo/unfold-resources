## Commentary
“…again He designates a certain day, saying in David, ‘Today,’ after such a long time, as it has been said: ‘Today, if you will hear His voice, do not harden your hearts.’”

The generation that heard the promise first did not enter because of disobedience (==Hebrews 4:6==)—and God’s response was not to withdraw the offer. He designated another day. Centuries after the wilderness, He spoke through David and named it: *Today* (==Psalm 95:7-8==). And the writer of Hebrews, centuries after David, tells his readers the day is still called Today. The offer that one generation forfeited was re-issued, and it has never expired.

Today is the language of God. He is YHWH, the I AM (==Exodus 3:14==)—to Him every moment is the present tense, and when He speaks, He speaks into now. Tomorrow is the language of the flesh and of unbelief. Scripture is consistent about this: do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring (==Proverbs 27:1==); do not tell your neighbor “come back tomorrow” when you have what he needs today (==Proverbs 3:28==); do not worry about tomorrow, but seek first the kingdom—today (==Matthew 6:33-34==). When God speaks a word to you and something in you answers, “That word is too big for today—the demand is too great, maybe tomorrow it will be easier”—that reply has a name in this passage. It is the beginning of a hardening heart.

Because that’s what delayed obedience actually does. The believer who answers God’s “Today” with a “tomorrow” of more suitable conditions doesn’t experience it as rebellion—it feels like prudence. But each deferral makes the heart less able to receive the same word the next time it comes. That’s why the warning is attached to hearing: “Today, *if you will hear His voice*.” The dangerous moment isn’t when God is silent. It’s when He has spoken and you are deciding when to respond. The mercy of this verse is that the day is still called Today—and the summons of it is that Today is the only day obedience ever happens on.
