## Commentary
In this verse Paul begins a sixteen verse discussion on wisdom. He begins here by saying the "wisdom of words" can render the cross of Christ "of no effect." The word translated "of no effect" is *kenoo*. This word means empty, neutralized, truth-less, powerless, without result or profit. Consider that leaning on the wrong wisdom (the wisdom of this world - v20) can render the sacrifice of Christ powerless in that situation. Choosing to trust in the wisdom of this age will cause the power of the cross to be neutralized in your moment. 

For example, the wisdom of this world says do whatever is necessary to get ahead. Lying is virtuous if it gets you where you desire to go (so says the world). But if I lie on my resume' to get a job, the blessings that Jesus died to give me are not going to be able to flow to me. By trusting in the wisdom of the world, I've cut off the power of the cross to bless with, and God will allow me to continue taking care of myself. Or if I refuse to forgive when I am wounded by another, the freedom from bitterness that Jesus died to give me will not profit me. 

It is almost inconceivable that anything could cause the cross of Christ to be of no effect; yet something that is so readily available us all — the wisdom of this world — something we are trained in from a young age, will do just that.

## Cross Reference
This passage reminds us of two other passages: ==Mark 7:13==, where Jesus tells the Pharisees and scribes that their traditions have made the word of God "of no effect" (the Greek word here is *akyroo* - to invalidate, make void, render ineffective, to take away the legal force). 

The second passage is ==Mark 4:19==, where Jesus tells the parable of the sower. Jesus says the thorny soil chokes the word of God, causing the life-giving power of the word of God to become "unfruitful" — unproductive.
