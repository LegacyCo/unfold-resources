## Commentary
“Moreover, because I have set my affection on the house of my God, I have given to the house of my God, over and above all that I have prepared for the holy house, my own special treasure of gold and silver.”

David had already prepared for the temple “with all my might” (==1 Chronicles 29:2==)—and then, on top of everything, he poured in his own personal treasure. His explanation is not duty but affection: “I have set my *affection* on the house of my God.” David was a man obsessed, and the object of his obsession was the house of the Lord. He vowed not to enter his own house, climb into his own bed, or give sleep to his eyes until he found a dwelling place for the Mighty One of Jacob (==Psalm 132:3-5==). His one desire was to dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of his life (==Psalm 27:4==). The zeal of God's house consumed him (==Psalm 69:9==)—a verse the disciples would later remember watching David's greater Son (==John 2:17==).

Why was David obsessed? Because God is obsessed. David was a man after God's own heart (==Acts 13:22==), and the desire in God's heart became the desire in David's: a dwelling place for God among His people. That desire runs from Eden to the end of the story, when the tabernacle of God is with men and He dwells with them (==Revelation 21:3==). A person after God's own heart in any generation will find the same affection forming in them—not for buildings, but for God having the dwelling He longs for among His people, a house built according to His design that He fills with Himself. What a man treasures follows what he loves; David's gold went where his affection had already gone.
