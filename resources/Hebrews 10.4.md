## Commentary
"For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins."

*Not possible.* Not "insufficient on its own" or "effective for a time." The writer of Hebrews closes the door entirely. Animal blood could *cover* sin ceremonially, could remind, could point—but it could never *take away*. God's people were in a continual state of sinning, so the priests were in a continual state of sacrificing. The endless repetition was proof the sacrificial system couldn't finish the job (==Hebrews 10:1-3==).

Why couldn't it? Because a bull never chose to die. A goat has no kinship with the man it dies for. And no animal's life is worth a human soul, let alone all of them. Sin required a sacrifice that was *willing*, that was truly *one of us*, and whose life was of *infinite* worth. Only one Person has ever fit that description. "A body You have prepared for Me... Behold, I have come to do Your will, O God" (==Hebrews 10:5-7==). Willing. Human. Divine.

That's the power of Jesus' blood: it accomplishes in one offering what thousands of years of blood offerings never could. Not another animal on the same altar, but a different *kind* of sacrifice altogether—one that doesn't need repeating because it actually removed what all the others could only rehearse. "By one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified" (==Hebrews 10:14==).

Some of us are still living like the blood of bulls and goats is all we've got—coming back to the altar again and again, hoping this time it takes. It took. The sacrifice was sufficient, and heaven proved it by raising Him (==Romans 4:25==).
