## Commentary
“Now it came to pass, when the vessels were full, that she said to her son, ‘Bring me another vessel.’ And he said to her, ‘There is not another vessel.’ So the oil ceased.”

A widow with nothing but a jar of oil is told by Elisha to borrow empty vessels from all her neighbors—“do not gather just a few” (==2 Kings 4:3==)—shut the door, and pour. The oil flowed for as long as there were vessels to receive it, and it stopped only when the vessels ran out. While the vessels sat alone in their separate houses, they held nothing. Gathered together, they received an outpouring that saved the widow's family. 

I believe this image has application for the church: as individual vessels gather, God pours, and there is real blessing in the gathering of the saints—where two or three are gathered in His name, He is there (==Matthew 18:20==).

But the gathering together is just the starting point. A way of thinking creeps into the church that says proximity to one another alone produces the oil—come, sit near other believers once or twice a week, and the gathering itself commands the blessing. The flesh loves this idea because it is the path of least resistance, and a church that believes it will settle for an attendance culture. Gathered stones are necessary for the house God is building (==1 Peter 2:5==), but no one lives in a house that has walls and nothing running through them. Without the internal connectedness—the wiring and the pipes tapped into the source—there is a structure, but no life in the walls. The house becomes a home when the stones are not just stacked but knit together in love (==Colossians 2:2==), and what God poured out at Pentecost makes that possible: one accord, one mind, nothing from selfish ambition, each considering others more important than himself (==Acts 2:46==; ==Philippians 2:2-3==). That kind of life together was impossible without the Spirit, which is why the new covenant alone can ask for it. Gathering is essential, and God honors it with oil. But the oil was never meant to be the end of the story—the vessels were gathered so that a household could live.
