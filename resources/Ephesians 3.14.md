## Commentary
"For this reason I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from whom the whole family in heaven and earth is named..."

*For this reason.* What reason? You have to go back to find out—because Paul actually started this sentence twelve verses earlier. He wrote "For this reason" in ==Ephesians 3:1==, got gloriously sidetracked into the mystery hidden for ages, and only now picks the thought back up. So what was the reason? It's the last thing he'd said before the detour: you are "being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit" (==Ephesians 2:22==).

*Because* you are the dwelling place of God—that's why Paul falls to his knees. And notice who he addresses: "the Father, from whom the whole family in heaven and earth is named." He's just described the church as a temple, a building, a holy structure—and the moment he prays about it, the metaphor changes to *family*. That's not an accident. God's house, built His way, looks like family. It's a family with a Father, and every family on earth takes its name—its very meaning—from Him.

That's why everyone who has ever been born understands something of what God is doing. Everybody has a family. That's not an accident either; it's design. God begins every human life in family so that every human being can instinctively grasp His eternal purpose (==Romans 1:19-20==). Marriage is a metaphor for Christ and the church (==Ephesians 5:32==). Fatherhood is named after *the* Father. The truth may be suppressed and the picture may be broken, but it's hidden in plain sight: God's plan is as easy to see as your own dinner table.

And look at what Paul asks for, on his knees, for this family: strength in the inner man, Christ *dwelling* in their hearts, being rooted and grounded in *love*, comprehending a love that surpasses knowledge, being filled with all the fullness of God (==Ephesians 3:16-19==). Not gifts. Not growth. Not strategy. Love, dwelling, fullness—family words. When the church gets family right, God gets His house, and glory fills the temple.

## Word Study
- *family* : **patria** (Greek) — lineage, family, clan; from *patēr*, "father." Paul's wordplay is impossible to miss in Greek: every *patria* is named from the *Patēr*. A family is, by definition, the people who share a father. The church is not named after its founder, its denomination, or its style. It's named after its Father.

Summary: "family of God" is not a warm metaphor for a religious organization. It's the literal description of a people who share a Father—and everything else about the church has to be built to fit that.
