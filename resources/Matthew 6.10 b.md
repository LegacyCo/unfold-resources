## Commentary
“Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.”

Most of us don’t realize the role we play in this prayer. We tend to pray “Your kingdom come” and then wait for God to act. But Scripture shows a consistent pattern in how God responds to that prayer: He gives the ones praying it the grace to become its answer. Israel cried out under Egyptian bondage, and God heard: “I have surely seen the oppression of My people… and have heard their cry… So I have come down to deliver them.” Then the commissioning: “Come now, therefore, and I will send *you*” (==Exodus 3:7-10==). The people prayed for deliverance; God answered by sending a man. The cry went up, and the answer came back down wearing sandals.

Paul understood that he himself was grace’s delivery method: “By the grace of God I am what I am, and His grace toward me was not in vain; but I labored more abundantly than they all, yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me” (==1 Corinthians 15:10==). The kingdom came to Ephesus, Corinth, and Galatia because grace worked through a person who carried it there. This is what it means that believers are ambassadors for the King (==2 Corinthians 5:20==): those who pray for the kingdom are enlisted in its arrival.

Larry Sparks writes in *Pentecostal Fire*: “Jesus did not give us safe perimeters for how much ‘on earth as it is in Heaven’ we could expect to see in the here and now. He just gave us that model to pray… So, how much of Heaven can we experience on earth? Perhaps as much as we are willing to press in for.” The full measure of the kingdom waits for the age to come—but the prayer Jesus taught assumes that heaven’s rule can be expressed on earth now, and that the people praying for it are part of how. To pray this line is to make yourself available for the answer.
