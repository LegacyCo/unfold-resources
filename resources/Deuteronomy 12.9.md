## Commentary
“…for as yet you have not come to the rest and the inheritance which the LORD your God is giving you.”

Notice where Israel was standing when God said this: the east side of the Jordan. Saved out of Egypt, done with Pharaoh forever—and still not at rest. Why not? “But when you cross over the Jordan… then there will be the place where the LORD your God chooses to make His name abide” (==Deuteronomy 12:10-11==). God wasn’t just bringing His people *into* rest; He was bringing them into *His* place of rest—the place where He Himself would dwell among them. And between them and that rest ran a river. Kingdom rest is always entered into. There is a crossing over that must take place, because it’s His rest, and it is only found where He is.

Rest is not the absence of activity, and it isn’t a feeling of well-being—if that were all Jesus offered, a day spa could compete with the cross. Bob Gladstone writes in *Marks of the Cross*, “Divine rest leaves a scar that signifies our death to fear and striving and our resurrection to the Messiah’s peaceful rule.” It leaves a scar because entering it requires pushing against the gravitational pull of a busy world and the inferior rest it sells: the bigger bank account, the twenty-year plan, the right network, the newest product promising to optimize your life. None of those are inherently bad—tools, fine; supplements, maybe. But they cannot be your source of peace. To enter true kingdom rest you have to cross over what the world offers and enter what only He is. 

So hear the good news and the invitation in one verse. Salvation isn’t the destination; it’s the starting line. Rest is the inheritance (==Hebrews 4:1==).
