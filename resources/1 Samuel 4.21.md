## Commentary
“Then she named the child Ichabod, saying, ‘The glory has departed from Israel!’ because the ark of God had been captured and because of her father-in-law and her husband.”

Israel still had the tabernacle. They still had priests, sacrifices, and the full apparatus of worship. What they no longer had was the glory—and a dying mother gave language to the moment: Ichabod, the glory has departed. The structures of worship can keep operating long after the presence has gone, and this is as true of churches as it was of Shiloh. When there is no evidence of God's presence in His house, the house must use something else to fill the void—hype, production, and the methods of the world compensating for the absence of power. Reinhard Bonnke said, “The less Holy Spirit we have, the more cake and coffee we need to keep the church going.”

The solution for Ichabod is not better organization. It is not better ideas, more money, more buildings, a better worship leader, or a more exciting children's ministry. The solution for Ichabod is always the same: repentance—repentance for building our own house and calling it church. We cannot build a house for ourselves and ask God to fill it; He fills empty things, and much of what we call church is too full of ourselves to be filled with Him. Israel's mistake at Shiloh was treating the ark as a tool to be deployed for their own battle plans rather than a Presence to be honored on His terms (==1 Samuel 4:3==)—and the glory left. It did not return through improved logistics. It returned when hearts turned back to the Lord: a generation later, Samuel told Israel, “If you return to the LORD with all your hearts… He will deliver you” (==1 Samuel 7:3==). A church will never experience God dwelling in its midst while it is content with His absence. The first step back from Ichabod is refusing to be.
