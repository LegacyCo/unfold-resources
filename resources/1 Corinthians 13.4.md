## Commentary
It's easy to read this passage and feel bad about yourself. We read “Love is patient, love is kind..." and more often than not, we wind up frustrated and discouraged because we aren't currently loving that way. 

But Paul doesn't write, "I do all these wonderful things"; he says "love does." And so this passage becomes a proposition: if you will receive God's love into your life, over time, the beauty of 1 Corinthians 13 will become marks of your life.


It is easy to read this passage and come away feeling bad about yourself. We hear, “Love is patient, love is kind...” and more often than not, we wind up frustrated and discouraged because we aren't currently loving that way. We measure ourselves against the passage, see how far short we fall, and feel condemned.

But notice Paul's language.

He doesn't say, “I do all these things,” or even, “You do all these things.” He says, “Love does.” Paul isn't handing us a crushing list of moral achievements to manufacture in our own strength. He's showing us the nature of love.

Love, in the truest sense, is not something we produce independently. God is love. So when His love is truly received into a human life, it begins to express its own character through the one who has yielded to it. Patience, kindness, humility, endurance—these aren't merely virtues we grit our teeth to imitate. They are the fruit of divine love at work *within* a surrendered life.

## Application
1 Corinthians 13 is a promise: if you will receive the love of God, abide in the love of God, and remain under the shaping influence of the love of God, then over time the features of this passage will become marks of your life. What love is, by grace, you will increasingly become.

This doesn't happen overnight. It happens as His love keeps confronting the carnal parts of Adam that still remain in us. It happens as we stop trying to perform love and start learning to live from love.

So don't read 1 Corinthians 13 as a list of everything you're not. Read it as a revelation of what the love of God looks like when it takes root in us.
