## Commentary
Isaiah sees the purpose of God extending far beyond the restoration of Israel. The Lord will gather “all nations and tongues,” and they will come and behold His glory. Those who escape His judgment will then be sent to people who have neither heard His fame nor seen His glory, carrying the declaration of God into the nations.

Then comes an astonishing promise: “I will also take *them* as priests and Levites.”

The wording “some of them” is in many English translations but is not present in the Hebrew. More literally, the Lord says, “I will take priests and Levites.” The Greek Septuagint includes the word _apo_—“from” or “out of”—suggesting that God will separate priests from among the Gentiles who have been gathered from the nations.

The point is this: the priesthood would not remain forever contained within the lineage of Aaron. Isaiah was looking ahead to a day when God would take people from every nation and bring them near to Himself, not merely as worshipers standing at a distance, but as priests who minister before Him.

This is fulfilled in the New Covenant. Peter writes that we are “a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special people” (==1 Peter 2:9==). What once belonged to a single tribe has now become the calling of the whole people of God. Every person who belongs to Christ has been brought near, made holy, and given a ministry unto the Lord.

The gospel does more than rescue people from judgment. It brings them into the presence of God and gives them a priestly identity. Those who had never heard His fame are now entrusted with declaring His praises. Those who had never seen His glory are now called to live in that glory and make it known.
