## Commentary
“Arise, go your way. Your faith has made you well.”

*Your* faith. If you've spent some time in the Scriptures, you know this isn’t a one-off. Jesus says the same thing to blind Bartimaeus: “Go your way; your faith has made you well” (==Mark 10:52==). He says it to the woman who washed His feet with her tears: “Your faith has saved you. Go in peace” (==Luke 7:50==). When four friends lower a paralytic through the roof, Luke says Jesus “saw their faith” and forgave the man’s sins (==Luke 5:20==).

But hold on—who healed the leper? Jesus. Who saved the woman? Jesus. So why does Jesus keep saying *your faith* did it? Because we have to make room in our theology for human agency. It’s the death of Christ that saves you, period—by grace, not merit (==Ephesians 2:8==). And it’s your faith that Jesus is who He says He is that grabs hold of everything His grace has made available. Faith is the landing strip of the kingdom—the ground where heaven touches earth. The power was fully present in Jesus as He walked past ten lepers; their cry of faith is where it touched down.

Think of a watermelon seed. Inside that one seed is the potential for endless generations of watermelons—real life, but in a latent state: existing, not yet manifest. What turns potential life into actual fruit? Put the seed in soil and water it. So which produces the harvest—the life in the seed, or the soil it lands in? Both. The seed carries the power; the soil receives it. His death is enough for the sins of the whole world (==1 John 2:2==)—the life is in the seed. So why are there unsaved, unhealed, unrestored situations all around us? The seed is still waiting for soil.

## Application
Stop waiting passively for God to someday fix what He’s already provided for. Identify one broken situation you’re standing in right now—in your body, your family, your workplace—and instead of only asking God to act, start believing He already has, and respond accordingly: pray from His victory, speak His promises over it, take the step of obedience the lepers took while they were still covered in leprosy. They were healed *as they went*. Be the landing strip.
