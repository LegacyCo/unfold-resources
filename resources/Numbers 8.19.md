## Commentary
This verse explains something important about the Levites that can easily be missed: _their presence around the tabernacle was an act of God’s mercy._

God warns that there could be a plague among Israel “when the children of Israel come near the sanctuary.” The danger was not that God did not want His people near Him. The problem was that sinful humanity could not casually enter holy space.

We have already seen how serious this was. Nadab and Abihu offered strange fire before the Lord and died (==Leviticus 10:1–2==). Later, Korah and those with him will challenge God’s appointed boundaries around the priesthood, and judgment follows (==Numbers 16:31–35==).

The Levites were positioned around the tabernacle partly to prevent Israel from approaching in a way God had not permitted. Earlier God had commanded them to camp around it “that there may be no wrath on the congregation” (==Numbers 1:53==).

So the boundaries surrounding God’s presence were not designed merely to _keep people out._ They were designed to make it possible for a holy God to dwell among His people without His holiness consuming them.

The restrictions were mercy.

This helps us appreciate just how radical the new covenant invitation really is. Hebrews says, “Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace” (==Hebrews 4:16==).

Something has fundamentally changed.

The God whose presence once required guards and boundaries has not become less holy. _We have been given a better Mediator._

God has not changed in the new covenant. His holiness has not diminished, and His standard has not been lowered. What has changed is the way humanity is able to approach Him.

Under the old covenant, access to God was mediated through an entire system of priests, sacrifices, washings, curtains, and boundaries. The Levites guarded the sanctuary. The priests could come close but not all the way into the presence. Only the high priest could enter the Most Holy Place, and even he could enter only once a year and never without blood (==Hebrews 9:6–7==). Every layer communicated the same reality: _sinful humanity cannot simply walk into the presence of a holy God._

Hebrews says that Jesus entered the Most Holy Place “once for all” by His own blood and obtained “eternal redemption” (==Hebrews 9:12==). His sacrifice does what the sacrifices of the old covenant could never finally accomplish: He cleanses the worshiper and opens the way into God’s presence (==Hebrews 10:19–22==).

That is why the curtain tearing at the death of Jesus matters so much (==Matthew 27:50–51==). The boundary did not disappear because holiness no longer mattered. The boundary disappeared because the problem the boundary represented had been dealt with.

We have a better sacrifice, a better covenant, and a better High Priest. So the command changes from _stay back_ to _draw near._

“Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace” (==Hebrews 4:16==).
