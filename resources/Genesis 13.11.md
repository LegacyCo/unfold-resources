## Commentary
“Then Lot chose for himself all the plain of Jordan, and Lot journeyed east. And they separated from each other.”

Two details in this verse carry the weight of the story. First, Lot “chose for himself.” Abram let God choose for him; Lot did his own choosing. Lot saw what the world had to offer; Abram trusted what heaven had to offer. Lot said, in effect, “I will take”; God said to Abram, “I will give” (==Genesis 13:15==). Lot chose a piece of land he would eventually lose (==Genesis 19:24-25==); God gave Abram a land that still belongs to his descendants. Lot chose what made sense; Abram trusted God for the impossible—and in the end Lot lost his family, while Abram received a family too large to count (==Genesis 13:16==).

Second, Lot “journeyed east.” Movement to the east often functions in Scripture as the direction away from the presence and will of God. When Adam sinned, he was driven out of the garden toward the east (==Genesis 3:24==). After Cain killed Abel, he went out from the presence of the LORD and dwelt east of Eden (==Genesis 4:16==). In Ezekiel’s vision, the glory of the Lord departed from the temple by the east (==Ezekiel 10:18-19==). By fixing his eyes on the beauty of the eastern plain, Lot turned his back on the ground that would become the apple of the Lord’s eye (==Zechariah 2:8==)—the same trap Reuben and Gad later fell into when they preferred the grazing land they could see over the inheritance God had promised (==Numbers 32:1-5==). Scripture sets these stories up as signposts: this is not the way the followers of God make their choices.
