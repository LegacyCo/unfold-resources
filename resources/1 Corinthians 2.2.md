## Commentary
“For I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified.”

Remember where Paul was walking when he made this determination. Corinth was a Greek city in love with rhetoric—a culture where traveling philosophers competed for followers with polished arguments and dazzling speech, where “Greeks search for wisdom” was simply the air people breathed (==1 Corinthians 1:22==). Paul had the equipment to compete in that arena; educated under Gamaliel, he could reason with the best of them (==Acts 22:3==). And he deliberately left the weapons at home: “I did not come with excellence of speech or of wisdom… I was with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling” (==1 Corinthians 2:1==; ==1 Corinthians 2:3==).

The word *determined* is important here. This was a decision, made in advance, to be reduced to one subject: Jesus Christ, and Him crucified. Listen to his reasoning: “my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power, *that your faith should not be in the wisdom of men but in the power of God*” (==1 Corinthians 2:4-5==). A faith argued into existence can be argued back out of it—whoever out-debates you owns your conclusions. But a faith born from an encounter with the power of God doesn’t rest on the cleverness of the messenger, and no smarter argument can repossess it.

The temptation in a culture of impressive words is to make the message impressive by our standards—polished, palatable, intellectually respectable. Paul chose the opposite: keep the message scandalous and simple, and trust the Spirit to supply the demonstration. The cross doesn’t need a lawyer. It needs a witness. So when you feel unqualified—not clever enough, not smooth enough, trembling like Paul in Corinth—you may be in the safest possible position: weak enough for the power to be unmistakably His (==2 Corinthians 12:9==).
