## Commentary
This verse gives us a sobering pictures of the tension of divine desire and human responsibility.

That word Jesus uses that we translate “wanted” is _thelō_. It means to will, to desire, to be resolved in purpose. Jesus is not describing a passing emotion. He is revealing the will of God. This is the same word used in verses like Ephesians 1:5 ("He predestined us to adoption as sons and daughters through Jesus Christ to Himself, *according to the good pleasure of His will*") and Galatians 1:4 ("Christ gave Himself for our sins so that He might rescue us from this present evil age, *according to the will of our God and Father*").

In Matthew 23:37, Jesus is revealing the will of God to gather His people, to cover them, and draw them under the safety of His wings.

But...

“*You* were not willing.”

The issue was not that God had no desire to save His people. The issue was that Jerusalem resisted the desire of God to save them. Jesus was willing, but they were unwilling. He wanted to gather, but they refused to be gathered.

This shows us something incredibly important about how God works in the earth. God does not simply impose His will upon unwilling people as though they were objects to be moved around on chess board called human history. He calls. He invites. He confronts. He grieves. He stretches out His hands. He makes His desire known. And He looks for yielded vessels to be the instruments of His will.

There are things God wants to do, that He *wills* to do, that require our yes.

All of His ways are an expression of His love, and love does not manipulate. Love never forces. Love gathers. Love calls. Love invites. 

## Application
“What does God want?” is a great question. But an equally important question is, “Am I willing?”
