## Commentary
“Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us. And if we know that He hears us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we have asked of Him” (==1 John 5:14-15==).

Follow John’s chain of logic, because it builds to something staggering. Ask according to His will → He hears us. He hears us → we *have* what we asked. Not “we might receive it.” We know that we have it. John is describing prayer with a settled outcome—the kind of confidence that belongs to people who pray from victory rather than for it.

But everything hangs on that phrase: *according to His will*. This is not fine print designed to explain away unanswered prayer. It’s the whole engine. Prayer at its highest isn’t persuading God to adopt our agenda; it’s discovering His agenda and asking for it out loud. That’s why the praying life and the listening life can’t be separated—we abide in Him, His words abide in us, and then we ask what we desire (==John 15:7==), because what we desire has been shaped at the source.

And there’s a warning folded in here for anyone who leads. It’s possible to be in His will with Him in prayer, and then move into our own will with the people. Moses received exact instructions at the tabernacle door—*speak* to the rock—and then walked out and struck it (==Numbers 20:8-11==). Hearing accurately isn’t the finish line. We can’t just pray; we must obey what He tells us in prayer. The confidence of verse 14 belongs to the person whose asking and whose acting both stay inside the will of the God who heard them.
