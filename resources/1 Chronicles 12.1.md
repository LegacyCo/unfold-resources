## Commentary
“Now these were the men who came to David at Ziklag while he was still a fugitive from Saul the son of Kish; and they were among the mighty men, helpers in the war.”

By this point in Chronicles, David has already been crowned. That happened in chapter 11. So chapter 12 is a flashback. It goes back several years to tell us who showed up for David when he didn't have a throne, a palace, or even a safe place to sleep in his own country.

He was living in Ziklag, a Philistine town Achish gave him while he was hiding from Saul (==1 Samuel 27:5-7==). The phrase translated “still a fugitive” describes someone who's shut in or restrained. Translation: David had been anointed king, but he couldn't even move freely in the land he'd been promised. So anyone who came to him at Ziklag was walking away from Israel to join a man Saul had declared his enemy.

That's what makes the loyalty of these men so significant. After Saul died, joining David was easy. Everybody did it—verses 23–37 count more than three hundred thousand of them. But *before* Saul died, joining David meant taking God at His word when nothing they could see backed it up. All they had to go on was that Samuel had anointed him (==1 Samuel 16:13==). They decided that was enough, and they came. 

That’s the same position every follower of Jesus is in today. He's risen, He's seated at the Father's right hand, and He is King. But Hebrews is honest about what that looks like right now: “now we do not yet see all things put under him” (==Hebrews 2:8==). The world doesn't recognize His crown yet. So following Him today still means going “to Him, outside the camp, bearing His reproach” (==Hebrews 13:13==). The night before the cross, Jesus told His disciples something that could just as easily have been said to these men of Ziklag: “You are those who have continued with Me in My trials. And I bestow upon you a kingdom” (==Luke 22:28-29==).

## Application
It's easy to be loyal to Jesus in a room where everyone already honors Him. The test comes in the places where He isn't honored—a job where faith gets mocked, a family that thinks you've taken this too far, a culture that treats His commands as outdated. Loyalty in those places is going to cost you something: your reputation, an opportunity, a place where you used to fit in. So where are you most tempted to stay quiet about belonging to Jesus until it feels safer? Decide to be known as His there, now. “If we endure, we shall also reign with Him” (==2 Timothy 2:12==).
