## Commentary
“Who is wise and understanding among you? Let him show by good conduct that his works are done in the meekness of wisdom.”

James asks for volunteers—who among you is wise?—and then immediately disqualifies every answer that comes in the form of a claim. The demonstration he accepts is not eloquence, credentials, or confidence, but *conduct*: a life whose works are done in the meekness of wisdom. In James, wisdom is never a mental possession; it is a way of living that can be observed. The wise person doesn’t announce wisdom. He shows it.

Bob Gladstone’s definition in *Marks of the Cross* matches the verse: “Wisdom is the practical knowledge of God’s unique value system… translated into a powerful, blessed way of life.” Two parts, and James requires both. Knowledge of God’s value system alone produces a theologian; translation into a way of life produces the person this verse is looking for. This immediately distinguishes heaven’s wisdom from its counterfeit, because the wisdom James goes on to describe as “earthly, sensual, demonic” shows itself in envy and self-seeking (==James 3:14-15==), while the wisdom from above is “first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits” (==James 3:17==). Both wisdoms are visible in conduct. The difference is what the conduct looks like.

This gives every believer an honest way to answer James’s opening question. Not “do I know wise things?” but “does my life align with what Scripture calls wise?” The wise restrain their words (==Proverbs 17:27==); they receive rebuke (==Proverbs 9:8==); they rule their spirit (==Proverbs 16:32==); they walk in meekness under pressure. Wisdom that exists only in the head has not yet become what James means by the word. It becomes wisdom when it shows.
