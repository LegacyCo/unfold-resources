## Commentary
“Moreover those who were near to them, from as far away as Issachar and Zebulun and Naphtali, were bringing food on donkeys and camels, on mules and oxen—provisions of flour and cakes of figs and cakes of raisins, wine and oil and oxen and sheep abundantly, for there was joy in Israel.”

The chapter starts with David shut in at Ziklag. It ends with a three-day feast at Hebron (==1 Chronicles 12:39==). And the Chronicler takes his time with the menu: flour, fig cakes, raisin cakes, wine, oil, oxen, sheep—all of it hauled in on every kind of pack animal, from tribes as far north as Naphtali. Then he tells us why: “for there was joy in Israel.”

Compare that with a scene from David's fugitive years. He once sent men to ask a wealthy man named Nabal for provisions, and Nabal said, “Who is David, and who is the son of Jesse?… Shall I then take my bread and my water and my meat… and give it to men when I do not know where they are from?” (==1 Samuel 25:10-11==). Nabal didn't recognize the king, so he held on to what he had. At Hebron the whole nation recognizes him. When people are glad about who's on the throne, they give freely.

The joy comes from the king. Israel had lived for years under a ruler God had rejected, and then through a civil war. Now the man God chose was finally in his place, and the gladness showed up around a table. Proverbs says it this way: “When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice” (==Proverbs 29:2==).

## Application
You can't get joy by trying to feel joyful. In this chapter, joy came after a decision about who would rule. So if joy's been missing, ask whether there's an area you've been keeping under your own rule—your money, a relationship, your plans for the future—and hand it over to Jesus as King. Then do what Israel did at Hebron: bring something to the table. Share a meal with other believers, and let your generosity say you're glad He reigns.
