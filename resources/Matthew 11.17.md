## Commentary
In this passage, Jesus compares His generation to children sitting in the marketplace, complaining that their friends will not participate in their games. “We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we mourned to you, and you did not lament.” The imagery comes from two familiar occasions in Jewish life: weddings and funerals. One was marked by celebration and dancing, the other by mourning and lamentation. The children were imitating these events, frustrated that others would not respond as they expected.

Jesus uses this illustration to expose the inconsistency of those who rejected both John the Baptist and Himself. John came living an austere life of fasting, separation, and prophetic confrontation, and they accused him of having a demon. Jesus came eating and drinking, sharing meals with tax collectors and sinners, and they accused Him of being a glutton and a drunkard (==Matthew 11:18–19==).

John's ministry carried the solemn urgency of a funeral dirge, calling Israel to recognize their sin and repent. Jesus' ministry carried the joy of a wedding celebration, announcing that the Bridegroom had arrived and the kingdom of heaven was at hand (==Matthew 9:15==). Both were sent by God, and both called people to respond to what God was doing. Yet the religious leaders found reasons to reject them both.

The problem was never really John's strict message or Jesus' fellowship with sinners. The problem was that neither fit the expectations of those who had already decided what a genuine move of God should look like. They wanted God to move according to their preferences, and when He didn't, they dismissed His messengers.

## Application
It is possible to become so committed to our expectations of how God should work that we fail to recognize what He is actually doing. We can be offended by the methods, the messengers, or even the people through whom God chooses to reveal Himself. We can celebrate certain expressions of His kingdom while criticizing others simply because they don't resemble what we have become accustomed to. As the old saying goes, the last words of any move of God are always the same: "We've never done it like that before."
