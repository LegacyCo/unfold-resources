## Commentary
When the Spirit rested on Eldad and Medad and they prophesied in the camp, Joshua was alarmed: “Moses, my lord, forbid them!” And Moses answered with one of the most remarkable wishes in the Old Testament: “Are you zealous for my sake? Oh, that all the LORD’s people were prophets and that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!”

Understand what Moses was wishing for. In the old covenant, the word of the Lord came to God’s people primarily through the prophet. Men like Isaiah, Elijah, and Ezekiel interacted with God in a unique way—He spoke to these individuals, and they in turn declared His word to the people. Hearing directly from the Lord was not the common experience. Moses wished it were. And for roughly fourteen centuries, that wish sat unfulfilled—until God picked it up and turned it into a promise: “I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy” (==Joel 2:28==).

Then came Pentecost, and Peter stood up and announced that the waiting was over: “This is what was spoken by the prophet Joel” (==Acts 2:16-18==). Trace the pattern through Luke and Acts and you’ll notice something: when the Holy Spirit comes upon someone, that person *speaks*. Elizabeth was filled with the Spirit and blessed Mary with a loud voice (==Luke 1:41-42==). Zacharias was filled with the Spirit and prophesied (==Luke 1:67==). The disciples were filled with the Spirit and began to speak (==Acts 2:4==). The Spirit’s presence produces speech—God’s heart coming out of human mouths.

That’s the covenant you live in. The Word became flesh and dwelt among us (==John 1:14==), and the Spirit has been poured out on all who believe in Him. Moses’ wish is now the church’s normal. Not every believer holds the office of a prophet—but every believer has received “the Spirit who is from God, that we might know the things that have been freely given to us by God” (==1 Corinthians 2:12==). Your ears are tuned to His frequency. What Moses could only long for, you were born again into.
