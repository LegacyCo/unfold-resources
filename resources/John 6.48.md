## Commentary
This is from "The Temptations of Christ" by Art Katz: Notice how Jesus' allusion to the eating of His body offended the religious Jews. Yet, to the measure that the Word of God has lost its value, and bodily things have been elevated, the same can be said for what has happened to the Lord's Supper, to Holy Communion itself. I can understand the reaction against the Roman Catholic celebration of the Eucharist as being the actual body and blood, but have we Protestants not been guilty of reacting in such a way as to make the Communion arid and insubstantial? Has it not been denigrated to a mere emblem or memorial? Could it be that Communion is a vital, life-giving provision given by God for the church's very health? The scriptures record a negative consequence for those who took it wrongly, so what about those who take it rightly?
  
Can you imagine what would happen if we met daily with believers in that frequency and intensity of life as described in the book of Acts? Our impatience, our mistakes and aggravations will inevitably find expression. We will soon realize that unless we are eating and drinking of the Lord, we will not have access to His patience, His forbearance, His kindness, His love and His grace. Typically, when we ourselves are devoid of these virtues, then the sacrament is mere religious ordinance. But it should become for us, and to the measure of our faith, a life-giving provision. Perhaps we need to eat and drink more frequently with a faith that believes we are partaking of something more than a mere emblem, even a measure of the substance of the life of God and His Spirit.
