## Commentary
When the prophet told King Hezekiah his sickness would end in death, the king turned his face to the wall and prayed one word above all the others: “*Remember* now, O LORD, I pray, how I have walked before You in truth and with a loyal heart.” Before Isaiah had even crossed the middle court, the answer came: “I have heard your prayer, I have seen your tears; surely I will heal you… and I will add to your days fifteen years” (==2 Kings 20:5-6==).

“Remember” is one of Scripture’s great prayers, and the saints reach for it again and again. The psalmist: “LORD, *remember* David and all his afflictions” (==Psalm 132:1==). Moses, standing in the breach after the golden calf: “*Remember* Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, Your servants, to whom You swore by Your own self” (==Exodus 32:13==). And when God told Moses His presence wouldn’t go with Israel, Moses prayed, “*Consider* that this nation is Your people”—and God said, “My Presence will go with you” (==Exodus 33:13-14==).

Now, God doesn’t forget—so what is this prayer actually doing? It’s covenant language. To pray “remember” is to bring God’s own words, His own promises, His own character into the courtroom and appeal to them. It’s less like jogging His memory and more like presenting the contract He wrote. That’s why the remember-prayer is so powerful: it never rests on our ideas of what God should do. It rests entirely on who He has already revealed Himself to be.

Practically, it sounds like this. Praying for healing: “Jesus, I thank You that You are the Healer. *Remember, Lord, that You took this sickness upon Yourself*” (==Isaiah 53:5==). Praying for provision: “Lord, *remember that You are my Provider*—everything I have comes from You.” Praying for wisdom: “Jesus, *remember that all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in You* (==Colossians 2:3==). Don’t let me lean on my own understanding—baptize me in Your wisdom.”

Pray the promises. Pray His character back to Him.
