## Commentary
“He who loves father or mother more than Me is not worthy of Me. And he who loves son or daughter more than Me is not worthy of Me.”

This is the call of the gospel: nothing—not even the nuclear family—is worth more than following Jesus. The demand is as old as the call of Abraham. God told Abram to leave his country, his family, and his father's house (==Genesis 12:1==), and the reason is given in Joshua: “your fathers, including Terah, the father of Abraham… served other gods” (==Joshua 24:2==). Abraham's father, grandfather, uncles, and cousins were all pagan idolaters. For God to bless Abraham and make him into a nation that would bless all nations, Abraham had to come out from the life he had inherited from his fathers. The household he loved was also the household that would have kept him from God.

Most believers did not grow up worshiping idols in demonic ceremonies—but sin is sin, and before Jesus invited us to follow Him we were as lost in our inherited life as Abraham was in his (==Ephesians 2:1-3==). The separation Jesus requires is total, and He is honest that it will sometimes run through the middle of a family: “I have come to set a man against his father, a daughter against her mother” (==Matthew 10:35==). He is not commanding less love for family; He is commanding more love for Himself—a love so far above every other loyalty that by comparison the others look like hate (==Luke 14:26==). Before we can come into the land He wants to take us, we have to come out of the life that used to define us—even the parts of it we were born into.
