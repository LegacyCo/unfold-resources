## Commentary
This is a picture of the posture of a godly leader when a decision needs to be made. Moses had enormous authority in Israel, but he did not assume that authority meant he already knew what to do. When he encountered something beyond what God had already revealed, he stopped and looked to the Lord for wisdom.

The issue involved men who had become ceremonially unclean through contact with a dead body and therefore could not participate in the Passover at the appointed time (==Numbers 9:6–7==). Moses could have reasoned through the situation himself or simply made a ruling based on what seemed most practical. Instead, he waited for God to speak. The answer that followed established provision for what became known as the second Passover, allowing those who were legitimately unable to participate at the appointed time to observe it one month later (==Numbers 9:9–12==).

There is an important principle here for spiritual leadership. A godly leader does not ask, “What do I think is best?” but, “What does the Lord say?” Leadership in God’s kingdom is stewardship, not ownership. Those entrusted with responsibility are still under authority themselves.

There will always be situations where Scripture gives us clear commands, and in those cases we do not need fresh direction—we need obedience. But there will also be decisions involving timing, circumstances, people, and wisdom where the answer is not immediately obvious. The mature leader does not confuse confidence with self-sufficiency.

Moses’ response shows that sometimes the wisest thing a leader can say is, “I need to hear from the Lord before I answer.”
