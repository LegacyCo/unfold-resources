## Commentary
“For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many.”

Rulers of this world govern by taking—God's warning to Israel about human kings repeats the word six times: he will take your sons, your daughters, your fields, your flocks (==1 Samuel 8:11-17==). The Son of Man rules by the opposite spirit. He came to serve and to give. He gives His rest to the weary (==Matthew 11:28==), His flesh as bread for the life of the world (==John 6:51==), His life as a ransom. Even His throne-room promises are extensions of His giving: the tree of life, the hidden manna, authority over nations, a seat with Him on His throne (==Revelation 2:7==; ==Revelation 2:17==; ==Revelation 2:26==; ==Revelation 3:21==). The whole gospel in wrapped up in one verb: God so loved the world that He *gave* (==John 3:16==).

A man becomes like the king he serves—so the greatness Jesus defines in this verse is not a technique but an inheritance. “Whoever desires to become great among you shall be your servant” (==Mark 10:43==), because a man surrendered to the King who only gives becomes a giving man. He gives his wife his attention, his honor, and the truth. He gives his children his time, his love, and his blessing. He gives the people he leads his own life, the way his King gave His. That's what leadership looks like in the kingdom: a person who has stopped taking because they are ruled by a King who gave them everything He had.
