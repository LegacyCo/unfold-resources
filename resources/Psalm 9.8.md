## Commentary
“He shall judge the world in righteousness, and He shall administer judgment for the peoples in uprightness.”

God’s judgment is not arbitrary, emotional, or corrupt. He judges “in righteousness” and “in uprightness.” In other words, His decisions are always consistent with who He is. He cannot be bribed, manipulated, deceived, or pressured. He does not misread motives or overlook evidence. He sees completely and judges perfectly.

This would have been especially comforting in a world where human rulers were often unjust. Kings could favor the powerful, exploit the weak, or use judgment to protect their own interests. David says God is nothing like that. His throne is established for judgment (==Psalm 9:7==), and from that throne He governs with perfect justice.

And God is not just the God of Israel. He is the Judge of all the earth (==Genesis 18:25==). Every nation, ruler, system, and individual ultimately stands before Him. Human injustice may survive for a season, but it will not survive forever.

For the oppressed, this is good news.

Biblical judgment is about setting things right. When God judges righteously, He confronts evil, vindicates the afflicted, and restores what injustice has distorted. This is why the Psalms can actually celebrate the coming judgment of God (==Psalm 96:10–13==; ==Psalm 98:7–9==).

The Christian hope carries this forward. Jesus is the One through whom God will judge the world in righteousness (==Acts 17:31==). The One who was Himself falsely accused, condemned, and crucified will one day sit as the true Judge of all humanity.
