## Commentary
“Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing all kinds of sickness and all kinds of disease among the people.” Notice that Jesus does not only announce the kingdom—He demonstrates it. Proclamation and restoration travel together.

The kingdom is the rule and reign of God breaking into the earth. It confronts everything that has come under the tyranny of sin, sickness, death, and the devil (==Acts 10:38==). When Jesus heals the sick and delivers the oppressed, He is showing what His kingdom does when it comes to bear on a community: it restores what was lost to sin. He brings order to the places darkness has destroyed.

This is why the gospel of the kingdom cannot be reduced to “pray a prayer and wait to go to heaven when you die.” The hope of resurrection is real and precious—but the purpose of forgiveness is not just an address change in the afterlife. God forgives sin *so that* we can become what we were always created to be: fully alive, fully restored, image-bearing people who show the world what God is like (==Genesis 1:27==).

Matthew’s ordering in chapter 4 traces the whole process of heaven coming to earth. First the kingdom is found in Jesus. Then it is established in a people who leave their nets. Then it extends into the world through them. Jesus—His people—more people. There is no further expansion of the kingdom without a people in whom the King actually reigns; the church stands in the delicate place between the King and those far from Him.

## Application
Let your understanding of the gospel be as big as Matthew 4:23. If your Christianity consists of forgiveness plus a future relocation, ask God to enlarge it: He intends to restore everything sin has broken, and He starts now. Pray for the sick. Contend for the oppressed. Expect demonstration, not just information.

Then take your place in the sequence. The kingdom extends through people in whom it has first been established—so the question is not only “what is God doing in the world?” but “does the King reign in me?” Identify one place of disorder within your reach—a relationship, a need, a corner of your community—and carry the kingdom there the way Jesus did in Galilee: announcing and demonstrating that God restores broken things.
