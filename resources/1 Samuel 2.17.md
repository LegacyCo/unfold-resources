## Commentary
“Therefore the sin of the young men was very great before the LORD, for men abhorred the offering of the LORD.”

The young men are Hophni and Phinehas, Eli's sons—priests at Shiloh who “did not know the LORD” (==1 Samuel 2:12==). The preceding verses describe their practice: when Israelites came to sacrifice, the priests' servant would plunge a three-pronged fork into the pot and take whatever came up, and the sons demanded raw meat for roasting before the fat was burned on the altar—by force, if the worshiper objected (==1 Samuel 2:13-16==). The detail about the fat is significant: under the law, the fat belonged to the LORD—“all the fat is the LORD's” (==Leviticus 3:16==). Hophni and Phinehas were not just rude or greedy. They were seizing God's own portion, helping themselves first from what the people had brought to Him.

This verse tells us why their sin was *very great*: “for men abhorred the offering of the LORD.” The people came to Shiloh to worship and left despising the sacrifice. Corrupt ministers had made God's own appointed worship repulsive to God's own people. This is how Scripture measures the sin of those who handle holy things: not only by what they do, but by what their conduct teaches everyone watching. The priest's lips were meant to keep knowledge, and the people to seek the law from his mouth—“but you have departed from the way; you have caused many to stumble” (==Malachi 2:7-8==). God confronts the same corruption in Malachi's day, priests offering the blemished and defiled while asking, “In what way have we despised Your name?” (==Malachi 1:6-8==). And Paul says it this way: “the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you” (==Romans 2:24==). Those who serve at the altar shape what the people believe about the God of the altar. Hophni and Phinehas made men despise the offering of the LORD, and the LORD counted that among the greatest of sins—great enough that the judgment fell against the whole house (==1 Samuel 2:27-34==). But in the same chapter, a hopeful contrast is already emerging: “but Samuel ministered before the LORD” (==1 Samuel 2:18==).
