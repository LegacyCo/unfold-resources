## Commentary
“Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life.”

The command assumes the heart requires keeping—that left alone, it does not stay as it is. Anyone who has planted tomatoes understands this. The first few years bring explosive growth and heavy fruit; planting seems easy. But stay with it long enough and the harvest shrinks, because tomatoes are heavy consumers of nutrients—over time they deplete the soil. If you only plant and water the same way every year, you get fewer and fewer tomatoes. Neglecting the soil diminishes the fruit.

The heart works the same way. Whether you have walked with Jesus one year or fifty, the soil must be tended, because life in a fallen world depletes it: offenses, disappointments, unmet expectations, grief, financial strain, difficult people, injuries, cancellations, pandemics, politics, war. All of it works to harden the soil of the heart and diminish fruitfulness (==Mark 4:18-19==). Scripture divides the labor: tending the garden of the heart is our responsibility; giving the increase is God's (==1 Corinthians 3:6-7==). Abiding in Him is what produces fruit (==John 15:5==)—amen—but anything left untended slips into decay, and a relationship with Jesus is not exempt. Sitting back year after year expecting last year's fruit, without ever pushing against the trajectory of decay, is a recipe for decrease. Yesterday's bread will not sustain you the way fresh bread will (==Exodus 16:19-20==). Keep the heart—with *all* diligence—because everything in life flows out of it.

## Application
Identify what has been depleting your soil this season—the specific offense, disappointment, or grind that has been hardening the ground—and tend to it deliberately: forgive what needs forgiving, grieve what needs grieving, and bring the depletion to God rather than planting over it another year. Then feed the soil on purpose: fresh seeking, fresh word, fresh worship. The fruit is His to give. The soil is yours to keep.
