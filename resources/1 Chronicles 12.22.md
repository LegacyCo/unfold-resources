## Commentary
“For at that time they came to David day by day to help him, until it was a great army, like the army of God.”

David's army didn't show up all at once. It started with about four hundred men at the cave of Adullam, and most of them were in distress, in debt, or discontented (==1 Samuel 22:2==). A while later it was six hundred (==1 Samuel 23:13==). This verse describes what happened at Ziklag, near the end of his fugitive years: men kept coming. A few today, a few more tomorrow. No single day would've looked like much. But by the time Saul died, it was “a great army, like the army of God.”

That phrase, “army of God,” may simply be the Hebrew way of saying *an extremely great army*. It's also what Jacob said when the angels of God met him on his way back to Canaan: “This is God's camp” (==Genesis 32:2==). Either way, what was gathering around David in the wilderness was God's doing. God had promised him a kingdom, and He was putting it together during the very years when David looked like he had nothing.

God builds a lot of things this way. Israel gathered manna one morning at a time (==Exodus 16:4==). The Lord added to the early church “daily” (==Acts 2:47==). And when the returned exiles were discouraged at how small the rebuilt temple looked, God's word to them was, “who has despised the day of small things?” (==Zechariah 4:10==).

## Application
If growth feels slow right now—in your ministry, your family, your own character—measure your faithfulness by the day and leave the total with God. You can't judge what He's building by what one day adds to it.
