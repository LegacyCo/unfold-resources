## Commentary
Paul turns from the obvious rebellion of Romans 1 to the hidden rebellion of the moral person in Romans 2. The person in Romans 1 suppresses the truth through unrighteousness. The person in Romans 2 may recognize unrighteousness clearly enough to condemn it in someone else, but not humbly enough to repent of it in themselves.

Paul is not saying all judgment or moral discernment is wrong. Scripture repeatedly calls God’s people to discern good from evil. The issue here is *hypocritical* judgment. The person condemns in another what they excuse in themselves. Jesus addresses the same thing in ==Matthew 7:1–5== when He warns against seeing the speck in a brother’s eye while ignoring the log in your own.

## Application
This passage reveals something deeply deceptive about the human heart. It is possible to have a strong moral vocabulary and still live in a way that is contrary to God's design. It is possible to hate sin in others while protecting it in ourselves. Paul is pressing the reader into the courtroom, not as the judge, but as the guilty one standing before the Judge.
