## Commentary
The queen of Sheba came from the ends of the earth to test Solomon with hard questions (==1 Kings 10:1==). She'd heard the stories, and she was sure they had to be exaggerated—things usually are in the telling. So at great cost to herself, she traveled to see with her own eyes. And look at what finally overwhelmed her. Not just Solomon’s answers—the *house*. The food on his table. The seating of his servants. The way his waiters served and how they were clothed. The ascent by which he went up to the house of the LORD. When she had seen all of it, “there was no more spirit in her.” The Hebrew literally means no breath, no resistance left. Breathless. Her own verdict: “indeed the half was not told me” (==1 Kings 10:7==).

It’s no different today. The world has heard the rumors about a King in Israel unlike any other king—a King so kind He would die for His people, so powerful He came back to life, now seated above every other name. It sounds too good to be true. So, like the queen of Sheba, the curious keep coming to see for themselves. And what do they examine? The same things she did. They look at His table—how this King feeds His people and eats with them. They look at His servants—whether they’re actually clothed in the righteousness and holiness of the King they claim to serve. They watch whether something genuinely transcendent lifts these people up to their God, or whether it’s just a show.

Which means the question is not whether Jesus will live up to the hype. He exceeds it—the half has not been told. The question is whether we, His house and His servants, will show the world what His kingdom is actually like. To the degree the King and His kingdom are seen rightly, the resistant queens of this world will end up right where Sheba’s queen did: out of arguments, out of resistance, out of breath.

## Application
Somebody in your life is quietly running the queen of Sheba's examination on you. A coworker, a neighbor, a family member who has heard the rumors about Jesus and is watching your life to see if they're true. So look at your house the way she looked at Solomon's. Your table: when did you last feed someone at it—especially someone who couldn't repay you? Your clothing: does the way you handle stress, money, and conflict look like the King you claim to serve, or does it look like everyone else's? Your ascent: is there a real, regular climb into the presence of God in your week, or just talk about one? Pick the weakest of the three and tend to it this week. You don't have to defend the King or win the argument—He exceeds the hype. You just have to let the examination find something real.
