## Commentary
“Blindness in part has happened to Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in.”

The word “until” is essential in this statement. Israel's present hardening is neither complete nor permanent. Paul identifies a point in God's redemptive purposes beyond which Israel's present condition will not continue in the same way. But what exactly does he mean by “the fullness of the Gentiles”?

The Greek word translated “fullness” is *plērōma*, a word already used in this passage. *Plērōma* means completeness or something brought to its intended measure. Many interpreters understand Paul to be speaking of the full number of Gentiles who will come to salvation. Perhaps this is Paul's intention. God, of course, knows the end from the beginning. But I believe there is also a *qualitative* significance that deserves our attention. The question isn't just how many Gentiles will be saved through faith in Jesus, but what God intends to produce among the Gentiles through their salvation.

Art Katz, who carried an extraordinary burden for the restoration of Israel and the recovery of an apostolic Church, understood this fullness as something far greater than the numerical expansion of Christianity. It involved the formation of a people who had so fully submitted themselves to the work of God that His character, holiness, humility, and sacrificial love had become visible in them.

This interpretation becomes particularly compelling when we consider what Paul has already told us in ==Romans 11:11==. The salvation of the Gentiles is intended to provoke Israel to jealousy. But what is it about the Gentile Church that would cause the Jewish people to become jealous?

Surely it can't be our numbers or institutional success. Israel is not going to be provoked to jealousy by impressive buildings, elaborate programs, or the cultural dominance of Christianity. What ought to open the eyes of a blind Israel is the recognition of her own God dwelling among a people who were once strangers to His covenants. Or to say it another way, God intends for Israel to look at His Church and see Him dwelling among a people who He should not be present among.

Imagine Jewish people encountering a community of Gentile believers whose lives genuinely reflect the character of the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. A people who tremble at His Word, who walk in humility, who love righteousness, who demonstrate extraordinary mercy, and who are willing to suffer for the sake of others. A people in whom the life of Israel's Messiah has become unmistakably evident.

This is the kind of fullness we should be longing for.

Paul describes something similar in ==Ephesians 4:13==, where he speaks of the Church coming “to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.” Although the context is different, the principle is the same. God's intention for the Church is not just numerical growth, but transformation into the likeness of His Son. 

This kind of fullness cannot be manufactured. It requires the work of the cross in the lives of God's people. The Church must become something more than a gathering of individuals who have received personal salvation. We must become a people among whom the life of God is genuinely expressed — living stones fit together for the dwelling place of His glory (==Ephesians 2:21-22==).

## Application
Perhaps we have spent too much time measuring the success of the Church by how many people we can gather and too little time asking what kind of people God is forming.

If Israel is to be provoked to jealousy through the salvation of the Gentiles, then we should be devoted to becoming the kind of people that reveal the Messiah to the people who are desperately looking for Him.
