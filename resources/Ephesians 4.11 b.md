## Commentary
“And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers.” Before looking at what these gifts do, notice three things the text says about them—who, why, and when. *Who* gave them: Jesus Himself. *Why*: not to create celebrities, but “for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry.” And *when* they’re needed: “till we all come… to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ”—which means the church isn’t done needing gifted leaders, because we’re not done maturing.

Don’t picture the fivefold as five competing architects, each building his own version of a house. Picture five trades working on the same house. The evangelist pours the foundation—bringing people to Christ. The shepherd builds the walls—protecting and caring for people. The teacher interprets the blueprints—explaining the ways of God. The prophet keeps an eye on the schedule—calling the crew back to what the Owner wants, when He wants it. The apostle makes sure the whole structure is tied into the foundation. Different gifts. One house.

And what is the house made of? People. “You also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house” (==1 Peter 2:5==). The measure of any of these ministries is not the size of the platform but the maturity of the saints they equip—people growing up into unity, into knowledge of the Son, into a stature that looks like Christ (==Ephesians 4:13==).

So receive your leaders as what they actually are: gifts from the ascended Jesus for your maturing (==Ephesians 4:8==). And if you carry one of these graces, remember which house you’re working on. The trades never own the house. They build it for the One who does.
