## Commentary
“Now the tunic was without seam, woven from the top in one piece.”

Why does Scripture bother to tell us this? The Holy Spirit doesn’t waste details. Of all the things John could record about the crucifixion, he pauses to note that Jesus wore a seamless garment—so seamless the soldiers wouldn’t tear it, casting lots for it instead, fulfilling the psalm (==Psalm 22:18==; ==John 19:24==). The King's clothing preference is on record: Jesus wears things that are woven in one piece, without seam.

Now hold that next to what Scripture says His people are becoming. We are living stones, “being built up a spiritual house” (==1 Peter 2:5==)—“fitted together,” growing into a holy temple, “built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit” (==Ephesians 2:21-22==). The stones are being shaped and molded so they fit each other seamlessly, because He wants to live inside of something He recognizes. The God who wore a seamless garment on the outside intends to wear a seamless people from the inside.

That’s why isolated Christianity can never give Jesus what He’s after. Disconnected, uncommitted individuals may be genuinely saved—but a pile of stones that refuse to be fitted together will never be the dwelling He desires, any more than a heap of torn cloth could be His tunic. It is in our joining—bearing with one another in love, keeping the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace (==Ephesians 4:2-3==)—that the fullness of Jesus gets revealed to the world and to each other. You carry a real and important part of who He is. But only the woven-together family reveals the King who prefers seamless garments.
