## Commentary
“When Solomon had finished praying, fire came down from heaven and consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices; and the glory of the LORD filled the temple.”

Notice what was in place when the fire fell. The temple was built according to the pattern David had received from the LORD (==1 Chronicles 28:19==). The sacrifices were on the altar. And Solomon had just finished praying—a long, public prayer of dedication giving the whole house to God (==2 Chronicles 6==). Fire from heaven was God’s answer: He accepted the sacrifice, and His glory moved in. The priests couldn’t even enter the house, “because the glory of the LORD had filled the LORD’s house” (==2 Chronicles 7:2==).

Heaven’s fire consistently falls on a prepared altar with a sacrifice on it. It happened at the dedication of the tabernacle, when fire came out from before the LORD and consumed the offering (==Leviticus 9:23-24==). It happened on Mount Carmel, where Elijah repaired the altar, laid the sacrifice, and then prayed (==1 Kings 18:38==). And it happened here at the temple’s dedication. In every case the order is the same: the house built God’s way, the offering presented, the prayer prayed—then the fire. God’s fire does not fall on empty altars; it is always answering a sacrifice.

God is still igniting living sacrifices. At Pentecost, the fire of God fell again—not on an altar of stone, but on people, the new temple He now fills (==Acts 2:3==). And the sacrifice the fire looks for now is the one Paul describes: “present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God” (==Romans 12:1==). The pattern of 2 Chronicles 7 is the pattern of the Spirit-filled life: build according to His design, put the offering on the altar, pray—and expect the God who answers by fire to fill His house with glory.
