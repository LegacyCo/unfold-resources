## Commentary
God’s design is that what has come before us becomes the foundation of hope we stand on.

The stories of Scripture were never meant to remain distant accounts of what God once did for someone else. They were written for our instruction so that, through endurance and the encouragement of the Scriptures, we might have hope. Every promise fulfilled, every impossible deliverance, every barren place made fruitful, and every long season of waiting brought to completion for someone else becomes something solid for us to stand on.

God knows that waiting can make the heart unstable. When the promise is delayed and circumstances appear to contradict what He has spoken, hope can begin to drift. So He gives us a record of His faithfulness. He allows us to look backward so that we can remain steady while looking forward.

This is what the writer of Hebrews is describing. God desired to show the heirs of promise (that's us) the unchangeable nature of His ways (==Hebrews 6:17==). He wanted those who had taken refuge in Him (again, us) to have the confidence to seize the hope that comes with being a child of God (==Hebrews 6:18==). Our confidence rests in His unchanging character rather than our changing circumstances.

The hope set before us is anchored in what God has already revealed about Himself. Abraham’s story becomes ground beneath our feet. Israel’s deliverance becomes evidence. The resurrection of Jesus becomes the final declaration that no situation is beyond redemption and no word from God can fail.

This hope is called “an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast” (==Hebrews 6:19==). An anchor doesn't remove the storm. It keeps the vessel from being carried away by it. In the same way, biblical hope does not deny the pressure of the moment; it fastens us to the faithfulness of God.

What God has done before teaches us how to wait for what He has promised. It's our foundation to stand on.
