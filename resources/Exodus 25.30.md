## Commentary
The bread has always spoken of presence.

In Exodus 25, when God gives Moses instructions for the tabernacle, He says, “You shall set the bread of the Presence on the table before Me always.”

There was no Hebrew word for face. The word that we translate "presence" was _panim_, which means “face.” The bread in the tabernacle was literally the bread of the Face. The bread set before God. The bread connected to His nearness. His abiding presence among His people.

This is the same word Moses uses in Exodus 33 when he says, “If Your Presence does not go with us, do not bring us up from here.”

In other words, Moses was saying, “If Your face is not with us, we are not going.”

That is the hunger of the people of God. Not just for provision, but for presence.

So when Jesus takes bread and says, “This is My body,” He is not inventing a disconnected image. He is fulfilling a long story woven through the pages of the Old Testament. The bread has always carried the mystery of presence. The bread has always pointed beyond itself.

Now, in Christ, the bread of the Presence has a name.

Jesus is the face of God turned toward us. He is the Word made flesh. He is the true bread from heaven. And at the table, He gives Himself to His people.

Communion is not merely bread that reminds us of an absent Christ. It is the bread of His Presence. The body of Christ given to us, received by us, and set before us as the mercy of God with flesh on it.
