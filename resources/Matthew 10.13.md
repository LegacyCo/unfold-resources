## Commentary
When Jesus instructs His disciples to enter a house and let their peace rest upon it if the household is "worthy," He is not suggesting that some people are morally deserving of God's blessing while others are not. The distinction He is making has to do with how people respond to the disciples and the message they carry.

The Greek word translated "worthy" is _axios_, which carries the idea of being fitting, suitable, or deserving of something. In this context, a worthy household is one that welcomes the disciples, receives their message, and demonstrates hospitality toward them.

To understand what Jesus means, we need to consider the larger context of His instructions. In ==Matthew 10:7–8==, Jesus commissions His disciples to proclaim that the kingdom of heaven is at hand, heal the sick, cleanse lepers, raise the dead, and cast out demons. They are being sent as representatives of Jesus, carrying both His message and His authority. Their arrival in a town or home is therefore an opportunity for people to receive what God is doing through Christ.

This helps explain why Jesus places such significance on how the disciples are received. Later, in ==Matthew 10:40==, He tells them, "He who receives you receives Me, and he who receives Me receives Him who sent Me."

The issue is not simply whether someone is willing to offer the disciples a meal or a place to sleep. Hospitality is the outward expression of something more significant: a willingness to receive the messengers and remain open to the message they have been sent to proclaim.

Jesus also tells the disciples to let their peace come upon the household. This reflects the traditional Jewish greeting of _shalom_, but it carries greater significance here. The disciples are not merely extending a polite greeting. As representatives of Jesus, they are pronouncing the peace of God's kingdom over those who receive them. This peace speaks of reconciliation, wholeness, and the blessing associated with God's presence and reign.

If the household proves unworthy, Jesus says their peace should return to them. This does not mean that the disciples somehow lose a portion of God's peace when they offer it to someone who rejects them. Rather, the blessing they pronounce is not received by those who refuse the message of the kingdom.

There is also an important distinction between being worthy of receiving God's grace and being receptive to it. No one earns the right to receive the kingdom through personal righteousness or good behavior. The disciples themselves have been instructed, "Freely you have received, freely give" (==Matthew 10:8==). The worthiness Jesus describes is not a qualification someone must achieve before grace can be offered. It is the response of someone who is willing to receive what God is freely giving.

This is important for us to understand as we share the gospel. Jesus does not instruct His disciples to determine who deserves to hear the good news. They are to proclaim the kingdom freely. But He also prepares them for the reality that not everyone will welcome what they have been sent to announce.

Our responsibility is to faithfully represent Jesus and extend the invitation of His kingdom. We cannot make someone receive what God is offering, nor should we measure our faithfulness by whether others accept our message. We are responsible for what we have been entrusted to give, while those who hear are responsible for how they respond.
