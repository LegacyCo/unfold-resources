## Commentary
The six days of creation read like the ordering of a temple. God forms the heavens and the earth, fills them with life, establishes order, and then places His own image within it. That image is humanity.

In the ancient world, temples contained an image of the god they honored. But the Creator does not place a statue in His sanctuary. He places living men and women there, made in His likeness, called to reflect His character and represent His rule within creation.

This is what it means to bear the image of God. Humanity was created to make the invisible God visible in the world—to cultivate what He made, extend His order, release His creativity, and return the praise of creation back to Him.

Sin, then, is more than breaking a rule. It is the failure of the image to accurately reflect the One whose image it bears. Humanity was created to reveal God within His creation, but rebellion distorted that reflection.

Yet God did not abandon His purpose. In Jesus, the true image of the invisible God, humanity is restored to its original calling. Through Him, God is forming a people who once again reflect His likeness and carry His presence into the world He made for Himself.

## Application
Your life is meant to reveal what God is like. You were not created to avoid sin, attend church, and make it to heaven someday. You were created to bear His image within His world. That means the way you love, create, lead, forgive, work, speak, and serve should make the invisible God visible.

Ask yourself, “Am I accurately reflecting the One whose image I bear?”

Where sin has distorted that image, Jesus restores it. As you behold Him and walk with Him, His likeness is formed in you again (see ==2 Corinthians 3:18==). Your calling is to carry His presence into ordinary places until your home, work, relationships, and community begin to reflect the character of the God who dwells within you.
