## Word Study
God made a promise to Abraham and "after he had patiently endured/waited, he obtained the promise." The word for "patiently endured" is *macrothymeo*. *Macrothymeo* means patience, longsuffering. It is the prolonged restraint of "thymos,"  anger or agitation. Positively it expresses persistence, or an unswerving willingness to await events rather than trying to force them.

In ancient Greece, _makrothymia_ is concerned primarily with the moulding of a man’s own character rather than a virtue exercised towards others.
## Application
God delays and puts us in a position to choose *macrothymeo* as He Himself is *macrothymeo*, as Peter writes, "The Lord is not slow concerning His promise...but is longsuffering toward us, not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance" (2 Peter 3:9) for "the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation" (2 Peter 3:15).

God desired to show the heirs of promise (us) even more the immutability (not subject to change) of His plan (Hebrews 6:17)...so that we who have taken refuge would have strong encouragement to take hold of the hope set before us (Hebrews 6:18). This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both secure and steadfast, a Hope which enters the Presence behind the veil (Hebrews 6:19).
