## Commentary
“Therefore, when you do a charitable deed, do not sound a trumpet before you as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory from men. Assuredly, I say to you, they have their reward.”

Jesus isn’t saying these people did nothing good. They gave. They prayed. They fasted. Some of them may have sacrificed considerably. He’s saying something more sobering: they’ve already been paid. They performed their righteousness before men *in order to be seen by them*, and they got exactly what they were after—the applause. The account is settled. There’s nothing left waiting for them in the age to come.

That means it’s possible to obey, even to suffer for that obedience, and still spend your entire reward here—if the motive is the approval of men. Jesus traced the deeper damage of that hunger in ==John 5:44==: “How can you believe, who receive honor from one another, and do not seek the honor that comes from the only God?” The craving to be seen doesn’t just redirect your reward; it undermines your faith. Contrast Noah, who built for years with no audience but heaven—unmoved by the opinions around him, moved only by obedience to a God he believed in and loved. His obedience produced reward in this life *and* in the age to come.

## Word Study
- *they have* : **apechō** — a commercial term stamped on ancient receipts, meaning to receive payment in full, account closed. Jesus is deliberately using marketplace language: the applause was the payout, and the hypocrite signed the receipt.

The word shows up elsewhere with the same force:

- ==Luke 6:24== — “Woe to you who are rich, for you *have received* (apechō) your consolation.” Comfort taken now, in full.
- ==Philippians 4:18== — Paul, gladly: “I *have* (apechō) all and abound”—his receipt written against the Philippians’ gift, which he calls credit accruing to *their* account (==Philippians 4:17==).

Summary: everyone gets paid; the only question is when, and by whom. Righteousness performed for human eyes collects its wages on the spot. Righteousness done in secret leaves the account open—“and your Father who sees in secret will Himself reward you openly” (==Matthew 6:4==).
