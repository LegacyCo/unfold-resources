## Commentary
“They devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer.”

We often read the book of Acts looking for miracles, salvations, supernatural power, and explosive church growth. But Luke shows us what surrounded those outcomes: devotion.

The believers didn't occasionally attend teaching when their schedules permitted. They devoted themselves to the apostles’ doctrine. They shared life. They ate together, prayed together, worshiped together, and opened their homes to one another.

Then Luke says, “The Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved” (==Acts 2:47==).

The addition came in the context of devotion.

We cannot pray for new-covenant miracles while rejecting new-covenant ways. We cannot ask God to pour out the wine of heaven while refusing to become the wineskin capable of carrying it. The early church had become a people in whom the life of Jesus could move freely.

Their life together was part of their witness. A fragmented world saw a community breaking bread with gladness, sharing possessions, caring for needs, and living with one accord. The gospel was not only something they preached. It was something their shared life made visible (==Acts 2:44-46==).

The Lord still desires to add people to His church. But addition requires somewhere for people to be added—a devoted community, not merely a weekly crowd.

## Application
Have teaching, fellowship, communion, and prayer become the organizing rhythms of our lives, or only activities we participate in when nothing else competes with them?

## Word Study

- _devoted themselves_ : **proskartereō** — to continue steadfastly, persist in, remain attached to, or give constant attention to something.

The same word appears repeatedly in the early chapters of Acts:

- ==Acts 1:14== — the disciples “continued with one accord in prayer.”
- ==Acts 2:46== — they “continued daily with one accord in the temple.”
- ==Acts 6:4== — the apostles devoted themselves “to prayer and to the ministry of the word.”

Summary: devotion is not intense interest for a short season. It is a life reorganized around what it has decided matters most.
