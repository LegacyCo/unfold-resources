## Commentary
“He who knew no sin became sin for us, so that we would become the righteousness of God in Him.” This single sentence is the gospel. Everything that belonged to us—our sin, our guilt, our condemnation—was placed on Jesus. And everything that belongs to Jesus—His righteousness, His acceptance, His holiness—is now given to us. In Christ we receive a righteousness we could never produce for ourselves.

The exchange matters because of where we start. The first step to becoming a child of God is knowing you are a child of Adam—a citizen of a fallen world, separated from God, holding no passport and no papers that could gain you entry to His kingdom (==Romans 5:12==). You can get as close as you want and plead your case at the border, but as long as your family line traces to Adam, effort cannot get you in. You must be born again (==John 3:3==).

And here is the good news: the King of that kingdom went on a rescue mission behind enemy lines to free us from our fallen one. He did not lower the entry requirements; He met them, and then He gave us a new identity. Anyone joined to Him by faith is born again into His family (==1 Corinthians 1:30==). Your birth certificate no longer reads “son of Adam” or “daughter of Adam.” It reads “child of God.”

Notice that the verse leaves no room for a partial trade. He did not take some of our sin, and He does not give some of His righteousness. The exchange is total—which is why the righteousness we now stand in cannot be improved upon, only believed.

## Application
Settle the direction of the exchange. Much of Christian anxiety comes from quietly running it backward—trying to hand God our record and hoping it is good enough. Your record has already been handed over; it went to the cross. The only record that now defines you is His.

So when guilt over a confessed sin returns, answer it with the trade: that belongs to Jesus now. And when you feel unaccepted by God because of a weak week, remember that His acceptance was never the wage of your performance—it is the gift of His. Stop trying to produce what can only be received, and start living like someone whose citizenship papers are already signed.
