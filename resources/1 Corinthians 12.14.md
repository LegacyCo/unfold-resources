## Commentary
“For in fact the body is not one member but many.”

Each of us carries a part of Jesus that is unique and important for the world to experience. That’s gloriously true. But Paul’s picture in this chapter comes with an edge we shouldn’t sand off: a member only makes sense *attached*. A hand joined to the rest of the body is powerful—it heals, builds, serves, blesses. A hand disconnected from the body is grotesque.

Try the thought experiment. You go for a walk on the beach tonight and you see a foot—just a foot—lying there on the sand. You don’t admire it. You recoil. You call the police. You tell everyone around you to get back, because something is terribly wrong: *where is the rest of that body?* An extreme example, sure. But so is the thinking that says we can be saved by Jesus, be part of His body, and live disconnected from the rest of His body. A detached member isn’t independent; it’s evidence of an injury.

And here’s what disconnection actually costs—not just the member, but everyone. It's in the coming together of the Body that the Spirit reveals the fullness of Jesus, to the world and to each other. You carry a real part of Him, but on your own you can't reveal or experience the *fullness* of who He is. The eye cannot say to the hand, “I have no need of you” (==1 Corinthians 12:21==)—and the hand cannot say to the body, “I’m fine over here by myself.” God composed the body so that the members “should have the same care for one another,” suffering together and rejoicing together (==1 Corinthians 12:24-26==). That only happens attached. So stay joined.
