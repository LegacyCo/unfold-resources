## Commentary
The NKJV translates this verse, "Therefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes." This is a horrible translation. The context is v5, "I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you." Under this translation, the takeaway of "abhorring myself" is this: now that I know who God truly is, my repentance is directly connected to abhorring myself. Abhor means to regard with disgust and hatred. So, in light of this way of translating v6, repentance involves a hating of self.

The NASB does a much better job handling the original language: "Therefore I retract, and I repent in dust and ashes."

The word in question is *ma-as*. It means to reject, refuse. There is no suggestion of hatred or abhorrence in the word. It has covenant overtones (as in Leviticus 26:44). Other times the word is used in Scripture: 

Hosea 9:17, "My God will *reject* them because they have not listened to Him."

Jeremiah 6:30, "They call them *rejected* silver, because the LORD has *rejected* them."

Isaiah 41:9, "...I have chosen you and have not rejected you."

If the overarching teaching of Scripture is that repentance begins with a hatred of self because — if repentance is self-focused and sin-conscience — then "I abhor myself" fits with that narrative. However, if the consistent teaching of Scripture is that repentance begins when we see God as He actually is —we see a glimpse of Him that contradicts everything we previously thought about Him — then "reject/retract" is the correct translation of this verse. 

Conclusion: the NASB correctly translates this verse - "I retract, and I repent in dust and ashes."

Repentance is not a hatred of self because of sin. Repentance is changing the way you think because you now see the truth. Repentance does not begin with us abhorring ourselves. It begins with us realizing God is nothing like we thought, and we realize He is infinitely more wonderful than we ever imagined.

It's not the abhorrence of yourself that leads you to repentance, "It's the kindness of God that leads you to repentance" (Romans 2:4).
