## Commentary
The Pharisees' accusation that Jesus was casting out demons by the power of Beelzebub reveals just how far their opposition to Him had progressed. They weren't denying that Jesus had performed a miracle. A demon-oppressed man who had been blind and mute was now able to see and speak (==Matthew 12:22==). The evidence of supernatural power was undeniable. What they refused to acknowledge was the source of that power.

The name Beelzebub (also rendered Beelzebul) was a designation for Satan, whom the Pharisees identified as the ruler of demons. The name is historically associated with Baal-Zebub, the false god worshiped in Ekron, whom King Ahaziah sought after being injured (==2 Kings 1:2–3==). By attributing Jesus' miracles to Beelzebub, the Pharisees were essentially accusing Him of being empowered by Satan rather than by God.

In the preceding verse, the crowds were beginning to ask, "Could this be the Son of David?" (==Matthew 12:23==). This was a messianic question. The people were witnessing the works of Jesus and beginning to consider whether He might actually be the promised King who would restore Israel. The Pharisees' accusation was a direct challenge to that possibility. Rather than acknowledging the significance of what God was doing in their midst, they attempted to discredit the One through whom it was happening.

Jesus responds by exposing the absurdity of their reasoning. If Satan were casting out Satan, his kingdom would be divided against itself and could not stand (==Matthew 12:25–26==). But Jesus takes the argument further. In ==Matthew 12:28==, He declares, "But if I cast out demons by the Spirit of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you."

This is the significance of the miracle. Jesus wasn't simply demonstrating His authority over demonic spirits. He was announcing the arrival of God's kingdom and the beginning of Satan's defeat. What Israel had been waiting for was taking place before their eyes, yet the very leaders who should have recognized the fulfillment of God's promises were attributing His work to the devil.

This also helps explain the seriousness of Jesus' warning concerning blasphemy against the Holy Spirit in the verses that follow (==Matthew 12:31–32==). The Pharisees accusation wasn't just a misunderstanding. They were confronted with the liberating work of God's Spirit and responded by identifying that work as demonic.

The Pharisees knew the Scriptures, but their knowledge had not produced the humility necessary to recognize the One to whom those Scriptures testified.
