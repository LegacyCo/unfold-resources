## Commentary
“When the Lord has washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion, and purged the blood of Jerusalem from her midst, by the spirit of judgment and by the spirit of burning…”

The glory comes in the next verse—the cloud by day, the flaming fire by night, the canopy over all (==Isaiah 4:5==)—but the burning comes first. Before the Lord dwells over Zion, He washes and purges her, and the instruments are named: the spirit of judgment and the spirit of burning. The cleansing is not the obstacle to the glory; it is the preparation for it. “Who can endure the day of His coming? For He is like a refiner's fire” (==Malachi 3:2-3==).

Most of us have never endured the burning of the presence we are praying for. Revival history is littered with spiritual giants who could not endure the fire they helped bring. Evan Roberts, whose leadership in the Welsh Revival helped birth Azusa Street, lasted nine months in the fire of revival before his body gave out in exhaustion; after his breakdown he came under the care of a controlling benefactor and never really preached again. William Seymour, who led Azusa Street itself, was swallowed up by legalism in his later years. These were not weak or compromised men—they were the very ones God used to bring the fire, and even they were not prepared to live in it.

Someone has to burn to bring in a move of heaven—but everyone must burn to endure one. So the real prayer, before the fire falls, is not just “send revival” but “touch me with the spirit of burning now.” What should keep a believer on his knees is not the fear that the fire won't fall, but that when it falls there may still be places in the heart that were never placed on the altar, never exposed to the cross (==Psalm 139:23-24==).
