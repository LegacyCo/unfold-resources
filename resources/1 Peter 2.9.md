## Commentary
When Peter calls the church a “royal priesthood,” he is not merely giving believers a new title. He is describing the nature of our life before God. Priests certainly served the people, but their first and highest responsibility was to minister to the Lord (==Exodus 28:1==; ==Ezekiel 44:15–16==).

God has determined to demonstrate Himself through His people. But we cannot accurately represent someone we don’t know, and we can’t carry the reality of a heavenly kingdom into the earth if we have not first bowed before the King. The priest comes out from the presence of God carrying something of the One before whom he has worshipped.

Much of what we call ministry today begins with human need. We see something broken and immediately rush toward it. But the priestly man doesn’t begin with the needs around him; he begins with what God desires. He stands before the Lord, learns His heart, receives His burden, and then moves toward people with something that has originated in God.

Our priesthood therefore begins in the secret place. Before we speak to men about God, we minister to God. Before we attempt to reveal Him, we allow ourselves to be formed by Him. We are “a royal priesthood” so that we may first belong wholly to the King and then “proclaim the praises of Him who called [us] out of darkness into His marvelous light” (see ==Revelation 1:5–6==).
