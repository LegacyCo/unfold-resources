## Commentary
"...who was delivered up because of our offenses, and was raised because of our justification."

Paul gives the cross and the resurrection each their own "because." He was delivered up *because of* our offenses—our sin is what put Him on the cross. And He was raised *because of* our justification—our being made right is what brought Him out of the tomb.

Paul doesn't say He was raised so that we *might* be justified, as if the resurrection started a process. He says He was raised *because* the justification was accomplished. The debt was paid in full on Friday; the resurrection on Sunday was heaven confirming the payment had cleared. Had one sin been left unatoned for, the grave would have had a legal claim to hold Him. It didn't (==Acts 2:24==). He walked out because there was nothing left to pay.

This is why the resurrection is not a happy ending tacked onto the story of the cross. It's the verdict on the cross. When Jesus said "It is finished" (==John 19:30==), that was the Son's word. When the stone rolled away, that was the Father's.

So when the accuser brings up your record, don't argue from your progress. Argue from the empty tomb. If your justification were incomplete, He would still be in the grave. He isn't. Which means neither is your case.
