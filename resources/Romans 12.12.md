## Commentary
“rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation, continuing steadfastly in prayer;”

Paul puts three things side by side: joy in hope, endurance in trouble, and consistence in prayer. The third is how the first two are kept up. Hope fades when a person stops talking to the God he's hoping in. Endurance runs out when trouble is faced alone. Prayer is where hope is fed and where trouble is handed over to God, so Paul tells the church to stay at it.

The NKJV says “continuing steadfastly in prayer.” Other translations say “devoted to prayer” or “constant in prayer.” The Greek verb form describes something ongoing, a settled habit. Paul isn't describing a burst of intense prayer during a crisis. He's describing a person who keeps showing up consistently as a way of life.

That was the pattern of the first church. Before Pentecost, the disciples “all continued with one accord in prayer” (==Acts 1:14==). After Pentecost, the new believers “continued steadfastly… in prayers” (==Acts 2:42==). When the apostles were being pulled into administration, they appointed others to handle it and said, “we will give ourselves continually to prayer and to the ministry of the word” (==Acts 6:4==). Each of those phrases uses the same word Paul uses here. They had learned it from Jesus, who told a parable for the stated purpose of teaching “that men always ought to pray and not lose heart” (==Luke 18:1==).

## Word Study
**Continuing steadfastly** is *proskartereō* (προσκαρτερέω)—to stay close to something, to persist in it, to be devoted to it. It joins *pros*, “toward,” to a word for strength and endurance. Outside of prayer, the New Testament uses it for people and things that stay at their post. Jesus asked for a small boat to be “kept ready for Him” in case the crowd pressed in (==Mark 3:9==). Cornelius had a soldier who “waited on him continually” (==Acts 10:7==). Paul says governing authorities are “attending continually” to their work (==Romans 13:6==). Summary: to be devoted to prayer is to stay on duty in it, like a boat kept ready at the shore or an attendant who doesn't leave his post.

## Application
Devotion to prayer is built by consistency more than by intensity. Daniel prayed three times a day, “as was his custom since early days” (==Daniel 6:10==), and he kept doing it after it became illegal. Choose a time and a place you can keep every day, even if it's just a few minutes, and protect it the way you'd protect an appointment with someone important.
