## Commentary
“Then the seventh angel sounded: and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, ‘The kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ, and He shall reign forever and ever!’”

That proclamation is where history is headed. But notice that it comes at the *end* of the story—which raises the question of how we get there. Scripture shows God's kingdom advancing in three movements. First, the kingdom came in the Lord Jesus: a Man in whom the rule of God was perfect and complete—“if I cast out demons by the Spirit of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you” (==Matthew 12:28==). Second, the kingdom is implanted in the church: a people who live under the rule of the King before the world does (==Colossians 1:13==). And third—the movement this verse announces—the kingdom extends to the whole world, when the kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ.

Here’s what that sequence means for us: the church occupies the place between the second movement and the third. The kingdom had to be found in Jesus before it could be established in His church, and it must be established in His church before it will be secured among the nations. Which puts real weight on the church’s surrender—if the church refuses to live under God’s authority, she interrupts the very sequence God has chosen to establish His kingdom in the earth. This is why Paul’s missionary journeys were never just about generating personal spiritual experiences or securing people an escape from hell. He was planting outposts of the third movement—communities living under Jesus as King, on earth as in heaven (==Acts 28:31==).

That reframes the mission of every local church. We aren’t just gathering converts who are waiting to leave; we’re a people among whom the King already reigns, holding the ground between what happened in Jesus and what will one day be announced over the whole world. The seventh trumpet hasn’t sounded yet. But everywhere a church actually submits to the rule of her King, the world gets a preview of the proclamation.
