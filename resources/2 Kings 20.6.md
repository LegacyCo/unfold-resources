## Commentary
Isaiah the prophet came to Hezekiah with a clear word from the Lord: his sickness would end in death.

But then Hezekiah prayed. He brought the word, the sickness, and his life before the Lord. And before Isaiah had even left the middle court, the word of the Lord came again: “Return and tell Hezekiah…I have heard your prayer, I have seen your tears; surely I will heal you” (2 Kings 20:5).

This is one of those passages that forces us to take prayer seriously. God had spoken, and yet Hezekiah prayed. He appealed to God's mercy. And in response to that prayer, the Lord added fifteen years to his life.

This doesn't mean God is unstable. It means God has chosen to work relationally with His people. His word is not an excuse for passivity. Sometimes the word of the Lord becomes the very thing that drives us into prayer. 

There is a kind of prayer that does not deny what God has said, but brings what God has said back to Him with tears. And somehow, in the mystery of His wisdom, the Lord has made room for those prayers to matter.

## Application
Don’t let hard news become the end of the conversation. Turn your face to the wall. Bring your tears before the Lord. Prayer may not always change the outcome, but Scripture will not let us pretend it never does.
