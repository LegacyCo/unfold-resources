## Commentary
“Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.”

For many believers, the ascension registers as a loss—the day Jesus *left*. We treat it as His absence: He was here, and now He’s gone, and we’re holding down the fort until He returns. But that’s not how the New Testament talks about the ascension. Paul, Hebrews, and Revelation all describe it the same way: not as Jesus’ departure from the world but as His enthronement *over* it—seated at the Father’s right hand, far above all principality and power, everything under His feet (==Ephesians 1:20-22==; ==Hebrews 2:9==). The cloud didn’t take Him away from us. It received a King to His coronation.

Here’s the wonder that unlocks: while Jesus walked the earth in a body, He was *localized*. If He was in Capernaum, He wasn’t in Jerusalem. To be near Him, you had to get to where He was. But the ascended King is present everywhere His Spirit dwells—which is why Jesus told the disciples something that must have sounded impossible the night He said it: “It is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you” (==John 16:7==). To *your advantage*. Better than having Jesus in the room is having Jesus in *you*—and in every believer, in every nation, all at once. That’s why His final ascension-era words hold both truths without contradiction: “All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth… and lo, I am with you always” (==Matthew 28:18-20==). He ascended, *and* He is with us always. The ascension isn’t absence. It’s omnipresence with a throne.

So don’t live like the fort is abandoned. The King you serve isn’t far away waiting to become relevant again at the second coming. He is presently reigning, presently ruling in the midst of His enemies (==Psalm 110:2==), and presently closer to you than He ever was to the crowds in Galilee.
