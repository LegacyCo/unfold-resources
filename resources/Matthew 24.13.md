## Commentary
“But he who endures to the end shall be saved.”

Endurance is the power to withstand hardship or pressure—inner fortitude that holds when circumstances press against it. Jesus places this sentence in the middle of a description of severe trial: deception, persecution, betrayal, lawlessness, and love growing cold (==Matthew 24:9-12==). The severity of what is coming is exactly why He names endurance as the requirement. Endurance is not an optional virtue for the especially devoted; Jesus makes it a prerequisite for those who will inherit salvation.

The rest of the New Testament agrees. James writes that “the testing of your faith produces patience,” and that when endurance is allowed to finish its work it makes a believer “perfect and complete, lacking nothing” (==James 1:3-4==)—endurance is the path to maturity. Paul lists it as a characteristic of love itself: love “bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things” (==1 Corinthians 13:7==). And endurance develops only one way: under pressure. It cannot be formed in comfort any more than muscle can be built without resistance. This is why it is God's goodness, not His severity, to bring His people to the end of themselves. The endurance that inherits salvation, expresses the character of love, and leads into maturity can be produced no other way.

## Word Study
**Endures** is the Greek *hypomenō* (ὑπομένω)—literally “to remain under”: to stay beneath a load rather than escape it. The noun *hypomonē* is the “patience” James says produces maturity (==James 1:3-4==), the quality Paul says tribulation produces on the way to character and hope (==Romans 5:3-4==), and the word Hebrews uses of Jesus, who “endured the cross” (==Hebrews 12:2==). Summary: biblical endurance is not passive waiting; it is remaining under the weight with faith intact until God's purpose in the pressure is complete.
