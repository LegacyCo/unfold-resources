## Commentary
No water in the wilderness, and the congregation turned on their leaders: “Why have you brought us up out of Egypt, into this evil place?” (==Numbers 20:5==). Watch what Moses and Aaron do with the assault: “So Moses and Aaron went from the presence of the people to the tabernacle of meeting, and they fell on their faces. And the glory of the LORD appeared to them.”

That’s the pattern for every leader facing opposition—sometimes from their own people. The response of the godly leader is not to contend with God’s *people* in arguments, but to contend with *God* in prayer. They left the presence of the people for the presence of the Lord, and on their faces they received what no argument produces: instructions. “Take the rod… *speak* to the rock before their eyes, and it will yield its water” (==Numbers 20:8==). That’s what prayer accomplishes—it connects us to God as the source of what we’re doing.

But the story turns sobering. Moses gathered the people and spoke—to *them*, not to the rock: “Hear now, you rebels!” Then he struck the rock twice. He had been in God’s will with God in prayer, and moved into his own will with the people. It cost him the promised land (==Numbers 20:12==). The man or woman of prayer has to hold this: we can’t just pray—we must *obey what He tells us in prayer*.

And here’s the part that should keep every leader in the secret place: the water still flowed. Abundantly. God will sometimes allow us to accomplish things in ministry in the arm of the flesh, because He loves people too much to leave them thirsty. Which means the miracle is never evidence of our character. The miracle is evidence of *God’s* character. 

So let the sobering part drive us deeper in, not further out: back to the secret place, asking Him to search us, test our motives, expose what’s selfish and carnal, and help us in our weakness (==Psalm 139:23-24==)—especially in the moments when leading God’s people is hardest. Fall on your face before the Lord. It’s still where the glory appears.
