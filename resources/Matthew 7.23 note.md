## Commentary
Jesus describes people who will stand before Him having prophesied in His name, cast out demons in His name, and done many wonders in His name—and He will declare, “I never knew you” (==Matthew 7:22-23==). Sit with how disturbing that is: real gifting, real power, real ministry activity—and no relationship. How is that possible? Because God loves people so much He’ll speak through a donkey if He has to (==Numbers 22:28==). Balaam prophesied truly while his heart ran crooked. The gift operating through a person is evidence of God’s love for the people receiving it—it's not evidence of the condition of the vessel.

I’ll be honest with you: prophecy doesn’t move me anymore. Gifting doesn’t move me the way it used to. Sure, he can sing the paint off the wall—but does he have the character to hold the weight of what he’s singing about? Sure, she can prophesy names and birthdays—but does she have the integrity to receive correction? As we move toward the end of the age and deception grows more sophisticated in the church, Jesus People will have to be mature and discerning—and Jesus told us exactly what to examine: “by their *fruits* you will know them” (==Matthew 7:20==). Not by their gifts. Gifting can be faked. Christ-like character cannot—not for any length of time, anyway. You can’t fake peace that surpasses understanding. You can’t fake a decade of laying your life down.

Now don’t hear what I’m not saying. Let’s prophesy. Let’s pray for healing, cast out devils, and contend for the Spirit to confirm the gospel with signs and wonders (==1 Corinthians 14:1==). But let me watch you in a disagreement, and we’ll see whether you’re living in Christ or still in Adam. Let me see how you are with your kids. Show me long obedience in the same direction. Show me who is discipling you, who you’re accountable to, who has permission to speak into your life. Living with integrity, loving people well, giving extravagantly without being preached at about it, staying content if no one ever knows your name because you know that *He* knows it—that’s the fruit of Jesus living inside of you. That’s verifiable evidence of new birth.

And notice: examining fruit requires actually doing life together. You can’t discern a tree from a distance; orchards are walked, not watched. Which is why the gift God gave you belongs in the context of a local church family—stewarded under accountability, surrounded by people who see you Monday through Saturday. Show your community the fruit of the Spirit, not just the gifts of the Spirit (==Galatians 5:22-23==). You may not demonstrate Jesus perfectly—nobody does. But is Christ being formed in you (==Galatians 4:19==)? Are you more like Him today than you were last month? That’s the question the fruit answers.

But come back to the words Jesus actually says, because the weight of this verse isn’t finally about examining other people’s fruit—it’s about you and Him. He doesn’t say, “Depart from Me, your fruit was insufficient.” He says, “I never *knew* you.” Knowing—the intimate, covenant knowing of two lives joined together—is the whole issue. The people in this verse had ministries; they didn’t have Him. Fruit matters precisely because fruit is what union produces: a branch abiding in the vine can’t help but bear it (==John 15:4-5==), which is why fruit is the reliable evidence and gifting isn’t. So the final question of Matthew 7:23 isn’t “how impressive is what I do in His name?” or even “how does my fruit look?” It’s the question underneath both: does He know me? Am I in Him, doing the will of the Father from a life joined to His (==Matthew 7:21==)? Get that right, and the fruit takes care of itself.
