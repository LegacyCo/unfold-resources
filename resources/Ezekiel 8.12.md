## Commentary
Then He said to me, “Son of man, have you seen what the elders of the house of Israel do in the dark, every man in the room of his idols? For they say, ‘The LORD does not see us, the LORD has forsaken the land.’”

The elders are not only worshiping false gods; they have convinced themselves that Yahweh neither sees nor cares about what they are doing.

“Every man in the room of his idols” suggests private chambers or hidden spaces devoted to personal idolatry. What Ezekiel is seeing is not merely public corruption but secret worship practiced behind closed doors. The men responsible for leading Israel have created hidden places where rival gods are honored.

Because Jerusalem had experienced disaster and foreign oppression, they interpreted God's apparent silence as His absence. If Yahweh had abandoned them, they reasoned, then they were free to seek help elsewhere.

But the irony is that God is showing Ezekiel everything they believed He could not see.

Their hidden rooms were not hidden from Him.

This verse exposes one of the great deceptions of sin: we begin to live as though God's silence means His absence and His patience means He does not see. But nothing is concealed from Him (==Psalm 139:7–12==; ==Hebrews 4:13==). The secret life still takes place before the face of God.
