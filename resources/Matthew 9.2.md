## Commentary
“Then behold, they brought to Him a paralytic lying on a bed. When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralytic, ‘Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you.’”

Matthew does not tell us that the paralytic asked to be forgiven. He does not record a confession of sin, a prayer of repentance, or even a request from the men carrying him. They simply bring the man to Jesus. And Matthew says, “When Jesus saw their faith…”

Faith had become visible.

Mark and Luke give us more detail. The house was so crowded that the men carrying the paralytic could not reach Jesus, so they climbed onto the roof, opened it, and lowered their friend into the room (==Mark 2:3–4==; ==Luke 5:18–19==). Their actions revealed what they believed: if they could only get this man to Jesus, Jesus could do something for him.

Jesus sees that faith and responds.

But He does not initially give them what they appear to have come for.

They brought a paralyzed man expecting healing, and Jesus said, “Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you.”

The visible need was paralysis. Jesus addressed the deeper need first.

This does not mean that every sickness is the direct result of a particular personal sin. Jesus explicitly rejects that simplistic assumption (==John 9:1–3==). The point is that Jesus sees the greater reality...and the greater need. They knew what they wanted Him to do. Jesus knew what the man ultimately needed.

Faith in this story is not presented as a formula that earns forgiveness. It is the posture that brings a person into contact with Jesus.

This pattern appears repeatedly throughout the Gospels. People approach Jesus looking for one thing and discover that He intends to give them something deeper. A woman comes for water and Jesus speaks about living water (==John 4:7–14==). Crowds come looking for bread and Jesus reveals Himself as the Bread of Life (==John 6:26–35==). A paralytic comes looking for functioning legs and Jesus begins with forgiveness.

We often come to Jesus because of the thing that hurts most visibly. We want the body healed, the circumstance changed, the door opened, the pressure lifted. None of those desires are insignificant to Him. Jesus eventually heals the man's body too.

But Jesus refuses to confuse our most obvious need with our deepest one.

Before the paralytic stands, Jesus calls him “Son.”

Before He restores his legs, He removes his guilt.

Before the man walks out of the house carrying his bed, he receives something far greater than the ability to walk: the declaration that his sins have been forgiven.

Sometimes faith simply brings us to Jesus asking for the thing we can see. And Jesus, in His mercy, gives us the thing we did not yet know how to ask for.

## Application
You don't have to understand the full extent of your situation before you bring it to Jesus. You do not have to diagnose your deepest need correctly. You may come asking for provision, healing, direction, relief, or an open door—and Jesus may address something else in your life entirely.

Bring Him the need you can see. Ask boldly. Tear the roof open if you have to. But leave room for Jesus to respond at a level deeper than your request.

Sometimes the greatest mercy is not that He gives us what we came asking for, but that He sees what we truly need and gives us more.
