## Commentary
“For I say, through the grace given to me, to everyone who is among you, not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think, but to think soberly, as God has dealt to each one a measure of faith.”

The first place Paul applies the renewed mind of verse 2 is to how a person thinks about himself. There's a wordplay in the Greek that English can't quite communicate. The root word for “think” appears four times in this one sentence, something along these lines: don't *over-think* of yourself beyond what you ought to *think*, but *think* in a way that is *sound thinking*. Translation: a renewed mind sees itself accurately.

Paul doesn't tell us to think badly of ourselves. He says not to think “more highly than he ought,” which means there is a way a person ought to think. The standard is “soberly,” which means clear-headed and in your right mind. Pride is one way of losing touch with reality. Constantly running yourself down is another, because it's still a mind occupied with itself. Sober thinking says three true things: this is what God has given me, this is what He hasn't given me, and none of it started with me.

The third of those comes from the end of the verse. God “has dealt to each one a measure of faith.” “Dealt” means portioned out, the way food is served. Interpreters read “measure of faith” in two ways. Some take it as differing portions: God gives each believer the faith that fits the gift and the work He has assigned, which matches verse 6, “let us prophesy in proportion to our faith.” Others take “measure” as a measuring standard: every believer has been handed the same yardstick, the faith itself, and should size himself up by the gospel and not by other people. On either reading, the answer to pride is the same. What you have, you were given. “What do you have that you did not receive? Now if you did indeed receive it, why do you boast as if you had not received it?” (==1 Corinthians 4:7==).

## Word Study
**Think soberly** is *sōphroneō* (σωφρονέω)—to be of sound mind, to be sensible and self-controlled. The clearest picture of the word is in ==Mark 5:15==. After Jesus cast a legion of demons out of a man who had been living among the tombs, the townspeople found him “sitting and clothed and in his right mind.” “In his right mind” is this word. Peter uses it for how believers should live as the end approaches: “be serious and watchful in your prayers” (==1 Peter 4:7==). Summary: to think soberly about yourself is to be in your right mind about yourself, seeing what is actually there and knowing where it came from.
