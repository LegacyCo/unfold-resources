## Commentary
“We love Him because He first loved us.”

This may be the most important verse in the Bible for understanding love, and its power of it sits in one word: *first*. God is the source of love. He loved before we did, before we could, before we even existed to be loved. And the source of something matters, because where something comes from determines its nature, its quality, and its purpose. When bottled water first became popular, the companies marketed the *source*—water from an artisanal spring in Peru, from a ten-thousand-year-old Arctic glacier—because they knew the source tells you what's actually in the bottle. So ask it of love: where does yours come from? Any definition of love that does not begin with God is incomplete, and any supply of love that does not begin with God runs on a dangerously small tank.

If I try to love you without knowing I’m loved by God, I am out of order. If I try to love you without loving God first, I am equally out of order. In both cases the source of my love is *me*—and love sourced in me will always be conditional: when my conditions are met, I’ll give it; when they’re not, I’ll withhold it. I cannot love you the way God commands me to love you without God. And I can’t expect you to love me that way without Him either.

This is why the two great commandments come in a fixed order (==Mark 12:30-31==). The love God gives me, I give back to Him first—heart, soul, mind, strength—and only then am I carrying something worth giving to my neighbor. Too many of us are trying to produce fruit without a root system: demanding of ourselves a love for others that we've never received from God, wanting the harvest without cultivating the tree. But the root is knowing I am loved by a God who loves perfectly. The fruit is loving others well. Get the order right—loved first, love back, love out—and the second commandment stops being an exhausting demand and becomes what John describes here: a response.
