## Commentary
“Moreover, when you fast, do not be like the hypocrites, with a sad countenance…”

Jesus does not say *if*. He does not say *should you ever decide to*. He says *when*—and He says it again in the next verse: “But you, when you fast…” (==Matthew 6:17==). It is the same construction He used for giving—“when you do a charitable deed” (==Matthew 6:2==)—and for prayer—“when you pray” (==Matthew 6:5==). The assumption is that in the same way Jesus People will give and pray, Jesus People will fast. So the first task in any conversation about fasting is to put it back in the same sentence as giving and praying. Most believers treat two of those practices as normal Christianity and the third as an elective for the super-spiritual. Jesus didn't.

What is fasting? Going without food, for a set time, in order to intentionally seek God. We call many denials “fasting”—fasting from social media, from sugar, from caffeine—and those may be useful disciplines, but they are not fasting in the biblical sense. The integrity of the word needs to be guarded, for a couple reasons. Fasting is hard; that's part of the point. And fasting is powerful precisely because it denies the body its most basic necessity, which surfaces things in the heart that are otherwise hidden. When Jesus said “when you fast,” everyone in the crowd knew He meant from food. Fasting is one of the most neglected gifts in the Body of Christ—and Jesus spoke of it as something as ordinary in His people's lives as their giving and their prayers.

## Word Study
**Fast** is the Greek *nēsteuō* (νηστεύω), built from the negative particle and the verb “to eat”—literally, *not to eat*. The word itself settles the definition: wherever Scripture speaks of fasting, abstaining from food is what it means (==Luke 4:2==; ==Acts 13:2==). Summary: the biblical vocabulary leaves no room for redefining the fast into something easier—the word means going without food, and Jesus assumed His people would do it.
