## Commentary
For God’s face to "shine" means God turns toward His people with favor, mercy, attention, and covenant kindness. It is the opposite of God hiding His face, which often represents judgment, abandonment, or the painful experience of divine silence (see ==Deuteronomy 31:17-18==, ==Psalm 13:1==, ==Isaiah 8:17== for examples of God hiding His face in judgment).

So in Psalm 80, the psalmist is praying something like: “Lord, turn toward us again. Let Your favor rest on us again. Look upon us with mercy, and we will be restored.”

This prayer is necessary because Israel is in distress. The psalm pictures God’s people as a ravaged vine, once planted and cared for by God, now broken down and exposed to enemies. The psalmist knows that Israel’s restoration will not come merely through military strength, political rescue, or national reform. They need the face of God to turn toward them again.

The heart of this prayer is: “Save us by turning Your face toward us again.”

## Application
In biblical thought, salvation is not simply God changing what is happening around us. God's saving power is in Him turning toward us in mercy. His shining face is the source of life, restoration, peace, and deliverance.
