## Commentary
Daniel had no problem with his name being changed to a name that represented the pagan gods of Babylon (Daniel 1:7). He had no problem going to school for three years to learn the Babylonian culture; a culture that had destroyed the culture of God's people in Jerusalem (Daniel 1:5). But when it came to eating from the king's table, that's where he drew the line.

The table is covenantal. The table joins us to spiritual realities. Symbols become sacred at the table. There is something about the table that joins those who eat from it together. 

There is something about the table that joins us to the spiritual reality that otherwise hides behind the busy distraction of this life. As Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 10:18, "Observe Israel after the flesh: Are not those who eat of the sacrifices partakers of the altar?"

Daniel was determined not to join himself to the altar that the king of Babylon celebrated. Learning the culture of Babylon is one thing, being joined to the spirit of Babylon is another.
