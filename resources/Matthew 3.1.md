## Commentary
_Where_ John preached his message was very significant: the wilderness. 

The wilderness exposes us. There are no distractions or comforts to hide your quirks. You can’t pretend in the wilderness.   

God uses the wilderness to show us who we really are.

==Deuteronomy 8:2== says He led Israel into the wilderness to humble them, test them, and reveal what was in their hearts…not so _He_ could see it, He already knew what was in their hearts. He wanted *Israel* to see it.

God loves the wilderness. He tells Israel in Hosea 2:14, “Behold, I will allure her and draw her into the wilderness and speak tenderly to her heart. And from _that place_ I will give her her vineyards.”

God wants to take each of us to the wilderness and speak tenderly to our hearts. 

But the wilderness is never meant to be permanent. 

The wilderness is the trailhead — the starting line of our journey with God. It’s where God reveals Himself to us in a unique way, and in that revelation of who _He_ is, we see who _we_ are, so that who we really are can be buried in the sands of repentance.   

And this is where a lot of people get stuck: they never leave the trailhead of repentance.

They build a life in the wilderness of repentance, camping out at the starting line of salvation.

What does that look like? It looks like: Constant introspection, but no transformation. Always aware of sin, but never victory over it. A cycle of guilt, confession. It looks like being defined by what you’re turning _from_, not Who you’re walking _with_.

Living in the wilderness of repentance looks like you measuring how satisfied God is with you by how often you repent, rather than the sacrifice of Jesus.

That’s not maturity. That’s camping at the trailhead.

And if your relationship with God is defined by your mistakes, you’ll never live the life of freedom repentance is intended to give you.
