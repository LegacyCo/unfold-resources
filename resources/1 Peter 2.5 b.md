## Commentary
"...you also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house..."

God builds with people. People build with bricks. 

Think about why homes are made of brick. Bricks are uniform. Bricks are predictable. Bricks are manufactured to spec, stack fast, and don't argue. The flesh loves bricks because the flesh wants efficiency, ease, and pragmatism—and it will call all three "good stewardship." Scripture has an opinion about that. The first building project after the flood begins with the words, "Come, let us make bricks" (==Genesis 11:3==)—and ends with a tower God has to come down and scatter. Egypt built its glory on brick quotas (==Exodus 5:7-8==). Bricks are what man makes when he wants to build something great in his own way.

Stones are different. Stones are found, not manufactured. Every one is a different shape. They have to be sought out, carried, and *fitted*—shaped against each other until they lock. It's slower, messier, and much harder. And it's what God insists on. His altar was to be built of stones no tool had touched (==Exodus 20:25==). His temple was built of stone (==1 Kings 6:7==). And His house now is built of *living* stones—broken, irregular, hard-to-get-along-with human beings, joined to each other on the Chief Cornerstone.

God is not going to possess a building, a denomination, or a ministry brand. He's going to possess a people. And He would rather build slowly with the actual person next to you—with all their edges—than quickly with a system that treats people like interchangeable units. When we trade stones for bricks in the name of growth, God says, "I appreciate the efficiency. But I asked you to build My house with *them*."

Notice, too, that Peter says you are *being built*. Passive. Continuous. You don't assemble yourself into the house; you let yourself be placed, shaped, and fitted by the Builder—next to stones you didn't pick.

## Application
Look at the people God has actually set you among—your church, your small group, the believers you'd honestly rather not be joined to. Those aren't obstacles to God's house. They're the materials. Pick one "hard stone" this week and take a step toward being fitted rather than avoiding the friction: a meal, an apology, an honest conversation, a commitment to stop keeping them at arm's length. That friction is the sound of a house going up.
