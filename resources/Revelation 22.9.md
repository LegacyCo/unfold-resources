## Commentary
“Then he said to me, ‘See that you do not do that. For I am your fellow servant… Worship God.’”

This is the second time John is corrected for falling at the angels feet. John fell at the feet of the messenger in Revelation 19 and received this same correction: “Do not do that! I am your fellow servant… Worship God” (==Revelation 19:10==). Three chapters later, overwhelmed again by what he has seen and heard, John does the same thing—and receives the same words. The apostle who leaned on Jesus’ chest, who saw the risen Christ in glory, who received more revelation than most of us combined, had to be told twice not to worship the messenger.

That should humble every one of us. Revelation does not immunize a person against misdirected awe. If John—overwhelmed by genuine encounter, in the middle of true revelation—could aim his worship at the one who delivered the word instead of the One the word was about, then no believer and no congregation is above that mistake. The danger isn’t limited to false prophets and counterfeit words; it’s present precisely where the revelation is real and the messenger is faithful. The more powerful the word, the stronger the pull to attach glory to the person who carried it.

And notice the messenger’s response, because it is the true heart of everyone who speaks for God: he refuses the worship instantly, identifies himself as a fellow servant, and redirects all of it—“Worship God.” He will not be the attraction. Anything genuinely prophetic deflects attention to Jesus, because the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy (==Revelation 19:10==). So the correction runs in both directions. To those who receive ministry: watch where your awe lands—gratitude for a vessel is right, worship of a vessel is not. And to those who minister: when the word you carry is powerful enough that people start looking at you, you’ve been handed the angel’s test. Pass it the way he did. Fellow servant. Worship God.
