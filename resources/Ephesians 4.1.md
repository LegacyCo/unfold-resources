## Commentary
Ephesians 4 begins with the word “therefore”—a word that demands we look back. For three chapters Paul has been stacking grace upon grace: blessed with every spiritual blessing, chosen before the foundation of the world, predestined to adoption, sealed with the Spirit, made alive when we were dead, raised and seated with Christ, saved by grace, brought into the household of God, rooted in a love that surpasses knowledge—and all of it from a God able to do exceedingly, abundantly beyond what we could ask or imagine (==Ephesians 1:3-14==; ==Ephesians 2:4-9==; ==Ephesians 3:19-20==). *Therefore*—walk worthy of the calling.

The order is essential to rightly understanding the gospel. The gospel is not “walk worthy so God will bless you.” The gospel is: because God has already bankrupted heaven for you, loved you from before the foundation of the world, and spared no expense to make you righteous and holy—*because* He has done all this—walk worthy of it.

Two Greek words unlock the verse. The word for “calling” is *kaleō*—an invitation from someone in a position of authority; in the ancient world, a summons to a king’s banquet. When I was in Iraq, I received an invitation from the governor of Mosul—a man with real authority over an important, historic region. My response revealed how much I valued the invitation: I rearranged my day, dressed appropriately, drove to where he was, honored his position, and spoke in a way that acknowledged his authority. My response to the invitation matched the value of the one who invited me. If we do that for earthly officials, how much more for the King of glory?

The word for “worthy” is *axios*—“of like value.” It’s a measurement word: picture an old set of scales, where the counterweight on one side determines the value of what sits on the other. Paul is saying, let the way you live weigh the same as the grace you’ve been given. Not to earn grace—to *reflect* it. 

How much is the sacrifice of Jesus worth to you? The answer isn’t in your words. It’s on the scales of how you live.
