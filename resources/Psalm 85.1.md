## Commentary
The favor of God on the land is inseparable from the restoration of His people.

The psalmist says, “Lord, You have been favorable to Your land.” Then he immediately explains what that favor looked like: “You have brought back the captivity of Jacob.” In other words, God showed favor to the land by restoring a people to it.

From the beginning, God gave dominion over the earth to His image bearers (==Genesis 1:26==). The land was never meant to be possessed by a people who misrepresented Him. Creation was made to flourish under the stewardship of sons who bear His image, walk in His ways, and fill the earth with His glory.

So when Israel was taken captive, the land suffered. It became desolate, not because the soil had done anything wrong, but because the people who were meant to carry God’s image in that place were absent, broken, and under judgment. The land was not receiving the blessing that comes when God’s people rightly possess what God has entrusted to them.

But when God brought Jacob back, He was not only restoring the people. He was showing favor to the land.

This is why the psalm continues, “Surely His salvation is near to those who fear Him, that glory may dwell in our land” (==Psalm 85:9==). Notice the order. Salvation comes near to the people, and glory dwells in the land. The land becomes a dwelling place for glory because the people have become a dwelling place for righteousness.

Then the psalm says, “Truth shall spring out of the earth,” and “our land will yield its increase” (==Psalm 85:11–12==). This is what happens when a restored people and a waiting land come back into alignment. Righteousness in the people produces fruitfulness in the land. Glory rests on the sons, and creation begins to respond.

The natural secondary consequence of God possessing a people is that His blessing begins to seep into everything His people possess.

The land is blessed because the people have been restored.  
The earth yields because the image bearers are rightly aligned.  
And glory fills the land because salvation has come near to those who fear Him.

## Commentary
God’s favor doesn’t stop with you. When He restores you, His blessing begins to touch what you touch. So don’t separate personal restoration from kingdom responsibility. Ask the Lord to make you the kind of image bearer whose life causes the spaces entrusted to you—your home, church, work, city, and land—to become more fruitful, more ordered, and more filled with His glory.
