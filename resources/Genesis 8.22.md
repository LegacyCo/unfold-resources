## Commentary
The reason God gives for His commitment to preserve the world sounds remarkably similar to the reason judgment came in the first place.

Before the flood:

“The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually” (==Genesis 6:5==).

After the flood:

“The imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth” (==Genesis 8:21==).

Humanity’s fundamental problem has not been solved by the flood.

The waters could remove violent people from the earth, but they could not remove violence from the human heart.

That is enormously important for understanding the rest of the Bible.

God does not look at Noah’s family and conclude that humanity has finally been fixed. He acknowledges that the same corruption remains. Yet instead of promising another flood, God commits Himself to preserving the world:

“While the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, winter and summer, and day and night shall not cease” (==Genesis 8:22==).

The future of creation will therefore depend not upon humanity becoming faithful enough to preserve it, but upon _God remaining faithful despite humanity’s unfaithfulness._

And this sets up the rest of the biblical story.

If judgment cannot change the human heart, something deeper is needed.

Eventually God will promise, “I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you” (==Ezekiel 36:26==). The answer to ==Genesis 6:5== is not ultimately another flood. It's a _new creation._
