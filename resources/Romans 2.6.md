## Commentary
Paul is not teaching salvation by works. That would contradict the whole argument of Romans, especially Romans 3–4. He is teaching that final judgment will reveal the truth about a person’s life. Works do not earn salvation, but they do bear witness to what kind of heart a person has.

This is a consistent biblical theme. Jesus says a tree is known by its fruit (Matthew 7:16–20). James says faith without works is dead (James 2:17). Paul himself will later say that believers are created in Christ Jesus for good works (Ephesians 2:10). So Romans 2 does not deny justification by faith. It exposes the danger of claiming righteousness while living in rebellion.
