## Commentary
This was not the first time David had been anointed as king. Roughly twenty-two years earlier, while Saul was still on the throne and David was just a young shepherd boy, Samuel had anointed him in the presence of his brothers and father (==1 Samuel 16:1–13==). At that moment, God had already chosen David to be Israel's next king. But being anointed for something and actually stepping into its fulfillment are not always the same thing.

The second time David was anointed is here in this passage (a parallel account is in ==2 Samuel 2:4==). David is thirty years old and the tribe of Judah anoints him king at Hebron. However, the remaining tribes of Israel still followed Saul's son Ish-bosheth, resulting in years of division and conflict. After David had ruled Judah for seven and a half years, the elders of all Israel finally came to Hebron and anointed him king over the entire nation (==2 Samuel 5:1–5==).

David did not become God's chosen king when the elders of Israel finally recognized him. He had been God's chosen king since the day Samuel anointed him. The recognition of men did not establish God's calling; it acknowledged what God had already determined.

## Application
There is a personal application in the years between David's first anointing and his eventual coronation. God had spoken, but the fulfillment did not come immediately. David experienced obscurity, opposition, betrayal, and seasons when the promise must have seemed impossibly distant. Yet the passing of time did not invalidate what God had said.

We sometimes assume that if God has called us to something, the path toward its fulfillment should be straightforward. David's life reminds us otherwise. There can be a considerable distance between the promise and its fulfillment, and what happens in that interval is often as important as the fulfillment itself. 

The final words of this verse bring everything into perspective: “according to the word of the LORD by Samuel.” Years had passed, circumstances had changed, and generations of political leadership had shifted, but God's word had not failed. What He had declared in the obscurity of Jesse's household was eventually acknowledged by the elders of the entire nation.

The question for us is whether we can remain faithful to what God has spoken even when the circumstances around us seem to contradict it. We do not need to manufacture the fulfillment of God's promises through ambition or manipulation. Our responsibility is to remain faithful to Him, allowing Him to shape our character as we walk toward whatever He has called us to do.
