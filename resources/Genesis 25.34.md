## Commentary
“And Jacob gave Esau bread and stew of lentils; then he ate and drank, arose, and went his way. Thus Esau despised his birthright.”

Here’s the thing we typically forget about this story: Esau got what he wanted. He came in from the field starving, his desire aimed at a hot meal, and he received exactly that—bread, stew, a full stomach. He wasn’t cheated. He was *paid*. The transaction completed precisely as he wanted it to. And that’s what makes Scripture’s closing line so heartbreaking. Not “thus Esau was robbed,” but “thus Esau *despised* his birthright.” The tragedy is that he looked at his birthright—covenant, inheritance, the blessing of Abraham flowing through his line—looked at a bowl of stew, and priced them as equal value.

This is a sobering principle of God’s dealings: you get what you aim at. Jesus said it about the hypocrites who gave to be seen—“they have their reward” (==Matthew 6:2==). Paid in full, exactly the currency they wanted, nothing further owed. God is so good that He will give you your heart’s desire—and that’s precisely what should sober us. If your heart wants applause, you’ll get applause. If it wants the stew, you’ll get the stew. Your desire will hit exactly where it was aiming. Hebrews holds Esau up as the permanent warning: a profane person “who for one morsel of food sold his birthright,” who afterward found no way to undo the trade, though he sought the blessing with tears (==Hebrews 12:16-17==).

But turn the principle over, because the same truth that ruined Esau is one of the best promises in the Bible for the righteous. Aim your desire at the Father, and you'll hit that target too: “your Father who sees in secret will Himself reward you openly” (==Matthew 6:4==). He “is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him” (==Hebrews 11:6==). So the question this story leaves us with isn’t whether you’ll receive what you’re seeking. You will—in full, either way. The question is what you’re aiming at, and whether it’s worth more than your birthright.
