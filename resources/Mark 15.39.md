## Commentary
“So when the centurion, who stood opposite Him, saw that He cried out like this and breathed His last, he said, ‘Truly this Man was the Son of God!’”

It’s easy to think the miracle is what will convince an unbelieving world that Jesus is who He says He is. If the man in the wheelchair runs through the street, they’ll believe. If the blind man sees, his whole family will believe. But look at the record. Jesus rebuked Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum—the cities where *most* of His mighty works were done—“because they did not repent” (==Matthew 11:20-24==). They saw the miracles. They stayed unmoved. Signs and wonders, by themselves, won’t always bring the lost to repentance. People get healed by the power of God and never surrender to the God who healed them. I've seen it firsthand.

Now look at who *did* believe at Golgotha. A Roman centurion—a career executioner who had watched men die by the dozens—saw no healing that day. Quite the opposite, actually. He saw an obedient death. He watched how Jesus suffered, how He cried out, how He gave up His spirit, and how creation itself responded to that obedience—darkness at noon, the earth quaking, rocks splitting (==Matthew 27:51-54==). And out of the mouth of a hardened pagan soldier came the confession Israel’s miracle-saturated cities never made: *truly, this Man was the Son of God.* What the miracles couldn’t produce, obedience did.

Don’t hear what I'm not saying. We should believe for miracles, pray for the sick, and contend for signs that confirm the gospel—amen. But let’s keep our hopes lying in the right place. A radical life of obedience is itself a witness that God is real. Noah preached that way for years—every obedient swing of his axe was a testimony to those around him and a plea for repentance (==2 Peter 2:5==). The world has seen our conferences and heard our claims, and like Chorazin, they have not repented. Perhaps they need the same thing the centurion say, radical obedience to and faith in God when it makes no sense.
