## Commentary
How Israel was to live under the old covenant had to be legislated for them. The law was sanctioned as the governing structure of their culture. It had to be, because the Spirit was not yet poured out.

Here, Paul tells the Galatians that they had slipped back to living under the schoolmaster of the law. Schoolmasters are for children. The schoolmaster is an announcement that the child is not yet mature enough to make the right decisions. So the tutor teaches and brings the child into a place of maturity. The child who is under guardianship of the tutor isn't given the freedom to choose what to do, they are told what to do. 

But when the Spirit was poured out, or as Paul writes to the Galatians, "when faith came," we were no longer under the tutor, we are in the Spirit. We have the ability now to do what we could not under the law. The law told us not to murder; the Spirit helps us not to hate our brother. The law told us to give 10%; the Spirit compels us to give all. The law told us not to commit adultery; the Spirit helps us not to look with lust in our hearts.

## Application
The standard has been raised to impossible heights because the impossibly powerful Holy Spirit has descended on us from on high. If we aren't walking in the Spirit, then we will naturally go back to the schoolmaster. This is the tension many believers find themselves in, because love can't be legislated. Preference for your brother can't be sanctioned. Honor for one another can't be demanded. The ways of the kingdom are chosen. The King Himself never demands to be followed, the choice is always given to either receive Him or reject Him. So also are the ways of His Kingdom. We can either choose to walk in the Spirit and love one another with evangelistic love, or we can choose to walk under the guardianship of the schoolmaster.
