## Commentary
God is giving Moses the consecration rites for Aaron and his sons. This is how priests were set apart for service before the Lord. The sacrifice was an essential part of the consecration ceremony, but it wasn't something they simply observed. 

After the ram was offered, Aaron and his sons were commanded to eat its flesh, along with the bread, at the door of the tabernacle.

That detail matters.

The very sacrifice by which atonement was made became the meal they received. The lamb that consecrated them also became the food that marked them as holy. They were set apart by sacrifice, and then they participated in that sacrifice by eating.

So when Jesus says in John 6, “Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you,” He is not introducing a disconnected idea. He is fulfilling the priestly pattern.

Under the old covenant, the flesh of the sacrifice consecrated Aaron and his sons for service in the tabernacle. But now the true Lamb of God gives His own flesh, not only to consecrate us, but to give us eternal life and bring us into union with Himself.

That means communion is not just remembering that a sacrifice happened. It is entering into that sacrifice by way of receiving the Lamb whose sacrifice made us holy.
