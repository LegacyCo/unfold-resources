## Commentary
“And your house and your kingdom shall be established forever before you. Your throne shall be established forever.”

This chapter turns on a wordplay. David wanted to build God a house—a temple of cedar and stone (==2 Samuel 7:2==). God declined, and then reversed the offer: “the LORD tells you that He will make *you* a house” (==2 Samuel 7:11==). The house God promises David in this verse is not a palace. It is a family—a lineage, established forever, that would one day bring forth the Messiah whose kingdom has no end (==Luke 1:32-33==). David offered God a building; God promised David a family. In that exchange is the whole biblical meaning of “house.”

Neither Hebrew nor Greek has a word for the small social unit we call the nuclear family. The Hebrew *bayit* and the Greek *oikos* both mean “house”—and both describe the building people live in *and* the people who live in the building. When Joshua says, “As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD” (==Joshua 24:15==), he doesn’t mean his floor plan; he means his family. Scripture uses one word for both because in its world, the dwelling and the family were one reality.

When God speaks of His house, the definition expands past architecture to the family who becomes the dwelling place of His presence. He does not live in a house made with human hands (==Acts 7:48==); He lives in a house made of human *beings*—living stones being built into a spiritual house (==1 Peter 2:5==), family members of the household of God (==Ephesians 2:19==). Even Psalm 127:1 reads differently in this light: “Unless the LORD builds the house, they labor in vain who build it”—the house the LORD is building is a family. He has been building it since He told David that thrones and temples pass, but a household established by God endures forever.

## Word Study
- *house* : **bayit** — dwelling, household, family; the building people live in and the people who live in the building, carried by a single word. Every occurrence of “house” in 2 Samuel 7 is bayit—which is what makes the chapter’s reversal possible: David hears “house” as a building; God means a family.

Watch the word’s range:

- ==Genesis 7:1== — “Come into the ark, you and all your *house* (bayit).” Not furniture—Noah’s family.
- ==Exodus 1:21== — because the midwives feared God, “He provided *households* (bayit) for them.” God’s reward for faithfulness was to build them families.
- ==Hebrews 3:6== — the Greek counterpart completes the thought: “Christ as a Son over His own *house* (oikos), whose house we are.” The house God is still building has people for its stones.

Summary: bayit holds together what English splits apart—the place and the people. When God promised David a bayit established forever, He was promising what He has wanted since Eden and will have in the end: not a structure that contains Him, but a family that dwells with Him.
