## Commentary
Mark presents here a striking limitation to the ministry of Jesus: “He could do no mighty work there… and He marveled because of their unbelief.” The language is careful. It does not suggest any deficiency in Jesus Himself, but it does establish that the expression of His power was restrained in that setting. The few healings that do occur only highlight the contrast—what was possible in part was not realized in fullness.

And the explanation given? Unbelief. The people of Nazareth encounter Jesus in familiarity rather than faith. Their knowledge of Him according to the flesh—His upbringing, His family—prevents them from receiving Him according to who He is. As a result, the Mighty One could not be Himself with His people.

This echoes the tension of Jeremiah 14:9, where the prophet asks, “Why should You be like a man astonished, like a mighty one who cannot save?” Jeremiah’s language reflects the perception of divine inaction—the sense that the One known for saving appears, in that moment, unable or unwilling to do so.

In Nazareth, what Jeremiah perceived as an abstract idea takes on flesh in the life of Jesus. The Mighty One stands present, yet the expected works are absent. The text does not diminish Jesus’ power, but locates the issue in the human response of those around Him. Unbelief does not nullify His identity, but it results in a limitation in what Jesus does next.

Mark 6 preserves a theological tension also present in Jeremiah: God remains the Mighty One, yet there are contexts in which His saving action is not expressed in its fullness.
