## Commentary
There is something remarkable about David's appeal to God in this verse. He prays, “Preserve my life, for I am godly/holy; You are my God; Save Your servant who trusts in You.” The word translated “godly" or "holy” in some translations is the Hebrew *chasid*, which is closely related to *chesed*, the word frequently used throughout the Old Testament to describe God's steadfast love, mercy, and covenant faithfulness. A *chasid* is someone characterized by this kind of loyal love—a faithful or devoted person who lives in relationship with God. It literally means *the faithful one* or *the godly one*.

David is not claiming to be morally perfect or without sin. He is identifying himself as someone who belongs to God, who has remained devoted to Him, and whose life is marked by covenant loyalty. Notice how he immediately reinforces this identity: “You are my God; save Your servant who trusts in You.” David understands himself in terms of his relationship with God.

David appeals to his covenant relationship with God as the basis for expecting God's mercy and protection. He is not approaching God as a stranger hoping to persuade Him to be kind. He approaches as someone who knows he belongs to the LORD, as a child who knows they belong to their parent, and has confidence in the covenant faithfulness of the One to whom he is praying.

This becomes even clearer as the psalm unfolds. In verse 5, David declares that the LORD is good, ready to forgive, and abundant in mercy (*chesed*) toward all who call upon Him. Then, in verse 15, he describes God as “full of compassion, and gracious, longsuffering and abundant in mercy and truth.” David's confidence is rooted in his understanding of God's character. He can appeal to God as a *chasid* because he knows the God to whom he belongs is characterized by *chesed*.

## Application
Through Jesus Christ, we have been brought into a covenant relationship established upon His finished work. We are not approaching God as outsiders hoping to convince Him to accept us. We come as His children, confident in the love and faithfulness of the Father who has already welcomed us through His Son (==Hebrews 4:16; Romans 8:15–17==).

Our confidence in prayer should be rooted in our covenant relationship with God.

When you pray, do you approach God as someone who belongs to Him, or as someone who must continually convince Him to care? David understood that he was a *chasid*, someone living in a relationship of covenant love with God. That understanding shaped the confidence with which he approached the LORD.

The more deeply we understand the covenant into which Christ has brought us, the more confidently we can approach the Father, knowing that His mercy is an expression of the steadfast love He has already committed to show us.
