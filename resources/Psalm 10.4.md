## Commentary
The Hebrew word translated “thoughts” in this verse is *mezimmah*, which can refer to a person's plans, intentions, purposes, or schemes. The psalmist is describing something more significant than a person who simply doesn't think about God very often. He is describing someone who has organized his life without reference to God. God has no meaningful place in his decisions, his ambitions, his relationships, or his understanding of right and wrong.

Notice how the verse begins: “The wicked in his proud countenance does not seek God.” The absence of God from his thoughts is directly connected to his pride. He has become so convinced of his own sufficiency that he sees no reason to seek the Lord. He does not consult God because he does not believe he needs Him. His own desires have become the authority by which he orders his life.

This doesn't necessarily mean the person is an atheist. In fact, later in the psalm, the wicked says, “God has forgotten; He hides His face; He will never see” (==Psalm 10:11==). He has some awareness of God, but he lives as though God is unconcerned with his actions and will never hold him accountable. His problem is not necessarily a lack of belief in God's existence, but a refusal to acknowledge God's authority.

Solomon writes, “In all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths” (==Proverbs 3:6==). The word all is important. God is not interested in being consulted only when we encounter a crisis or face a decision too difficult to make on our own. He intends to be acknowledged in every part of our lives.

And acknowledging God means more than occasionally remembering that He is present. It means giving His Word and His ways authority over our plans, allowing His character to shape how we treat others, and submitting our desires to His will.
