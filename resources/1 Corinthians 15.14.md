## Commentary
"And if Christ is not risen, then our preaching is empty and your faith is also empty."

Paul is not exaggerating. Everything hinges here. Without the resurrection there may still be a sacrifice, and there may still be blood—but there is no announcement from heaven that the sacrifice was *accepted*. A lamb on an altar is only an offering. It's God's response to the offering that makes it a covenant.

Think of the Old Testament pattern. When Elijah built the altar on Carmel, the fire falling was heaven's verdict on the sacrifice (==1 Kings 18:38==). When Solomon dedicated the temple, the glory filling the house was God saying, "Accepted" (==2 Chronicles 7:1==). The cross was the offering. The empty tomb was the fire falling. When the Spirit raised Jesus from the grave, it was the Father's public declaration that the blood was sufficient, the debt was paid, and sin and death had been defeated (==Romans 1:4==, ==Acts 2:24==).

That's why a dead Messiah leaves your faith empty—not because His love wasn't real, but because you'd have no way of knowing whether it *worked*. The evidence of His love for you is the cross. The proof that love accomplished its goal is the empty tomb.

Paul says the same thing from the other side a few verses later: "if Christ is not risen, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins" (==1 Corinthians 15:17==). Still in your sins—because a sacrifice with no resurrection is a sacrifice with no receipt. But He *is* risen. Which means everything God required on behalf of your sin has been paid in full.
