## Word Study
The word for "uphold" is *tāmak*. It means to take hold of, to grasp firmly. It is the same word used in Isaiah 41:10 - "Do not fear, for I am with you...I will also uphold you with My righteous right hand." 

The word for "integrity" is *tōm*. It means purity, perfection, innocence, blamelessness, completeness. A new testament equivalent would be *telos* - maturity, perfection, completeness (see Hebrews 10:14 - "For by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified").

The question we have to ask is this: Is God looking for the blameless to take hold of and preserve in His presence *or* are those who are taken hold of by God made pure by His presence? 

I believe Scripture teaches us the latter.

Some other verses that speak into this:
	- Jude 24 - "Now to Him who is able to keep you from stumbling, and to cause to stand blameless in the presence of His glory with exceedingly great joy..."
		- He keeps us from stumbling and keeps us in His presence (literally "to put into an upright position")
	- 1 John 5:18 - "We know that whoever is born of God does not sin; but He who was born of God keeps him, and the wicked one does not touch him" (NASB)
		- He keeps us (literally "to be the keeper of, to look after") and because of His protection the wicked one does not touch us...therefore, those born of God can live in victory over sin. This is the foundation that John stands on in his letter when he writes five times that the born-again believer should live free from sin (1 John 2:6, 2:1, 3:6, 3:9)

## Application
Our role is to hold fast to the One who is holding on to us. What do you do if you're slipping and someone grabs hold of your arm? What do you do if you're drowning and someone throws you a life line? What do you do if you're in a burning building and a fireman grabs your shirt? You reach out and lay hold of the one who is laying hold of you.

That's the language Paul uses in his first letter to Timothy, "But you, O man of God, flee these things (the love of money, pride, division, strife, envy, etc) and pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, gentleness. Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold of eternal life" (1 Timothy 6:11-12).

The Greek for "lay hold of" is *epilambanō*. It has a dual meaning: a violent apprehension of *and* a trustful holding of the hand. It's the word used to describe Jesus reaching out to grab Peter when he was drowning.

The One who is Eternal life has laid hold of Timothy, and the necessary response is to turn and violently and trustfully lay hold of Him.

This is the image that Paul paints for the Philippians, "Not that I have already obtained (my goal), or am already perfected; but I press on, that I may lay hold of (*epilambanō*) that for which Christ Jesus has also laid hold of me (*epilambanō*. Brethren, I do not count myself to have laid hold of it (*epilambanō*); but one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind and reaching forward to those things which are ahead, I press toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 3:12-14). Jesus has laid hold of Paul, so now Paul's response is turn and lay hold of Jesus.

This is the goal of every born-again believer; we don't focus our attention on the behavior or the life circumstance, we focus on laying hold of Jesus. If my aim is not sinning, finding peace, or fixing my problem, then I'm focusing on the wrong thing. If my aim is Jesus, then everything else in the kingdom becomes my inheritance — and *He* keeps me in perfect peace, *He* keeps me from falling into temptation, *He* keeps me in righteousness. "Seek *first* the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all of these things will be added to you" (Matthew 6:33).
