## Commentary
God’s word does not need reinforcement. He is not a man, that He should lie (==Numbers 23:19==), and this same chapter declares it is impossible for God to lie. His speech contains no binding and non-binding categories, no technically-true statements with escape clauses. When He speaks, reality obeys: “Let there be light,” and there is light. “Peace, be still,” and the storm stops (==Mark 4:39==). “Lazarus, come out,” and a dead man walks. His word does not describe reality—it creates it, because His word and His character are one.

That is what makes this verse astonishing. When God made His promise to Abraham, “desiring even more to demonstrate to the heirs of the promise the fact that His promise is unchangeable,” He “confirmed it with an oath.” The One who cannot lie, who has never broken a word, swore anyway. Why? The next verse answers: so that “we who have taken refuge would have strong encouragement” (==Hebrews 6:18==). God did not swear because His word needed a brace. He swore because our faith did. A long history of broken promises has taught humanity to read fine print into everything—sometimes even into the promises of God. So the God who needs no oath stooped down into the economy of our broken language and gave us one.

And His oath has a name. Every promise God has ever made is embodied in the man Christ Jesus, “the Amen, the faithful and true Witness” (==Revelation 3:14==), “for all the promises of God in Him are Yes” (==2 Corinthians 1:20==). For anyone whom betrayal has made unable to take another person at their word, this is where trust begins to be rebuilt—the same way it was broken, one word at a time, from the One whose words are always kept.
