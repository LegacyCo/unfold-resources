## Commentary
“For the needy shall not always be forgotten; the expectation of the poor shall not perish forever.”

The poor and needy may be overlooked by people, but they are never overlooked by God. Scripture repeatedly presents God as the defender of those who have little power, influence, or protection. “The LORD is a refuge for the oppressed, a refuge in times of trouble” (==Psalm 9:9==). What feels forgotten on earth is still fully seen in heaven.

The phrase “shall not always be forgotten” acknowledges something important: there are seasons when it can feel as though God has forgotten. Deliverance does not always come immediately. Justice is not always visible. The poor may wait, pray, and suffer for a long time. But delay is not abandonment.

David’s confidence is that the present condition is not the final condition. God will act. The forgotten will be remembered. The oppressed will be defended. The cry that seems unanswered will not disappear into silence forever.

“The expectation of the poor shall not perish forever” speaks of hope that survives because it is anchored in God. The word “expectation” carries the idea of what someone is waiting and hoping for. The poor may have very little in their hands right now, but they still have somewhere to place their hope.

God draws near to those the world pushes aside (==Psalm 34:6==; ==Psalm 72:12–14==; ==Luke 4:18==). His kingdom repeatedly reverses human systems of value, lifting the lowly and confronting those who use power without mercy.

Psalm 9 does not promise that the needy will never suffer. It promises that their suffering will not have the final word.
