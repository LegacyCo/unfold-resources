## Commentary
“The rainbow shall be in the cloud, and I will look on it to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth.”

God attaches His own remembering to a visible sign. He does not need a reminder—but He builds one into the sky, and in doing so He teaches His people the practice of remembering. The same God later told Israel to carry stones out of the Jordan and pile them up—uncut, unshaped—so that when their children asked what the stones meant, the story of what God had done would be told again (==Joshua 4:6-7==). Memorial stones—a keepsake from an answered prayer, an object tied to a moment of provision—remind a believer who God is and what it means to be in covenant with Him.

Once the memorial becomes just an object, a person disconnects from how God has blessed him in the past and begins looking around for future blessing, comparing what he has with what others have. This is what happened to Israel. When they forgot what the stones by the Jordan represented, they began comparing themselves to other nations, worshiping other gods, and asking for a king like everyone else had (==1 Samuel 8:5==). If your mind isn't filled with the memory of God's activity in your life, it will be filled with something else… and that will uproot and dissuade your affection onto other things. God looks on the sign to remember. His people are meant to do the same.

## Application
Keep tangible reminders of what God has done—and use them. Tell the stories behind them to your children and friends often, the way Israel was meant to answer, “What do these stones mean?” When doubt comes, or when comparison starts pulling your gaze sideways, go back to the memorial stones and rehearse His faithfulness out loud. A heart filled with the memory of God's activity has no room for envy of someone else's.
