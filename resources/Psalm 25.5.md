## Commentary
To wait on God is one of the highest uses of the human will. We often think freedom means doing whatever we want, whenever we want, however we want. But in Scripture, the will reaches its noblest expression when it freely bows before God and says, “Lead me. Teach me. I will wait for You.”

Andrew Murray said it this way in _Waiting on God_: “There is nothing more noble or more blessed in the exercise of our free will than to use it in waiting upon God.”

That is exactly what David is doing in Psalm 25. He is not waiting because he has no other choice. He is waiting because he has chosen God over his own understanding. He is taking the freedom of his will and placing it under the leadership of the Lord.

Waiting on God is the will refusing to run ahead, refusing to take control, refusing to make itself lord of the moment. It is the soul actively placing itself under the government of God.

There is nothing more noble we can do with our will than give it back to the One who gave it to us.
