## Commentary
“Therefore lay aside all filthiness and overflow of wickedness, and receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.”

The power is in the word: it is “able to save your souls.” No word from God arrives without the ability to perform what it says (==Luke 1:37==). The soil is in the receiver: “receive with meekness.” The word carries the power of transformation, and how you receive it determines whether that power is released in your life (==Mark 4:20==). And James names a step we tend to skip—*lay aside* filthiness and wickedness first. A farmer clears the ground before he plants. The word was never meant to be received into a heart still crowded with the things that choke it (==Mark 4:18-19==).

Notice who James is writing to and what he says the word saves. These are believers—“my beloved brethren” (==James 1:19==)—so he isn’t describing initial salvation. The word, received rightly and continually, keeps saving the soul: renewing the mind, healing the desires, forming Christ in us. It works the way a seed works in a field—out of sight, from underneath, until what was planted shows up in the harvest.

## Word Study
- *implanted* : **emphytos** — planted within; engrafted so that it takes root and grows as part of the one who received it. This is the word’s only appearance in the New Testament. James doesn’t say the word is taught to us or delivered to us. He says it is planted in us—a seed in soil, a graft in a tree—so that its life and ours grow together.

The picture runs through Scripture:

- ==Mark 4:14== — “The sower sows the word.” Jesus established the image: God’s word is seed, and the heart is ground.
- ==1 Peter 1:23== — born again “through the word of God which lives and abides forever.” Incorruptible seed, already alive in the one who received it.

Summary: a word that has been implanted cannot be handled like information. It has to go into the ground of a meek heart, where it can take root and do what it came to do. So the question this verse leaves with us is a simple one: has the word you received been planted, or is it still lying on the surface?
