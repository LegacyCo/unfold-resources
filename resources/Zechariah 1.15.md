## Commentary
The Lord often uses men as instruments of His judgment—and this verse shows that the instrument's cruelty is never His. When Israel broke covenant, God raised up Babylon as the instrument of judgment against them (==Habakkuk 1:6==). Judgment for sin was necessary. But the NIV renders the verse, “I was only a little angry, but they went too far with the punishment.” Babylon acted wickedly and was far more severe than the discipline required, and God declares Himself exceedingly angry about it. The same distinction appears with Assyria, the rod of God's anger that “did not mean so,” intending destruction beyond its commission (==Isaiah 10:5-7==). God's purposes can run through human agents while the agents' wickedness remains entirely their own.

This distinction matters most at the cross. The leaders of Israel, to whom Jesus was delivered for the judgment of our sin, went too far in exactly Babylon's way. What atonement required was blood from the Lamb (==Hebrews 9:22==)—not torture. Nowhere in the Law did it say the sacrificial lamb was to be brutalized in order for the atoning sacrifice to be made. But this Lamb, unlike every lamb before Him, suffered: mocked, scourged, spit upon, brutalized at the hands of men in the permitted hour of darkness (==Luke 22:53==; ==Matthew 16:21==). The prophets foretold the suffering (==Acts 3:18==) because they saw what wicked men would do when restraint was lifted—not because the Father required cruelty. The cross accomplishes God's judgment of sin, and at the same time the hands that bloodied His Son acted “with evil intent,” answerable for every blow (==Acts 2:23==). God required blood. Men supplied brutality. The verse teaches us never to confuse the two.
