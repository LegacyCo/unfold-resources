## Commentary
“Then He said to the disciples, ‘It is impossible that no offenses should come.’”

Jesus doesn’t say offenses are unlikely, or that mature believers will eventually rise above the reach of them. He says it is *impossible* that they won’t come. In this age, you will be wounded—misunderstood, wronged, let down, betrayed. Which means the question of your life is never whether you’ll have the opportunity to be offended. The question is what you’ll do with the offense when it arrives: hold it, let it take root—or release it with forgiveness.

Offense held is more dangerous than we think, because it doesn’t stay still. It grows, and it distorts how we see. John Bevere writes in *The Bait of Satan*, “Offended people will be able to find Scripture passages to back their position, but it is not the correct division of God’s Word. The knowledge of God’s Word without love is a destructive force because it puffs us up with pride and legalism. This causes us to justify our offense rather than repent of the unforgiveness.” That’s the trap: offense armed with Bible verses feels like discernment while they metastasize into bitterness.

Now notice what Jesus does *not* mean by forgiveness. Two verses later: “If your brother sins against you, rebuke him; and if he repents, forgive him” (==Luke 17:3==). Forgiveness isn’t being a doormat—sin gets confronted, in love, with truth. But what if they never repent? Search the new covenant and you will find no permission to withhold forgiveness while you wait for repentance. God demonstrated His own love toward us “in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us” (==Romans 5:8==). Jesus took on the scars of forgiveness before we ever said we were sorry. So we don’t have the luxury of waiting on our neighbor’s repentance before we bear the cross of forgiving them. Offenses will come—that’s guaranteed. Whether they take root in you is not.

## Word Study
- *offenses* : **skandalon** — originally the bait-stick of a trap: the movable trigger an animal bumps that springs the snare shut. From it we get our word *scandal*. A skandalon isn’t just something that hurts you—it’s something designed to *catch* you. The offense is the bait; the trap is what closes around the one who takes it.

Watch the word work elsewhere:

- ==Matthew 16:23== — Jesus to Peter: “Get behind Me, Satan! You are an *offense* (skandalon) to Me.” Even well-meaning words from a friend can be bait toward the wrong path.
- ==Romans 14:13== — resolve “not to put a stumbling block (skandalon) in our brother’s way.” We can become the trap for someone else.
- ==Matthew 13:41== — at the end of the age the angels gather “all things that *offend* (skandalon)” out of His kingdom. The traps don’t survive the harvest.

Summary: Jesus is saying the woods are full of traps—that’s guaranteed. But a trap only works on the animal that takes the bait. The offense that comes to you is the trigger-stick; unforgiveness is you springing it on yourself. Recognize the bait for what it is, and you can walk past a trap that was never able to close on its own.
