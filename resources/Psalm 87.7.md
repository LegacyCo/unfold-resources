## Commentary
“Both the singers and the players on instruments say, ‘All my springs are in you.’”

The psalm ends with the citizens of Zion, including the ones God has just registered from Egypt, Babylon, and the other nations (==Psalm 87:4-6==), singing and playing, and the chorus of their song is: “All my springs are in you.”

A spring is a source. The Hebrew word for spring is *ma'yan* (מַעְיָן), which means a place where water comes up out of the ground on its own. That's different from a cistern, which only holds what's been poured into it. So the singers are saying that everything that keeps them alive comes from this city, and from the God who lives in it.

That was a bold thing to sing about Jerusalem. The great powers named in verse 4 were built on great rivers. Egypt had the Nile. Babylon had the Euphrates. Jerusalem sat on a hill with no river at all and one modest spring, the Gihon. By any natural measure she was the dry city. But another psalm of the sons of Korah says, “There is a river whose streams shall make glad the city of God” (==Psalm 46:4==). Zion's water supply was God Himself. “For with You is the fountain of life” (==Psalm 36:9==).

The prophets used similar language when they spoke about Jerusalem. Ezekiel saw water flowing out from under the threshold of the temple, getting deeper the farther it went (==Ezekiel 47:1-5==). Zechariah said, “living waters shall flow from Jerusalem” (==Zechariah 14:8==). And Jeremiah warned what happens when God's people look for water somewhere else: “they have forsaken Me, the fountain of living waters, and hewn themselves cisterns—broken cisterns that can hold no water” (==Jeremiah 2:13==).

Fast forward to when Jesus stood up in Jerusalem, at a feast, and said, “If anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink” (==John 7:37==). He told a woman at a well, “the water that I shall give him will become in him a fountain of water springing up into everlasting life” (==John 4:14==). The spring the Old Testament sang about turned out to be a Person. And the Bible's last picture of the city of God is “a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding from the throne of God and of the Lamb” (==Revelation 22:1==).

## Application
“All my springs” is a big claim. It means joy, identity, strength, peace, and purpose all come from one place. So it's worth asking where you actually go when you're dry. For some of us it's work. For others it's another person's approval, or food, or a screen. Those are cisterns. They hold a little for a while, and then they're empty. Identify the one you go to most. The next time you reach for it, go to Jesus first. Open the Scriptures, pray, and tell Him that He's your source. Isaiah says, “with joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation” (==Isaiah 12:3==), and “wells” there is the same Hebrew word as “springs” here.
