## Commentary
The word James uses for “be patient” is _makrothymeō_. It carries the idea of being long-tempered, slow to anger, slow to lose heart, and willing to endure without giving way to agitation. This is not passive waiting. This is the active resistance of impatience.

James has just used the picture of a farmer waiting for the precious fruit of the earth. The farmer is not lazy, but he is also not in control. He can plant. He can tend the ground. He can remain faithful. But he cannot force the rain, and he cannot hurry the harvest. There is a kind of waiting that requires strength because everything in you wants to dig up the seed just to see if anything is happening.

That's why James says, “Establish your hearts.” Waiting exposes the instability of the heart. It reveals our need to make something happen, our fear that God has forgotten, and our suspicion that the promise needs our impatience in order to survive.

But patience is faith stretched across time. It is consciously choosing hope in the middle of delay. It is standing firm when the whisper comes, “Just do something.” And sometimes the most faithful thing you can do is refuse to move before God moves.

There is nothing passive about patiently enduring. _Makrothymeō_ is the holy refusal to surrender your heart to agitation while you wait for what God has promised.
