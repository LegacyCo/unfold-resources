## Commentary
A man falls overboard in the middle of the ocean. He’s unconscious, sinking. If you throw him a manual titled *How to Swim*, it won’t help. If you shout encouragement from the deck, it won’t help. He doesn’t need advice or motivation—he needs rescue. Someone has to jump in and do for him what he cannot do for himself. That’s grace.

But as good as that sounds, it’s an incomplete picture of the grace God gives. So adjust the story. Imagine the man went overboard while trying to escape capture—he was being pursued because he had just hurt *you*. You reach him before the authorities do. He needs rescue, and he doesn’t deserve rescue. And you jump in anyway, pull him out, and breathe life back into him. *That’s* the kind of grace God shows us: “when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son” (==Romans 5:10==).

That’s why the first words of this verse may be the most beautiful hinge in the Bible: “*But God*, who is rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ.” Paul has just spent three verses describing our condition—dead in sin, walking according to the course of this world, children of wrath (==Ephesians 2:1-3==). Dead men don’t swim. Dead men don’t even reach for the rope. Every other religion throws instructions to a corpse. The gospel is God diving in.

Here’s a working definition of grace worth keeping: the unearned, undeserved, and unstoppable favor of God, freely given to sinners because of His great love, accomplished through Christ, and received by faith. Notice where it starts—not with our sin, but with His love. Mercy holds back what we deserved; grace hands us what we never could have earned. And both flow from the same fountain: a God who is *rich* in mercy, and whose love reached us at the bottom of the ocean.
