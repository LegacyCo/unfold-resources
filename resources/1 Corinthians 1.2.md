## Word Study
Paul opens 1 Corinthians by writing, “To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called saints.”

That is a stunning way to address this church, especially when you know what comes next. Corinth was not exactly a model congregation. They were divided, immature, confused about sexuality, lawsuits, communion, spiritual gifts, and resurrection. And yet Paul does not begin by calling them failures. He begins by calling them *saints*.

The NKJV says “called to be saints,” but “to be” is not actually in the Greek. Paul is not saying they are called to eventually become saints if they get their act together. He is saying they are called saints. The wording is the same as the previous verse, where Paul calls himself “an apostle of Jesus Christ.” Just as Paul’s calling identified him as an apostle, their calling identified them as saints.

The word “saint” is _hagios_, which means holy, consecrated, set apart, sanctified. So Paul is not using “saint” as a title for unusually spiritual Christians. He is using it as the basic identity of everyone who belongs to Jesus.

That means holiness begins with what God calls us, not with what we have managed to prove. The Corinthians still had to grow. They still needed correction. Their lives still had to come into alignment with Christ. But Paul corrects them *from* identity, not *toward* identity.

They are not trying to become holy enough to be called saints. They are saints learning how to live holy.
