## Commentary
“But when He saw the multitudes, He was moved with compassion for them, because they were weary and scattered, like sheep having no shepherd.”

The Greek verb for what Jesus feels in this verse is _splagchnizomai_, a word used for deep, visceral compassion. It comes from a word referring to the inward parts or bowels, which in the ancient world were associated with the deepest emotions. Jesus is not moving through life with detached sympathy. He is affected deeply by what He sees.

Matthew describes the people around Jesus as “weary and scattered,” though the underlying words carry an even stronger sense. They can describe people who are harassed, troubled, distressed, thrown down, or left helpless. Jesus describes them as “sheep having no shepherd.”

When Moses asked God to appoint someone to lead Israel after his death, he prayed that the people would not be “like sheep which have no shepherd” (==Numbers 27:17==). Centuries later, the prophets repeatedly used shepherd language to describe Israel’s leaders. Kings, priests, and rulers were supposed to care for God's flock, protect them, feed them, and lead them faithfully. But many had failed.

Ezekiel delivers one of the strongest accusations against Israel’s shepherds:

“The weak you have not strengthened, nor have you healed those who were sick, nor bound up the broken, nor brought back what was driven away, nor sought what was lost” (==Ezekiel 34:4==).

Because the shepherds had failed, God promised, “Indeed I Myself will search for My sheep and seek them out” (==Ezekiel 34:11==). Jesus is the answer to that promise.

Where others may see inconvenience, immaturity, sin, ignorance, or neediness, Jesus sees sheep who need a shepherd. He understands what prolonged neglect does to people.

Sheep without a shepherd wander. They become vulnerable. They lack direction. They are easily scattered and easily wounded. The problem is not that the sheep have behaved badly. The problem is that they have not been properly cared for. And Jesus’ response is compassion.

This is especially significant because Jesus is surrounded by religious leadership throughout Matthew. There were teachers, Pharisees, scribes, priests, and synagogue leaders all around. Israel did not lack religious activity. It lacked shepherding.

People can be surrounded by religion and still be spiritually exhausted and uncared for.

In the next verse Jesus says, “The harvest truly is plentiful, but the laborers are few” (==Matthew 9:37==). Then in ==Matthew 10:1== He gives His disciples authority and sends them out. Jesus sees the condition of the people, feels compassion for them, and then raises up people to care for them.
