## Commentary
The sons of Sceva attempted to use the name of Jesus they had heard Paul preach. Their language was correct, but the reality behind the language was absent. They knew the formula, but they did not know the Lord whose name they invoked.

The evil spirit answered, “Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are you?” The spiritual realm recognized the difference between borrowed vocabulary and authentic authority.

There is a danger in learning how other people speak about God without living lives of surrender to God ourselves. We can imitate language, methods, tone, and outward expressions of spiritual life (==2 Timothy 3:5==) and lack the power of a transformed life (==1 Corinthians 4:20==). We can dance around another person’s fire while carrying none of our own.

Authentic authority cannot be borrowed. Paul carried the name of Jesus because his life had been conquered by Jesus. His words came from union, obedience, suffering, and continual fellowship with Christ. The sons of Sceva attempted to use publicly what had never become inwardly real.

Everything will eventually be tested. Spiritual language may impress people for a time, but it cannot withstand the day of confrontation if there is no life of God behind it (==Matthew 7:22–23==).

We can’t just learn what to say. We must come to Jesus, abide in His presence, and allow the cross to become the governing reality of our lives.
