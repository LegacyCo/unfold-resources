## Commentary
Standing in the middle of Athens—a city crowded with magnificent temples—Paul makes the announcement that puts every religious building ever constructed in its place: “God, who made the world and everything in it, since He is Lord of heaven and earth, *does not dwell in temples made with hands*.” Stephen preached the same truth in Jerusalem, about the temple itself, and it cost him his life (==Acts 7:48-50==). The Most High doesn’t live in buildings. He never has.

So do we need a building to gather in? For the most part, yes—families need a place to meet. And it can be as large and beautiful as an elaborate sanctuary or as small and simple as a living room; whatever helps the family gather, the building should serve that purpose. But hold the order straight: the building serves the church. The trouble starts when the church starts serving the building—when the mortgage drives the ministry, when the facility becomes the identity, when “church” in people’s minds means an address instead of a family. A congregation defined by its building has more in common with old-covenant, temple-oriented Israel than with the new-covenant church—because the King is not seated in the house made with human hands.

Where does He dwell, then? Paul answers a few sentences later: He is “not far from each one of us,” and He “gives to all life, breath, and all things” (==Acts 17:25-27==). Under the new covenant, His address is His people: “you are the temple of God, and… the Spirit of God dwells in you” (==1 Corinthians 3:16==)—living stones built together into a spiritual house (==1 Peter 2:5==). That’s what makes a gathering church: not the room, but the indwelling Spirit manifesting Jesus in the midst of His family. The early church knew this instinctively—they exploded in growth while owning no buildings at all, gathering at the temple courts and from house to house (==Acts 2:46==). Keep the building. Be grateful for it. Just never forget which one is the house of God—the structure you walk into, or the people walking in.
