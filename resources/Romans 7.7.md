## Commentary
“I would not have known sin except through the law.” The law is very good at its job. It reaches into the places where we have renamed sin as something more respectable and shines a light on it. What we call ambition, the law rightly calls greed. Fear gets renamed discernment, pride becomes confidence, selfishness becomes self-care. The law refuses the soft language.

It also removes our ability to hide behind comparison. Most of us feel righteous as long as we are allowed to choose whom we are compared to: “I’ve made mistakes, but I’ve never done what they did.” The law does not compare us to our neighbor. It reveals the righteousness of God—and when God is the standard, everyone falls short (==Romans 3:23==).

This exposure is not cruelty; it is the greatest gift the law can give. Its purpose is to show us how fundamentally different God is from us and to drive us to a Savior (==Galatians 3:24==). But here we reach a crossroads. Once the law has done its job and exposed what is wrong in us, the temptation is to keep holding its hand and hope it will eventually fix us: “I’ll do better. I’ll try harder. I’ll build enough rules to keep myself from breaking God’s rules.” That is not the law’s job. The law is the alarm telling you the building is on fire—but the alarm cannot carry you out of the building.

Trying to become righteous through the law is like trying to get out of prison by memorizing the legal code. The code explains the standard and proves the sentence is just, but it has no power to rehabilitate the prisoner or open the cell. What the prisoner needs is mercy—someone with the authority to unlock the door.

## Application
Let the law finish its work on your vocabulary. Ask God to show you where you have renamed sin into something respectable—the “ambition” that is really greed, the “discernment” that is really fear, the “venting” that is really slander. Call each one what the law calls it. Sin that keeps its alias never gets confessed.

Then refuse the crossroads temptation. When conviction comes, do not respond with a self-improvement plan—more resolve, more rules, more effort. That is memorizing the legal code inside the cell. Let conviction do what it was designed to do: drive you to the One with authority to open the door. The right response to the alarm is not to study the alarm. It is to run to the Rescuer.
