## Commentary
“And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.”

Paul gives two commands here, and in Greek both are passive. “Do not be conformed” means don't let yourself be shaped. “Be transformed” means let yourself be changed. Paul isn't describing a person who shapes himself. He's describing a person who is always being shaped by something, and he gives us the only two possibilities that exist.

The first is “this world.” The word is literally “this age,” meaning the present order of things with its values, its fears, and its assumptions about what's of value. Paul calls it “this present evil age” (==Galatians 1:4==), and John says it “is passing away” (==1 John 2:17==). This age shapes people without asking their permission. It does it through what we watch, what we're afraid of, and what everyone around us treats as normal. J. B. Phillips paraphrased the line this way: “Don't let the world around you squeeze you into its own mould.” Nobody has to try to be conformed. It happens automatically to anyone moving through life with no anchor in the age to come.

The second possibility for being shaped is transformation, and Paul describes it as, “ the renewing of your mind.” God changes a person from the inside, beginning with how that person thinks. Verse 1 asked for the body to be presented to God. Verse 2 deals with the mind, because what we do with our bodies follows what we believe in our minds.

Paul describes the same process in another letter: “we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image” (==2 Corinthians 3:18==). We're changed as we look at Jesus. And God has already decided what the finished result will be. He “predestined (us) to be conformed to the image of His Son” (==Romans 8:29==). So a believer is going to be conformed to something. Paul's command is to refuse the world's shape and yield to Christ's.

The verse ends with what a renewed mind is able to do: “prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.” To “prove” is to test something and recognize it as genuine. Many of us treat God's will as hidden information we need to get hold of before we can obey. Paul treats it as something a transformed person learns to recognize. Hebrews describes mature believers the same way, as those who “have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil” (==Hebrews 5:14==).

## Word Study
**Conformed** is *syschēmatizō* (συσχηματίζω)—to be shaped to a pattern. It's built on *schēma*, the outward form or fashion of a thing. Peter uses it too: “not conforming yourselves to the former lusts” (==1 Peter 1:14==). **Transformed** is *metamorphoō* (μεταμορφόω), where we get *metamorphosis*. It appears only four times in the New Testament: twice of Jesus when He “was transfigured” on the mountain (==Matthew 17:2==; ==Mark 9:2==), once in ==2 Corinthians 3:18==, and here. Summary: conformity is pressure from the outside that changes how a person looks and acts. Transformation is a change on the inside that eventually shows on the outside, the way Christ's glory shone out on the mountain.

## Application
Be honest about what is shaping how you think. Add up the hours in a normal week that go to news, social media, and entertainment, and then the hours that go to Scripture and prayer. A mind is renewed by what feeds it. Choose one input to cut back this week, and give that time to God.
