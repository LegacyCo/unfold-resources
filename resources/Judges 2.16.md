## Commentary
“Nevertheless, the LORD raised up judges who delivered them out of the hand of those who plundered them.”

When we hear the word *judge*, we hear something to be feared—a courtroom, a gavel, a fifty-fifty chance of being found guilty if the judge is in a bad mood. But that courtroom picture is a modern, western framework read back into Scripture; taking our biases and filtering the text through them is what scholars call eisegesis, and it reinforces the bias rather than revealing the truth. If we want to know what the Bible means when it calls God a judge, the Bible should interpret it for us—and here we have a whole book named Judges. 

The recurring story of Judges is that God's people turn from Him and serve other gods, His hand of protection lifts, they are oppressed by enemies—and then God raises up a judge who *delivers* them (==Judges 2:11-16==). The situation was of Israel's own making; they joined themselves to foreign gods and brought the judgment on themselves. Yet God, in mercy, raises up deliverers. That is what a judge does in Scripture. That is who God is, our deliverer from every enemy oppressor.

This is why mature saints do not run from God's judgments; they long for them. “My soul breaks with longing for Your judgments at all times” (==Psalm 119:20==). “When Your judgments are in the earth, the inhabitants of the world will learn righteousness” (==Isaiah 26:9==). All of God's judgments are aimed at whatever interferes with love—which means His judgment is aimed at what oppresses His people, not at His people's hope. Jesus comes as the final Deliverer, judging everything that would keep God's people from knowing Him, dividing us from whatever is dividing us from Him (==John 12:31==). If the Father were not good, it would be wise to fear His judgments. Because He is good, the Davidic heart runs toward them.

## Application
Pray the prayer that only makes sense once you know what a judge is: *Father, come and cut away anything in my life that causes me to see You the way Adam saw You*. Ask for His judgment against the accusation, the fear, the false picture of God you have carried—those are the plunderers His judgment targets. It only hurts when you cling to what He has come to remove.
