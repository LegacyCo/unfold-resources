## Commentary
“And Abraham said, ‘My son, God will provide for Himself the lamb for a burnt offering.’ So the two of them went together.”

This statement gets to the guts of Abraham’s test. Remember what Abraham didn’t have: Scriptures. There was no Bible study he could attend to learn about this God, no written revelation to consult. And remember what surrounded him: a world crowded with gods, each with its own quirks and demands. Molech, the god who devoured babies. Baal, the bloodthirsty god of Canaan. Child sacrifice wasn’t unthinkable in Abraham’s world—it was religion as usual. So when this God asks Abraham for his son, the question underneath the question was enormous: *what kind of god is this God?* Is He turning out to be just like the others, demanding the same horrible things? Or is He something the world had never seen?

Isaac asks it innocently on the way up: “We have the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb?” And Abraham’s answer is one of the great confessions of faith in all of Scripture: *God will provide for Himself the lamb.* With no Bible, no precedent, and a knife in his hand, Abraham bet everything on the character of God—this God is not like the other gods. The other gods demand; this God provides. And he was right. The angel stopped his hand, and a ram stood caught in the thicket (==Genesis 22:13==).

But notice—it was a *ram* that day, not a lamb. Abraham's answer to Isaac hung in the air, waiting, for two thousand years. Then a prophet standing in the Jordan looked up, saw a Man walking toward him, and finished the sentence: “Behold! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” (==John 1:29==). God did provide for Himself the lamb—and the Lamb was His own Son, offered on the same mountain range where Abraham said it. The test of Genesis 22 was never really about what Abraham would give God. It was about revealing, once and for all, what kind of God this God is: the One who provides the sacrifice Himself.
