## Commentary
"Love does no harm to a neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of the law."

The word translated "harm" is *kakon*, the ordinary Greek word for evil. Translation: love works no evil to its neighbor. In English it's easy to miss, but that same word runs all through this part of Romans.

"Repay no one evil for evil" (==Romans 12:17==).
"Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good" (==Romans 12:21==).
"Rulers are not a terror to good works, but to evil" (==Romans 13:3==).
The ruler is "an avenger to execute wrath on him who practices evil" (==Romans 13:4==).
And here: love works no evil to a neighbor (==Romans 13:10==).

For two chapters Paul has been showing the church how evil gets dealt with. Believers don't repay it. They overcome it with good. The government restrains it with a sword. Now Paul names the one thing that keeps evil from starting in the first place.

The sword works from the outside. Fear of punishment keeps a man from stealing what he'd otherwise take. But love works from the inside. A man who loves his neighbor doesn't need a sword hanging over him to keep his hands off his neighbor's things, because his desire is for his neighbor's good. 

Where does a love like that come from? Paul answers that earlier in the letter. Christ came "that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit" (==Romans 8:4==). Notice that it's fulfilled *in us*. The love that fulfills the law is the life of Jesus flowing through people who are in Him.
