## Commentary
The word Paul uses here for "kindness" is the same one he uses in Romans 2:4, "It's the kindness of God that leads a man to repentance." The Greek word is *chrestos*.

Paul is saying here in chapter 11 that if we make the same mistake that Israel did, we, too, will be cut off and experience the severity of God. That sounds harsh, until you see what exactly He is demanding we remain in; the kindness and goodness of God that led to repentance in the first place. That kindness is given to us by grace alone (Romans 11:5-6), it is not by works. Israel did not continue in that grace, they tried to attain righteousness by works — which led to their fall. The same will happen to Gentiles. Continue then, in the grace of His kindness. Do not move from grace to works. Remain in the stream of His goodness.

Paul saying this is equivalent to a flight attendant telling the passengers to remain on the plane after it takes off. If you open the door and jump out, you will no longer be part of this flight. It's absolutely suicidal and beyond reason that someone would do that. So, too, is the idea from heaven's perspective that someone would jump ship from God's kindness and try to attain righteousness by works of the law. Can it happen? Scripture seems to think so. But if it does, that's an indictment against the individual, not God. Do we fault the airline if someone jumps out of the plane? Do we say that they are cruel for having emergency exit doors? Of course not. And if someone wants to abandon God's goodness and grace and try to fly on their own, that's a statement about their nature, not God's.
