## Commentary
Achan did not merely hide the treasures of Babylon in his tent; he wanted to wear them. That is what makes this story so revealing. His sin was not only greed. It was identification. He saw a beautiful robe from Babylon and desired to clothe himself in what Babylon represented. In Scripture, Babylon is more than a city. It becomes a symbol of the world system organized in defiance of God—human pride, self-exaltation, false worship, and independence from the Lord (Genesis 11:1–9; Revelation 17–18). Achan wasn't content to possess something worldly; he wanted to be wrapped in it.

That same temptation still confronts the people of God. It is one thing for us to live in the world. It is another thing for the spirit of the world to live in us. The Lord has called His people to be distinct, consecrated, and holy. We cannot carry Babylon in our hearts and expect to walk in the power of Zion. We cannot put on the values, loves, and ambitions of the world and still speak with clarity to a generation trapped within it. If the church clothes itself in Babylon, it loses its witness. We cannot influence the world around us when the world has already discipled our desires from within.
