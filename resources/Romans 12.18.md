## Commentary
“If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men.”

The command in this verse is “live peaceably with all men.” That covers everyone: other believers, family, neighbors, coworkers, and the enemies Paul has just told us to bless (==Romans 12:14==). But Paul puts two phrases in front of the command, because peace takes two people and a Christian only controls one of them.

The first phrase is “if it is possible.” Sometimes it isn't. The other person may refuse to be reconciled. Or the only way to keep the peace would be to give up something God asks of you. James says the wisdom from above is “first pure, then peaceable” (==James 3:17==), in that order. Paul himself opposed Peter to his face when the truth of the gospel was at stake (==Galatians 2:11==), and Jesus said that following Him would divide some households (==Matthew 10:34-36==). Peace bought by giving up truth or righteousness isn't the peace Scripture is commanding.

The second phrase is “as much as depends on you.” This one takes away our excuses. Whatever part of the conflict is mine, I'm responsible for all of it, and I'm to go first. If I've wronged someone, I leave my gift at the altar and go make it right (==Matthew 5:23-24==). If someone has wronged me, I go to him privately (==Matthew 18:15==), and I don't repay evil for evil (==Romans 12:17==). I apologize without adding “but.” I forgive what there is to forgive. When all of that is done and the other person still won't have peace, I have obeyed this verse.

So the verse frees a believer from two things. One is false guilt over a relationship the other person refuses to mend. The other is the assumption that a conflict is entirely someone else's fault. It leaves one question to answer: is there anything on my side that I haven't done?

## Application
Bring one strained relationship to mind and make an honest list of what depends on you. Is there an apology you owe? A debt? A conversation you've been avoiding? Something you've said about them to other people? Do those things this week without waiting for the other person to go first. Then leave their response with God. “When a man's ways please the LORD, He makes even his enemies to be at peace with him” (==Proverbs 16:7==).
