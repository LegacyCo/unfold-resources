## Commentary
“I will make you a great nation; I will bless you and make your name great; and you shall be a blessing.”

Certain events shift the trajectory of history—the printing press, the assembly line, the internet; certain leaders, thinkers, and movements after which nothing is the same. Genesis 12 is one of those moments in the biblical story. It is not just another account of a man; it is the announcement that from this point forward God is going to have a people and a nation for Himself, through whom He will demonstrate who He is to every other nation. Martyn Lloyd-Jones said, “Understanding the story of Abraham is absolutely pivotal to understanding the message of the Bible.”

Everything before this chapter has been decline. Man sinned and separated himself from God; man placed himself under the influence of the devil; man built Babel. Now, through one man and his family, God begins creating a separate people who will be His own possession and will show the world who He is—“a kingdom of priests and a holy nation” (==Exodus 19:5-6==), “His own special people” who proclaim His praises (==1 Peter 2:9==). The promise has three parts: a great nation, a great name, and a blessing that reaches outward. Abram is not blessed for his own sake. God is calling a man, but the story is not about one man; it is the beginning of God working through that man to bless all men.
