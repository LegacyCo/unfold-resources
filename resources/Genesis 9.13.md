## Commentary
“I set My rainbow in the cloud, and it shall be for the sign of the covenant between Me and the earth.”

There are always signs that announce a covenant and the people who belong to it. A wedding ring that has never come off a finger is an announcement that its wearer is in covenant, and a daily reminder of it. God gives Noah a sign, and the sign is not arbitrary. The sign of a covenant speaks to the nature of that covenant. For Noah, the sign was placed in the storm clouds, because the promise was that God would never again destroy the earth by flood. For Abraham, the sign was circumcision (==Genesis 17:11==), because the promise concerned offspring. For Israel, the sign was the Sabbath (==Exodus 31:16-17==), because the promise was the rest God was bringing them into. And for the followers of Jesus, the sign of the new covenant is the blood of the Lamb that washes and makes new: “this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many for the remission of sins” (==Matthew 26:28==).

The covenant family honors the covenant by honoring its sign. As long as Israel kept the Sabbath, they announced they were in covenant with the LORD. As long as Abraham's descendants were circumcised on the eighth day, they announced the same. And as long as the followers of Jesus gather around His broken body and shed blood, they announce that they are in covenant with Christ—“as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord's death till He comes” (==1 Corinthians 11:26==). This is why the church takes communion whenever it gathers. Every time Noah's children saw the rainbow, it was an occasion to stop and remember what God had done. Every time the church gathers at the Lord's table, it has the same occasion—to remember and to say to its children, “We do this because we are sons and daughters of the new covenant.”
