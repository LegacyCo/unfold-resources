## Commentary
The consecration of Aaron and his sons reveals how differently God evaluates things. What seems valuable to us is not always valuable to Him, and what we would naturally disregard may be the very thing He chooses. Consecration requires us to surrender our own judgment and allow God alone to determine what is holy, what must be offered, and what must be put away.

Art Katz writes in _Apostolic Foundations_:

“What was burned in the consecration sacrifice and what was considered holy unto the Lord is the opposite of what man would have done. This shows how much heaven is antithetical to earthly reckoning and consideration. We would have saved the things that God commands to be destroyed—the hide and the flesh—and those things that we would have cast aside as disgusting and worthless are the very things that God calls the sacrifice—the inner parts.

The flesh and the hide, which we would have deemed as having great value, were burnt outside the camp; such is God’s disgust for flesh.”

Man places great value on what can be seen, admired, and presented before others. God looks past the exterior and lays claim to the inner parts. He is concerned with the hidden life—with motives, desires, affections, and thoughts that no one else can see.

This is what makes consecration so costly. We cannot decide for ourselves what God should receive and what we are permitted to keep. The very thing we would preserve may be the thing He wants carried outside the camp, while the thing we consider insignificant may be what He requires on the altar.

True consecration is not the offering of our best according to human estimation. It is the surrender of the whole life to the judgment of God. He alone determines what is holy unto Him, what must be consumed upon the altar, and what must be removed entirely.

## Application
Consecration means giving God the right to decide what remains and what must be burned. The question is not, “What am I willing to give?” but, “What has God called holy, and what has He commanded me to put away?”
