## Commentary
Paul makes a startling comparison here. He is rebuking the Corinthians for treating sexual immorality as though it were merely physical. As though what a person does with the body has no consequence in the spirit. Your body belongs to Christ.  

So when a believer joins himself to a prostitute, Paul says he is taking what belongs to Christ and joining it to another. An unholy act of consummation has taken place. That's why the language is so severe: “Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them members of a harlot? Certainly not!”

Then Paul reaches all the way back to Genesis: “The two shall become one flesh.”

In other words, sexual immorality is not just an act. It is a kind of joining. A false union. A counterfeit consummation. Two strangers become one flesh through an act that was meant to belong to covenant.

Then Paul turns the whole argument upward: “But he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him.”

There is a joining that corrupts, and there is a joining that gives life.  
There is a union that defiles, and there is a union that makes us holy.  
There is a counterfeit intimacy that attaches us to death, and there is a covenant intimacy that brings us into the life of Christ.

Which raises the question: what is the act of consummation that expresses our union with the Lord? Is it too much to see communion in this light?

I don’t think so.

Jesus said, “He who eats My flesh and drinks My blood abides in Me, and I in him” (John 6:56). That's the language of union.

In communion, one receives into themselves the life of Another. The body and blood of Christ are given, taken, eaten, received. And through that holy receiving, Jesus says, “You abide in Me, and I in you.”

This is why communion must never be reduced to a ceremonial memory aid. It is remembrance, yes. But biblical remembrance is never mere recollection. It is participation. It is covenant renewal. It is the Bride receiving again the life of the Bridegroom.

## Application
Could it be that heaven sees the table with far more reverence than we do? Could it be that what we often treat casually is, in the eyes of God, an intimate act between Bridegroom and bride? The covenant meal is where the Church receives the flesh and blood of her Lord and is reminded again: we are not merely forgiven by Him; we are joined to Him.
