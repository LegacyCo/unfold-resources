## Commentary
“So Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering and laid it on Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand, and a knife, and the two of them went together.”

Isaac carried the wood he was to be sacrificed on. Let that picture settle, because two thousand years later it happens again on the same ridge of hills: “And He, bearing His cross, went out to a place called the Place of a Skull” (==John 19:17==). Another beloved Son, another load of wood laid on His back, another walk up Moriah’s slopes. Genesis 22 isn’t just a story about Abraham’s faith—it’s a prophecy acted out in flesh, a father and son rehearsing the gospel two millennia early.

And then, “the two of them went *together*” (==Genesis 22:6==; ==Genesis 22:8==). Isaac was no small child—he was strong enough to haul the wood, which means he was strong enough to resist an elderly father. But he didn’t. He went together with his father, all the way to the altar. That, too, is the gospel: Calvary was not a reluctant victim dragged by an angry deity. The Father and the Son went up together—one will, one heart, one purpose (==John 10:17-18==).

There is one place the shadow and the substance part ways. On Moriah, a voice from heaven stopped the sacrifice: “you have not withheld your son, your only son, from Me” (==Genesis 22:12==). On Golgotha, no voice interrupted. Paul deliberately echoes the language of Genesis 22 when he writes, “He who did not *spare* His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?” (==Romans 8:32==). What God stopped Abraham from doing, God Himself did—for you.
