## Commentary
“Let your eyes look straight ahead, and your eyelids look right before you.”

Solomon puts the eyes at the center of a life that stays on course, and the surrounding verses fill out the picture: keep the heart with all diligence (==Proverbs 4:23==), ponder the path of your feet (==Proverbs 4:26==), do not turn to the right or the left (==Proverbs 4:27==). The eyes lead; everything else follows. Anyone who has learned to drive knows this. More than the speedometer, more than the engine, it is the eyes that determine whether you arrive at your destination or end up in a ditch. Riders learn to look through the turn; surfers learn the same law: wherever you look is where you go. God has built this into the natural world to point at a spiritual reality—whatever you look at, you move toward. And whatever you look at, you empower.

This is why disciplined eyes are a matter of leadership and not just personal holiness. Every man is leading somebody—a wife, children, a younger man at work who is watching how he lives—and the people you lead end up wherever you end up. A man's eyes are never just his own business; someone is in the car with him, and they are not watching the road, they are watching him. The New Testament gives the same command a focal point: “looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith” (==Hebrews 12:2==), and “we do not look at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen” (==2 Corinthians 4:18==). When the eyes are fixed on Jesus, everything else comes into order; when the eyes start falling, things jump the rails. The direction of a life—and of everyone following it—is set by the direction of its gaze.
