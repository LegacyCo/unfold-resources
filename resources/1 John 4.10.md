## Commentary
"In this is love, not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son to be the propitiation for our sins."

John defines love, and the definition is a *direction*. Love is not first something that moves from us toward God. It's something that moved from God toward us—while we were still facing the other way (==Romans 5:8==).

He is the initiator. He initiated creation. He initiated the covenant with Abraham while Abraham was asleep (==Genesis 15:12-18==). He initiated the exodus before Israel had done a single thing to deserve it. He initiated the incarnation, the cross, and even our repentance—for it is His kindness that leads us there (==Romans 2:4==). We did not find Him. He came looking (==Luke 19:10==). Even our love for Him is a response: "We love Him because He first loved us" (==1 John 4:19==).

This corrects a wrong way of thinking that many believers have: that the relationship began with our decision and is sustained by our devotion. It didn't, and it isn't. It began with His decision, and it is sustained by His faithfulness. Your love for God is real and it matters—but it's the echo, not the voice.

Notice what John says His love *did*: it sent the Son to be the propitiation for our sins. Love was not sentimental. It paid. And it paid before we asked.

## Application
The next time you pray, notice how you begin. If your instinct is to open with what you've done, failed to do, or promise to do, you've started the story with yourself. Start where John starts. Before a single request, say back to God the truth of this verse: "You loved me first. You sent Your Son. You moved before I did." Let everything else you say flow from that. Prayer built on His initiative sounds different from prayer built on our performance.
