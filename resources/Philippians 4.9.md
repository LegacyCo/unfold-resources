## Commentary
“The things which you learned and received and heard and *saw in me*, these do, and the God of peace will be with you.”

Read Paul’s list carefully—learned, received, heard, and *seen*. Paul told the Philippians to look at the testimony of his *life*. He could tell the Ephesian elders, “You know, from the first day I came to Asia, in what manner I always lived among you” (==Acts 20:18==), and remind the Thessalonians, “you know how blamelessly we behaved among you” (==1 Thessalonians 2:10==). He even dared to say, “Imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ” (==1 Corinthians 11:1==). His discipleship method wasn’t just the transfer of information. It was the witness of a transformed life, lived close enough to be watched.

The modern church often does discipleship the other way: we emphasize the transfer of knowledge rather than the witness of a life. We produce believers who have completed the class, filled in the workbook, and downloaded the content—but who have never *watched* anyone forgive an enemy, handle money with open hands, stay gentle in conflict, or suffer with hope. And so the knowledge stays theoretical, because some things in the kingdom aren’t taught. They’re caught.

This cuts two directions. First: who are you watching? Get close enough to someone further along that you can see their life, not just hear their content—Sunday sermons can’t be imitated; lives can. Second, and more sobering: who is watching you? Somebody is learning what following Jesus looks like from what they see in you—your kids, a younger believer, a coworker. Paul’s promise attaches to the whole chain: put into practice what you’ve seen in a life worth imitating, “and the God of peace will be with you.”

## Application
Answer these two questions this week. First, name your Paul: one person further along whose life you can actually observe—then get proximity, not just content. Share a meal. Ask how they handle money, conflict, disappointment. If no one comes to mind, that’s your prayer assignment. Second, name your Timothy: one person who watches you—a child, a newer believer, a coworker—and let them in closer. Let them see you apologize, give, forgive, and pray, not just hear you talk about it. Discipleship happens at the speed of proximity.
