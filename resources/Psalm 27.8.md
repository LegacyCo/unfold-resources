## Commentary
“When You said, ‘Seek My face,’ my heart said, ‘Your face, LORD, I will seek.’” Notice the structure of the verse: it begins with God’s invitation and ends with David’s response. That’s who our God is—a God who *invites* the seeking. He isn’t hiding from you; He’s calling you.

But look closely at what David does with the invitation, because it’s more than agreement. Four verses earlier he said, “One thing I have *desired* of the LORD, that will I *seek*” (==Psalm 27:4==). Desired—and sought. There’s a big difference between the two, and I’d argue it’s the single most important quality history makers possess: they don’t just desire; they put feet to their desire.

Desire that never moves to seeking is just curiosity—an idea that lives in the heart but never becomes a reality. I can *desire* thirty thousand dollars to drill a well in Kenya, or I can actively *seek* thirty thousand dollars to drill a well in Kenya. One of those wells gets drilled. True desire seeks. Which is exactly how Scripture puts it—not “desire and you shall find,” but “*seek* and you shall find” (==Matthew 7:7==).

So the honest question isn’t “Do I want more of God?” Almost everyone answers yes to that. The question is: what is my desire *doing*? Does it have a time of day, a quiet room, an open Bible, a canceled commitment? God has already spoken His half of the verse—“Seek My face.” The only thing left undecided is your heart’s reply.
