## Commentary
Before the Kohathites could carry anything, Aaron and his sons had to cover the holy objects first. God's presence is a gift, but it is never common. His holiness is not something people are free to handle however they want. The work of God must be done in the way God commands.

This theme shows up later when David brings the ark to Jerusalem. In 2 Samuel 6, the ark is placed on a cart, and when the oxen stumble, Uzzah reaches out and touches it. Scripture says the anger of the Lord burned against Uzzah, and he died there beside the ark (2 Samuel 6:1–7). The issue was not willingness to serve. The issue was obedience to the ways in which God is served.

David realizes this later. When the ark is brought up again, he tells the Levites that the Lord broke out against them because they “did not consult Him about the proper order” (1 Chronicles 15:13). So this time, the Levites carry the ark on their shoulders, as Moses had commanded (1 Chronicles 15:14–15).

God’s work must be done God’s way.

## Application
This is a needed word in a generation that often treats spiritual things casually. Passion is not a substitute for obedience. Passion matters, but passion does not give us permission to handle the things of God carelessly.

Ministry is not ours to reinvent however we want. Worship is not ours to reshape around personal preference. The Word of God is not ours to bend to fit the point being made.

The Lord invites His people near, but nearness must produce reverence. As Hebrews says, we are to “serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear,” because “our God is a consuming fire” (Hebrews 12:28–29).
