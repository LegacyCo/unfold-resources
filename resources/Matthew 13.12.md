## Commentary
“For whoever has, to him more will be given, and he will have abundance; but whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.”

This sounds unfair at first. Why would the person who already has get more, and the person with little lose what he has? But Jesus is still answering the disciples' question about parables, and the verses around it show us what He means.

In verse 11, the disciples *have* something. The mysteries of the kingdom have been given to them. In verse 13, the crowd hears the same parables, but “seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand.” Both groups heard Jesus. What they did with it was different.

The disciples didn't understand everything right away. But when they didn't understand, they came to Jesus and asked for clarity (==Matthew 13:36==). Jesus explained, and they understood more. By the end of the chapter He asks them, “Have you understood all these things?” and they say, “Yes, Lord” (==Matthew 13:51==). That's how verse 12 plays out. They had a little, and more was given.

Jesus also shows us what “taken away” looks like. Some of the sower's seed falls on the path, and He explains: “When anyone hears the word of the kingdom, and does not understand it, then the wicked one comes and snatches away what was sown in his heart” (==Matthew 13:19==). The word really was sown. It just didn't remain. Luke records Jesus saying that even what that person “seems to have will be taken from him” (==Luke 8:18==).

Jesus says the same thing again in the parable of the talents (==Matthew 25:29==). The servant who lost what he had was the one who hid it in the ground.

So “having” has less to do with how much you know and more to do with what you do with what God gives you. When you receive it, hold on to it, and come back to Him with your questions. Jesus says that person “will have abundance.”
