## Commentary
Listen closely to the language of this verse: “I have spoken these things to you so that *in Me* you may have peace. *In this world* you will have tribulation… but take courage, I have overcome the world.” Two locations. Peace lives in one; pressure lives in the other. And Jesus’ point is that you can be walking through the second while seated in the first.

That’s exactly how Paul describes the believer: God “raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus” (==Ephesians 2:6==), so that our citizenship *is*—present tense—in heaven (==Philippians 3:20==). To the degree that you live as if you’re seated in this world, the gravity of this age will press down on your soul. To the degree that you live as though you’re seated in heavenly places in Christ, the spirit of this age has nothing in you.

I learned this in an interrogation room. When I was in Iraq, I was arrested—questioned for two hours at a local station, then driven to the regional station and questioned for three more. I was nervous, but underneath the nerves I was confident everything would be okay. Why? Because I’m not an Iraqi citizen. I knew there was only so much they could do to me, so I sat in that room knowing I wasn’t really seated in Iraq—I was seated in America. When they finally released me, the man questioning me admitted it: “If you were an Iraqi citizen, we would’ve put you in prison for at least three months. But because you’re an American, there’s nothing we can do.”

Whatever interrogation room the enemy tries to seat you in, you can sit with that same confidence. He can question you, pressure you, threaten you—but your citizenship limits his jurisdiction. You belong to a King who has overcome this world, which means you don’t have to agree to this world’s terms or the accuser’s charges.

## Application
The mission of the believer is not to hold on until Jesus comes back or death sets us free. It’s to bring the culture of where we’re presently seated into the situation we’re presently walking in. So next time you feel defeated, ask the location question: which seat am I living from right now? Then answer the pressure the way a citizen answers a foreign officer—calmly, from jurisdiction: “I’m in Him. He has overcome this world.”
