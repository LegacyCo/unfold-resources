## Commentary
The Second Temple was impressive on the outside, but something was missing on the inside. The innermost and holiest place—the place where God had once made His presence known—was empty. The people still came to worship. The priests still served. The sacrifices continued. It was still called the house of God, but God was not at home.

That is what gives the promise of Malachi 3:1 such weight: “The Lord, whom you seek, will suddenly come to His temple.” Israel was waiting for the presence of God to come back.

When Solomon dedicated the first temple, the glory of the Lord filled the house so powerfully that the priests could not continue ministering (==1 Kings 8:10-11==; ==2 Chronicles 7:1-2==). But before the exile, Ezekiel saw that glory depart from the temple (==Ezekiel 10:18-19==; ==Ezekiel 11:22-23==). Although the temple was later rebuilt, Scripture never records the Shekinah returning to the Second Temple in the same way.

The structure came back, but the Presence did not. Haggai promised that the glory of the latter temple would be greater than the former (==Haggai 2:6-9==), yet that greater glory would not come through gold, architecture, or religious activity. It would come through a Person.

The Lord returned to His temple, not as a cloud filling the sanctuary, but as a man walking through its courts. Jesus was brought into the temple as a child, and Simeon recognized Him as the salvation and glory of Israel (==Luke 2:27-32==).

The Presence of God had returned in a form they did not recognize. The One the temple had always pointed toward was now standing inside it. Jesus could therefore speak of His own body as the true temple, the place where God and humanity meet (==John 2:19-21==). He was the Word made flesh who “dwelt” among us, revealing the glory of God in human form (==John 1:14==).

Malachi’s promise reminds us that the goal of the Temple was never just a sacred building. The goal was God dwelling among His people. The emptiness of the Second Temple was crying out for Jesus—the true temple, Emmanuel, God with us (==Matthew 1:23==), and the ultimate fulfillment of God’s desire to dwell among humanity (==Revelation 21:3==).
