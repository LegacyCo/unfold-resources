## Commentary
“Then Moses brought Aaron and his sons and washed them with water.”

Watch the sequence of Aaron’s consecration, because every step matters. God tells Moses to gather *all the congregation* at the door of the tabernacle—this is public, before the eyes of God’s people. Then Aaron is stripped of his common clothing, washed with water, and clothed with the tunic, the robe, the sash, and the ephod (==Leviticus 8:3-7==). Then comes the anointing oil (==Leviticus 8:12==). Washed, clothed, anointed—before Aaron could stand before God and minister to Him as priest, he first had to stand before God’s people and pass through the water. Consecration means to be set apart for God, and consecration always involves a bath and a change of clothes.

Now open the New Testament, and what’s the first scene? A wild man in the desert, and *everyone* streaming out to be baptized (==Matthew 3:5-6==). Under the old covenant, only the sons of Aaron could pass through the washing into priesthood. But through the prophetic ministry of John the Baptist, God began announcing something that Isaiah had seen from a distance—a day when He would take priests from among all the nations (==Isaiah 66:21==). The washing was no longer for one family from one tribe. The waters were open.

This is one of the things I believe happens in baptism: it is a consecration into the priesthood. A ceremonial washing that sets you apart to stand before the Lord and minister to Him. It’s why Peter can look at a congregation of ordinary believers—Gentiles included—and use words no first-century Jew would ever use casually: “you are a chosen generation, a *royal priesthood*, a holy nation, His own special people” (==1 Peter 2:9==). Peter isn’t throwing precious words around. The pattern of Aaron has been fulfilled and thrown open: come to the waters and be washed. Be clothed in garments of righteousness (==Isaiah 61:10==). Be anointed with the oil of the Holy Spirit (==2 Corinthians 1:21-22==). Aaron’s bath made one man a priest for one nation. Yours consecrates you into a priesthood drawn from every nation, tribe, and tongue—a kind of priest the world had never seen before.
