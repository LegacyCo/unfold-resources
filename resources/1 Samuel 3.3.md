## Commentary
“…and before the lamp of God went out in the tabernacle of the LORD where the ark of God was, and while Samuel was lying down.”

The narrator sets the scene by telling us where each man slept. Eli “was lying down in his place” (==1 Samuel 3:2==). Samuel was lying down in the tabernacle of the LORD, where the ark of God was. The wording is deliberate. Eli, the high priest—the man whose office existed for nearness to God—has a *place* of his own, and that is where he settles for the night. Samuel, a boy with no office at all, makes his bed inside the house, near the ark, the seat of God's presence in Israel (==Exodus 25:22==). One man's location is defined by his own spot; the other's is defined by the presence of the LORD.

When the voice comes in the night, it comes to the one lying near the ark—and it takes the boy three trips to Eli's room before the old priest recognizes what is happening, because Samuel “did not yet know the LORD” but Eli, who did, had stopped expecting God to speak (==1 Samuel 3:4-8==). The detail about Eli's eyes grown dim (==1 Samuel 3:2==) is contrasted with the detail about the lamp of God burning low but not yet out (==1 Samuel 3:3==): the man in his place is losing his sight, while the light still burns beside the boy. Position is not everything in this story, but it is not nothing. Samuel was in earshot of the voice because he had made his bed where God dwelt, and the word of the LORD that was rare in those days found the person who stayed closest to the presence. The psalmist echoes this language: “I would rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God than dwell in the tents of wickedness” (==Psalm 84:10==)—and in Shiloh it was the doorkeeper's bed, not the high priest's place, where the silence of a generation was broken.
