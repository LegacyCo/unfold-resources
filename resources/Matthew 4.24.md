## Commentary
Once the Kingdom is established in a people, the Kingdom can move _through_ that people. That’s where Matthew takes us next in this passage. Jesus brings the kingdom within reach, proclaims that kingdom, and then _demonstrates_ it — healing the sick and casting out demons.

The Kingdom is the rule and reign of God breaking into the earth. It confronts everything that has come under the tyranny of sin, sickness, death, and the devil. When Jesus heals the sick and delivers the oppressed, He’s showing us what His Kingdom does when it comes to bear on a community — it restores everything that was lost as a result of sin.

This is why the gospel of the Kingdom cannot be reduced to, “Pray a prayer and go to heaven when you die.” Praise God for the hope of resurrection. 

But the purpose of forgiveness is not just an address change when you die. Jesus came to restore everything, everywhere.

God forgives sin _so that_ we can become what we were always created to be: fully alive, fully restored, fully image-bearing people who show the world what God is like. 

That’s why Jesus doesn’t *just* preach in Matthew 4. 

He heals. He casts out demons. He brings order to the places darkness has destroyed.
