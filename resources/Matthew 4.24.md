## Commentary
“Then His fame went throughout all Syria; and they brought to Him all sick people who were afflicted with various diseases and torments, and those who were demon-possessed, epileptics, and paralytics; and He healed them.”

Jesus does not only announce the kingdom—He demonstrates it. He proclaims the gospel of the kingdom in the synagogues (==Matthew 4:23==), and then this verse shows what the proclamation looks like when it touches actual bodies and actual torment: healing the sick and casting out demons. Proclamation and restoration travel together.

The Kingdom is the rule and reign of God breaking into the earth. It confronts everything that has come under the tyranny of sin, sickness, death, and the devil (==Acts 10:38==). When Jesus heals the sick and delivers the oppressed, He’s showing us what His Kingdom does when it comes to bear on a community—it restores everything that was lost as a result of sin. He brings order to the places darkness has destroyed.

This is why the gospel of the Kingdom cannot be reduced to, “Pray a prayer and go to heaven when you die.” Praise God for the hope of resurrection. But the purpose of forgiveness is not just an address change when you die. Jesus came to restore everything, everywhere. God forgives sin *so that* we can become what we were always created to be: fully alive, fully restored, fully image-bearing people who show the world what God is like (==Genesis 1:27==). That’s why Jesus doesn’t just preach in Matthew 4. He heals. He casts out demons. And the fame of a kingdom like that spreads throughout all Syria.

## Application
Let your understanding of the gospel be as big as this verse. If your Christianity consists of forgiveness plus a future relocation, ask God to enlarge it: He intends to restore everything sin has broken, and He starts now. Pray for the sick. Contend for the oppressed. Identify one place of disorder within your reach—a relationship, a need, a corner of your community—and carry the kingdom there the way Jesus carried it through Galilee: announcing and demonstrating that God restores broken things.
