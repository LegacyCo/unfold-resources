## Commentary
“God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” Read it carefully: what did the death of Christ demonstrate? Not primarily the seriousness of sin—His *love*.

Jesus didn’t die for sin. Jesus died for love. The cleansing of sin is what His death *accomplished*, but it isn’t what *drove* Him to the cross. Scripture keeps making this exact point. Why did God send His Son as the sin offering? “This is love… that He loved us” (==1 John 4:10==). Why did Christ give Himself as a sacrifice? Because He “has loved us” (==Ephesians 5:2==). Why did the Son of God give Himself for you? He “loved me and gave Himself for me” (==Galatians 2:20==).

The distinction matters more than it might seem. If I believe my sin is the primary reason Jesus died, and the removal of my sin is the starting point of our relationship, then I’ll forever see God through the lens of my sin. I may be grateful for salvation, but I’ll keep Him at a distance—because the only way I know how to relate to Him is through forgiveness. But if I believe Jesus died because He *loves* me, and forgiveness is His love in action, then my relationship with God stands on the foundation of who He is, not who I am. 

Here’s an illustration. When I met my wife, Lindsey, her identity was “single.” But I didn’t marry her because she was single—I married her because I love her. And that love produced a covenant, and the covenant changed her identity. In the same way, before you came to faith, your identity was “sinner.” But Jesus didn’t die because of your identity; He died because of His great love. And by entering covenant with Him, your identity changed: bride, adopted, holy, righteous, redeemed of the Lord.

One more picture: if someone kidnapped my wife, I would move heaven and earth to get her back. Why? Because she’s lost? No. Because I love her. Her being lost is the *condition*. Love is the jet fuel that moves me to do whatever is necessary to get to her. That’s the cross. Your lostness was the condition. His love was the motivator. And nothing was going to stand in the way of Him getting you back.
