## Commentary
This passage continues to give attention to the families of Levi. The Gershonites, Kohathites, and Merarites are each counted and assigned specific responsibilities connected to the tabernacle.

The Gershonites were positioned west of the tabernacle and were responsible for the curtains, coverings, and hangings (Numbers 3:21–26). The Kohathites were positioned south of the tabernacle and were responsible for the most holy objects: the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars, and the sanctuary vessels (Numbers 3:27–32). The Merarites were positioned north of the tabernacle and were responsible for the frames, bars, pillars, bases, and structural pieces of the tabernacle (Numbers 3:33–37).

No one carried everything. But everyone carried something.

This is a picture of ordered service before the Lord. God did not give every family the same assignment, but every assignment mattered. The tabernacle could not function if the curtains were ignored, the holy objects were mishandled, or the structural pieces were forgotten.

This same principle carries into the New Testament picture of the body of Christ. There are different gifts, different functions, and different assignments, but one body and one purpose (Romans 12:4–8; 1 Corinthians 12:4–27; Ephesians 4:11–16).

## Application
Comparison loses power when we understand assignment. The question is not whether your role looks like someone else’s. The question is whether you are faithful with what God has placed in your hands.
