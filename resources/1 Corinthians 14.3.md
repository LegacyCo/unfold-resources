## Commentary
“But he who prophesies speaks edification and exhortation and comfort to men.”

Look at where the prophetic word is aimed: *to men*. Not inward, for the private enrichment of the one who hears—outward, for the building up, the stirring, and the comforting of others. Eleven times across 1 Corinthians 12–14 Paul makes the point that the gifts are given for the benefit of others: “the manifestation of the Spirit is given to each one for the profit of all” (==1 Corinthians 12:7==). God speaks to us for the sake of those around us.

Genesis 18 shows this before prophecy was ever defined. On the road to Sodom, the Lord says, “Shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing?” (==Genesis 18:17==)—and then discloses His plans to His friend. Now watch what Abraham does with what he hears: “Abraham came near and said, ‘Would You also destroy the righteous with the wicked?’” (==Genesis 18:23==). He receives the word and immediately begins to intercede for a city. God told one man what He was about to do, and that man stood before God on behalf of people who had no idea what was happening. That is why God tells His friends things (==James 2:23==; ==Amos 3:7==). He shares what He is about to do for the sake of those who cannot hear Him.

This reorders how we handle revelation. The question that follows any word from God is not only “what does this mean for me?” but “who is this for?” The word you received in prayer may be aimed at your discouraged coworker. The Scripture that gripped you this morning may be comfort God is sending to someone at your table tonight. A prophetic people hears for the sake of others—and a person who hears God only for himself has misunderstood why God was speaking.

## Application
Practice the Abraham response this week. When God highlights something to you—a verse, a burden, a person’s name—do two things with it before anything else. First, intercede: bring that person or situation before God the way Abraham brought Sodom, standing in the gap for people who don’t know a conversation about them is happening. Second, deliver what is meant to be delivered: if the word is edification, exhortation, or comfort, it isn’t finished until it reaches the person it’s for. Test it against Paul’s three marks—does it build up, stir up, or bind up? Then speak it humbly and let the hearer weigh it (==1 Corinthians 14:29==).
