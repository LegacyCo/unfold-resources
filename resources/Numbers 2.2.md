## Commentary
Numbers 2 gives the arrangement of Israel’s camp. Each tribe is to gather under its own standard (or flag), beside the emblems of its father’s house. While the language is both communal and military, this would not have been a foreign concept to Israel.

In Egypt, army divisions were often named after deities, and the standard for each division would bear a representation of that god. The army marched under divine symbols, but those symbols represented Egypt’s gods and Egypt’s power. Their identity, protection, and victory were tied to the gods whose names they carried.

Israel’s camp was different.

It is reasonable to assume each tribal standard displayed some symbol connected to the tribe. The standards identify the tribes, but the tabernacle identifies the nation.

Israel is not a collection of families traveling through the wilderness. They are a redeemed people with the presence of God at the center. Every tribe has its place, but no tribe is the center. Every household has its emblem, but no emblem is ultimate. The camp is arranged so that Israel’s life is visibly ordered around the Lord.

Their identity is no longer shaped by Egypt. They are now embarking on a journey of being formed by the presence of God.

This is one of the significant themes of the wilderness experience. God is not only bringing Israel out of Egypt geographically. He is bringing Egypt out of Israel spiritually. He is retraining their worship, their community, and their identity as a people.

In Egypt, the people lived under the symbols of false gods and oppressive power. In the wilderness, they learn to live under the ordered presence of the true God.
