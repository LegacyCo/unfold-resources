## Commentary
“I will make mention of Rahab and Babylon to those who know Me; behold, O Philistia and Tyre, with Ethiopia: ‘This one was born there.’”

Psalm 87 is a song about Zion, the city of God. And right in the middle of it, God starts reading a list of names no one in Israel would have expected.

Rahab is a nickname for Egypt, the nation that enslaved Israel. Babylon is the empire that burned Jerusalem and carried her people away. Philistia is the old enemy next door, going back to the days of Samson and Goliath. Tyre is the rich trading city up the coast. Ethiopia, or Cush, was about as far south as the known world went. So the list covers Israel's oppressors, her enemies, her neighbors, and the ends of the earth.

And this is what God says about them: “This one was born there.” Born in Zion. He says it three times in three verses (==Psalm 87:4-6==). In verse 6 He's pictured taking a census: “The LORD will record, when He registers the peoples: ‘This one was born there.’” People from the nations that hated Jerusalem are being written into her records as native-born citizens. 

The phrase “to those who know Me” can also be read “among those who know Me.” Either way, the nations on the list end up in the same place: known by God and belonging to His precious city. Isaiah saw the same thing: “Blessed is Egypt My people, and Assyria the work of My hands, and Israel My inheritance” (==Isaiah 19:25==).

How does someone born in Egypt or Babylon get a birth record in Zion? The psalm doesn't explain it. But the New Testament does. Jesus said, “unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God” (==John 3:3==). Those who receive Him are “born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God” (==John 1:13==). Paul tells Gentile believers they were once “aliens from the commonwealth of Israel” and are now “no longer strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints” (==Ephesians 2:12==; ==Ephesians 2:19==). He says, “the Jerusalem above is free, which is the mother of us all” (==Galatians 4:26==). And Hebrews says we have come “to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God… to the general assembly and church of the firstborn who are registered in heaven” (==Hebrews 12:22-23==). That's Psalm 87. 

So where, and who, you came from is no longer the immovable past it once was. Those things are part of your story, but in Christ, they no longer define you. You've been born again, and your new birth gave you a new identity. When God looks at His record, what He reads next to your name is, “This one was born there...in Zion”

## Word Study
**Rahab** here is the Hebrew *rahab* (רַהַב)—“the proud one” or “the stormer.” In Hebrew poetry it's the name of a sea monster God defeats (==Psalm 89:10==; ==Isaiah 51:9==), and the prophets use it as a nickname for Egypt: “I have called her Rahab-Hem-Shebeth” (==Isaiah 30:7==). It's a different word from the name of Rahab of Jericho, which is spelled differently in Hebrew (*rachab*, רָחָב). Summary: when God says Rahab was “born there,” He's talking about Egypt, the proud power that once held His people as slaves.
