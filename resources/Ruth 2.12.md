## Commentary
When Boaz meets Ruth, he already knows her story. He has heard how she left Moab, clung to Naomi, and came to Bethlehem to live among a people who were not her own (Ruth 1:16–17; 2:11).

Boaz blesses her with one of the most important images in the book: “under whose wings you have come for refuge.”

This is covenant language. Ruth has come under the protection of the God of Israel. She is not merely seeking food in a field. She is seeking refuge under the wings of the Lord.

The image of God’s wings appears throughout Scripture as a picture of protection, nearness, and mercy. The psalmist says, “Hide me under the shadow of Your wings” (Psalm 17:8). Psalm 36 says the children of men put their trust under the shadow of God’s wings (Psalm 36:7). Psalm 91 says the Lord will cover His people with His feathers, and under His wings they will take refuge (Psalm 91:4).

But here is what makes Ruth 2 so powerful: Boaz becomes part of the answer to his own blessing.

He prays that Ruth would be rewarded by the Lord, under whose wings she has come for refuge. Then Boaz himself becomes an expression of that refuge. He protects her, provides for her, speaks kindly to her, gives her access to his field, commands his men not to touch her, and makes sure she has more than enough (Ruth 2:8–16).

## Application
Boaz shows us what it looks like to become an instrument of God’s kindness. He uses his strength to protect. He uses his position to provide. He uses his words to restore dignity.

This is covenant kindness in action.

Some people need more than a blessing spoken over them. They need someone willing to become the answer to the prayers they are praying.
