## Commentary
Paul says, “Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread.” That means communion is not only a participation in Christ. It is an intensification of our unity with one another.

We come to the table as many.  
Different stories.  
Different backgrounds.  
Different wounds.  
Different weaknesses.

But we receive one bread.

And in receiving the one bread, we are reminded that we are not independent Christians having private spiritual moments with Jesus. We are members of one Body.

This is why communion cannot be reduced to personal reflection alone. It certainly includes personal examination, but it is bigger than that. The table does not only confront my sin. It confronts my isolation. My individualism. My refusal to be joined deeply to the people Jesus has joined Himself to.

There is a famous saying in the writing of Saint Augustine. Whenever he would give communion to someone for the first time, instead of holding up the bread and saying, "The Body of Christ," he would hold it up and say, "Receive what you are." The table of the Lord changes us so that we become what we receive; one people, one family, one Body under one Lord.

## Application
We don't come to the table merely to remember that Christ died for us. We come to remember that by His death, He has made us one.

God's mathematical equation is always one. The Trinity: three in one. Marriage: two become one. The cross has the power to bring us into the mathematical realities of heaven...we are now one in Christ.
