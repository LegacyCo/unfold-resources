## Commentary
“Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams.”

Look at what Saul actually did, because his sin seems subtle. God told him to completely destroy the Amalekites and everything they had. Saul obeyed—mostly. He kept back the best of the sheep and oxen, and his stated reason sounded impressively spiritual: they were spared *to sacrifice to the LORD* (==1 Samuel 15:15==). Saul disobeyed because what God said didn’t make sense to him—why waste perfectly good livestock when it could be offered on an altar?—and then he dressed the disobedience up as devotion. His plan honestly seemed smarter than God’s instruction.

Samuel’s answer cuts through every bit of that reasoning: God’s delight is not in what you offer Him instead of obedience. It’s in obedience. You cannot substitute sacrifice for submission, worship for yieldedness, spiritual activity for simply doing what He said. Partial obedience with a worship service attached is still disobedience—and it cost Saul the kingdom (==1 Samuel 15:23==; ==1 Samuel 15:26==).

Here’s the root of it: our obedience to God cannot be built on our ability to understand His instructions and agree with them. That’s too subjective—and it rebuilds God in our own image, a god who only says things that make sense to us. Art Katz put it plainly in *Apostolic Foundations*: “How many of us predicate our decisions on our own reason and logic, but at the expense of being led by the Spirit of God… We cannot meet the demands of our high, heavenly calling on the basis of our own reason.” Kingdom people don’t offer God impressive alternatives to what He said. They heed His voice—and that, Samuel says, delights Him more than the fat of rams.
