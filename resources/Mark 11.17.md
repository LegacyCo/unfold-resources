## Commentary
Tables are flipping, doves are scattering, and in the middle of the chaos Jesus gives His definition of the place: “Is it not written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations’?” He’s quoting Isaiah, who saw foreigners joined to the Lord and made joyful in His house of prayer (==Isaiah 56:6-7==). Three words—house, prayer, nations—and those three words are what Jesus thinks when He says the word “church.”

*House* speaks to the family of God in communion with one another. Not a building—a household. And not just gathered, either: stones stacked in a pile are a good start, but a house has wiring and plumbing running through its walls. Without that internal connectedness there may be a structure, but there’s no life in it. It’s only as living stones are knit together in love that a gathering becomes a home (==1 Peter 2:5==; ==Colossians 2:2==).

*Prayer* speaks to the family of God in communion with Him. A house where the Father lives is a house where the family talks with Him—not once a week, not only in emergencies, but as the normal, ongoing conversation of the home. And notice what Jesus did *not* name His house. He didn’t say it would be called a house of preaching, or a house of worship music, or a house of events. Those things all have their place, but when Jesus named His house, He named it after prayer. That tells us what He considers the main activity under His roof. A church can be strong in many things, but if prayer is thin, the house is missing the very thing Jesus named it for.

*Nations* speaks to God’s desire for the people who are not yet in His family to be brought in. Remember where Jesus is quoting from: Isaiah saw foreigners—outsiders with no natural claim on Israel’s God—being joined to the Lord and made joyful in His house (==Isaiah 56:6-7==). So God’s house was never meant to be a place where the family hides from the world. It’s meant to have an open door and a growing table. Notice that Jesus didn’t say “a house of prayer for us.” He said “for all nations.” A church can love its own people and love to pray, but if the lost aren’t on its heart, it has cut off the end of Jesus’ sentence. He didn’t stop at “house of prayer,” and neither can we.

Here’s what I’ve noticed in churches all over the world: so many emphasize one of these three and neglect the others. Some do community beautifully but barely touch the lost. Some are passionate about outreach but never disciple their people into mothers and fathers. Some pray fervently and stay strangers to each other. But Jesus didn’t offer three options—He gave one three-word definition. And at the end of the day, His definition of His house is the only one that matters. He gets to define the culture of His house; we don’t. So it’s worth asking honest questions about what we call church. Is what we’re building a house of prayer for all nations? If not, we know whose definition needs to change.
