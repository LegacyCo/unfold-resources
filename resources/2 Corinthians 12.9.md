## Commentary
“And He said to me, ‘My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.’ Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

God fills empty things. He wants to fill His people with Himself—His plans, His love, His faith, His heart for the people around them, His power—and so He brings them to the end of themselves in order to make room. That is the logic behind the answer Paul received. The thorn was not removed; the weakness was where the power of Christ would rest. Jesus Himself walked this road first: He “emptied Himself” (==Philippians 2:7==), and because of the Son's obedience to what the Father wanted, the Father could do whatever He wanted through the Son. God asks nothing of us He has not already done in Christ.

Ernest Glaive, a prophetic voice from the lineage of the Welsh revival, told Bob Gladstone shortly before his death: “The only thing that pleases God is what He does Himself.” The statement sounds severe until you see how God does what He does. Gladstone explains: “God always performs His works through people… people who have their own wills, their own minds, their own hearts. They can choose whether to be in agreement with God or not. But when a person puts themself in union with God to obey the Lord, and God does His works through them… God receives the glory… by acting through His people.” God has determined to glorify Himself by demonstrating Himself through His people (==Ezekiel 36:23==). That requires people who have been brought to the end of themselves—because a vessel still full of its own strength leaves no room for His.

## Word Study
**Made perfect** is the Greek *teleō* (τελέω)—to bring to completion, to reach the goal, to finish. It is the verb behind Jesus' cry from the cross, “It is finished” (==John 19:30==), and John uses the related form of love that has been “perfected” in us (==1 John 4:12==). Summary: God's power does not merely coexist with weakness—it arrives at its full expression there. Weakness is where His strength finishes its work.

## Application
Stop treating your weakness as the obstacle to usefulness. Name the area where you feel most inadequate—the assignment you are not equipped for, the limitation you cannot fix—and instead of hiding it, bring it to God as the place you are asking Him to fill. Boast in it the way Paul did: not by celebrating the weakness itself, but by expecting Christ's power to rest precisely there. What you can do in your own strength, you will be tempted to take credit for. What He does through your emptiness brings glory to His name.
