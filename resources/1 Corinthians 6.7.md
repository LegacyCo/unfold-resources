## Commentary
“Now therefore, it is already an utter failure for you that you go to law against one another. Why do you not rather accept wrong? Why do you not rather let yourselves be cheated?”

Paul is not denying that real wrongs had been done. He is telling the church that for a people who belong to Jesus, there is something worse than being wronged—and that is destroying the testimony of the family of God in front of a watching world to get your money back (==1 Corinthians 6:6==).

This is what bearing the cross can look like in our relationships with one another. The daily choosing to forgive when it hurts, love when it's hard, and absorb a wrong rather than avenge it (==1 Peter 2:23==; ==Romans 12:19==). Every objection the Corinthians could raise—“but it's not fair; do you know what he did to me, how she talked about me?”—comes to the same conclusion: bring it to the cross, not the courts. The One who was wronged most absorbed the most: Jesus, wronged by every one of us, accepted the wrong and paid for it (==Colossians 3:13==). A church knit together in love will be a church full of people who have accepted wrongs that they had every right to demand justice for—because they serve a King who did exactly that for them. God's house is not built by people who win their disputes but by people who lay them down (==Philippians 2:5-7==).
