## Commentary
“And Lot lifted his eyes and saw all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered everywhere… like the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt as you go toward Zoar.”

Lot had good eyesight, and he depended on his good eyesight to make decisions. Instead of lifting his eyes to heaven to let God direct his steps, he let his eyes fix on the most beautiful land in view, and what he saw with his eyes determined what he did with his life. Warren Wiersbe observes: “The eyes see what the heart loves. Abraham had taken Lot out of Egypt, but he could not take Egypt out of Lot. Outlook determines outcome. While Abraham’s eyes were on the holy city of God (==Hebrews 11:13-16==), and he went on to walk with the Lord and inherit the blessing, Lot’s eyes were on the sinful cities of men, and he went on to worldly success, spiritual failure, and a shameful end.” The text itself gives the heart away—the plain reminded Lot of “the land of Egypt.”

There is nothing wrong with wanting good land or providing well for a family. That is not the point Scripture is making. The point is *how* the righteous make decisions. Decisions driven by the senses—by reason alone, by emotion, by the desire of the eyes—operate outside of faith, and “without faith it is impossible to please Him” (==Hebrews 11:6==). God does not reward good decision-making; He rewards faith. So Paul writes, “we do not look at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporary, but the things which are not seen are eternal” (==2 Corinthians 4:18==), and again, “we walk by faith, not by sight” (==2 Corinthians 5:7==).

## Application
Take an honest inventory of how you make major decisions. When a choice is in front of you, does the deciding vote go to what looks best—the numbers, the appearance, the comfort—or to what God has said? Identify one decision you are currently facing and deliberately reverse Lot’s order: before you evaluate what you can see, lift your eyes to the Lord in prayer and ask Him to direct the choice (==Proverbs 3:5-6==). The eyes will always vote for what the heart loves; the question is what your heart has learned to love.
