## Commentary
On the night He was betrayed, listen to what Jesus did *not* pray. He didn’t pray, “Father, I pray that they may be saved from their sins.” He prayed, “that they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You; that they also may be one in Us.” Hours before the cross, with the weight of the world’s redemption in front of Him, the longing on Jesus’ heart was union.

The gospel of the kingdom isn’t good news because it promises salvation for sinners or heaven for the hell-bound. Those are wonderful blessings we should all be grateful for. And if that's all the gospel ever accomplished in our lives, it would be more than we deserve. But the gospel is so much more than salvation. The gospel promises union between God and man. 

The mystery of the gospel is that one Man died for all men—and “if One died for all, then all died” (==2 Corinthians 5:14==). All who believe are joined to the Man who died. His death becomes your death; His resurrection becomes your resurrection: “if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection” (==Romans 6:5==). Whatever happened to Jesus happened to everyone who is joined to Him. The early church father Irenaeus put it this way: “He became what we are, that He might bring us to be what He is.”

And notice—*with* and *in* are two very different things. There were two thieves on crosses beside Jesus that day. Both of them were with Christ. Only one ended his life in Christ.

Most Christians believe Jesus died to save them from their sins. But saving you from sin was the starting point, not the goal. Think of it this way: before I married my wife, her identity was “single.” But I didn’t marry my wife to set her free from being single—I married her because I love her. And the covenant we made out of that love joined us together as one. In the same way, Jesus didn’t go to the cross because of your identity as a sinner. He went because of His great love for you. Forgiveness of sins is what the cross *accomplished*—it isn’t what *motivated* it. Sin had separated us from the Father, and that was not okay with Him.

Salvation is a great place to start and a terrible place to stop. You weren’t born just to be saved. You were created to be one with God—and on His last night, that’s exactly what Jesus asked His Father to give you.

## Application
Take an audit of your prayer language this week. How much of it asks God to come near—“be with me, meet me, show up”—as if He were somewhere else? Union changes the vocabulary. Try starting your prayers from position instead of pursuit: “Father, thank You that I am in You and You are in me.” Then pick one ordinary moment each day—the commute, a meal, walking into work—and let it become a deliberate acknowledgment that the prayer Jesus prayed in John 17:21 was answered.
