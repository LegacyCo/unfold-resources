## Commentary
“He is the brightness of His glory and the express image of His person, upholding all things by the word of His power.” Jesus is not a reflection of the Father—a reflection is a secondhand image thrown off by something else. He is the very radiance of God’s glory and the exact representation of His nature: the same essence, the same substance. “He who has seen Me has seen the Father” (==John 14:9==). Jesus is exactly what God wants to say about Himself. “For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily” (==Colossians 2:9==).

And here’s the part of this verse that will carry you through your week: what this word describes is *constant*. The engraving on a coin doesn’t come and go. When the coin is in your pocket, hidden from sight, it’s still engraved with the image. Are there stars in the sky in the afternoon? Of course there are—still burning, still radiating, just overshadowed by something brighter. In the same way, Jesus is always with you, always radiating the glory of God, always revealing the Father—even in the moments He seems hidden. He hasn’t stopped shining. Something inferior has just captured your attention.

Which means His radiance in your life is not a question of His presence but of your gaze. The North Star doesn’t stop being the North Star when you fixate on the blinking light of a passing airplane—but the blinking light becomes the brightest thing in *your* universe the moment you give it your attention. You get to determine which star shines brightest in your solar system. Fix your eyes on Jesus (==Hebrews 12:2==), and the Radiance of glory resumes His rightful place—brightest thing in the sky, upholding all things, including yours, by the word of His power.

## Word Study
- *express image* : **charaktēr** (Greek) — the embossed stamp on a coin; the impress of a seal; the exact imprint of the die that struck it. This is the only time charaktēr appears in all of Scripture—reserved for one Person. It describes what is innate and unalterable: the engraving on a coin doesn’t fade, develop, or improve. It just *is*. It’s the quality of a snowflake that makes it unlike any other snowflake ever formed.

Summary: the author of Hebrews reached for a word he would never use again to say something that will never change—Jesus is the exact, constant, permanent imprint of the Father’s nature. What God is, Jesus shows. Always has. Always will.
