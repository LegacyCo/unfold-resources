## Commentary
The gift the Lord gave His people in the Old Testament was the law. The gift the Lord gave His church in the New Testament is the Spirit. The similarities of when both were given are interesting to observe. 

Consider this: Exodus 19 is the story of Israel receiving the law. The chapter begins 45 days after the first Passover. Verse 3 and verse 8 detail two days spent with Moses going up the mountain to meet with God, communicating what He said to the people of Israel below, gathering their response, and returning up the mountain to relay their response to God. 

Verse 15 details 3 days of preparation. 

Then in v16-20, the Lord *descends* upon the mountain in *fire* and the Lord gives the gift of the law to Moses. 

That mean the law was given fifty days after Passover...specifically, "in the morning" of the fiftieth day.

Fast forward to Acts 2, the day the Holy Spirit was poured out—descending upon the disciples in tongues of fire: Pentecost is fifty days after Passover ("penta" meaning five, "pentecost" meaning fiftieth day.) Acts 2:15 tells us the Holy Spirit was poured out in "the third hour of the day" (9 a.m.).

The law and the Spirit were both given in displays of fire, fifty days after Passover, and in the morning. 

Jewish tradition *also* says that when the Lord spoke the 10 commandments from Mount Sinai, everyone in the "mixed multitude" heard it in their own language. And, of course, here in Acts 2, the mixed multitude in Jerusalem heard the followers of Jesus "speak in their own language" (Acts 2:6).
