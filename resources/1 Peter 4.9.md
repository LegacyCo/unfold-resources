## Commentary
Most of us hear the word _hospitality_ and immediately think of a curated event, a spotless house, good lighting, and a meal impressive enough to post online. We think Martha Stewart more than Jesus.

But entertainment and hospitality are not the same thing.

Entertainment is often about exclusion. You invite the right people, the inner circle, the crowd that makes the room feel impressive.

Hospitality is about inclusion. It is an open table. It is the willingness to make room for the person who may not be able to return the favor.

Entertainment is about performance. It says, “Look what I prepared. Look how beautiful my home is. Look how well I can host.”

Hospitality is about service. It says, “There is room for you here. You are not an interruption. You are not invisible. You are welcome.”

Entertainment is sporadic. It is an event scheduled weeks in advance.

Hospitality is a rhythm of life. 

Entertainment often works by reciprocity. I have you over, and then eventually you have me over. 

Hospitality is generosity. It gives without keeping score. It welcomes without calculating return. It opens the door because God has opened the door to us.

In Scripture, hospitality is tied to justice, especially for the poor, the stranger, the outsider, and the vulnerable. It is one of the ways the people of God resist the culture of the world.

The world builds tables that reinforce status.

The kingdom builds tables that announce grace.

This is why Jesus tells us not to invite only those who can invite us back, but to welcome the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind. In other words, don’t just use your table to strengthen your social circle. Use it to reveal the heart of God.

That's hospitality.

And Peter says to do it without grumbling because grumbling exposes when our hospitality is still centered on us. It reveals when we want the appearance of generosity without the inconvenience of love.

True hospitality will cost you something.

It may cost you comfort.

It may cost you privacy.

It may cost you the illusion that your life belongs only to you.

But this is the way of Jesus. He didn't welcome us because we were impressive guests. He welcomed us when we were strangers. He brought us near when we had nothing to offer. He gave us a seat at the table because of His great love. Consider what it cost Him to welcome us to His table.

Christian hospitality is not entertainment with Bible verses sprinkled on top.

It's the kindness and compassion of Jesus extended to the world through His people.
