## Commentary
“A sound heart is life to the body, but envy is rottenness to the bones.”

Solomon puts two interior conditions side by side and tells us what each one does to a human being. A sound heart—a heart at peace, at rest, settled—is *life* to the body. And envy—the restless craving for what someone else has—doesn’t just sour the mood; it rots the bones. The proverb is blunt about something we’ve made complicated: the state of your inner life writes itself on your outer one.

Notice what makes the two conditions different. Envy is the heart in a state of comparison—always measuring, always aware of what it lacks, always agitated by someone else’s blessing. It can never rest, because someone somewhere always has more. But the heart at rest has stopped measuring. It’s content, not because it possesses everything, but because it trusts Someone (==Philippians 4:11-12==). Rest isn’t the absence of desire; it’s the presence of trust.

This is one of the marks of kingdom people: they carry rest. They’re so rooted in what God says about them that they have no need to make things happen, prove their spirituality, or give their attention to every storm and crisis. Shakings don’t move them, because they’re seated in an unshakable kingdom (==Hebrews 12:28==). And here’s what the proverb adds to that picture: their bodies benefit. A person at rest in God isn’t just spiritually healthier—the peace of God stands guard over heart *and* mind (==Philippians 4:7==), and life flows to the whole house.
