## Commentary
We’ve been taught that repentance is changing the direction you’re moving in. That’s partially true…but incomplete. Repentance is FIRST changing the way you think at such a core level that you THEN change the direction you're moving in. If you change your direction without changing your understanding of who God is, you’ve received a gospel of behavior modification…not the gospel of the kingdom. 

Repentance first and foremost is a renewing of the mind…the greek word for repentance is metanoia. Look in any lexicon and the first definition for metanoia is “change of mind.” This is why John the Baptist could look at the Pharisees, who were blameless according to the law, and to them to repent. They knew the law, but they didn’t know the God of the law.

If repentance is changing the way you think, then what is the first thought that must change? Most of us would say that we must understand that we are sinners? So religion has taught us that it’s our awareness of sin that leads to repentance. There’s only one problem with that…the Bible. The bible doesn’t say that it’s the awareness of sin that leads to repentance. It’s the kindness of God that leads to repentance.

Repentance doesn’t begin when I see myself as filthy…repentance begins when I see God as kind. This isn’t just semantics…this is huge. If repentance begins with me and my sin, when the Bible says true repentance begins with the revelation that He is kind, then my relationship with God has sin-consciousness as it’s origin instead of God-consciousness. How things begin matter.

Repent means I acknowledge that I’ve been believing a lie, I’ve been entertaining values that are contrary to my design in Christ. God forgive me…I’ve compromised my own soul. It’s not a moment for shame, it’s a moment for deliverance. It’s a moment of forsaking what is warring to contaminate my heart and alter my perception of God and His creation.

God intends for repentance to wash over us when the Holy Spirit gives us eyes to see God…to see Him high and lifted up, when we can actually see Him as He truly is, and we realize, “My God, You are not who I thought You were.” Repentance begins with Him. 

When you see Him as kind then you will see yourself as loved. Repentance brings us into agreement with who God says we are…Loved.
