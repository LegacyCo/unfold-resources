## Commentary
The word translated “refreshed” is fascinating. It means to exhale, to breathe freely, or to catch one’s breath. It describes the release that comes when pressure is lifted and a person is finally able to breathe again.

This verse says that after six days of creation, God “ceased from labor, and was refreshed.” This doesn’t mean that God became tired or depleted. Scripture is clear that the Creator doesn’t grow weary (==Isaiah 40:28==). The language is meant to show us the rhythm God built into creation. He worked, He stopped, and He took delight in what He had made.

The only two other passages that use this word help us understand its meaning.

Exodus 23:12 says, “Six days you shall do your work, and on the seventh day you shall rest, that your ox and your donkey may rest, and the son of your female servant and the stranger may be refreshed.”

Here, refreshment is connected to relief from labor. The Sabbath created space for everyone—even servants, foreigners, and animals—to stop carrying the weight of production and catch their breath. Rest wasn’t reserved for the powerful. It was built into God’s covenant as a gift for the weary.

The same word appears in 2 Samuel 16:14: “Now the king and all the people who were with him became weary; so they refreshed themselves there.”

David and his people had been fleeing from Absalom. They were physically exhausted, emotionally overwhelmed, and under enormous pressure. When they finally stopped, they were able to breathe again.

This gives us a deeper picture of biblical rest. Rest is more than the absence of activity. It’s the God-given space where the soul can exhale. It’s the moment when we stop striving, release the pressure to keep producing, and remember that the world continues to turn because God is sustaining it—not because we are.

Sabbath is an invitation to breathe freely in the presence of God. It reminds us that we are creatures, not the Creator; sons and daughters, not machines. We work, but we are not defined by our work. We carry responsibility, but we were never meant to carry the weight of everything.

Sometimes the most spiritual thing you can do is stop, breathe, and receive the rest God has already provided.

## Application
Make room to exhale, but don’t reduce Sabbath to a single day on the calendar or mistake rest for inactivity. In the new covenant, we are a Sabbath people. Through Christ, we have entered the rest of God, which means rest is now our positional reality. We are *in* the Prince of Peace, and His peace has become the place from which we live.

Rest isn’t the absence of activity. Jesus lived from perfect rest and still worked, served, healed, taught, and carried out the will of the Father. Biblical rest is an inner posture of trust that allows us to remain at peace even while we are active. We can work without striving, serve without trying to prove ourselves, and carry responsibility without believing everything depends on us.

So when pressure rises, stop and exhale. Remember where you are. You are in Christ, and Christ is your peace. You aren’t working for rest. You are living and working from it.
