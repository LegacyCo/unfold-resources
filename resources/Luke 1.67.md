## Commentary
“Now his father Zacharias was filled with the Holy Spirit, and prophesied, saying: ‘Blessed is the Lord God of Israel, for He has visited and redeemed His people.’”

Zacharias had been mute for nine months. When he doubted Gabriel’s word in the temple, the ability to speak was taken from him until the day the promise was fulfilled (==Luke 1:20==). Now the child is born, and the moment Zacharias writes “His name is John” on the tablet—coming into agreement with the word God had given—his mouth is opened (==Luke 1:63-64==). And notice what a priest who has been silent for the better part of a year says first. Not a complaint about the discipline. Not a recap of what he missed. He is filled with the Holy Spirit, and his first words are prophecy: “Blessed is the Lord God of Israel.”

Two things stand in this verse. First, the connection between agreement and release. Zacharias’s tongue was loosed when he aligned himself with what God had said. Unbelief had silenced him; agreement with God’s word opened his mouth. Second, the pattern Luke has been building through this chapter: the Spirit fills, and the person speaks. Elizabeth was filled and blessed Mary aloud (==Luke 1:41-42==); now Zacharias is filled and prophesies; at Pentecost the whole company will be filled and speak (==Acts 2:4==). When the Spirit of God comes upon a person, speech follows.

There is mercy worth noticing here, too. Zacharias’s season of silence was discipline, but it was not disqualification. The same man who doubted the word became the man who prophesied over its fulfillment—nine months of silence ending in a Spirit-filled declaration that the church still reads. God’s correction had an end date, and on the other side of it, Zacharias’s voice was more surrendered than it had ever been. If you are in a silent season under the dealings of God, take this verse as its trajectory: the discipline is preparing your mouth, not closing it for good.

## Cross References
The connection between agreement with God’s word and an opened mouth runs through Scripture: ==Luke 1:20== (the mouth closed by unbelief), ==Luke 1:63-64== (the mouth opened the moment Zacharias wrote what God had said), ==Psalm 51:15== (“O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth shall show forth Your praise”), ==Romans 10:9-10== (the mouth confessing what the heart believes), and ==2 Corinthians 4:13== (“I believed and therefore I spoke”—faith’s speech follows faith’s agreement).
