## Commentary
“No one puts new wine into old wineskins… new wine must be put into new wineskins.” We understand the picture: we’re the wineskin, and what Jesus pours out is the wine. But there’s a detail most of us miss because of translation—Jesus uses two *different* Greek words for “new” in this one sentence, and the difference is the whole point.

The wine is brand new. The wineskin is *re*-newed. In ancient times, old wineskins weren’t thrown away—they were far too valuable. The hides were soaked in oil for hours, sometimes days, stretched and reworked until they became soft and supple again. Without that renewal, the skin would burst under the pressure of fermenting wine. So Jesus isn’t saying, “Discard the old vessel and get a different one.” He’s saying that what has grown calloused must be tenderized and made vulnerable again.

That’s the work God does in the space between seasons. Every season leaves its mark—trials bruise us, people disappoint us, unmet expectations quietly harden us behind layers of self-preservation. And before God pours the new wine of a new season, He goes to work on the vessel: the methodical softening of a heart that calcified in the last chapter. It’s as if He says, “I love what you held. But if you want to hold what I’m about to do next, you have to let Me soften you again.”

Renewal isn’t a luxury; it’s a necessity (==Romans 12:2==). And nothing becomes tender by accident. It takes oil. It takes stretching. It takes time. If the process feels slow, that’s not God forgetting you—that’s the tannery.

## Word Study
- *new* (wine): **neos** — chronologically new; fresh; that which has not existed before.
- *new* (wineskin): **kainos** — new in quality, not in time; re-newed, re-stored, made new again. This is the word in “if anyone is in Christ, he is a *kainos* creation” (==2 Corinthians 5:17==) and “Behold, I make all things *kainos*” (==Revelation 21:5==). God’s pattern isn’t replacement. It’s restoration.
