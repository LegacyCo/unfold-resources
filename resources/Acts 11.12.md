## Commentary
Peter had acted in obedience to what the Lord had showed him, but he had not acted alone. By taking six others with him, he had witnesses who could verify his story. He may not have known the potential scandal that he was stepping into, but there was wisdom in not going alone. When the church in Judea heard what Peter had participated in (going into a Gentile home...and *eating* with them!) they had serious questions. Thankfully, it wasn't just Peter who was answering these charges. He had witnesses.

## Application
There is a principle of the kingdom referred to as being "above reproach" (see 1 Timothy 3:2, Colossians 1:22). This means you are above the accusation. This doesn't mean you won't face situations were you may be accused or misunderstood, but your character and your actions testify on your behalf. Character is something we demonstrate daily. The consistency in the way we live becomes our witness when an accusation comes against us. And to the best of our ability, not putting ourselves in situations where we have no witnesses to speak on our behalf is wisdom.
