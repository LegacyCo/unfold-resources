## Commentary
This long chapter ends with Moses entering the tent of meeting to speak with the Lord. There, from above the mercy seat between the two cherubim, Moses heard the voice of God speaking to him. After all the offerings, carts, oxen, bowls, basins, grain, incense, and sacrifices, the chapter resolves in communion.

This is the whole point of the tabernacle. God was not giving Israel a religious system, although it may look that way as we read through the details of tabernacle worship. In reality, God was making a way to dwell among His people. The offerings mattered. The altar mattered. The priesthood mattered. But they all served a greater purpose: access to the presence and voice of God.

The voice came from above the mercy seat, the place of atonement. God speaks to His people from the place where mercy is established. His voice is not detached from His covenant grace. He speaks as the God who has made a way for sinful people to draw near.

This finds its fulfillment in Jesus. He is our mercy seat, our place of atonement, and the final Word of God to humanity. In Him, God has not only spoken from between the cherubim; He has come near in flesh and spoken face to face.
