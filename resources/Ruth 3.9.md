## Commentary
Ruth 3 brings us to the threshing floor, where Ruth asks Boaz to act as her kinsman-redeemer. Her request may sound strange to modern readers, but Ruth is not making a random romantic gesture. She is making a covenant appeal. Boaz is a close relative of Elimelech’s family, and Ruth is asking him to step into the role of redeemer.

In Israel, a kinsman-redeemer was a close relative who had the responsibility to rescue, restore, or preserve the life and inheritance of a family member in need. If land had been lost because of poverty, the redeemer could buy it back so it remained within the family line (==Leviticus 25:25==). If a man died without a son, there was also a responsibility connected to preserving his name and family line through marriage to the widow (==Deuteronomy 25:5–10==).

That is what is happening in Ruth. Elimelech and his sons have died. Naomi has returned to Bethlehem empty. Ruth is a Moabite widow with no obvious future, no husband, and no child to carry on the family name. The family line is in danger of disappearing.

So Ruth comes to Boaz and says, “Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative.”

In other words, “You have the ability to redeem. Will you cover me? Will you preserve this family? Will you bring restoration where death and loss have emptied everything?”
