## Commentary
When Paul talks about “living in the flesh,” especially in Romans 8, he does not mean living in a physical body. The Greek word is _sarx_ (σάρξ), “flesh.” Paul can use it literally for physical flesh, but in Romans 8 he is using it more broadly for human life organized apart from the Spirit of God—humanity in Adam, under the power of sin and death.

Obviously, the Roman believers were still physically embodied. They still had skin, bones, appetites, emotions, and mortal bodies. So “in the flesh” cannot just mean “having a body.”

It means belonging to the old realm.

Throughout Romans, Paul has been contrasting two humanities: humanity “in Adam” and humanity “in Christ” (==Romans 5:12–21==). The flesh belongs to that old Adamic order—the human person trying to live from natural capacity, distorted desire, self-rule, and independence from God. The Spirit belongs to the new creation that has begun in Christ.

So when Paul says, “those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh” (==Romans 8:5==), he is describing an orientation of life.

The flesh says, in effect, _I will live from myself._

The Spirit says, _I receive my life from God._

This is why Paul says: “For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace” (==Romans 8:6==).

The issue is not that physical things are bad and spiritual things are good. Paul is not teaching a Greek-style rejection of the body. In fact, the climax of the chapter is the resurrection and redemption of the body (==Romans 8:11==; ==Romans 8:23==). God intends to redeem embodied human life, not rescue us from it.

The problem is independence.

To live “according to the flesh” is to live from what humanity has become under sin rather than from what humanity is becoming through the Spirit.

That can take obvious forms—sexual immorality, greed, hatred, jealousy, drunkenness—but it can also look very respectable. Paul himself had once tried to establish righteousness through his own religious performance. That too was confidence “in the flesh” (==Philippians 3:3–9==). Flesh is not limited to scandalous sin. Flesh is whatever human life attempts to produce apart from dependence upon God.

In Romans 8, Paul is therefore describing a transfer of realms.

“You are not in the flesh but in the Spirit.”

That is first an identity statement before it becomes a behavioral command.

The believer no longer belongs to the old age dominated by sin and death. The Spirit of the risen Christ has taken up residence within them. They are now living from a new source of life.

That helps explain Paul's command a few verses later: “If by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live” (==Romans 8:13==).

Notice what Paul does not say: destroy the body.

He says put to death the _deeds_ of the body that belong to the old life.

The body is not the enemy. Sin is.

And the solution is not stronger willpower. It is life through the Spirit.

So, in simple terms, “living in the flesh” means living from the resources, desires, values, and capacities of fallen humanity apart from the Spirit’s life and leadership.

It is Adam-life instead of Christ-life.

It is self-sufficiency instead of dependence.

It is attempting to generate life from a source that can only produce death.

Romans 8 announces that the Christian has been given another source altogether: “the Spirit is life” (==Romans 8:10==).
