## Commentary
This verse promises that Jerusalem will once again be called a "mountain of holiness.” That promise is astounding because Jeremiah gives it while Israel is in exile. Jerusalem has been devastated. The temple has been destroyed. The people have been carried away to Babylon in judgment. At that moment, there is nothing about Jerusalem that looks holy. It looks abandoned, judged, and ruined.

But God is not speaking about the condition of the land. Because it's His presence that will make the land holy.

Jerusalem is holy because God says He will return, restore, and dwell with His people there once again. And later in the chapter, the promise gets even more shocking. God says the whole valley, even the place filled with dead bodies and ashes, will be holy to the LORD (==Jeremiah 31:40==). The Kidron Valley — which even still today is a burial site — was associated with the unclean, yet God says even *that* place will belong to Him.

That's redemption. God doesn't just preserve what already looks clean. He comes to the places polluted by death and makes them holy by His presence. The promise of Jeremiah 31 is that exile will not have the final word, judgment will not have the final word, and death will not have the final word.

God will restore His people, reclaim His land, and make even the ruined places holy again.
