## Commentary
“As He is, so are we in this world.”

Sit with that sentence for a second. If it weren’t in the Bible, it would be heresy. As the glorified, enthroned Jesus *is*—right now—so are *we*, in this world. Not “so will we be someday.” So are we, present tense, here.

But it’s not heresy. It’s the gospel. And it measures just how powerful the gospel actually is: it transforms enemies into image bearers and sinners into saints (==2 Corinthians 5:17==). God intends for anyone who bears His name to be able to say, “If you want to know what God is like, look at my life. I not only bear His name—I bear His image and likeness.” Not a claim of perfection; a claim of possession. His grace is sufficient, and He’s conforming me to the image of Jesus day by day, from glory to glory (==2 Corinthians 3:18==).

This was always the design. Adam’s job as an image-bearer was to demonstrate who God is to all of creation (==Genesis 1:26==). Sin shattered the mirror—but the gospel doesn’t merely forgive the mirror, it *restores* it. That’s why Paul can say we are ambassadors for Christ, “as though God were pleading through us” (==2 Corinthians 5:20==). God did what only God could do: He reconciled us to Himself. And now that we’re restored to the original design, the original assignment resumes—image bearers filling the earth with the knowledge of His glory.

So don’t shrink this verse down to something manageable. The hurting people in your life aren’t waiting for a better argument for God. They’re waiting to *see* Him—and the gospel’s audacious claim is that they can: in a people of whom it is written, “as He is, so are we in this world.”
