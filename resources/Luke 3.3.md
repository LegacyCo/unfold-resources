## Commentary
John's gospel of repentance was aimed at changing the behavior of his hearers. His gospel of repentance made the "crooked places straight and the rough ways smooth" (v5). When the people asked John "what shall we do?" his response was to point them to the law and modify their behavior: "He who has two tunics, let him give to him who has none, he who has food let him do likewise...collect no more than what is appointed for you...do not intimidate anyone or accuse falsely" (v11-14).

John’s gospel was a gospel of behavior modification — because it was still an *Old Covenant* message. We can't make the mistake in thinking just because John’s story is told in the New Testament that he preached the New Covenant gospel. He did not. John prepared the way for the Messiah, but his message was still an Old Covenant message of behavior modification.

This is all that the gospel of repentance has to offer, because only the One mightier, who comes *after* John, can baptize in the Holy Spirit, which changes a man from the inside out. Only the One who baptizes in fire can burn away the efforts of the law and reveal the Father. Only the Spirit of Christ can change the way a man thinks about God.

## Application
The gospel of the New Covenant is not, “*Stop sinning*.” The gospel of the kingdom is, “You _can’t_ stop sinning. Admit it. Give up.” 

And it’s in that _surrender_ where we find healing for the disease of sin. That’s the beautiful starting line of repentance; the realization that we can’t heal ourselves.
