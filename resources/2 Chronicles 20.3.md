## Commentary
“And Jehoshaphat feared, and set himself to seek the LORD, and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah.”

Jehoshaphat was one of Judah's good kings—a reformer who sent teachers through every town with the Book of the Law (==2 Chronicles 17:7-9==). And when three enemy nations came against him at once, the text says he was afraid. Fear does not disqualify a leader. What matters is where the fear sends him. Jehoshaphat took his fear and let it turn him toward the Lord—he set himself to seek God, and he called the whole nation to a fast. The same fear that drives one man to scheming, self-reliance, or paralysis drove this king to prayer. Fear is a fork in the road: it will push a man somewhere, and the direction is his choice (==Psalm 56:3==).

The fast is part of the seeking. Confronted with a force he could not survive, Jehoshaphat's first act was not mobilization but humility—the embodied confession that Judah could not save itself (==Esther 4:16==; ==Ezra 8:21==). The rest of the chapter flows from this verse: the honest prayer of emptiness (==2 Chronicles 20:12==), the word of the Lord through Jahaziel—“the battle is not yours, but God's” (==2 Chronicles 20:15==)—and the victory God won while His people sang (==2 Chronicles 20:22==). None of it happens if the fear had sent the king anywhere else.

## Word Study
**Seek** is the Hebrew *darash* (דָּרַשׁ)—to seek with care, to inquire of, to pursue; the word for resorting to God as the source of counsel and help. David uses it of crisis prayer: “I sought (*darash*) the LORD, and He heard me, and delivered me from all my fears” (==Psalm 34:4==). The Chronicler makes it the measure of every king—Judah's kings rise or fall on whether they *darash* the LORD (==2 Chronicles 26:5==). Summary: *darash* is fear converted into pursuit—Jehoshaphat did not just feel his need of God; he set himself to go after Him.
