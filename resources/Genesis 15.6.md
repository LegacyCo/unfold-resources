## Commentary
“And he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness.”

On what basis did Abraham believe? What proof was he given? What miracle swayed him, what theological argument demonstrated that this God was better than the gods of Ur? None of those things were given to him. Abraham's story is simply this: the word of God came to him, and he believed it. God did not make an exception for Abraham and supply special evidence. That is exactly why righteousness was transferred to him—it was a righteousness that comes only by faith (==Romans 4:3==), and faith is only present where there is no proof. Where there is verifiable evidence, what is at work is not faith but good decision-making. “Faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen” (==Hebrews 11:1==).

Abraham's friends and family must have asked the obvious questions. You have a new God? You are giving up everything you have here to follow this God into the wilderness and live in tents in a place you have never seen—and what evidence do you have that this God is real, that this land exists, that any of it is true? The life of a sojourner was the cost; Abraham would live in tents and be a minority in a foreign land. But there was something about this God that he could trust. Abraham believed that what God said was true, and that whatever he gave up in his hometown was worth it, because something better could only be found in a new land with this God.

If Abraham is the father of the faithful (==Romans 4:11==), then nothing exceptional was made available to him that is not available to everyone after him. The invitation is the same: “Come out from the life you are living and follow Me.” The objection is the same: “On what basis can I believe You?” And the answer is the same: “On the basis that you believe I am who I say I am” (==Hebrews 11:6==).

## Word Study
**Believed** is the Hebrew *aman* (אָמַן)—to be firm, to be established, to lean one's whole weight upon; the root behind *amen* and *emunah*, “faithfulness.” Isaiah uses it in a play on words: “If you will not believe (*aman*), surely you shall not be established (*aman*)” (==Isaiah 7:9==), and Habakkuk's “the just shall live by his faith (*emunah*)” (==Habakkuk 2:4==) is the verse Paul builds justification by faith on (==Romans 1:17==). Summary: Abraham's believing was not agreeing with a proposition; it was putting his full weight on God's word with nothing else beneath him—and God counted that as righteousness.
