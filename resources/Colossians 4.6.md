## Commentary
"Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one."

Paul names the base ingredient of Christian speech: grace. Not sometimes—*always*. Whatever else is in the sentence, grace is what it's cooked in. Then he adds the seasoning: salt. In Paul's world salt did two things—it preserved food from rotting, and it made it worth eating. Speech seasoned with salt does the same. It keeps conversations from decaying into gossip, complaint, and corruption (==Ephesians 4:29==), and it has *flavor*—words with life in them, words a weary person actually wants to receive.

Grace and salt together produce something rare: gentleness with authority. Words of love that still have an edge of truth. Jesus spoke this way constantly—full of grace and truth at the same time (==John 1:14==).

And notice why Paul says we need this: "that you may know how you ought to answer *each one*." Not "everyone"—each one. Seasoned speech is fitted to the individual in front of you. The same truth is served to a skeptic, a struggler, and a scoffer. A mouth governed by grace learns to season to the person, not just to the topic.
