## Commentary
“If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new.”

Paul does not say that anyone in Christ is slowly becoming a new creation. He announces a present reality: if you are in Christ, the new creation has already begun.

The gospel is more than God improving the person you used to be. The old life was crucified with Christ (==Romans 6:6==). It passed through His death and was buried with Him. What came out of the grave was not Adam repaired. It was resurrection life.

This means your former identity no longer has the authority to introduce you.

You may remember what you did, but what you did is no longer the truest statement about who you are. You may still encounter temptations that belonged to the old life, but temptation is not identity. The enemy works by presenting something dead as though it were still alive and then persuading you to answer to its former name.

Paul says the old has passed away. Let it stay passed away.

The difficulty is that we often possess a new life while continuing to think according to the old one. An inheritance can belong to you while remaining unused. The resources are present, but you must learn to live from what has been placed in your account.

How you see your new life in Christ will determine how much of that life you walk in. When you see yourself primarily as a sinner trying to become holy, every failure confirms the identity you already expected. When you see yourself as a new creation learning to walk in the holiness Christ has given, failure becomes a contradiction to confront rather than a name to accept.

Identity does not eliminate obedience. It makes obedience possible. You put on the new man because the new man has already been created “according to God, in true righteousness and holiness” (==Ephesians 4:24==).

The new creation is not waiting for you in heaven. It has already entered the world in you.

## Application
Listen to the names you use when explaining your failures:

“That is just how I am.”

“My family has always been this way.”

“I will never change.”

Those statements agree with a creation that Paul says has passed away. Replace them with the truth: *I am in Christ. The old has gone. The new has come.* Now bring your habits, relationships, words, spending, desires, and decisions into agreement with who you actually are.
