## Commentary
“Now the boy Samuel ministered to the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread revelation.”

The statement describes the spiritual condition of an era. In the days of Eli, God was not speaking often—prophetic word was scarce, and there was “no widespread revelation,” no vision breaking through to the nation. Scripture does not present this as God's arbitrary silence, as if God just didn't have anything to say in those days. The surrounding chapters describe the condition of the priesthood that was supposed to carry God's word: Eli's sons were corrupt priests who did not know the LORD, treating the offerings with contempt and the women at the tabernacle door as their own (==1 Samuel 2:12==; ==1 Samuel 2:17==; ==1 Samuel 2:22==), while Eli honored his sons above God (==1 Samuel 2:29==). It was the era of the judges, when everyone did what was right in his own eyes (==Judges 21:25==). The scarcity of the word corresponded to the condition of the people appointed to receive it. Amos later describes the same scarcity as a judgment: “I will send a famine on the land, not a famine of bread… but of hearing the words of the LORD” (==Amos 8:11==).

The chapter's details reinforce this. Eli's eyes “had begun to grow so dim that he could not see” (==1 Samuel 3:2==), and “the lamp of God had not yet gone out” (==1 Samuel 3:3==)—the priesthood's sight failing, the light in God's house burning low but not extinguished. And in that setting the silence ends, not with a reformation of the institution but with a boy ministering to the LORD who, when the voice came, answered, “Speak, for Your servant hears” (==1 Samuel 3:10==). By the end of the chapter, the LORD is revealing Himself again at Shiloh, and He “let none of Samuel's words fall to the ground” (==1 Samuel 3:19-21==). The word of the LORD was rare in those days because listeners were rare. When God found one, the silence ended.

## Word Study
**Rare** is the Hebrew *yaqar* (יָקָר)—precious, costly, prized; the word used elsewhere for precious stones (==2 Samuel 12:30==) and for what is highly valued in God's sight (==Psalm 116:15==). The word of the LORD was *yaqar*: scarce the way a treasure is scarce, and valuable for the same reason. Summary: in Eli's day the word of God was a rarity to be treasured rather than a commonplace to be presumed on—and the chapter shows what kind of person God entrusts it to when He breaks the silence.
