## Commentary
“Just as David also describes the blessedness of the man to whom God imputes righteousness apart from works.”

Don’t rush past the word *blessedness*. It’s not describing a mild religious happiness—it’s the deep well-being, the complete wholeness, that comes inside a person when God’s declaration of righteousness is heard over their life. 

Complete wholeness doesn’t come when you die and finally receive a glorified body. It doesn’t come through discipline and self-effort—we don’t fast and pray our way into wholeness. Complete wholeness comes *when God’s declaration of righteousness is heard over your life*—when you actually hear Him tell you who you are.

The moment you believed in Him who raised Jesus from the dead, God released that declaration over you: *this one is now righteous* (==Romans 4:24==). Righteousness means restored to the original design—so when God calls you righteous, He’s saying, “You remind Me of Me” (==Genesis 1:26==). Wholeness was never meant to be the finish line you crawl toward. It was meant to be the starting point you live from.

This matters for the broken. God is near to the brokenhearted (==Psalm 34:18==)—but He doesn’t come near just to leave you broken and help you limp through the pearly gates someday. He comes near to make you whole, now, in this life. And how you react when someone tells you “in Christ, you are as righteous as Jesus” is a fairly reliable indicator of how whole you are. If everything in you flinches at that sentence, it may be because you’ve been listening to voices—religion, the world, your own mistakes—that have been telling you who you are louder than God has.

The answer for a broken world is not just a God who is righteous and holy, but a *people* who have become righteous and holy—not a people who never mess up, not a people keeping strict religious laws, but a people who know who God says they are. God intends to show the world He is so holy that He takes dirty, broken, dead sinners—former enemies—and makes them holy too. That doesn’t diminish His holiness. It exalts it.
