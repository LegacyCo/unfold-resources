## Commentary
“So God blessed Noah and his sons, and said to them: ‘Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth.’”

The first thing God does with Noah outside the ark is bless him—in the same words He spoke over Adam at creation (==Genesis 1:28==). The world is beginning again, and it begins with blessing. This is the first mark of the covenant family of God: they are a blessed people, and they know where the blessing comes from. Blessing is the story of everyone who has entered into covenant with God—provision when there was no money, healing when there was no doctor, direction when there was no way forward, understanding when there was confusion.

The mistake the covenant family makes is not in receiving blessing but in shifting their gaze. When a believer stops looking to God for blessing and starts looking around—comparing what he has with what others have, or looking at what the world can offer—his understanding of what a blessing is and where it comes from becomes contaminated, and he stops acting like a covenant son. Israel's wilderness gives us a picture of this. When God gave them water from the rock, it was pure (==Exodus 17:6==). When they drank the water that came out of the desert ground, it was bitter (==Exodus 15:23==). Desert water is brackish from its contact with the earth; the flavor of the ground has gotten into it. So it is with blessings sought from the world: whatever the world gives will carry the taste of earth and turn bitter in the end. God may use the things of this world as the instrument of blessing, but the covenant family knows the origin is always Him: “Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of lights” (==James 1:17==).

## Application
Check the direction of your gaze. Comparison is the first sign that it has turned from vertical to horizontal—when you find yourself measuring your house, income, or opportunities against someone else's, you have started looking to the ground for what only comes from above. Deliberately trace each blessing in your life back to its source, name God as the giver, and refuse to drink from the sand. Whatever He gives will be pure.
