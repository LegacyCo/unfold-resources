## Commentary
“By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to the place which he would receive as an inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going.”

There’s a story about a French general who sent a young soldier to the bridge of Basle with strict orders: stay one hour, watch everyone closely, report back what you see. The soldier obeyed, though the assignment seemed pointless. He watched soldiers come and go, children play, a girl sell flowers—and an old man tap his stick three times against the bridge wall. He returned disappointed and confused, and reported it all. “How many times did the man tap his stick?” the general asked. The soldier had no idea he had just delivered the prearranged signal to attack a neighboring fortress that very night. Neither the man tapping nor the soldier watching understood why they had been sent to that bridge. But both obeyed—and that was enough.

That’s the quality of Abraham’s faith in this verse. He obeyed *not knowing*. No destination, no map, no explanation—just a voice and a direction. Scripture doesn’t say Abraham made a wise decision. Kingdom people don’t make smart decisions; they obey God’s word. The moment I require understanding as a prerequisite to obedience, I’ve inserted my need for answers into a system that runs entirely on faith—and without faith it is impossible to please Him (==Hebrews 11:6==). Obedience in the *absence* of understanding is the very thing that becomes a witness: the evidence of things not seen—or understood (==Hebrews 11:1==).

So the question isn’t whether the assignment makes sense—go here, give that, forgive them. The question is whether we’re willing to stand on the bridge without knowing why. God is watching for the ones who will.
