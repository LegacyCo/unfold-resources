## Commentary
“There is one who scatters, yet increases more; and there is one who withholds more than is right, but it leads to poverty.”

The proverb describes an economy that runs backward from the world’s. In the world’s math, scattering depletes and withholding preserves. In the kingdom’s math, the scatterer increases and the withholder ends up poor. The next verse doubles down: “The generous soul will be made rich, and he who waters will himself be watered” (==Proverbs 11:25==). Solomon isn’t describing a lucky exception. He’s describing how the kingdom works: sowing and reaping. “Honor the LORD with your possessions, and with the firstfruits of all your increase; so your barns will be filled with plenty” (==Proverbs 3:9-10==).

And don’t shrink this to money. Kingdom people are always sowing—finances and resources, yes, but also love, wisdom, hospitality, forgiveness, time, and honor. The hand-written thank-you card. The “I just wanted to tell you how thankful I am for you” phone call. And because they’re always sowing, they’re always reaping—the same things, pressed down and running over (==Luke 6:38==). This may be the open secret of why kingdom people always seem to have what they need: their storehouses overflow because their scattering never stopped.

## Application
Here’s the principle in work clothes. When it’s moving day and you need half a dozen people to load your U-Haul, it’s too late to reap from soil you never sowed into. But if you’re the first to say yes when someone *else* in the community needs their truck loaded—if you’ve been bringing the meals, watching the kids, showing up early to set up chairs—then when your moving day comes, you won’t have to recruit help. You’ll reap people who genuinely want to be there, because you already sowed into that ground. So don’t wait until you’re in need to discover what you’ve planted. Scatter now—into the household of faith especially (==Galatians 6:10==)—and let the harvest surprise you.
