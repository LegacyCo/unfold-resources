## Commentary
“I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that you present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service.”

Fire doesn’t fall on empty altars. When Solomon dedicated the temple, the fire came down from heaven and consumed *the sacrifice*—the offering was already on the altar (==2 Chronicles 7:1==). Elijah on Carmel laid the bull on the wood before he prayed (==1 Kings 18:33==). The fire of God is always looking for a sacrifice to consume—and Paul tells you what the new covenant sacrifice is: your body. Your life, presented, laid out, kept on the altar. That’s why so few believers are actually burning. It isn’t that God is withholding fire; it’s that there’s nothing on the altar to feed the flame. Without fuel, fires go out.

Notice what Paul doesn’t prescribe for the church at Rome. He doesn’t tell them to tarry in an upper room until the fire falls—Pentecost already happened; the church’s birth certificate is already dated. Yet so many of us are waiting for something external to ignite us: the next conference, the anointed minister praying over us, revival finally hitting our church. Do we pray for one another and long for revival? Absolutely. But while the church waits for God to send something new, God is waiting for a people who will apprehend what He already sent. “The promise is to you and your children” (==Acts 2:39==). What if the source of revival fire in our day isn’t God doing something new—but God’s people believing that what He already gave is actually for them?

Some will say this sounds like works: “We’re under grace—He desires steadfast love, not sacrifice.” But the same pen that wrote “by grace you have been saved through faith… not of works” (==Ephesians 2:8-9==) also wrote *present your bodies*, keep your spirit boiling (==Romans 12:11==), and fan the gift into flame (==2 Timothy 1:6==). Everything we receive is grace—and grace makes room for human responsibility. He sends the fire; you supply the fuel. So the question this verse leaves on the table isn’t whether God will move. It’s simpler and more uncomfortable: is your life a living sacrifice? Are you flammable?
