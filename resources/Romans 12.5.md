## Commentary
“so we, being many, are one body in Christ, and individually members of one another.”

Paul has just said that a human body has many parts and they don't all do the same job (==Romans 12:4==). Here he says the church is like that, and he tells us where its unity comes from. We are one body “in Christ.” Believers aren't one because we share a background, a culture, a political outlook, or a taste in music. We're one because each of us has been joined to the same Lord. Union with Christ comes first, and union with each other flows down from it. That's why Jesus could say to a man who was arresting Christians, “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?” (==Acts 9:4==). To lay hands on the body was to lay hands on Him.

Then Paul adds a significant phrase: “and individually members of one another.” He could have stopped at saying we're each members of Christ. He says we're members of *each other*. A “member” here is a body part, a limb or an organ. A hand belongs to the body, and it also belongs to the arm, the eye, and the foot, because they depend on it and it depends on them. Paul is saying that about you and the other believers in your church. You belong to them, and they belong to you.

That works in two directions. Your gifts aren't yours to keep. They were given for the body, which is why Paul goes straight on to list them (==Romans 12:6-8==). And you can't live the Christian life by yourself. “The eye cannot say to the hand, ‘I have no need of you’” (==1 Corinthians 12:21==).

Much of the rest of the chapter depends on this verse. “Be kindly affectionate to one another” (==Romans 12:10==) and “weep with those who weep” (==Romans 12:15==) only make sense if we're actually attached to each other. Paul uses the same reasoning for honesty in Ephesians: “let each one of you speak truth with his neighbor, for we are members of one another” (==Ephesians 4:25==).

## Application
This verse doesn't fit with treating church as an event to attend. So ask two questions about your local church. Who there is being helped by what God has given you? And who there knows you well enough to help you? If the answer to either one is “no one,” start there. Join a small group, begin serving somewhere, or invite someone into your life this week.
