## Commentary
The mandate to be fruitful and multiply is one of the most important redemptive storylines in scripture. God uses the same language throughout Scripture:
	-when He makes a covenant with Noah after the flood (Genesis 9:6-7), 
	-when He makes a covenant with Abraham (Genesis 17:2, 6, Isaiah 51:1-2), 
	-when He makes a covenant with the descendants of Abraham (Genesis 22:17-18), -when He makes a covenant with Isaac (Genesis 26:4), 
	-when He makes a covenant with Jacob (Genesis 28:3, Genesis 35:11), 
	-when He makes a covenant with Israel in Egypt (Genesis 47:27, Exodus 1:7), 
	-when He makes a covenant with Israel in the wilderness (Leviticus 26:9), 
	-when He makes a covenant with Israel in Babylonian captivity (Jeremiah 3:16), 
	-when Israel was scattered in all of the nations (Jeremiah 23:3, Ezekiel 36:10). 

And this covenantal mandate to fill the earth isn’t just for the Old Testament. The book of Acts refers to the Genesis 1:28 mandate four times in describing how the word of God is multiplying and filling the earth; (Acts 6:7, Acts 12:24, Acts 19:20). For example, Acts 6:7 says,

“The word of God spread and the number of disciples multiplied greatly.” 

The two verbs that Luke uses (spread & multiplied) are *identical* to Genesis 1:28. 

Paul does the same thing in Colossians 1:6,

“The gospel has come to you...and is bringing forth fruit.”

In Jesus' instructions to His disciples in Matthew 28 you can hear echoes of the Genesis 1:28 mandate to multiply and fill the earth,

"Go and make disciples of all the nations" (Matthew 28:19).

Interestingly, what does Jesus say to His disciples right before He tells them to be fruitful and multiply?

"All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth" (Matthew 28:18).

When the Son of Man regained humanities authority, He reiterated the Genesis 1:28 commission to go multiply His image in the earth.
