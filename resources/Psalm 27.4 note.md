## Commentary
“One thing I have desired of the LORD, that will I seek: that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to inquire in His temple.”

David does not stop at desire. “One thing I have desired… *that will I seek*.” Desire is good, but if it never moves into seeking, it is just curiosity. “Seek, and you will find” (==Matthew 7:7==)—the promise is attached to the seeking, not the wanting. The call of God comes by pure grace, exclusive of anything deserved or earned. But what happens next must be intentional. People do not stumble their way into passion; no one accidentally finds themselves burning for God. Some things have to be removed and other things put in place so that a person can burn. Abraham packing up and leaving Ur was an intentional act (==Genesis 12:4==). The call is grace; what you do with it is a choice. There has to be room in our theology for human responsibility.

We understand this in every other area of life. No one succeeds in business, sports, fitness, or marriage by accident; a strong marriage exists because two people intentionally prioritize and pursue one another, and a bodybuilder does not stumble into his discipline. Yet we often treat a mature spiritual walk as something that should just happen. Jesus' charge against the church of Ephesus was that they had left their first love (==Revelation 2:4==)—not because they stopped believing, but because they stopped seeking, stopped burning, stopped living surrendered. A man can remain married and still leave the love he had at first, if he stops pursuing. The distinction is the difference between sonship and maturity: a son is born into his father's house and does nothing to earn it, but maturing into a grown man requires intentionality. Sonship just happens; maturity never does. So Scripture commands pursuit in the language of effort: “Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold of eternal life” (==1 Timothy 6:12==); “forgetting those things which are behind… I press on toward the goal” (==Philippians 3:13-14==).

## Application
Move one desire into seeking this week. Take the thing you have wanted in God—deeper prayer, a burning heart, a recovered first love—and treat it the way you would a goal in any other part of life: decide what has to be removed, decide what has to be put in place, and pursue it on purpose. Ask the Ephesian question honestly: have I stopped seeking what I still claim to love? The fire does not come by accident, but it does come to those who seek.
