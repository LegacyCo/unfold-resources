## Commentary
Six hundred years before Nicodemus climbed the stairs to meet Jesus at night, God made a promise through Ezekiel: “I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean; I will cleanse you from all your filthiness and from all your idols. I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes” (==Ezekiel 36:25-27==).

Read the sequence of that promise: cleansing water, a new heart, a softened heart, God’s own Spirit put *within*. Now listen to Jesus tell Nicodemus, “unless one is born of *water and the Spirit*, he cannot enter the kingdom of God” (==John 3:5==). Jesus isn’t inventing new vocabulary—He’s announcing that Ezekiel’s promise has arrived. And that’s why He presses Nicodemus: “Are you the teacher of Israel, and do not know these things?” (==John 3:10==). A teacher of Israel should have heard “water and Spirit” and immediately thought of Ezekiel 36. 

Notice also who does everything in the promise. I will sprinkle. I will cleanse. I will give. I will take out. I will put within. Six hundred years of Israel’s history had proven that hearts of stone can’t soften themselves and filthy people can’t clean themselves. So God promised to do Himself what His people could never do—and that’s exactly what the new birth is. Not a self-improvement program for the old heart, but a transplant: the heart of stone lifted out, a heart of flesh set in, and the Spirit of God taking up residence to empower from the inside what the law could only demand from the outside (==Ezekiel 36:27==; ==Romans 8:3-4==).

If you’re in Christ, this promise is your biography. You were sprinkled clean (==Hebrews 10:22==). You received the transplant. His Spirit lives in you and causes you to walk in His ways—not by pushing you from behind, but by beating inside your chest.

## Cross References
The new-birth promise across the prophets: ==Jeremiah 31:33== (the law written on hearts), ==Joel 2:28== (the Spirit poured out on all flesh), ==Ezekiel 11:19-20== (the promise given earlier, almost word for word), ==2 Corinthians 3:3== (written “not on tablets of stone but on tablets of flesh, that is, of the heart”).
