## Commentary
"And according to the law almost all things are purified with blood, and without shedding of blood there is no remission."

Why blood? Of all the ways God could have dealt with sin, why the one that costs a life?

Because sin is not a smudge to be wiped off; it's death that has entered the world (==Romans 5:12==, ==Romans 6:23==). And the answer to death is life—and Scripture says the life is *in the blood* (==Leviticus 17:11==). Blood on the altar was never about violence for its own sake. It was life given in the place of life forfeited. That's what remission cost from the very beginning.

And notice when the sacrificial system started. Not at Sinai. In the garden. The moment man sinned, God Himself made the first sacrifice—He clothed Adam and Eve in skins, which means something died so their shame could be covered (==Genesis 3:21==). Think about what that reveals. Sin had just created a separation between God and the object of His love, and God's *immediate* response was to establish a way to keep that separation from being permanent. Blood was God's provision, not God's demand. Love was already building a bridge on the very day the chasm opened.

Every lamb, every bull, every goat after that was a shadow pointing at the same thing (==Hebrews 10:1==): the day when God would stop accepting substitutes and give the real one. "The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world" (==John 1:29==). Not blood *for* God to be appeased, but blood *from* God to redeem—His own (==Acts 20:28==).

So when you read that without shedding of blood there is no forgiveness, don't hear a God who is hard to satisfy. Hear a God who was willing to pay whatever it took to keep His people near.
