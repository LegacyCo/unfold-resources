## Commentary
Faith does not wait for the situation to look realistic before it believes God can move. Naturally speaking, nothing about this moment looked promising. But Jonathan knew the Lord was not limited by his ability or his available resources. 

Many or few made no difference if God was the One saving. This is the same lesson the Lord taught Gideon when He reduced his army, saying, “The people who are with you are too many for Me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel claim glory for itself against Me, saying, ‘My own hand has saved me’” (==Judges 7:2==). God will often reduce what man would rather increase so that the victory cannot be confused with human strength.

It's tempting to think, “When the team grows, when the money comes, when the opportunity opens, when the numbers make sense, then I’ll step out.” But that kind of thinking reveals that we still believe increase is the source of possibility.

Jonathan understood that miracles begin with God, not favorable conditions. The Lord can use an army, and He can use two men climbing up a rocky pass with nothing but courage and confidence in Him.

This is why we must not despise the day of small beginnings (==Zechariah 4:10==). What looks small in the eyes of man may be the exact place where God intends to reveal that the victory was never dependent on man in the first place.

Jonathan’s faith was not arrogance. He says, “It may be that the LORD will work for us.” He wasn’t presuming on God. He was making himself available to God. He knew he could not control the outcome, but he also knew lack was not an obstacle to the Lord.
