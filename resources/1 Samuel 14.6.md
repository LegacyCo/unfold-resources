## Commentary
We restrain what the Lord wants to do by waiting for the *many* when He intends to use the *few*. For example, Gideon's army was too big. Glory could be claimed by Gideon or Israel if they defeated their enemy with such a large force. So God trimmed it down to 300 (see Judges 7:2).

God tells Zechariah, "Do not despise the days of small beginnings" (Zechariah 4:10). The context of these words to Zechariah is the remnant of Israel has returned to Jerusalem from exile to rebuild the temple. Their numbers are few and the work is filled with challenges. But the Lord encourages His people, "It is not by might, nor by power, but by My Spirit" that the work will be accomplished.

He not only saves by a few, He builds with a few.

## Application
What if the Lord intends to do something impossible with "the few" that you see around you? Could the presence of "the many" (resources, people, talents, etc) dilute the response of the world to the miracle God want's to accomplish through you?
