## Commentary
“Or do you not know, brethren (for I speak to those who know the law), that the law has dominion over a man as long as he lives?”

Paul is stating a legal principle that no one would argue with: the law's authority over a man has a time limit, and the limit is his life. The law is a powerful master over the living. It commands, binds, accuses, and collects. But its dominion stops at the grave. 

The law's dominion over an individual is legitimate. Which means there is only one legitimate way out from under it: not reform, not exemption, not trying harder to satisfy it, but death. And that's exactly what the gospel says every follower of Jesus has experienced. “For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God” (==Colossians 3:3==). The principle of verse 1, applied to a person who has died with Christ, means the old jurisdiction of the law has lost its claim. The man it had dominion over is no longer alive to it.

## Word Study
**Has dominion** is the Greek *kyrieuō* (κυριεύω)—to be lord over, to exercise mastery. Paul has been dethroning one *kyrieuō* after another: “death no longer has dominion over Him” (==Romans 6:9==), “sin shall not have dominion over you” (==Romans 6:14==), and now the law's dominion is bounded by death. Summary: every old master's lordship ends out at the grave.
