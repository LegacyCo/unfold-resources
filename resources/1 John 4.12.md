## Commentary
“No one has seen God at any time. If we love one another, God abides in us, and His love has been perfected in us.”

The implication of what John is saying here is staggering. John states the problem—no one has ever seen God—and then, instead of pointing to an argument or an apologetic, he points to a community: if we love one another, God abides in us. Our love reveals the unseen God.

John had used almost identical words once before: “No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son… He has declared Him” (==John 1:18==). Jesus made the invisible God visible. And now, in his letter, John dares to put the church in that same sentence structure. The Son revealed God by His life; the church reveals God by her love. When a community of believers walks in humility and gentleness—bearing with one another in love, forgiving one another, refusing to divide (==Ephesians 4:2-3==)—the invisible Christ becomes visible again.

This is why our life together is never just about us. The world cannot see God. It can only see what He looks like when He abides in a people. Every act of patience with a difficult brother, every forgiveness extended instead of a grudge kept, every refusal to split over what could divide—each one is a small unveiling. And the reverse is just as true: a church devouring itself testifies to a god no one would want to see.

Jesus prayed for exactly this before the cross: “that they all may be one… that the world may believe that You sent Me” (==John 17:21==). The unity and love of His people was His chosen evidence. It still is.
