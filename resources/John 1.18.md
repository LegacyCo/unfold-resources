## Commentary
The way the NASB and ESV translate this verse is beautiful. Specifically, I love the way the middle of the verse is translated, "God the only Son." It is true to the original Greek. 

The NKJV wrongly translates that part of the verse, "The only begotten Son." By translating it this way, the NKJV leaves out entirely the second occurrence of the word *theos* (God). It is very important to note that John uses *theos* twice in this verse; once at the beginning, "No one has seen *theos* (God) at any time," and once in the middle, "*Theos* (God) the only Son."

Why is this important? John is saying the only Son, who is in the arms of the Father, who has come to reveal the Father, He is God.

Another important word to point out in this verse: *exegeomai*. Exegeomai means to make know, to explain, tell, unfold, to interpret.

God the only Son, who is in the arms of Father, has come to make known God in a way that no one has ever seen before. Only the Son can reveal the Father, because only the Son knows Him as He truly is. While Moses saw YHWH, He knew nothing of the Father — which is why John leads with this distinction in v17, "The law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ."

Many had seen God before (Jacob - Genesis 32:30, Aaron, his sons, and the seventy elders of Israel - Exodus 24:9-11, Samson's parents - Judges 13:22, Isaiah - Isaiah 6:1, Moses - Numbers 12:8). But what they all saw was just a *glimpse* of God, not the fullness. Only the Son can reveal the fullness of who God is. This is why Jesus says in John 14:9, "If you've seen *ME* you've seen the Father."

God revealed Himself to humanity in a progressive manner. To Abraham He was revealed as El Shaddai. To Moses He was revealed as Yahweh ("I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as aGod Almighty, but by My name, LORD, I did not make Myself known to them" - Exodus 6:3). To put it another way, Abraham did not know God the same way Moses did. Had God changed? Of course not. God just explained a different aspect of Himself to Moses that He had not previously made known.

And then for 1,300 years, during the time between Moses and Jesus, Israel knew God as Yahweh. But when the fullness of time had come, Jesus reveals yet *another* name for God, Abban — "Abba, I have revealed Your name to them" (John 17:6).

Each of God's names reveal a part of His nature and character. He is the healer, so His name is Jehovah Rapha (the Lord our healer). He is the creator, so His name is Elohim (Creator, mighty and strong). He is the self-existent One, so His name is Yahweh (I am who I am). And many other names reveal His nature, His character, and how He relates to humanity. So every time Jesus would refer to God as Abba, He was making an astounding claim: He is a Father. And not only "Father," He is Pappa, or Dadda. Abba would be the affectionate term a child would call their father. The syllables are some of the first sounds a child can make, often coming out as "babba" which is the Arabic term for abba.
