## Commentary
This is from "The Holiest of All" by Andrew Murray: How many Christians are there who, after the profession of faith in Christ, come to a standstill…So many rest contented with the thought that their sins are pardoned, and that they are in the path of life, but know nothing of a personal attachment to Christ as their Leader, or of a faith that lives in the invisible and walks with God. With many this is the consequence of the hopelessness that came from the failure of their efforts to live as they desired. They struggled in their own strength; they knew not Christ as the secret of strength; they lost heart, and went back. Their profession of faith is not cast away; their religious habits are kept up; but there is nothing to show that they (are maturing). The power of the world, the spirit of its literature, the temptations of business and pleasure, all unite to make up a religion in which it is sought to combine a comfortable hope for the future with the least possible amount of sacrifice in the present.

## Application
It is possible to begin well and still be choked out.

That's the warning of this parable. Jesus describes those in whom the word truly fell, but as they went on, it was crowded out by the cares, riches, and pleasures of life. The issue was not open rejection. The issue was gradual interference. Other things entered in, pressed in, and eventually suffocated what began to grow.

That's where many believers live. They have enough faith to maintain a profession, enough sincerity to keep religious habits, enough conscience to want heaven, but not enough attachment to Christ to keep growing. Somewhere along the way, disappointment, weariness, self-effort, and worldly distraction dulled the edge of their pursuit. They haven't rejected Jesus. They've just settled into a version of faith that costs little and produces little.

A man can still speak the language of faith while no longer pressing on in faith. He can keep the forms of religion while losing the inward zeal of life with God. He can call Jesus Savior while no longer knowing Him as Lord. And once that happens, the world doesn't need to make him deny Christ outright. It only needs to keep him crowded, preoccupied, tired, entertained, and unwilling to pay the cost of maturity.

Don't confuse maintained religion with maturing life. Don't assume that because you still believe the right things, you are still growing the way God intends. Ask whether your life is actually moving deeper into Christ. Ask whether the word is bearing fruit, or whether it is being choked by the weight of lesser things.

A comfortable Christianity with minimal sacrifice is usually a sign that something is being suffocated.

The answer is never more self-effort. That may be what exhausted you in the first place. The answer is to return to Christ Himself. The Christian life matures by abiding (see John 15:1-9).
