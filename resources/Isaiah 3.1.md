## Commentary
Isaiah ministered in Judah during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah (==Isaiah 1:1==), placing his prophetic ministry in the eighth century BC. This was a time of increasing political pressure. Assyria was rising as the dominant empire in the region, the northern kingdom of Israel would eventually fall to Assyria in 722 BC, and Judah itself would later face invasion by Sennacherib in 701 BC (==2 Kings 17:5–6==; ==2 Kings 18:13==; Isaiah 36–37).

In Isaiah's day Judah is living under the shadow of a rising enemy empire.

But Isaiah doesn't begin by saying Judah’s biggest problem is Assyria. He looks beneath the geopolitical crisis and exposes the spiritual one. Judah has leaned on everything *but* the Lord.

This chapter opens by saying God will take away “the stock and the store,” or literally, the support and supply. The Hebrew words carry the idea of a staff, prop, or support — something a person leans on for stability. Isaiah is saying that God is going to remove the props holding the nation upright.

And the list is comprehensive: Bread and water. Mighty men and warriors. Judges and prophets. Elders and counselors. Captains, honorable men, craftsmen, and skilled speakers.

In other words, God is not only removing material security. He is removing leadership, wisdom, strength, protection, and social stability.

Judah had become a people who still used religious language but no longer trusted God with their lives. They had leaned on human strength, political systems, military power, and wealth. So God says, in effect, “I will remove what you have trusted in so you can see what has really been holding you up.”

## Application
Sometimes God exposes what we've been leaning on by allowing it to fail. This is His mercy showing us that something other than Him has become our support.

If your peace depends on money, God may touch your security. If your confidence depends on what others say about you, God may touch your reputation. If your stability depends on an organized calendar, God may touch your plans.

The question Isaiah 3 asks us is: What are you leaning on?

Because whatever we lean on in place of God will eventually prove too weak to hold us.
