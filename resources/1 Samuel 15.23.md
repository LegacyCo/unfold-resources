## Commentary
God had rejected Saul from being king, yet Saul remained in that position for years afterward. He still had the title, the throne, the army, and the public recognition. Outwardly, very little changed. But inwardly and spiritually, the kingdom had already been torn from him.

Then God anointed David.

In God's eyes, David was the king, even though he did not yet occupy the throne. He carried the anointing without possessing the position. Saul carried the position without the anointing.

That contrast exposes something important about how God works. Heaven can recognize something long before earth reflects it. There can be a great distance between what God has declared over your life and what has become visible in your circumstances. David was not less king because Saul was still sitting on the throne. The delay did not invalidate the anointing. It was forming the man who would eventually carry it.

David also refused to grasp for what God had promised. He had opportunities to kill Saul and take the throne for himself, but he would not demand the position before God's appointed time. He understood that what God gives does not have to be secured through manipulation, striving, or self-promotion. If God had anointed him, then God could establish him.

Saul did the opposite. He held tightly to a position God had already rejected him from. His identity became so attached to the throne that he could not release it. Instead of repenting, he protected his image. Instead of surrendering, he became threatened by anyone who carried what he had lost.

One man had the position but no longer had God's approval. The other had God's approval but not yet the position.

## Application
We must be careful not to judge our lives only by what is visible. A title does not always reveal what God is doing, and the absence of a title does not mean the absence of calling. Sometimes the person on the throne is already being removed, while the person in the wilderness is already being prepared.

David teaches us that we do not have to force what God has spoken. We can carry the promise confidently, honor the process, and trust the Lord to open the door in His time. What God has ordained does not need our flesh to make it happen.
